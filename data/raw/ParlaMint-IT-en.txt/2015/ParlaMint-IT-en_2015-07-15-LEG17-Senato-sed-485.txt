To date it has been signed by 94 countries and ratified by 46 - including 10 members of the European Union, namely Austria, Belgium, France, Germany, Lithuania, Malta, the Netherlands, Portugal, Slovakia and Spain. Normally associated with the Latin American dictatorships but not foreign to other contexts, the phenomenon of forced disappearances, sometimes ascribed to the responsibility and complicity of the Armed Forces and often covered by measures to promote the impunity of the authors, is still sadly present in many areas of the world, as evidenced by the work of the Working Group of the United Nations Council for Human Rights. This body, which is responsible for helping the families of missing persons to ascertain the fate of their missing relatives and to monitor compliance by States with their international obligations in this field, has in about thirty-five years transmitted more than 54,000 cases to 104 governments. Italy, which participated actively in the Working Group and made an important contribution to the promotion of the text, signed the document on 3 July 2007. The Convention constitutes as an offence the arrest, seizure and any other form of deprivation of liberty carried out by agents of the State or persons acting with the support of the State, followed by the refusal to recognize the deprivation of liberty or the concealment of the fate reserved for the missing person and the place where he is. In this way the person is placed outside the protection of the law (according to Articles 2 and 4). The main obligation for States Parties is to provide, within the framework of national legislation, for a rule condemning the practice of forced disappearances as a criminal offence (Article 4). The Convention also defines the widespread and systematic use of forced disappearances as crimes against humanity (Article 5). In order to combat this criminal practice, each State is required to take all necessary measures to ensure that the criminal liability of any person suspected of carrying out, ordering, soliciting or causing forced disappearance is established (Article 6). Chapter II of the Convention concerns the establishment of a Committee of Experts, composed of ten members competent in the field of human rights, responsible for monitoring the application of the Convention and, where appropriate, for examining individual cases. Each State Party shall submit to the Committee a report on the measures taken to meet its obligations within two years of its ratification. The Committee is called upon to examine the report and to make comments, comments or recommendations. It is up to the States Parties to accept the Committee's competence to receive and examine communications from individuals complaining of violations of the Convention. Furthermore, in accordance with Article 33, the Committee may, if it receives reliable information, after consulting the State concerned, carry out a mission in the country and submit a report. In the event of suspected widespread and systematic forced disappearances, the supervisory body shall be authorised to refer the case to the UN General Assembly. The report accompanying the measure shows that in Italian legislation there are provisions for the sanctioning of the conduct integrating to the aforementioned criminal case, such as Articles 605 (sequestration in person), 606 (illegal arrest), 607 (illegal limitation of personal freedom) and 608 (abuse of authorities against prisoners or arrested) of the penal code. The draft ratification law consists of three articles which form the basis for the authorisation of ratification, order of implementation and entry into force of the text. The document consists of 19 articles. It indicates, in Article 3, the areas of cooperation and interest (including, among others, the human exploration of space, the observation of the universe and the Earth), and the ways in which to achieve them (satellites, scientific instruments, satellite and aerial platforms, or human missions and explorations). Article 6, which, in the matter of civil liability of the parties in the event of damages, provides for the reciprocal waiver, except in specific cases, of any claims for compensation. The Agreement then regulates the arrangements for the recording of space objects, the transfer of technical data and goods and the intellectual property regime. It also lays down arrangements for the dissemination to the public of information relating to sector cooperation and the exchange of staff and for mutual access to structures. The draft law of ratification consists of four articles, which contain respectively the authorization to ratify, the order of execution, the clause of financial invariance and the entry into force. Article 3, in particular, specifies that the implementation of the law must not result in new or greater burdens for public finance and that the Italian Space Agency is authorised to take charge of those arising from any customs or other taxes. Overall, Italy and the United States can boast fifty years of cooperation in space. This Agreement therefore aims to better regulate and consolidate, also in view of future challenges, technological and scientific synergies between the parties. The Agreement shall be limited to ten years, unless an extension is agreed in writing or an early termination provided for in Article 19. The Agreement does not conflict with Community law and, in terms of international law, is in line with the 1967 Treaty on the Exploration and Use of the Extra-Atmospheric Space. The Agreement is also in line with the European Commission's Communication "Towards a European Union Space Strategy for Citizens" of 4 April 2011, which outlines three types of objectives (social, economic and strategic) related to space policy within the European region. In conclusion, it is proposed that the Assembly approve this measure.
