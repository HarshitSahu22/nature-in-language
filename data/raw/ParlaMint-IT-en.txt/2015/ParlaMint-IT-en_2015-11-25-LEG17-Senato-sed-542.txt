I listened carefully to many of the speeches. I welcome for everyone, on behalf of the Senate, the demands of Senator Uras, who calls on the Government to intervene uniformly with regard to natural disasters. I want to take five minutes to sum up what the government is doing. If it is true, as it is true, that in the past, apart from the two great earthquakes on which specific rules have been introduced for reconstructions, all other types of natural disasters have seen in the second phase, that is in the phase of reconstruction, diversified interventions - therefore, on the analysis of the problem we absolutely agree with what emerged in this debate - we cannot fail to emphasize, even with some pride and some energy, that instead we are heading exactly towards a new organic legislation. It will have to guarantee to all those who have suffered, at least since 2013, and who will suffer a natural disaster - hopefully as little as possible - a treatment that will be the same for all. Of course, earthquakes are excluded from this reasoning. I summarize the work that the Government is facing on the reform of natural disasters. There are two stages. The first involves immediate intervention as soon as the facts happen. This is the responsibility of civil protection, and that is what we call phase one in jargon. The rules concerning civil protection are quite recent. The latest revisions date back to 2013, but a further reform was approved at first reading, which is a draft delegation law on civil protection, which will further improve the efficiency of civil protection intervention in the first phase. At that stage, civil protection can access national resources that are allocated to the so-called National Emergency Fund (ENF), which for 2015 is being refinanced since, like so many of you, colleagues from Calabria, Sicilian and Campania, have highlighted in this House, in this last month there have been numerous calamitous demonstrations. We refinance it, therefore, for 50 million with the measure that is being published. In addition, in the Stability Act, already approved at first reading by this authoritative Chamber, additional 100 million for 2016 are allocated, in addition to the 150 million already foreseen. The National Emergency Fund - I have seen that many senators have used it to cover reconstruction interventions - we do not use it to rebuild, but for the first interventions. I would point out that although the Stability Pact for Municipalities has ceased to exist since 1 January 2016, the resources of the National Emergency Fund are not subject to the Stability Pact. Therefore, the resources allocated for stage one of civil protection intervention are not subject to traditional budgetary rules. In phase two, we have to deal with a number of issues. This decree-law speaks of the Stability Pact. We have added to the House article 3-a, which provides for a fund that collects the spaces of the Stability Pact not used during this financial year. In this way, simply through an administrative act, if there are any needs on the part of the municipalities for which the state of emergency will be declared within the current year, there may be access to that fund in order to be able to use those resources freely, without constraints of the Pact. It is obvious, dear senators, that the use of the Stability Pact involves payment. The closer we get to the end of the year, the more the measure of the Stability Pact becomes useless for the municipalities, because it is quite clear that the payment of the interventions that are being made now and which will begin in December, will slip into the new year, where the budgetary rule is a different rule. In Article 26 of the Stability Act, which you passed last week, there is the first leg of the reform of the so-called phase two. The first leg of the reform provides for reimbursement to private individuals. We have had a positive experience with the earthquake in Emilia during the reconstruction phase as to how we are intervening to repay private individuals, whether they are companies or citizens with respect to the real estate in which they live (with particular attention to the first houses). We have built a mechanism that is a child of that experience, and we have introduced it in Article 26 of the law of stability. Since April 2016, through a series of deliberations of the Council of Ministers and on the basis of the proposal that will be made by the Civil Protection regarding the detection of private damages by regional commissioners (because everywhere the commissioners are the presidents of the Regions), refunds will be recognized through the mechanisms of tax credit to private citizens from the companies that have suffered these calamitous events. In addition, in the transition to the House of Deputies we are thinking about introducing another leg of the reform at this stage two (which is that of reconstruction), relating mainly to the part of the infrastructure. The vast majority of the almost 50 emergency situations declared from April 2013 to December 2015 present major problems with regard to the situation of roads and bridges, state, provincial and municipal. We believe that we can intervene in the House when we vote on the law of stability so that we can also introduce a second pillar, that of public intervention for reconstruction on the roads. Then there are some open issues, mainly related to the postponement/regressation of taxes for citizens and entrepreneurs who have undergone these calamitous events, but I do not want to go too far, because there would be further details. I would, of course, ask my colleagues, those who are more sensitive to these issues, to be also very proactive, especially at this stage in the construction of the reform linked to phase two, that is to say to the reconstruction phase. We are in a position, even in the context of a discussion we have opened with Europe, to be able to build equal criteria and principles for all, precisely because this Government does not want to treat the territories in a differentiated way, especially where there have been episodes that have caused pain and, in some cases, even died, as happened in my land, the land of Emilia. We believe that this is an act, not only of justice, but also of great hope. [[Applause from the PD Group.]]

Now, every European country responds to the banking crises on its own, with its own resources, with the own legislation of each country. This text is an essential step in the process of achieving the Banking Union, the acceleration of which is absolutely necessary to break the vicious circle between sovereign debt crises and banking crises in some eurozone countries, which has threatened and could continue to threaten the survival of the common currency. The Treaty, by express decision of the European Council, accompanies the Single Resolution Mechanism Regulation (SRM), which is the third pillar of the Banking Union, after the rules on capital and deposit insurance. The main content of the European Agreement that we are examining today is the obligation for Member States to transfer the contributions of banks based in their territory to the Single Resolution Fund, which becomes the tool for common intervention on the crises of the banking system. So let us put a burden on the Member States and give strength to the European Union in relation to the banking crises. The simultaneous presence of adequate capital and effective deposit insurance and resolution systems is essential, together with an effective surveillance mechanism that eliminates the possible trends of national supervisors to protect their banks, in order to secure a banking system that is increasingly interconnected. The fund, financed by the contributions of banks (and not by States), will initially consist of national branches, which will gradually be exceeded during a transitional phase, which will last no more than eight years. The pooling of funds will start with 40% in the first year and a further 20% in the second year, and then gradually increase. The fund should reach at least 1% of the amount of protected deposits of all credit institutions authorised in the Member States of the Banking Union, with an estimated amount of around €55 billion. The transitional period is long enough and represents the compromise point between the different countries of the Union. The Agreement, signed by 26 EU states, with the exception of Sweden and the United Kingdom (two countries that have not joined the euro), has also been signed by countries that do not belong to the euro area (Bulgaria, Croatia, Denmark, Poland, Czech Republic, Romania and Hungary), which will of course have to comply with their obligations only when they join the single supervisory and resolution mechanisms. The Agreement consists of 16 articles and two annexed declarations. Article 1 defines its object and scope. Article 2 lays down detailed rules for its application, including in relation to general Community legislation. Title III (Articles 3 to 10) governs the arrangements for the transfer of contributions. The technical report specifies how the division into sectors is functional to a progressive mutualisation of the costs of resolution procedures and banking crises. However, it is clear that the resources, even if in the national sectors, once transferred are of relevance to the fund and are no longer in the availability of individual States. The same Title III also regulates the functioning of the sectors and the arrangements for the transfer of additional contributions, including the temporary transfer of funds between sectors. Article 9, responding to concerns that have emerged in several countries, then allows States to address the Court of Justice in order to assess the compatibility with the division of competences between the Union and Member States of any future changes to the rules on resolution. Title IV (Articles 11 to 16) sets out general and final provisions. These include Article 15, which establishes an undertaking to compensate States not participating in the Banking Union if the general budget of the Union is used for non-contractual liability. The draft ratification law consists of four articles providing for the authorisation to ratify, the order of execution, the clause of financial invariance and the entry into force. Since these contributions are paid by banks, there are no direct charges for public finances. In conclusion, in a statement annexed to the text, the signatory countries expressed their intention to complete the ratification process promptly, so that the resolution system would become operational from 1 January 2016. That is also why there is a certain urgency to ensure that the agreement is ratified quickly.

ParlaMint-IT_2015-11-25-LEG17-Senato-sed-542.ana.u321	Mr President, the draft law brings about the ratification and implementation of the Agreement, signed in September 2013, between Italy and Somalia, on cooperation in the field of defence. It is an agreement of great importance, also for the future prospects of Somalia, which has been engaged for years, with the support of the international community, in the consolidation of the state reality and in the fight against terrorist groups, starting from the dangerous Islamist component of al-Shabaab. The Agreement, consisting of a preamble and nine articles, aims to increase bilateral cooperation between their armed forces, strengthening defensive capabilities and improving mutual understanding on security issues. The intention is also to induce positive indirect effects in some productive and commercial sectors of the two countries and to exert a stabilising action for a region, that of the Horn of Africa, of particular strategic and political value. The contents of the Agreement are consistent with other agreements of the same subject. Article 1 specifies that the Agreement aims to encourage, facilitate and develop cooperation in the field of defence, on the basis of the principle of reciprocity and in accordance with the respective legal systems and international commitments entered into by the two countries. Article 2 provides that cooperation shall develop on the basis of annual and multiannual plans drawn up by the Parties and shall explain how they are to be managed by the two Ministries of Defence, whose representatives may consult each other, alternately in Rome and Mogadishu, in order to conclude specific agreements and cooperation programmes. The same Article 2 identifies the areas and modalities of cooperation, including security and defence policy, research and development, peace support operations and humanitarian operations, training and military health services. The subsequent Articles govern cooperation in the field of defence materials, with the aim of rationalising controls and procedures related to them (Article 3) and undertake the Parties to ensure the protection of intellectual property, including patents, with regard to activities carried out in accordance with the Agreement (Article 4). Article 5 regulates the status of Italian staff engaged in activities in Somalia, judicially and fiscally. The subsequent Articles lay down the modalities for the settlement of disputes (Article 6), for the entry into force of the understanding (Article 7) and for the conclusion of additional protocols in specific areas (Article 8). Finally, Article 9 regulates the validity and termination of the Agreement, establishing that it shall remain in force until the denunciation of one of the Parties. The draft law of ratification consists of five articles, which contain respectively the authorisation of ratification (Article 1), the order of execution (Article 2), the financial cover (Article 3), the clause of financial invariance (Article 4) and the entry into force (Article 5). The economic costs relating to official visits and operational meetings between their delegations are estimated at around EUR 5,000 every other year. The Agreement does not present any profiles of incompatibility with national law, Community law and other international obligations entered into by our country.
