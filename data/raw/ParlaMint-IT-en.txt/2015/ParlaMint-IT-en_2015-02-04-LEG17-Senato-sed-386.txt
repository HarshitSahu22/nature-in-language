ParlaMint-IT_2015-02-04-LEG17-Senato-sed-386.ana.u23	Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, the first of the two draft laws ratifying international agreements against the evasion and avoidance of capital abroad and the exchange of information is the bill No 1549 which brings the ratification and enforcement of the Agreement between the Government of the Italian Republic and the Government of the Isle of Man (a small island between Great Britain and Ireland) on the exchange of information in tax matters, done in London on 16 September 2013. The bilateral agreement, signed in London in September 2013, follows the model prepared by the Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development, in the framework of initiatives for fiscal transparency and appears in line with the guidelines shared by Italy in the various multilateral fora for the strengthening of instruments to combat the phenomenon of international tax evasion. The purpose of the agreement is to promote cooperation between the tax administrations of the two parties through an exchange of information on tax matters necessary to ensure the achievement of adequate levels of transparency: a very important factor. In addition, the use of a genuine Convention against double taxation has been avoided, given the reduced trade between the two countries. In this sense, the Agreement is part of those negotiated arrangements, provided for by the 2008 Finance Act, as useful tools for identifying States with a tax regime in accordance with the standards of legality adopted by the European Union, which are important for the consequences that might arise in the fight against tax avoidance and evasion. More specifically, the Agreement consists of 13 articles, the first of which define the object and scope of the bilateral agreement, the guidelines and jurisdictional areas (Articles 1 and 2) and the taxes subject to the possible exchange of information (Article 3). Article 4 provides a framework of definitions necessary to eliminate possible interpretative discrepancies, while Article 5 regulates the procedures for the exchange of information, regulating in detail the types of information that may be required and providing, inter alia, for the overcoming of banking secrecy. Subsequent Articles 6 and 7 govern the possibility for the parties to carry out tax checks in their respective territories, as well as the detailed assumptions in which it is possible for one of the parties to evade the disclosure request. Article 8 lays down a strict principle of confidentiality in the management and exchange of information, while Article 9 stipulates that the ordinary costs incurred in providing information shall be borne by the requested party, the extraordinary costs borne by the applicant, including a commitment to occasional consultation between the competent authorities on these aspects. The concluding articles require the Parties to adopt the internal legislative amendments necessary to implement the understanding (Article 10), the procedures for the amicable settlement of any disputes (Article 11) and the deadlines for entry into force and possible termination of the Agreement (Articles 12 and 13). The draft law of ratification consists of three articles which have, respectively, the authorization to ratify, the order of execution and the entry into force. No financial charges are foreseen. The Agreement does not present any profiles of incompatibility with national legislation, nor with the Community law, but on the contrary to complete the international obligations already assumed by our country in the field of administrative cooperation. Therefore, it is pointed out to the House that rapid approval of this agreement would be a significant step forward in attacking the node of tax evasion and avoidance in tax havens, allowing the Government of the Isle of Man, as a foreign contracting party, to be included in the white lists of states and territories with a tax regime that complies with the standards of legality and transparency adopted by the European Union. In conclusion, it is proposed that this bill be approved by the Assembly.

ParlaMint-IT_2015-02-04-LEG17-Senato-sed-386.ana.u49	Mr President, the draft law, which has already been adopted by the House of Members, provides for the ratification and enforcement of the Agreement between Italy and Afghanistan on preventing and combating illicit trafficking in narcotic drugs and psychotropic substances. It establishes the commitment of the two countries to strengthen operational cooperation and mutual assistance in this area. The understanding, in the absence of framework security agreements, is necessary in order to achieve bilateral cooperation between their counterparts bodies committed to combating drug trafficking, in compliance with the provisions of their respective legal systems and international obligations. With this Agreement, the Parties undertake to cooperate, through an exchange of information, investigative techniques, studies, research and joint analysis on criminal organisations dedicated to drug trafficking. Afghanistan - as is well known - is one of the world's largest producers of cannabinoid substances. In recent years there has been a proliferation of mobile refining laboratories, widespread mainly in the most remote areas of the country, and a flow of chemical precursors not controllable from the borders of Pakistan and Central Asian countries. These aspects therefore require constant monitoring and control to avoid the diversion of these substances to illicit markets. Negotiations for the definition of the Agreement were launched in February 2009, on a proposal from the Central Directorate for Drugs Services, at a time when Italy was leading the European project COSPOL, for the control of illicit drug trafficking from Afghanistan to Europe. The Agreement, in fact, lays down the definitions of the terms 'craziers', 'psychotropic substances' and 'chemical precursors' and extends the scope of application to synthetic drugs which will be invented in the future and to any amazing substance which has been declared illegal by the competent international bodies. In particular, the commitment of the Parties to promote cooperation in the prevention and combating of illicit drug trafficking, within the limits of their respective legal systems, as well as international obligations, and with the exclusion of aspects related to criminal judicial assistance and extradition, is confirmed. The areas of cooperation between the Parties shall include prevention and combating illicit trafficking, study and joint analysis of criminal organisations engaged in drug trafficking, possible planning of targeted intervention strategies, mutual updating of criminal phenomena, training of personnel employed in drug-related activities, investigation techniques and up-to-date information on operational standards and procedures. Cooperation arrangements, such as the exchange of information, including of an operational nature, on the situation of drugs and the criminal threat in their respective countries, the adoption of measures necessary for the execution of special investigative techniques, technical and legal assistance, the establishment of joint working groups and the exchange of experts, as well as the organisation of regular meetings for the evaluation of the state of cooperation. In addition, the modalities for the formulation and transmission of requests for cooperation and assistance, as well as the formal and substantive requirements for their submission, are laid down. The Agreement also regulates the processing of sensitive personal data contained in the information and documents transmitted and defines the sharing of financial burdens. The body responsible for the application of the Agreement, for Italy, is the Ministry of the Interior, and precisely the Central Directorate for Drugs Services at the Department of Public Security. The Agreement also provides for the means of verifying the state of implementation of the understanding, the results of cooperation and the improvement of effectiveness, also establishing that the settlement of any disputes, with regard to the interpretation and application of the act, is the subject of negotiations and meetings between the parties, and defines the procedures for the possible revision of the Agreement. The draft ratification law, which is now the subject of the vote in this House, consists of four articles which have respectively the authorization to ratify, the order of execution, the financial cover and the entry into force of the bilateral Act. The charges are estimated at around 100,000 euros per year. The legislative action does not indicate constitutional or Community criticalities and takes into account the provisions of international law in this area, explicitly recalling in the preamble, among others, the 1988 Convention against drug trafficking and the 2000 United Nations Convention against Transnational Organised Crime. It is therefore proposed, in conclusion, that this bill of ratification be approved by the Assembly. [[Applause from the PD Group and Senator Casini.]]

Composed of 19 articles, the Treaty provides first of all a definition of the terms used (Article 1) and then identifies the general principles of the Agreement (Article 2). Article 3 lays down the conditions for the transfer, providing that the sentenced person is a national of the receiving State (or has permanent residence in that territory) and that the facts are also a criminal offence under the law of the receiving State. It is also expected that the sentence which led to the detention will be final, that the sentenced person will have to serve a sentence of at least 12 months, that the death penalty has not been imposed and that agreement will be reached on the transfer order between the two States. The central authorities responsible for implementing the measures are the Directorate-General for Criminal Justice of the Ministry of Justice for Italy and the Ministry of Justice for Brazil. The following articles outline the procedure for the transfer, establish the need for consent by the sentenced person and lay down rules on the basis for the transfer decision, starting from certain factors, such as the seriousness of the crime and any criminal precedents. Articles 9 and 10 govern the effects of the transfer of the sentenced person, and Article 15 regulates the financial aspects of the Agreement, specifying that the costs are borne by the receiving State, with the exception of those incurred exclusively in the territory of the transferring State. The Treaty states that it may also apply for convictions prior to its entry into force and provides that disputes between the parties should be resolved by diplomatic means. The draft law of ratification of government initiative No 1622 consists of four articles which have, respectively, the authorization to ratify, the order of execution, the financial coverage of the related charges and the entry into force of the law. The charges (essentially for travel expenses for the transfer of prisoners and for translation costs) are quantified in just over 37,000 euros per year. Modernity lives a strange contradiction: we believe animals like us, when we use them, through experimentation, to prove on them everything that can do good or bad to our organism, but we judge them different from us, when it comes to recognizing them rights. This condition, as I have repeatedly said in this House, is made clear in many areas: from criminal law, where sanctions against animal feeling are not repressive, to civil law, in which animals are even assimilated to res. Unfortunately, the situation in the health sector does not change. In fact, animal health in our country is not exactly at the forefront above all at the normative level, in which there are still gaps of discipline on important aspects. In particular, the minimum levels of health care are not fixed and also on the subject of professional responsibility of the veterinarian the ground is incomplete, to use a euphemism. To cite some examples, there are no obligations for the veterinarian to keep the reports, a owner cannot claim the release of the clinical record, there is no obligation of informed consent in case of interventions even risky on animals, there are no requirements, even in terms of instrumentation, for the opening of a veterinary study. I'll stop, but the list could go on. In a nutshell, we do not have a clear, timely regulatory framework that can ensure that animals are protected against essential rights such as health rights, which should be recognised to all living beings. I take advantage of this animal day to ask for the timetable for our bill on animal protection and protection, which has already been announced in this House. In our legislative proposal, animal health is regulated in a number of respects, but above all specific requirements are laid down for the veterinary profession and minimum standards are laid down for the quality of health care provided. I believe that the modernization of this country can only take place by evolving in terms of rights, beginning with the protection of the weakest, especially those who have no voice.
