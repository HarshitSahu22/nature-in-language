Collaboration in counter-terrorism activities provides for the exchange of information on the techniques and operating arrangements of organisations and individual suspects, as well as on their funding channels. Specific provisions also concern the exchange of information on the fight against illegal immigration and trafficking, in particular data on illegal immigration flows, travel arrangements and forgery of documents and visas. In these areas, the Agreement also lays down further forms of cooperation to be carried out through seminars, exchanges of experience, training courses and specialist training. ParlaMint-IT_2014-04-02-LEG17-Senato-sed-222.ana.u135	Mr President, the draft law before us provides for the ratification and enforcement of the Agreement between Italy and the Government of Jersey on the exchange of information in tax matters. The bilateral agreement, signed in London in March 2012, follows the model drawn up by the OECD (the Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development) within the framework of the Global Forum for the Transparency and Exchange of Tax Information. The Global Forum is a multilateral structure within which it is active in favour of transparency and for the exchange of information on taxation between Member States and non-members of the OECD itself since 2000. The Global Forum Summit held in Mexico in September 2009 was, however, the real turning point. In response to the demands of G20 leaders for the adoption of higher standards of transparency and exchange of tax information, the Global Forum was redefined as an organisation of peer members with the ambitious agenda to improve transparency and exchange of tax information. On that occasion he agreed for a three-year term in order to promote the rapid adoption of higher standards. The Global Forum currently consists of 120 Member States plus the European Union. The bilateral agreement under discussion therefore appears to be in line with the guidelines shared by Italy in the various multilateral fora for the strengthening of instruments to combat the phenomenon of international tax evasion. The purpose of the agreement is to promote cooperation between the tax administrations of the two parties through an exchange of information on tax matters necessary to ensure the achievement of adequate levels of transparency. As reported in the explanatory report accompanying the provision, the Agreement in question can constitute one of the bases in order to be able to insert the Jersey Baliate in the white list of countries that allow an adequate exchange of information, to be issued pursuant to the law 24 December 2007, n. 244 (financial law 2008), which in article 1, paragraph 83, has previewed a reformulation of the Italian anti-evasive provisions, providing for the overcoming of the method of identification of tax havens (to be included in the black lists) with that of the list of countries that meet the criteria of the European Union (so in white list). The entry into force of this Agreement may therefore have important repercussions in the fight against tax avoidance and evasion. More specifically, the Agreement under ratification consists of thirteen articles, the first of which define the object and scope of the bilateral agreement, guidelines and jurisdictional areas (Articles 1 and 2). The information will mainly concern the determination, determination, application, collection (including co-active) of taxes, relating to persons (physical or legal) or investigations into them. It should be noted that the last period of Article 1 provides that: The rights... insured to persons under the laws... of the requested Party shall remain applicable in so far as they do not prevent or unduly delay the effective exchange of information. This definition, which the OECD could objectively better formulate, could lead to litigation between parties due to the different possible interpretations of the due or undue duration of a delay in the production of information to the applicant party. Article 3 defines taxes subject to the possible exchange of information, i.e. personal income tax (IRPEF), corporate income tax (IRES), regional production tax (IRAP), value added tax (VAT), etc. Article 4 provides a framework of definitions necessary to eliminate possible interpretative discrepancies, while Article 5 regulates the arrangements for the exchange of information. The subsequent Articles 6 and 7 provide for the possibility for the parties to carry out tax checks in their respective territories, as well as for the detailed assumptions in which it is possible for one of the Parties to avoid the information request. Article 8 therefore lays down a strict principle of confidentiality in the management and exchange of information, while Article 9 stipulates that the ordinary costs incurred in providing information are borne by the interrogating Party, the extraordinary costs borne by the applicant. Finally, the concluding articles require the Parties to adopt the internal legislative amendments necessary to implement the bilateral understanding (Article 10), lay down the procedures for the amicable settlement of any disputes arising from the application and interpretation of the text (Article 11) and regulate the time limits for entry into force and for the possible termination of the Agreement (Articles 12 and 13). On the other hand, the draft law ratifying the Agreement under consideration consists of three articles which have the authorization to ratify, the order of execution and the entry into force of the act in question respectively. No article is specifically dedicated to the financial coverage of the measure. In this respect, the technical report specifies that any extraordinary costs for the exchange of information referred to in Article 9 of the Agreement are of a purely possible nature. In the event that they become necessary, they should be dealt with through the adoption of a specific legislative measure. The content of this forecast, among other things, was the basis for the expression of the non-obstacle opinion given by the Budget Committee on the measure. The Constitutional Affairs and Justice Committees have also spoken on the same text, with non-obstacles. The Foreign Affairs Committee, at its meeting on 6 March, unanimously supported this draft law. In conclusion, the draft law is proposed by the Assembly.

This Agreement lays down general principles of cooperation within the framework of Galileo and the rights and obligations of Norway also on issues not directly covered by the Security Programme. The text of the Agreement, which consists of 12 articles, first of all in Article 1 as the intended objective is to further strengthen cooperation between the parties in the field and, in Article 2, provides a precise definition of the terms used. Article 3 defines the principles governing cooperation activities, within the legal framework offered by the Agreement on the European Economic Area, relating to the freedom to provide satellite navigation services and to use Galileo and EGNOS, the necessary cooperation in security matters related to the Global Navigation Satellite System, compliance with international obligations in respect of the necessary ground installations. Articles 4 and 5 define cooperation on radio spectrum issues within the framework of the International Telecommunications Union - the United Nations Specialised Agency dealing with communication and information technology issues - and measures for the management of ground-based installations of the Global Navigation Satellite System in terms of location, maintenance, protection, safety and operation. Article 6 of the Agreement provides that the European Union and Norway, in recognising the need for the protection of satellite navigation systems, shall take all appropriate measures to ensure continuity and security of services. The subsequent Articles govern the exchange of classified information (Article 7), the control of exports and the non-proliferation of Galileo data and technology (Article 8) and the recognition by Norway of the importance of the Public Regulated Service as a factor for its participation in the Global Navigation Satellite System programmes (Article 9). Finally, Article 10 highlights the importance of a coordinated approach within international standardisation and certification bodies with regard to global satellite navigation services. The draft law ratifying the Agreement consists of three articles which have the authorization to ratify, the order of implementation and the entry into force of the Agreement in question respectively.
