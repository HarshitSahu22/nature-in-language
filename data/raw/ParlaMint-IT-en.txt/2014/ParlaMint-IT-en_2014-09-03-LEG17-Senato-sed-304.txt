ParlaMint-IT_2014-09-03-LEG17-Senato-sed-304.ana.u13	Well found to all of you! Mr De Cristofaro, rapporteur. The draft law under consideration includes the authorisation to ratify and implement the Treaty in the field of criminal judicial assistance between Italy and the People's Republic of China, signed in October 2011. This agreement is closely linked to the agreement - recently approved by the Senate - on mutual criminal judicial assistance and is intended to further strengthen bilateral cooperation in the specific field of extradition procedures. The Treaty, which consists of 21 Articles, lays down in Article 1 the mutual commitment of the parties to surrender themselves to persons sought, to pursue criminal proceedings or to allow the execution of a conviction. In accordance with Article 2, extradition shall be granted, in accordance with the principle of double indictment, for offences punishable by penalties restricting personal freedom for at least one year, or, in the case of requests for the execution of a conviction, where the penalty still to be served is not less than six months. A temperament of the principle of double indictment is provided for customs, tax or financial offences, for which extradition is carried out even if the requested party does not provide for the same criminal configuration of such cases. In Articles 3 and 4, the Treaty also regulates mandatory and optional cases in which either party must or may refuse extradition, including, among others, cases of political crimes (excluding those for terrorism), military offences, in the event of political asylum being granted or where there is a reasonable reason to believe that the same person may be punished for reasons of discrimination. It should also be noted that the possibility of extradition is not granted where there is a reasonable reason to believe that the requested person may suffer, in relation to the specific offence, torture or other degrading and inhuman treatment or in cases where extradition itself could lead to the execution of penalties prohibited in the order of the requested party, such as, for example, for the Italian law on capital punishment. The Treaty also regulates the possible refusal to extradite its citizens (Article 5); it identifies the authorities of each country responsible for transmitting requests (Article 6) and sets out the procedures and documents necessary to make such requests (Articles 7 and 8). The text also covers the possibility of provisional arrests in cases of urgency (Article 9), the arrangements for the delivery of the extradited person, whether deferred (Articles 11 and 12), and cases of requests for extradiction made by several States (Article 13). The subsequent Articles contain, inter alia, forecasts for the authorisation of transit of a person extradited from a third State (Article 16), for the notification of the result (Article 17) and for the provision of expenditure relating to the implementation of the Treaty (Article 18). The resolution of interpretative or application disputes of the Treaty is entrusted to diplomatic consultation (Article 20). The draft law under consideration consists of four articles providing for the approval of ratification, the order of execution, the financial coverage of the related charges and the entry into force of the text. The analysis of the compatibility of the intervention does not indicate any constitutional problems, nor does it conflict with the Community and international regulations to which Italy is bound and, in particular, with the Convention of the Council of Europe of extradition of 1957. In conclusion, therefore, it is proposed that the Assembly approve this measure.

ParlaMint-IT_2014-09-03-LEG17-Senato-sed-304.ana.u60	Mr President, the draft law before us, already approved by the House of Members, authorizes the ratification of the optional Protocol of December 2008 to the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights. The Protocol, adopted by the UN General Assembly in December 2008, aims to strengthen the protection of economic, social and cultural rights, guaranteeing the same protection mechanisms already provided for civil and political rights. Italy, as a member of the Group of Friends of the Protocol, has been among the main countries supporting its adoption and among the first signatories, since September 2009. To date, the document has been signed by 45 countries and ratified by 15 countries, including Belgium, Finland, Portugal, Slovakia and Spain. The International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights is a UN treaty, born from the experience of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, adopted in 1966 and entered into force in January 1978. Unlike the Covenant on Civil Rights, the Covenant on Economic and Social Rights did not originally provide for any specific supervisory body. Only in 1985 did the United Nations Economic and Social Council opt for the establishment of a Committee made up of 18 independent experts, responsible for monitoring the implementation of the Pact by States and analysing their periodic reports. With this Protocol, the Committee's competence is extended to individual communications for serious violations of the rights enshrined in the Pact. The Protocol, which consists of 22 articles, commits States to recognising the Committee's competence to receive and consider communications from individuals, or groups of individuals, who consider themselves victims of violations of one or more of the rights enshrined in the Pact (Articles 1 and 2). The text also gives the Committee additional powers, including to receive and consider interstate communications (Article 10) and to require a State to take urgent measures in exceptional circumstances. The procedure outlined in Article 8 for the examination of communications by the Committee, which provides for the possibility for this body to make use, as well as of all the documentation received, of that from other United Nations bodies or mechanisms, international and regional organisations, as well as comments and comments from the State Party concerned, is particularly important. The special mechanisms introduced are only available to those States which have recognised the Committee's competence. Similarly, the procedure described in Article 14 (technical assistance and international cooperation) provides for the necessary consent of the State concerned. The other articles of the Protocol lay down rules on the follow-up to the investigation procedure (Article 12), on protective measures (Article 13) and on the forms of technical assistance and international cooperation which the Committee may activate (Article 14). The Protocol also provides for the publication of an annual report on the Committee's activities (Article 15) and the commitment by States to make their citizens aware of the mechanisms for protecting the Pact and this Protocol (Article 16). The draft law consists of three articles concerning the authorization to ratify, the order of execution and the entry into force of the text. The measure does not involve additional burdens on the state budget, as the Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights is already in place and is not substantially modified in its aspects of operation by the Protocol in question. In conclusion, it is proposed that the Assembly approve this measure.

The same Article 3 (2) provides that the information exchanged between the two States shall be kept secret and communicated only to authorities entrusted with the task of establishing, collecting or verifying the procedures relating to them. The following paragraph 3 contains a safeguard clause under which the provisions of the preceding paragraphs do not give rise to any obligation for the two Contracting States to take administrative measures derogating from the legislation, to provide information which cannot be obtained under their legislation or administrative practice, or to provide information which may reveal commercial or industrial secrets. Finally, paragraphs 4 and 5 require the two Contracting States to use their powers to collect the information required, even if it relates to banks or financial institutions. Finally, the Exchange of Letters between the Italian and Luxembourg authorities, also in June 2012, states that the exchange of information on demand may include income or income falling within the scope of the Community Directive on the taxation of savings income. It is also envisaged that the competent authority of the requesting State, in order to demonstrate the relevance of the information contained in the request, shall provide sufficient information to identify the person audited or investigated, as well as the tax purpose for which the information is required. The draft law consists of three articles, which form the basis for the authorization of ratification, the order of execution and the entry into force of the text. The measure does not involve any expenditure or the establishment of new offices. The technical report accompanying the draft law shows that the pending abolition of the banking secrecy that the Protocol provides may lead to a potential recovery of revenue for the Italian Treasury, which cannot yet be quantified. The Agreement is, moreover, the completion of the international obligations already assumed by our country in the field of administrative cooperation.
