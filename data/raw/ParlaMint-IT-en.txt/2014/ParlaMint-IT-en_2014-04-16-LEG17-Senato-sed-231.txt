This measure allows the Bank of Italy to make use of third parties for the exercise of information and inspection activities on banks and banking groups, for the purposes of the in-depth evaluation carried out by the European Central Bank in accordance with the European Regulation. The establishment of the Single Supervisory Mechanism, which will start in autumn 2014, is one of the steps planned for the implementation of the Banking Union in Europe, aimed at creating an integrated financial framework to safeguard financial stability and minimise the costs of bank failures. Its components will be the Single Supervisory Mechanism and the new integrated deposit guarantee and resolution frameworks of credit institutions. The single banking supervision system is governed by two regulations: No 1024 of 2013, which gives the European Central Bank powers for the supervision of all banks in the euro area; and No 1022 of 2013, which aligns the Regulation establishing the European Banking Authority with the new banking supervision framework. Regulation No 1024 gives the European Central Bank specific tasks in respect of the prudential supervision policies of credit institutions established in Member States whose currency is the euro, while maintaining the residual powers of national supervisory authorities. In this context, the opening of the Single Supervisory Mechanism with the subsequent assumption by the European Central Bank, as from 4 November 2014, of the supervisory tasks provided for in the Regulation require prior, in accordance with Article 33 (4) of the Regulation, that an in-depth assessment be carried out by the credit institutions of the Member State. This evaluation will end in October 2014 and is an essential element of the Single Supervisory Mechanism, useful for clarifying the banks that will be subject to direct supervision by the European Central Bank. Not all banks will be evaluated in depth, but only those considered significant according to certain parameters: whether the total value of assets exceeds EUR 30 billion; whether the ratio of total assets to the GDP of the Member State where the institution is established exceeds 20%; and whether the credit institution is one of the three largest institutions in a participating Member State. On the basis of this selection, 130 credit institutions in the 18 Member States account for around 85% of the total banking activities of the euro area. The in-depth evaluation shall be carried out in three complementary phases: risk analysis for supervisory purposes, assessment of asset quality and stress testing. The European Bank will conduct the in-depth evaluation in complete independence and will work in cooperation with the competent national authorities during the process. National competent authorities, therefore, will also be able to use a company, which has already been identified as an ECB consultant and which is part of the Oliver Wyman International Group, which will provide independent advice on the methodology, as well as contribute to the establishment and implementation of the evaluation at the executive level. It is also stressed that the competent national authorities will, in order to conduct the evaluation, contact private sector experts - consultants, auditors, audit firms and/or others - to receive assistance in relation to tasks such as on-the-spot examination, analysis and investigation. Subsequently, in February 2014, the ECB highlighted how the asset quality review was carried out, in turn, at three stages: portfolio selection, execution (i.e. the actual asset analysis) and reporting of the results, which should take place in October 2014. In this context, the ECB has also specified that, after the selection of portfolios, the competent national authorities and the specialized third parties of which they will be used (i.e. auditors, consultants, etc.) will carry out the analysis of the processes, policies and accounting practices of banks, will examine credit exposures and provisions and assess their collateral and real estate activities. The general use of private sector companies is necessary, not only in view of the complexity of the exercise, but also in order to strengthen its independence and credibility; therefore, in practice, in order to enhance the full credibility of the European credit system and thus encourage investment by companies. The content of the decree-law contains only four articles. Article 1, paragraph 1, states that the Bank of Italy can also rely on the advice of third parties of high professionalism, which is the main reason for the enactment of this decree-law. Up to now, in fact, Italian legislation does not allow this option. The advice of third parties will cover the exercise of information and inspection activities on banks and banking groups, in accordance with Articles 51, 54, 66 and 68 of the single bank text (TUB). According to the following paragraph 2, all the information, information and data of which such third parties come to know or in possession because of their involvement in the exercise of in-depth evaluation, are covered by the secrecy of office, pursuant to article 7, paragraph 1, of the TUB, which establishes that all the news, information and data in possession of the Bank of Italy, because of its supervisory activity, are covered by the secrecy of office also with respect to public administrations, with the exception of the Minister of Economy and Finance and the President of the Interministerial Committee for Credit and Savings. Paragraph 3 prescribes the obligation for third parties in question to report exclusively to the Governor of the Bank of Italy the irregularities, even if integrating hypotheses of crime, of which they become aware in the exercise of their supervisory activities. Paragraph 4 provides that the Bank of Italy and the Ministry of Economy and Finance agree on the modalities of sharing information relating to the exercise of in-depth evaluation, also in derogation from the provisions on official secrecy from the aforementioned article 7 of the TUB. The rule therefore allows the Ministry of Economy and Finance to acquire relevant information for the possible exercise of its functions. It should be noted in this regard that the technical report calculates the costs arising from the measure - fully borne by the Bank of Italy - in about 25 million euros. Hence, the approval of this decree-law must not result in new or higher burdens for public finance. The Commission accepted two amendments with the Government's favourable opinion. The first covers the possibility that third parties may be in a situation of conflict of interest in the activity carried out to carry out an in-depth assessment, both for previous activities and personal requirements, as well as for conditions arising during the mandate. In both cases, the term of office lapses. The second amendment defines with greater formal precision the scope in which information on irregularities may emerge, even if integrating hypotheses of crime, that third parties are required to report exclusively to the Governor of the Bank of Italy.
