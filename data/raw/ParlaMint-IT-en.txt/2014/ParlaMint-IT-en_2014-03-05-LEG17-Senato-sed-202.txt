I apologise, Mr President, but in the rush to give opinions on the amendments I forgot to give an explanation - which seems to me, among other things, the most important - of the favourable opinion on amendments 1,205 and 1,18. Both amendments provide for the deletion, in paragraph 1, of the words: "i.e. by means of omissions." The original text provided for criminality, not only in the event of prosecution, therefore through the conduct of violence or threat referred to in the text submitted to the attention of the Senate, but also when omissions were committed. Obviously this expression had reference to the non-missive conducts of the same active conducts punished in the text of Article 612-a. It was, however, preferred to remove the expression "i.e. by omission" for two fundamental reasons. The first of these is of a case-law nature, but it is linked to a precise provision of law existing in our penal code, i.e. Article 40, which in the paragraph provides for a kind of clause of equivalence between the causal actions and the non-prohibitive omissions, so that the expression "i.e. by default" is superfluous since, by virtue of this provision contained in the general part of the penal code, jurisprudence substantially converts the causal actions into non-prohibitive omissions. At this point the maintenance of the expression was not only superfluous, but also such as to generate possible interpretative doubts: in fact, in the face of a consolidated conversion practice, it could have been considered that the expression referred to omissions of other active conducts, i.e. conducts not indicated in the text of the article, or other than those. For this reason it is considered precisely to eliminate the expression, as it is evident that if torture manifests itself by means of an omissive conduct, provided that it represents the exact conversion of the conduct described in positive terms, it remains punishable.
