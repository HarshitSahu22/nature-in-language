ParlaMint-IT_2014-12-19-LEG17-Senato-sed-368.ana.u426	Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, I wanted to say first of all, with particular regard to the speech by Senator De Petris, that I first regret that I was unable to convene the Commission to take note of the timetable in the Chamber of the electoral law. All my colleagues have seen that this was impossible because of the lack of continuity between the Conference of Leaders and the beginning of the work of this House. The text before the House was approved by the Chamber of Deputies on 12 March 2014. It deals with an electoral system that concerns only the renewal of the Chamber of Deputies, thus stating that it is a reform deliberately confined to the method of composition of what will be the only elective Chamber, because of the approval of the constitutional reform of overcoming the perfect bicameralism and of assigning to the Chamber of Deputies alone of the vote of confidence. This choice emerges in an unambiguous manner from the work carried out in the other branch of Parliament and, in particular, from the request made by the majority rapporteur, Mr Sisto, to delete Article 2, which concerned the electoral system for the Senate. To further clarify and support the request, Mr Giorgis said: "Mr President, as almost all the auditors in the Commission have pointed out and as many experts have pointed out for a long time, in order to avoid the risk that in a political order tending to be tripolar from the elections no clear and solid majority of government will emerge, it is necessary first of all to overcome the perfect bicameralism and to predict that only a Chamber gives confidence to the Government. The reform of the electoral law alone is unable to fully resolve the needs of representativeness and governability. With this in mind, for these reasons, in order to link the reform of the electoral law to that of the Senate, we have tabled an amendment deleting Article 2 and which, at the request of the President, has turned into a request for deletion. This is the reason why the Commission began the discussion of the text received from the Chamber and the related bill No 1449, the only one among the various draft laws submitted to the Senate in electoral matters concerning the method of election of the Chamber of Deputies alone. During the course of the procedure, some senators suggested the need to further combine the other electoral bill, but this request was not formalised with the request for a vote in the Commission. Moreover, the President's invitation to reformulate those draft laws was not accepted by limiting its content to the electoral system of the Chamber of Deputies alone, so that it could be combined. The examination began and a cognitive survey was arranged during which 26 teachers under constitutional law were audited, as well as the Minister of the Interior and the official of the Directorate-General for Election Services. The general discussion was attended by 33 speeches by fellow senators. At its meeting on 5 December, the rapporteur concluded by stressing the need for electoral reform to strike a balance between the need for stability of governments - an objective deemed constitutionally legitimate by the Constitutional Court - and a broad guarantee of representativeness, the latter's need all the more felt in a constitutional system which will provide for a single elected Chamber. In this respect, the rapporteur also took up the suggestion from the general discussion that the threshold for access to seats should be lowered and stressed another common suggestion: to increase the share of the votes which, in the new electoral system, would entitle the majority prize to be awarded to the list, and to introduce a system of gender rebalancing in representation. It was reserved for the subsequent construction of a political understanding to examine further issues. At its meeting on 9 December, a very large majority adopted an order of business setting out the majority of the points of reform agreed upon, including, of course, those on which the rapporteur drew attention in her conclusions and on which the rapporteur presented consistent amendments; but at the end of the deadline for tabling amendments and sub-amendments, a total of 17,767 were included. In expressing my thanks for the work and collaboration of all my colleagues, I would like to stress the very heavy work done by the Offices, with the usual diligence and punctuality, to order and fascicolare the unusual amount of amendments and sub-amendments submitted to the Commission.
