ParlaMint-IT_2014-03-12-LEG17-Senato-sed-206.ana.u14	Mr President, I am replacing the rapporteur, Senator D'Ascola, who had a domestic accident this morning and is currently in hospital. The measure under consideration, which comes from the Chamber of Deputies, intervenes on the discipline of precautionary measures, meaning both protective measures in prison and other measures such as house arrests or interdittive measures. The stated purpose of this measure is to make effective the principle that exists within our code, and that clearly has a constitutional response, according to which custody in prison must be considered an extreme ratio. From this point of view, the interventions of the legislator, or rather the interventions made on the discipline by the Chamber, all tend to render less abstract the motivation with regard to the precautionary measure, yes - as I said before - to make effective the criterion of the extrema ratio and, on the other hand, to have favorable effects on the so-called prison overcrowding, which, as is known to all, sees about 30 percent of the prisoners waiting for judgment. Taking for granted the House's knowledge of the general rules on custody, in my report, which is very brief, I will only deal with the points that have been amended, namely the most important points. Let us say immediately that in the text before us today a sacrosanct principle is confirmed, moreover affirmed in our Constitution, which clearly guarantees the right to silence, represented by that old Latin broker, nemo tenetur edere contra se, on the basis of which the refusal to make statements or the lack of confession by the accused, of the person subject to investigation, is not a circumstance that can be used adversely and, in other words, cannot contribute in the judge's assessment in negative terms to the issue of the precautionary measure. Compared to the text that comes to us from the Chamber, in the Justice Commission to the Senate it was considered to reinsert the reference to the person subject to the investigation, in order to avoid any kind of misunderstanding, that is, in order to avoid that a less specific formula could allow the judge to use unfavorably for the person subject to the investigation the refusal to make statements or the non-confession. Moreover, with regard to the danger of escape and the danger of further offences being committed (which are two of the precautionary requirements that are the prerequisite for issuing the order), it was considered to be necessary to ask the judge for a more specific reason, with reference not only to the elements from which to derive the existence of the aforementioned dangers, but mainly to be related to the actuality of the danger itself, which clearly amounts to saying that the assessment and the motivation of the precautionary measure cannot be abstract, but will have to come down in concrete terms and define in specific terms the current reasons for which the judge considers that certain danger exists. In the Senate's Justice Commission, a further amendment was made to the House's text, which, in the Commission's view, was due to a previous rule of law introduced by Parliament a few months ago, whereby, basically, custody in prison can be granted only for offences for which a maximum sentence of more than five years is provided. It has been specified that, in addition to these offences, custody may also be issued in prison for the offences referred to in Article 7 of Law No 195 of 1974, concerning illicit financing of parties, which makes the current discipline absolutely in line with the current legislation. This provision also makes it impossible for the judge to apply a precautionary measure not only when in the prognostic judgment it is assumed that the conditional suspension of the sentence can be granted, but also - and this is an amendment to the current text - when, always in the prognostic judgment, it is assumed that the sentence actually granted can then be declared suspended under Article 656 of the Code of Criminal Procedure. With regard to certain offences, which are the most serious, it is assumed that the judge's assessment should be different from the assessments concerning, at the general level, all other crimes. In the case of more serious offences, it is considered that the court may confine its statement of reasons to explain why it considers that the other measures (I am thinking of house arrests or the same interdictive measures) are not sufficient to meet the precautionary requirements. Another intervention was made with reference to the escapes from house arrests. According to the current legislation, in the case of a prisoner under house arrest who, by hypothesis, leaves the house arrests, it is compulsory and automatic the entry of the latter into prison, that is, the issue of a protective custody measure in prison. It is considered that this rule must remain in our legal system, obviously because of the deterrent effect it carries with it, but it is also believed to be able to mitigate the automaticity of the entry into prison, allowing the judge an assessment of the lightness of the fact, which is equivalent to saying that, in the case in which the judge considers that the evasive fact is of minor importance, the issue of a precautionary measure does not necessarily have to come down. A certainly significant intervention is the one that requires the judge who issues the precautionary measure an independent assessment respect, for example, to the request that comes from the public prosecutor, which is equivalent to previewing a cause of nullability of this order whenever one is in the presence of a motivation for relationem, both from the formal point of view, and from the substantive point of view. In other words, due to a series of events that characterized judicial life (i.e. a completely flattened precautionary order on the positions of the prosecutor's office), it was considered necessary to require the judge to avoid any kind of flattening and to carry out the evaluation of the elements carried out in support of the public prosecutor's request in a completely autonomous manner. In the same way, the judge is required, at the time of issuing the precautionary measure, to also take into account any other reasons put forward by the defence in any of its acts. The rules governing interdictive measures are amended, meaning that these measures currently only apply for months 2 can be applied up to a maximum of months 12. This is equivalent to saying that, in the event that these interdictive measures, for assumptions imposed for months 3 or months 4, require an extension, it may be granted, even with regard to certain types of crime and with reference to the persistence of evidence requirements. This change is important, because often, not considering that the short period of two months is sufficient to guarantee the needs of the inquiry, the judicial authority preferred to issue protective measures in prison. With this change on interdictive measures, it is clear that the judge is given the possibility to choose in a wider way between the interdictive measure and the precautionary measure, bearing in mind that, always in the general draft of the discipline referred to in the draft law in question, there is the possibility of issuing more measures other than the precautionary measure, and the judge is obliged to take into account, in his explanatory statement, why he considers that he has to issue the precautionary measure and considers, on the other hand, not only other precautionary measures or interdictive measures, but even an imposition of more measures in the same context. It also amended, from the procedural point of view, the rules of review before the Court of Freedom, the review of the appeal always concerning protective measures and, finally, the judgment of referral from the Cassation, in the sense that the terms which are now laid down in this discipline are no longer of a authorising nature, but assume the value of peremptive terms. This means that, if there were to be a breach of the deadline for the transmission of documents by the public prosecutor, i.e. the filing of the statement of reasons for the judgment, the consequence would be the loss of effectiveness of the precautionary measure, with liberation. It was considered by the Senate Justice Commission to include, from this point of view, a further clause, which was strong in our experience of non-compliance with the five-day period prescribed for warranty interrogation. This non-compliance also led to the loss of effectiveness of the precautionary measure, but allowing the judicial authority to immediately re-issue the precautionary measure. As regards measures relating to the freedom of citizens, and indeed not considering that any malfunctioning of judicial proceedings can fall solely and exclusively within the hands of citizens, it was considered to have, in addition to the loss of effectiveness of the measure, the impossibility of its renewal. Even here, however, the criterion is not absolute, because, having to take responsibility also for the security needs of citizens, the possibility of renewing the measure was allowed, but only in the presence of exceptional protective requirements. This is essentially the point of the measure, in which I am pleased to say that in the Commission justice, as is usually the case, has been achieved, with regard to the amendments, a substantial unanimity.
