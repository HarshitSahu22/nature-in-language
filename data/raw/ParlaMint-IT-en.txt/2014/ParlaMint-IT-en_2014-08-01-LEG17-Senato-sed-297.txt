Mr President, I will vote in favour of amendment 2.1439, although I did not sign it, because, if I understood it correctly, it intends to maintain the number of 100 senators, of which 95 regional councillors, that is, without the participation of the mayors. I agree that mayors in Italy are elected by an electoral body other than that of parliamentarians and regional councillors. In fact, the active electorate that elects mayors, city councilors and neighborhood members is composed of Italian citizens, but also citizens of the European Union resident in Italy, so not only Italians. In my opinion this circumstance represents a vulnus so that a mayor can become a senator and therefore legislator in Italy. In fact, it should also be said that introducing the bond that some mayors will become senators, and therefore legislators, could also be an obstacle to the extension of the participation of foreigners in local political life. I recall that the Treaty on European Union of 1991, the Maastricht Treaty, in Article 19, provides that every citizen of the European Union residing in a Member State of which he is not a citizen has the right to vote and to stand as a candidate in municipal elections in the Member State in which he resides, under the same conditions as nationals of that State. Italy has not yet implemented Article 19 for this aspect, but I imagine it will have to do so over time. I also recall the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union, adopted in 2000, which, in Article 40, speaks of the right to vote and to stand as a candidate in municipal elections, which must be extended to every citizen of the Union, who has the right to vote and to stand as a candidate in municipal elections in the same Member State in which he resides and under the same conditions as nationals of that State. Italy has not yet done so, but I believe that from a Europeanist point of view, which we all hope will go towards this. We will therefore find ourselves in the case of having non-Italian mayors. Now Italy does not admit it: the mayor and the deputy mayor can only be Italian; in the future it can also become a foreigner. I wonder if this future non-Italian mayor of the European Union will at this point be able to stand and be elected by a regional council, as the basic text provides, and become a senator, who at this point would become a legislator. I think we have to ask this question. For now, in my opinion, it is not appropriate to introduce this conflict into the Constitution, if we have not properly examined and studied it. As far as I know in the Committee on Constitutional Affairs, this has not been the case, because of the haste in the work of the Committee on Constitutional Affairs. I therefore believe that it is appropriate at this time to maintain the 95 senators elected from among the regional councillors. Eventually, in future changes to the Constitution, it will be necessary to understand the situation and decide whether to introduce the representativeness of the mayors. I therefore call on all my fellow Members to vote in favour of this amendment, which I have not signed, but which I believe should be welcomed.

ParlaMint-IT_2014-08-01-LEG17-Senato-sed-297.ana.u289	DI BIAGIO (PI). Mr President, the amendment 2,2026, which we are about to vote on, focuses on one aspect only touched on by the fierce debate, namely the legitimacy or not of the removal of the foreign constituency from the future configuration of the Senate. There has been talk of opportunities for its livelihood, on which different positions and ideas can exist. But when faced with a vice dictated by a clear illegitimacy, the responsibility of the legislator is to reflect and invite reflection. And in this constructive perspective - and I repeat constructively - this amending proposal is inserted, which is intended to overcome a gap which, in my opinion, risks creating a constitutional vulnus, which perhaps at a later stage will be the responsibility of the Constitutional Court to settle. Frankly, I believe that certain impasses deserve to be avoided with all the instruments available to date. I therefore call on my fellow Members to address the amending proposal without prejudice, but by analysing the issue for what it is, namely a potential constitutional vulnus and to take a clear position in this regard that we should take into account in the next steps. It is precisely in this perspective that I highlight my vote in favour of the proposal, which is why I disagree with the Group to which I belong. Entering the substance, pursuant to Article 57, it is in fact excluded a representative presence of the foreign constituency, understood as 21st Italian Region and nevertheless, pursuant to Article 48 of the Constitution, it is expected that: The law lays down requirements and procedures for the exercise of the right to vote of nationals residing abroad and ensures their effectiveness with the specification - introduced in the measure under consideration - that such recognition applies only to the election of representatives of the Chamber of Deputies. This in fact introduces a disharmonious level of representation in the two chambers. I would like to stress that the foreign constituency, like any other constituency, is one of the electoral districts in which the territory of a State is divided for the election of a collegiate body. One cannot help but consider it, colleagues. The foreign constituency lives in our system as the twenty-first Italian electoral region, therefore the overcoming of the direct electivity of the representatives of the new Senate does not seem to me that it inevitably has to be translated into a cancellation of the foreign constituency, but rather with its recontextualization within the parameters of the new Chamber. If I'm not mistaken, we are configuring the new Senate as a representation of specific territorial realities, placing it as a connecting structure. Well, this new configuration must not be without the presence of a representation of the foreign constituency, obviously no longer intended as an electoral district, but as a twenty-first Italian Region, therefore territorial structure. In fact, while it is true that the Italian communities abroad are characterized by a very special relationship with the territories, it is equally true that they must be considered an integral part of the national community and, as such, they are also worthy of a representation and a specific link with the institutions of the State, even more so if we consider that the new Senate will have to participate in the direct decisions, the formation and the implementation of the legislative acts of the European Union (we have also voted for amendments to this effect), without neglecting the fact that the Italian communities present mainly in Europe, through their representation, could bring a wealth of experience and participation not negligible. In the new Senate, the condition of direct elected representatives is less, I believe that a transition of the concept of representation area, from the regional electoral constituency to the region itself, is in fact legitimate. The abolition of a representation of the foreign constituency in the Senate would be tantamount to admitting its subalternity with respect to the other Italian electoral districts, which today are equivalent to the individual Italian Regions. And I don't think that's the intention. The lack of recognition, among the representatives of the new Senate, of a relative to the foreign constituency would in fact amount to the lack of recognition of the twenty-first Region of the country. And I understand that nowhere does the Constitution categorize in terms of democratic importance or representative pre-eminence of the different regions. Nor is it emphasized in the constitutional text the potential sacrificability - in terms of parliamentary representation - of one or the other. Finally, given that the parliamentary representation of the foreign constituency has been maintained in the representative composition of the Chamber of Deputies, in legitimate compliance with the constitutional rule of 2001, I believe that the balance of representation of the two branches of Parliament should be perfectly guaranteed, even if the two branches are no longer an expression of perfect bicameralism. Otherwise, the legitimacy of the verification and control functions recognized by the new Senate may be made partial. This position of mine is not motivated by the completely personal will to safeguard a representative reference area, nor to feed a provocative approach, which frankly does not belong to my work, given the repeated and renewed desire to support the measure under consideration. But I believe it is necessary to safeguard these areas of confrontation within which to express unpopular or disharmonic positions in relation to the majority approach. Above all, as I said, if we are faced with a sort of constitutional lightness, such as the one I intend to overcome with this very proposal. Therefore, in the light of the reverberations of legitimacy which the current configuration of Article 2 could bring, I repeat my vote in favour of this amendment, and I would urge my fellow Members to do likewise in the name of the balance of representation and the safeguarding of parliamentary harmony which, at this new constituent stage, we cannot afford to ignore.
