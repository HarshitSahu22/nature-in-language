ParlaMint-IT_2014-09-18-LEG17-Senato-sed-313.ana.u12	When, during a sitting, the Presidency intends to make communications to the Assembly and the Assembly itself is not called upon to vote, it does not constitute an obstacle to the President's announcement, nor does the absence of a quorum, nor does the reference to the order of business or to the Rules of Procedure as they have been made, as the Presidency's communication takes on a prominent significance compared to the subsequent completion of the proceedings. This principle derives directly from the provision of that Rule 8 of the Rules of Procedure which you know very well and was last confirmed at the meetings of 28 December 2013, n. 158, and 2 January 2014, n. 159. The reference to these precedents is particularly important as these were communications made to the Assembly in accordance with Article 77 of the Constitution. Therefore, there is no escape from the fact that, in the present case, the President's statement on the convening of Parliament in a joint sitting derives directly from the constitutional forecast regarding the election of the judges of the Constitutional Court and the members of the Supreme Council of the judiciary. That is the decision of the Presidency. [[Senator Calderoli's comments.]]
