Since my decision was requested before I began the examination of the articles, before taking this decision, in giving account to the Board for the Rules of Procedure of the various types of amendments for which secret ballot was requested, I recalled the rules and precedents of secret voting of the two branches of Parliament on amendments relating both to constitutional drafts and to ordinary drafts of law. In this meeting I found that the members of the Junta differed from the criteria for the eligibility of the secret ballot, as well as from the detailed reasons. As a result of the in-depth debate, the Junta unanimously agreed - in the sense that the only unanimity achieved was this - to return to the Presidency's determination. In this case, I have recorded a number of secret voting requests which have no precedent in parliamentary practice: 920 requests for secret voting on amendments. That is why I think it is appropriate first of all to state the principles which I have understood and intend to stick to. Article 113, paragraph 4, of the Rules of Procedure distinguishes the deliberations concerning the norms on linguistic minorities, referred to in Article 6 of the Constitution, from the deliberations concerning civil and ethical-social relations, referred to in articles of the Constitution expressly recalled (from 13 to 32, second paragraph, except Articles 23, 28 and 31), in addition to the deliberations concerning amendments to the Rules of the Senate. With different lexical diction, the Rules of Procedure of the Chamber of Deputies, Rule 49, speaks of votes that affect the principles and rights of freedom, referred to in the same constitutional articles recalled in the Rules of the Senate. I have taken into account the practice of both branches of Parliament on the admissibility of the secret vote on amendments relating to draft constitutional law and the different regulatory scope of the two disciplines, as applied to the different cases of the protection of linguistic minorities, on the one hand, and the rights of freedom, on the other. I therefore believe that, also because of the lexical content of the wording of Article 113 (4) of the Rules of Procedure, the use of secret ballots is always admissible where reference is made to the protection of linguistic minorities, with the warning that - by innovating in relation to the previous procedural practice in relation to the high number of requests - the adoption of the secret ballot will not preclude the application of the so-called kangaroo rule, i.e. the voting of the common parts of the amendments with consequent preclusive effect on the subsequent amendments in the event of rejection. On the other hand, with regard to the amending proposals which refer in different ways to Articles 13 et seq. of the Constitution on civil and ethical social relations, as referred to in Article 113 (4) of the Rules of Procedure, the Presidency - again in the light of the debate held in the Junta - considers to admit the secret ballot on the only amendments referring to the functions of the Chambers (Articles 1 and 18 of the draft law at our examination) and not to the legislative procedure (Article 10 of the draft law). That's the decision. On the basis of it, it will be possible to determine which amendments will be the subject of a secret vote. The list will be distributed as soon as possible. In the meantime, we are beginning to vote; then, from time to time, on the basis of these principles, which refer to the matter, we will see what type of vote to adopt. Unfortunately, the large number of amendments force us to move forward, considering how to vote, following the principle set out in this decision.
