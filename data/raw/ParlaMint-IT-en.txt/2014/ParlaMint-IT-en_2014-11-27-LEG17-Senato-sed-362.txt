ParlaMint-IT_2014-11-27-LEG17-Senato-sed-362.ana.u28	Mr President, I would like to thank the interrogators for these questions which give me the opportunity to address even more general issues. I would like to organise my response in two parts: the first on macroeconomic issues, the second more specifically on the issue of the privatisation process. So, you will excuse me if I have a different order than the questions, but I hope to answer them thoroughly. Let me say one thing about debt: the main way to reduce GDP debt is to increase GDP; the other solutions that are often overshadowed by the press in an explicit and ambiguous manner are dangerous. It is the Government's intention to continue to put at the centre of the issue of the slowdown in growth and then the reversal of debt, which is a fundamental objective, real growth - however, nominal growth is out of control by the actions of a member government of monetary union - in maintaining a fiscal adjustment position. I am referring to the fact that Italy is one of the very few countries that has maintained and continues to maintain a positive primary surplus for an uninterrupted period, excluding 2009, which has been an exceptional year. That is the first point. In this context, all other measures should be included. As I said, there are some magnitudes that are out of government control. The fact that the debt-to-GDP ratio sees growth before starting to fall again in 2016, depends in large, if not total, on adverse macroeconomic considerations: I am referring to the continuing recession and the fact that eurozone inflation is far from the equilibrium value of 2%. If today we had a balance value of eurozone inflation close to the actual 2%, the debt would already be in a long-term descent. Having said that, on the macroeconomic situation I add one thing to the issue of output gap. Without entering into the technical question I answer the question, which was addressed to know how this is decided. The value of the potential income of each country and, consequently, the measure of output gap is decided jointly at European level, first at technical level, in the specific working group on this issue and then at political level. I would add that in political terms, that is to say in the Ecofin discussions in which I took part, or rather that I chaired as President-in-Office, there is growing awareness in many finance ministers, not in all, that the output gap measures decided upon and used by the Commission are currently being decidedly off-line. We are therefore preparing to review, first at the technical level and then at the political level, these measures which, I repeat, are jointly decided upon. So in the past the Italian representative participated in a context of collective decision and approved measures that, when they were taken, were far more reasonable than now. The problem is that after three years of prolonged recession the measures decided with a statistical method suitable for the past are no longer so they need to be revised. On the question of privatisation, first of all I would remind you that the objective of privatisation is not only debt reduction, and in this respect it is an important contribution, but it is also a process that aims to expose the companies involved by the State to positive market pressures and to give a stimulus to the improvement of the management of many of these. Therefore, in defining the privatisation path that consists of various elements and therefore of various enterprises that are the subject of the process that have specific dynamics and characteristics, we must not forget this aspect, on which I will return in a moment. However, I would like to stress that, precisely in order to express the best of the process of valorisation of an enterprise that is preparing to be exposed on the market, there may be considerations that lead to slowing down, but certainly not to interrupt the privatisation process. You have rightly pointed out that the results initially imagined are lower than expected. Part of the explanation for this must be attributed to the less favourable macroeconomic dynamics and the resulting less favourable market dynamics, but in some cases also to the fact that it has been preferred to improve and complete the process of internal exploitation of some of these enterprises in order to choose the ideal time. The Government's target of 0.7% of proceeds from privatisation in 2015 and for three years remains confirmed, although it is aware that 2014 will close not with zero results, but with some results. I remember that in 2014, in addition to the listing of Fincantieri was completed the privatisation operation of RAI Way, which recorded a double demand compared to the supply with the presence of many foreign operators; it was finalized the sale through competitive procedure of more than 30 percent of CdP Reti, holding that holds 30 percent of Terna and Snam. With regard to the privatisation of companies directly involved, as some have mentioned, two decrees of the President of the Council of Ministers have been approved that determine criteria for the privatization of the participation in the capital of Poste Italiani and ENAV. Specifically, these arrangements provide for compliance with the objectives of widespread ownership and stability of ownership, also in view of the protection of the characteristics of public utility services. In addition, last week began to operate a joint working group between the Ministry of Infrastructures and Transport and the Ministry of Economy and Finance on the process of listing Ferrovie dello Stato, of which it is initially expected to activate the process relative to Great Stations. Moreover, at the most favourable time from the market point of view, further disposals of ENEL's shares will be decided, while maintaining control of the company. With regard to local participants, which many senators have mentioned, they are clearly part of the more general privatisation strategy while respecting the decision-making autonomy of the individual participating administrations, with several advantages for the public sector: there will be cost reductions (which has been mentioned), loss plans, lower charges for service contracts and various transfers. I remember that the state of the participants, which are in very large numbers, is very diversified: some of them are already on the market and operate in efficient conditions, in many other cases, unfortunately, they are extremely inefficient bodies and agencies. Therefore, when this sector is introduced into the privatisation strategy, the context of rationalisation must also be assessed. This requires, for example, the aggregation of many of these entities and, from this point of view, the stability law currently being discussed in Parliament contains measures which contain incentives for aggregation. In particular, the incentive offered by Article 43 of the Stability Act is the exclusion from the Internal Stability Pact of investment expenditure for local authorities which are making total or partial disposals of the participants. This will allow, directly and indirectly, to take into account the need to achieve much greater efficiency, in some cases, than those involved in the various sectors in which they operate. Finally, I would point out that the privatisation strategy also includes the plan for the development and disposal of the State's real estate, which, according to the 2014 Stability Act, should guarantee an income of not less than 500 million per annum between 2014 and 2016. The Government is proceeding to the progressive transfer of public assets, both at national and local level, also through the establishment of dedicated real estate funds that could subsequently be listed on the market. Finally, there was a question about the employment implications in the case of some enhancement processes. There may be, but it is to be examined on a case-by-case basis, consequences on employment structure with a view to increasing exploitation. In my view, it will be necessary to assess on a case-by-case basis, taking into account existing instruments and new instruments that may be introduced to deal with transition situations from an employment point of view.
