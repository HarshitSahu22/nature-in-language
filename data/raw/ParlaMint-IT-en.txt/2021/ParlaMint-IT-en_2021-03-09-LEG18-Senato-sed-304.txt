ParlaMint-IT_2021-03-09-LEG18-Senato-sed-304.ana.u34	ANGRISANI (Mixed). Mr President, I would like to speak to the House about the growing problem in today's news pages, particularly in the Covid era, of people suffering from pathologies for whom integrated home care is necessary. Integrated home care (ADI) is a set of social and health care services and interventions provided to the patient within his/her home. It is performed by different professionals, with the aim of avoiding, as far as possible, the hospitalization of the patient or its location in a residential structure beyond the strictly necessary time. It is a home service that avoids a traumatic situation for the patient, as well as an increase in the cost of assistance in economic terms. However, ADI procedures and types of care do not seem to be sufficient solution for many Italian families. There are more and more requests for help, the cries of desperation of families within which there are subjects presenting the pathology of SMA (spine muscular atrophy) that need precisely ADI. These are subjects who do not walk, as in the case of the nine-year-old Christian, who is entitled to sixty-five hours of weekly nursing care and who uses a structure to which the ASL gives as much as 13,000 euros per month. They are connected to an H24 lung fan, need salivary suction and need to be helped with maneuvers and machinery to expel the catharrus in abundance. However, these subjects, who would need instead to live a full life, socialize, see and know the world, cannot do so because, by law, they are provided exclusively home care. Subject ADI and the operator in charge cannot leave the house. Often this staff is trained over the years for that type of specific intervention and is then recruited by other hospital bodies, inexorably breaking the thread of trust and affective relationship that binds them to the patient. In the era of relaunching inclusion I find it a social flaw, a vulnus of a huge system, to allow this problem to still afflict such a large number of families, already fragile and scourged by the medical problem itself. For all these reasons, it would be appropriate to regulate home care with appropriate discipline and to promote in the appropriate fora of personalised assistance projects involving the reintegration of staff trained for that type of intervention. Staff should be recruited by ASLs or regions with an indefinite contract. This would be a guarantee for families, because in this way the staff would not leave the patients for a better assignment, as happened over the years. Nurses during the hours of care should be able to follow the subject and, in particular, the patient, the child, wherever he wants and should go - to school, to medical visits or to take walks - reducing significantly additional costs for families in terms of other assistants or of their own hours of work taken away. The project should include, in addition to nursing staff, figures such as physiotherapists, speech therapists, anesthetists, cardiologists and specialized assistants. Thank you for your intervention and I hope that this will take place as soon as possible. Hi, Christian. [[Applause.]]
