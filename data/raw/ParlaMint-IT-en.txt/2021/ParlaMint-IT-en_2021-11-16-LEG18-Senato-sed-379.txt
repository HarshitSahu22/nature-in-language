With regard to the verification referred to in Article 126 (4) of the Regulation, it should be recalled that the State's balance sheet, pursuant to Article 14 of Law No 243 of 24 December 2012, is subject to a balance rule by which the value of the net balance to be financed or to be used in it must be consistent with the policy objectives of public finance. This consistency must be taken into account both in the technical report and in the technical-illustrious note annexed to the draft budget law, pursuant to Article 21 (12) (a) (c) and 12-c (a) of Law No 196 of 2009. With regard to financial profiles, the technical report provides information on the consistency of the policy value of the net balance to be financed or used with the policy objectives. In particular, the technical report sets out a correlation table between the net balance to be financed on a programme basis and the programmed public administration account, which represents the consistency between the policy balances relating to the state budget and the policy objective set out in the Update Note to the 2021 Economy and Finance Document. It is essentially, as the same technical report shows, the link between the net balance to be financed from the State budget, as a result of the general overview of the draft budget, and the net programmatic debt of the State, i.e. including the effects of the public finance manoeuvre on the state sector, and the link between the said net debt and the programmatic debt of the general government as a whole. The technical report shall, in particular, set out the subsequent steps which, starting from the draft integrated budget law, allow the objectives to be defined in accordance with the provisions of the parliamentary resolutions approving the DEF 2021 Update Note. In short, it is noted that the link table shows a net balance to be financed, in terms of competence of 202 billion for 2022, due to final revenues of 628 billion and final expenditure of 830 billion. In 2023 the net balance to be financed amounted to 179 billion due to final revenue of 638 billion and final expenditure of 818 billion. In 2024 the net balance to be financed amounted to 116 billion with final revenue amounting to 645 billion and final expenditure at 761 billion. Moving on to the corresponding values of the net debt of the general government, it is noted that in 2022, the final revenues for the Public Administration amounted to 891 billion, the final expenses amounted to 996 billion and the net debt amounted to 105 billion; in 2023 the final revenues amounted to 926 billion, the final expenses amounted to 1.002 and the debt amounted to 76 billion; in 2024 the final revenues amounted to 934 billion, the final expenses amounted to 1.001 and the debt amounted to 66 billion. These net balance values appear to be consistent, net of roundings, both with the maximum level of the net balance to be financed set out in Article 1 of the draft budget bill and with the net programmatic debt, as a result of the trend values set out in the DEF 2021 Update Note, modified in the light of the overall effects of the manoeuvre, as represented by the summary statement annexed to the 2022 draft budget law. It is therefore proposed to bring to the attention of the President of the Senate an opinion of nulla preclude in relation to the profiles referred to in Rule 126 (4) of the Rules of Procedure. With regard to the verification of the content of the draft budget law, it should be remembered that Article 21, paragraph 1-d, of Law No 196 of 2009, as amended by Law No 163 of 2016, in implementation of Article 15, paragraph 2, of Law No 243 of 2012, places precise limits on the content of the first section of the draft budget law, establishing that it, in any case, must not contain rules of delegation, of an ordinamental or organizer nature, nor interventions of a localistic or micro-sectoral nature or rules that provide for the direct variation of the income or expenditure forecasts contained in the second section of the same bill. The above-mentioned content notes both for the purpose of the removal of the foreign provisions, returned to the President of the Senate pursuant to Rule 126 (3) of the Rules of Procedure, and as a criterion to be used for the evaluation of the admissibility of the amending proposals during the parliamentary examination. Therefore, for the purposes of the presidential determinations referred to in article 126, paragraph 3, of the Rules of the Senate, the following provisions are indicated: - Article 58, which introduces the possibility to proceed also by electronic means to the joint examination of the situation relating to the protection of workers in the case of the communication of suspension or reduction of the working time; - Article 59, which intervenes on the identification of the INPS headquarters competent to grant ordinary wage supplements; - Article 64, which introduces the possibility of the examination also by telematic means of the business situation in the case of trade union consultation following the request for extraordinary treatment of wage integration; - Article 90, paragraph 2, which provides for the postponement to 31 December 2022 of the deadline for the use of the App-Immunity and the relative platform; - Article 93, paragraph 3, which entrusts national federations of the health professions with the tasks of organization and management of a unitary network of connection, interoperability and software. The above provisions appear to have legal content and have no financial impact. Furthermore, the text does not contain rules of a clear localistic or micro-sectoral nature. Finally, there are no explicit delegation rules within the meaning of Article 77 of the Constitution.

ParlaMint-IT_2021-11-16-LEG18-Senato-sed-379.ana.u42	BINETTI (FIBP-UDC). Mr President, only a few days ago in this House we voted and were convinced that the extension of our Commission of inquiry into the events of the Forteto was approved. We have done so for different reasons, but all of us believe that we cannot tolerate what is now a systematic way of affecting the rights of children. Next Saturday, 20 November, will be the 30th anniversary of the UN Convention on the Rights of Childhood and Adolescence. The real issue is that violence is combined, against these children, which sometimes ripen in the family context to violence not less serious - in my opinion even more serious - that mature in an institutional or para-institutional context, as the events of the Forteto confirm from day to day, as our deepening continues. The dramatic thing, President, that I put to your attention is that, if the Forteto represents a story that has ended - bad, but it has ended, because with sixteen suicides of teenagers and young people we can frankly say that it has ended badly - today similar events and events are repeated, not in a "Community" way, as happened to the Forteto, but certainly concrete. I am talking about children who are driven away from families, parked in family homes, with a whole series of problems that another Commission will be able to explore, but above all without being returned to the family; in many cases, with the paradoxical effect, described in question 3-02832 that these children are then entrusted to the parent initially accused of violence. As if to say: a mother reproaches or in any case denounces a father for the erotic games, not to mention violence, with the daughter; then, after a long stay, confused and messy, in the family houses, the child is entrusted to the father, ignoring the requests that concern the possible availability of grandparents and many other things. It almost seems that these works teach us nothing. I wonder how it is possible for the Ministry of Justice never to answer our questions on these points and never feel the need to clarify situations that have an indication of drama that is not imaginary or imaginary, but real, concrete and documented.
