Mr President, Senators and fellow Senators, on behalf of the Democratic Party Group, pursuant to Rule 55 (3) of the Rules of Procedure of the Senate, I am in favour of amending the timetable to introduce and schedule the motion proposed by the Democratic Party on inclusion income. Our goal is to discuss in the parliamentary classrooms, not on Facebook and not chasing statements through the press, a problem felt by many citizens in need, by local authorities and by the third sector, to give certainty on such an important issue. Parliament has been blocked for a hundred days, the Commissions have not yet set up because we have to wait for more or less transparent negotiations on much more. We could discuss the decree-law earthquake already this week; it was decided to do so next week. We call for urgent and important measures and problems to be discussed in this House. Why Inclusion Income? Because from the first of July this measure introduced in the last legislature will become fully universal, thanks to the resources allocated in the last budget law - at last, whatever it may be said - and also in the government programme, despite the fact that it is still complaining, staying a little behind the debate of the nineties, that only our social state system, together with Greece, does not have a minimum income measure under the test of the means. In truth, this is an old debate because, in the last legislature, the seventeenth, the Governments of the Democratic Party have included a unique measure of combating poverty: income of inclusion. When we arrived at the government, in this country 40 million euros were spent experimental for the fight against poverty; at the end of the legislature almost 3 billion structural resources were allocated to the regime, to finance a single instrument, the income of inclusion, to the fight against poverty. A tool that, in the near future, will become fully universal, reaching 700,000 families and 2.5 million people in conditions of need and social fragility in our country. We must give a clear answer to the families, to the people involved, to the resources that are there to finance the income of inclusion. We hear, in the media, of miraculous proposals to combat poverty, without cover, without certainty about the times. Some members of the majority and the government talk about postponing the introduction of citizenship income for a few years because first it is necessary to reorganize employment centers. Throughout this programme, which still knows a lot about election campaigns and very little about its proximity to citizens' problems, it is not clear what will happen to the EUR 3 billion allocated to inclusion income, to those questions and to that instrument. In all welfare systems there are two tools to guarantee income to those in need: a more insurance instrument, linked to contributory history, and a welfare one, subject to the test of means. Finally, as I said, in the last parliamentary term the Italian social state also had the second, with certain resources and regime. In the debate on newspapers and social media these days we continue to confuse the tools: those related to contributory history and those subjected to media testing. To give certainty not only to families in need but to the municipalities, to the third sector, to associations that for too many years have been left alone to fight in trenches against social marginality and poverty conditions, from Parliament and the Government must come clear words about the resources and measures that are being put in place. Certainty of resources means not only giving certainty to the citizens who must use them, but also to all the operators who must accompany them. For us the income of inclusion was not only a monetary transfer, but also an instrument of social activation, even before work, and there must be, not for nothing, certain resources not only for employment centres but also for social services, for the third sector and for those who have to live the services, accompanying families in conditions of social fragility. In Parliament, not on Facebook or in other places, there must be clear and clear words about the resources and instruments that are being put in place to combat poverty. There are two ways to be close to the citizens: one is to make fun of them with miraculous electoral proposals; the other is to look them in the eyes and tell them clearly what the instruments will accompany them, especially when conditions of need and fragility are found. The Democratic Party, in the last legislature, has chosen the second approach, we ask the majority and the government to tell us what theirs is. [[Applause from the PD Group.]]

ParlaMint-IT_2018-06-12-LEG18-Senato-sed-10.ana.u29	ROJC (PD). Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, on Sunday, June 3rd, Senator Mario Toros, one of the most significant political and institutional figures of the post-war period in Friuli-Venezia Giulia, died in Udine, at the age of ninety-five, and a reference point for many political representatives of my region. To him and to his political-administrative action Friuli-Venezia Giulia owes much, in the context of the difficult years of post-war reconstruction and post-earthquake; years in which the Region has managed to travel new ways and achieve ambitious goals, for some defined even unexpected. Mario Toros was born in a peasant family of Feletto Umberto. Having attended professional schools, he has self-taught training. Mario Toros joins the Catholic Action and ACLI groups. At the age of fourteen he entered the Bertoli foundries of Udine as a worker: an experience - he often repeated - that had taught him much. As an antifascist Toros participated in the Resistance, fighting in the Partisan Brigade Osoppo Friuli. In 1950 he was one of the founders of the CISL and soon joined the Christian Democracy. Aclista and Catholic convinced, in Friuli devastated by the war becomes a political figure of reference, first as city councilor in Tavagnacco and, then, in Manzano. Elected in the first Provincial Council of Udine, he serves as city councilman. In 1958 he was elected to the Chamber, where he will be confirmed deputy in 1963 and 1968. In 1972 there was instead his election to the Senate, where he held the post of senator until 1987, completing seven legislatures. Mario Toros was a politician of rank, so much so that he was nicknamed "the senator" for his competence and great authority. He had charisma, sensibility and crazy ability to reach the hearts of people, as one of his five grandchildren said about him in the aftermath of his disappearance. In the course of his long Roman experience he was Undersecretary at work with three different Presidents of the Council and several times Minister of the Republic, first to the Regions and then to work and social security. Toros pays a lot for the autonomy and specialty of the Friuli-Venezia Giulia Region. He was a faithful interpreter of the cultural identity of his own land, with multiple roots. With him disappears a noble and enlightened witness of a difficult time. The present and the past are means - he will say - the end is the future. That's what you like to remember Mario Toros. I think it is crucial not to leave in oblivion those who preceded us, in particular when a person identifies himself with his own community and becomes its spokesperson par excellence, as was Mario Toros, loved and esteemed by his people for the great respect for the institutions he had served with honesty and dedication. To the daughters Carla and Franca and her five grandchildren there is the rightful condolences of this House. [[Applause from the PD, M5S, L-SP, FI-BP and Aut groups (SVP-PATT, UV).]]
