ParlaMint-IT_2020-09-16-LEG18-Senato-sed-258.ana.u7	MALAN (FIBP-UDC). Mr President, I would like to ask a few questions. I recall that the Rules of Procedure of the Senate, which are provided for in the Constitution, states that the Government must respond within 15 days of their submission. On 18 August we filed, at the first signature of Senator Ferro and according to my signature (some dozen other senators follow), the question 3-01873 on the question of changing the definition of the residence tax. This change has been approved in the framework of a bill voted with the usual confidence, comprising hundreds of arguments. By coincidence, it has meant that the partner's father - it is not gossip - of the President of the Council no longer risks up to ten years and six months in prison, but simply - and only perhaps - an administrative penalty for having withheld the money of the residence tax, rather than paying it to the City of Rome, of which it should have some confidence being administered, as we know, by an exponent of the Movimento 5 Stars. I would have expected that there would be a request from the President of the Council to come and clarify the situation; instead, not only is there no request, but, against the Rules of Procedure of the Senate, there was no reply, nor, as provided for in paragraph 3 of Rule 148, an explanation of the reasons for the failure to reply. On the occasion, I would also like to ask the answer to questions, first signed by Senator Gallone, 4-03905 and 4-03999, on the question of school benches: Commissioner Arcuri announced more than 2.4 million school benches, but, more than a month after the announcement of these contracts made by Dr Arcuri himself on 12 August, we have no idea how much they have been paid and who provides them. Of the only contract that emerged was communicated lately and informally, that it had actually been cancelled; but this contract provided for a price for the individual dealer of 247.78 euros, when I could not find it on the Internet at a price above 102 euros. To buy one! Also in this case, the Government, rather than hastening, since July 28, with reference to the first question, or from September 1, when another came, maintains a total lack of transparency on a waste (which probably a member of a certain party, very battling on expenses and the fight against waste, would have called rubria) of 360 million euros, at least, relating the cost to the highest price available on the Internet. I find it shameful that there is no answer and I find the lack of transparency shameful. I invite the Government, which is authoritatively represented here, to give an answer, even outside the parliamentary classrooms (as it usually does, for the truth), because I believe that the Italians must go and vote on an important referendum and for many regional and local elections knowing if the State has thrown away 360, at least, millions of euros of taxpayers and knowing if the President of the Council has done a big favor to the father-in-law with a measure that should be of extraordinary necessity and urgency. Perhaps there was necessity and urgency, but the Constitution refers to urgency for the state, not for family members, even in informal families.
