ParlaMint-IT_2017-10-31-LEG17-Senato-sed-907.ana.u7	Ladies and gentlemen, the President of the Republic sent the following letter: "Rome, 27 October 2017 Honourable President, I transmit to you the message with which I ask the Chambers for a new resolution, pursuant to the first paragraph of Article 74 of the Constitution, regarding the law: "Measures to counter the financing of companies producing anti-personnel mines, ammunition and cluster submunitions," approved by the Senate of the Republic on 6 October 2016 and by the Chamber of Deputies on 3 October 2017. Please accept, Madam President, the assurance of my highest consideration. ==References====External links== I therefore read the message sent to the Chambers by the President of the Republic: "Rome, 27 October 2017 Ladies and gentlemen, I have been submitted, for promulgation, the law on "Measures to combat the financing of companies producing anti-personnel mines, ammunition and cluster submunitions." The measure (which consists of seven articles) was approved by the 6th Permanent Commission of the Senate in its deliberations on 6 October 2016 and, ultimately, by the Chamber of Deputies on 3 October 2017. It introduces, in Article 1, for authorised intermediaries (as defined in Article 2), the total prohibition on the financing of companies, which, in Italy or abroad, directly or through subsidiaries or associates, carry out activities in any way related to the production or distribution or marketing of anti-personnel mines, ammunition and sub-munitions clusters. The law contains innovative aspects, which are undoubtedly positive because they strengthen the measures to combat the production of such dangerous and treacherous weapons, including administrative responsibility on the part of the bodies. At the same time, it must be noted the presence within it of a provision that clearly results in contradiction with the declared aims of the regulatory intervention and that appears characterized by important critical profiles. I refer to Article 6, under the heading "sanctions," which in paragraph 2 has no criminal relevance for the financing of enterprises producing anti-personnel mines and cluster bombs, if carried out by subjects who have apical positions for the whole of the authorized intermediary bodies. In that paragraph, a pecuniary administrative penalty of 50,000 to 250,000 euros is provided for natural persons who have the roles of administration or management of authorised intermediaries or who, on their behalf, perform control functions, in the event of a violation of the prohibition to finance companies operating in the field of anti-personnel mines and cluster munitions. The subject matter of this provision is already governed, in general, by criminal rules contained in Law No 374 of 1997 and Law No 95 of 2011. The latter provides for the banning of cluster munitions and ratification and implements the Oslo Convention (made in Dublin on 30 May 2008), incriminating in Article 7 financial assistance for anyone who employs, develops, produces, acquires, acquires, stores, preserves or transfers, directly or indirectly, cluster munitions or parts thereof and sanctioning such behaviour with imprisonment from three to twelve years and fine from EUR 258.28 to EUR 516.456. The conduct of "financial assistance" (contemplated among those prohibited by Article 1 of the Oslo Convention) is therefore perfectly comparable to that prohibited by Article 1 of the law in question, for which only the administrative penalty is provided by Article 6, paragraph 2, if carried out by the qualified persons indicated above. As far as anti-personnel mines are concerned, Article 7 of Law No 374 of 1997 sanctions, with the same penalties, anyone who uses, sells, disposes of, exports, imports, holds the same anti-personnel mines or parts thereof, or uses or transfers, directly or indirectly, patent rights or technologies for manufacturing, in Italy or abroad. This provision is expressly without prejudice to Article 9 of Law No 106 of 1999, which ratified and implemented the Convention on the Prohibition of the Use, Storage, Production and Transfer of Anti-Personal Mines and their Destruction, signed in Ottawa on 3 December 1997. The financing granted for activities prohibited by Article 7 shall include one of the conduct referred to in Article 1 (1) of the Ottawa Convention, which explicitly commits States to prohibit, inter alia, "to assist, encourage or induce, in any way, anyone to undertake" the activities prohibited by the Convention. The two incriminating forecasts are the result of the implementation of international obligations contained in the Conventions, ratified by Italy, which explicitly require, both under art. Amendment No 9, the imposition of criminal sanctions to prevent and suppress any activity prohibited by them. Therefore, their regulatory core cannot be changed without directly affecting the principle protected by Article 117 of the Constitution, which provides for the obligation to exercise legislative power "in compliance with the constraints arising from Community law and international obligations." The Constitutional Court, since its judgments n. 348 and 349 of 2007, has clarified that Article 117 of the Constitution is suitable to attribute a particular position in the system of sources to international norms as norms interposed in a possible judgment of constitutionality, so that the laws that conflict with international treaties that have already been implemented in the internal order are vitiated by unconstitutionality. From this comes a force of the laws of execution of the aforementioned Treaties so as to exclude that the conduct of financial assistance to prohibited activities may remain without criminal sanctions and this in order to ensure that the violation of the prohibition is effectively opposed. The regulation contained in Article 6 (2) of the law in question, which does not include the "criminal safeguard clause" (unless the fact constitutes a criminal offence) is included in the above-mentioned legal framework. This would result, by virtue of the principle of specialty of administrative unlawfulness laid down in Article 9 of Law No 689 of 1981, in the effect of depriving of criminal importance the conduct of arson of financing, carried out by qualified persons, which would be sanctioned only by administrative means, contrary to international obligations. This effect would relate not only to future conducts of violation of the prohibition of financing, but also to those committed before the entry into force of this law, in respect of which, without transitional rules, administrative offences would not be applicable (Penalty, United Section, judgment n. 25457 of 2012). Under different, but related, aspects, I still have to point out that the existence, for the same conduct of financing, of two different punitive regimes - the one criminal, the other administrative - solely because of the task held by the agent in the context of an authorized intermediary or of the nature of the user (company and not individual entrepreneur), poses profiles of dubious constitutional compatibility in relation to art. 3 of the Constitution. In fact, natural persons who exercise the functions of administration and management of authorised intermediaries or, on their behalf, carry out control functions would benefit, as a result of the discipline contained in Article 6 (2), from a purely administrative penalty scheme. On the other hand, all other entities providing financial assistance outside the authorised intermediaries' circuit (i.e. due to the absence of an administrative act of authorisation) would remain subject to the criminal sanctions outlined above. For the reasons set out above, Article 6 (2) of the measure presents clear profiles of constitutional illegitimacy. These can only be overcome by a limited, but necessary, intervention that ensures the criminal importance of financial assistance, by anyone carried out, to the activities prohibited by Article 1; relevance which, by contrast, would be irremediably less with the entry into force of the law. Therefore, I ask the Chambers - pursuant to the first paragraph of Article 74 of the Constitution - for a new resolution on the law passed on 3 October 2017. f.to Sergio Mattarella countersigned Paolo Gentiloni.
