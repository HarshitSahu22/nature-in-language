ParlaMint-IT_2017-05-25-LEG17-Senato-sed-830.ana.u22	Mr Russo (PD). Madam President, I only speak to reassure my fellow Members and to explain at least in part the reason for this delay. The subject, paradoxically, is not financial coverage, as Senator Mario Mauro recalled, indeed the delay is linked to the fact that it is a very complex measure and, regarding at least five or six Ministries, it has required technical reports from all these Dicasteries; and as these reports arrived, fortunately it has been found that many of the forecasts inserted as framework norm in this measure already received financial coverage from other existing legislation. Paradoxically, therefore, we have made clear the financial capacity of this measure and therefore it simply remains to be counted on the final result. To Senator Rizzotti, who followed the measure and therefore knows it well (but especially to those who did not follow it), I simply want to remind you that all the relevant ministries have been involved, there have been successive meetings, and in the Assembly obviously the contact person of the competent Ministry comes. What we are discussing here is a regulation that is born in the Committee on Constitutional Affairs precisely because it is a norm of principles, a framework standard that has the great value, I believe (and the debate held last week witnessed it with the words of many colleagues) to put at the centre for the first time the theme of the deaf community in our country and to offer an overall vision also with respect to the different philosophical and ideological orientations that are housed today in the world of associationism linked to auditory disability. We are really a step away from approval. There is a technical problem, I think also of overload of work, in these hours, for the Ministry of Economics and Finance for the maneuver that commits the Ministry to the House, we must say it with great frankness. I hope that things will be settled this weekend, but I would like it to remain in the acts that there is neither a problem of coverage nor a lack of commitment compared to a measure that, outside our classrooms, is widely awaited and has had a great recognition. I therefore call on my fellow Members to have some more confidence, hoping that next week will be the good one to conclude the examination of the measure.
