ParlaMint-IT_2013-06-11-LEG17-Senato-sed-38.ana.u5	He died yesterday in Viareggio, at the age of 64, cut off from a serious illness, Milziade Caprili, Vice President of this Assembly in the 15th legislature. Milziade Caprili was born in Viareggio on 24 October 1948 and, in a spirit of continuity with his paternal militancy, had begun to engage in politics when he was very young, joining, at only 15 years of age, the Communist Youth Federation of his city. After passing to the Italian Communist Party, he had become one of the protagonists of the local political and administrative life: city councilman for over twenty years, several times councilor, as well as deputy mayor of Viareggio. In 1983, at the beginning of the 9th legislature, candidate on the PCI lists, he was elected to the Chamber of Deputies and confirmed by the electoral corps in the two successive legislatures. After 1991 he had dealt with the changes taking place in his party choosing to join the political formation of the Communist Refoundation, in whose ranks he was elected on the occasion of the 1992 general election. He then returned for fourteen years to the political scene of his city, covering on several occasions important administrative positions, to be then candidate for the Senate in the lists of Communist-European Left Refoundation in the 2006 elections. He became a member of this Assembly, of which he was elected Vice President. In that difficult parliamentary season he was able to give his contribution of rigour and style, leading our work in an exemplary way. It was also an authoritative member of the Commission's public works and communications, and subsequently of the Industry Commission, as well as of the Parliamentary Security Committee of the Republic. I am sure that I interpret the sentiments of the whole House in expressing the condolences and participation of the Senate in the pain of Milziade Caprili's family and fellow citizens and in inviting colleagues to observe, in his memory, a minute of silence and recollection. Mr President, with the death of Milziade Caprili, a historical figure of the political, administrative and social life of my city and of Versilia as a whole is missing. A careful politician, and I consciously use this term, because I am convinced that he would appreciate all its value and meaning. An active scholar in the history of the workers' movement, but also a pungent and ironic animator of the city's administrative debate. Caprili was a politician not only viareggio, but at national level, as President Grasso recalled. When he was very young he was the leader of the Communist Youth Federation and then militant and leader of the Communist Party. His has been a personal history that has its roots in the Resistance and anti-fascism, both by family tradition and as a conscious and consolidated cultural heritage. He lived a long parliamentary career first in the ranks of the PCI, then in the ranks of the Refoundation, and finally as Vice President of the Senate. Caprili lived the years of change and the crisis of politics with strong autonomy of judgment and great intellectual curiosity. He was a respectful man of the institutions and always ready to criticize conformism and conservation wherever they nested. Hence his life choices, which more than once led him into collision with the party and with the comrades with whom he fought. In recent years he has come to the world of volunteering; he has committed himself as President of the Green Cross, as if to mean that politics, in order to change and understand the great changes that are taking place, should start from there: from the spirit of service, from active citizenship, from the practice and culture of solidarity, from concrete issues; but, above all and always, from the needs of the weakest and from the protection of the rights of the world of work. As President of the Green Cross he experienced the days of the dramatic railway accident of 29 June 2009 that struck my city, bringing out with determination all the importance and role that the world of volunteering can play in helping, supporting and promoting human and social cohesion. In recent times, aware of the institutional delays of our land on socio-health issues, he has committed himself to setting up the observatory on socio-health policies to draw the attention of politics and citizens to the major issues, the limits and the prospects of Versilia's welfare. On several occasions I also had opinions different from those he supported, but the comparison with his ideas has always been marked, as well as by respect, by deep and motivated arguments, however never trivial. I'm sure Viareggio will not forget his thought and work. To the wife and family, to all the volunteers of the Green Cross of Viareggio a heartfelt hug and my deepest condolences and those of all my Group. Today I struggle to separate political memories from personal ones: discussions with him about the highest systems, as we liked to say, but also about the books we read and about sport, another of his passions. I remember clearly the small room, on the third floor of via del Policlinico, where Caprili worked for years in the organization department of PRC. It was in the early 1990s: a new generation of young people had approached politics and, starting from the student movements of those years, wanted to contribute to the reconstruction of a new political left. The collapse of the Berlin Wall, the end of real socialism, the tight criticism, intransigent towards those regimes that had demeaned and mortified the aspiration to the real movement to abolish the state of things present did not seem to us for a moment an obstacle to that ideal that - we thought - was much stronger than its concrete application. Those young people, of whom I was a part, had in Caprili a sure cultural and moral reference, as well as political. Then that room was often crowded: there was discussion with him about political cultures, the history of the workers' movement and the extraordinary figures of those women and those men who had animated it, starting from that Enrico Berlinguer who for Caprili had always been an indispensable point of reference. The refoundation of an ideal without falling into the mistakes of the past, the reflection on the power and tragedy of twentieth-century communism, the constant commitment to peace against war were the great issues that he wanted to put at the center of his political commitment and, together with these, the continuous research on that topic, today very current, that Caprili had grasped long before others: the crisis of politics and its forms of representation, the end of the mass parties and above all the risk that, without a necessary self-form, without a profound innovation and revisiting also cultural of the cornerstones of the twentieth century, our democracy would be weaker, more exposed to the authoritarian and demagogic drifts that already prepotentiously faced in those years. It was also for this reason that he placed his research in the heart of the political struggle: because he did not want to give up the idea that an intellectual, as in reality he was, was also and first and foremost a political militant. He left us too soon Milziade Caprili and, even though our streets had separated, as all too often happened in the tormented history of the Italian left, I will not forget and we will not forget his teachings, his irony, his experience and especially that refinement that we would still need very much.
