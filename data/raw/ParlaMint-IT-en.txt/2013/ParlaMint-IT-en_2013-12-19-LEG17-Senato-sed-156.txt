The public gaming activity, pursuant to Article 110 (6) (a) and (b) of the Single Text of the laws of public security, can be legally exercised only if the apparatus is connected to a telematic network managed by concessionaires of the chosen State - I remind all senators - through procedures of public evidence and submitted, both at the time of the granting of the concession and during the activity, to strict public order checks and of an economic and financial nature. The concessionary regime allows to channel within a legal network an activity that would otherwise be carried out by criminal organisations, without adequate controls and in a completely submerged way, thus subtracting the entire sector and its financial resources from the state. In view of the sensitivity of these issues, it is necessary, in addition to the sectoral law on public gambling, which has not yet existed, to provide for adequate discipline to regulate the crisis of the concessionary relationship in the event of the withdrawal or revocation of the concessionaire or of the exclusion of the selective procedure or of the failure to award of companies already concessionaires. I would remind all the senators that there was no legislation to date to regulate this kind of situation, which is dealt with by Amendment 1,150. In all these scenarios, in fact, the lack of specific legislative provisions, aimed at regulating the transfer of activities (not the maintenance of activities) carried out by the outgoing concessionaire as a result of the crisis, can cause a number of negative consequences, such as the passage of all or part of the activities carried out by the concessionaire of the legal world to the illegal one managed by organized crime; it can result in situations of unfair competitive advantage for some of the concessionaires present on the market to the detriment of others, in violation of the principle of equal treatment, as well as the risk of a prolonged suspension of the activity that would cause huge losses of revenue and consequent repercussions on the employment levels. To date the overall structure of these entities is not regulated in any way so that, in fact, in the event of a dealer's crisis these categories of entrepreneurs are without any safeguard measures. The approved provisions aim to: regulate crisis situations which could occur, with the aim of safeguarding the existence of the legal network and therefore the revenue related to the exerory, the protection of public order, the good faith and the protection of the weak categories; to ensure pardicio between all dealers, thus avoiding the forms of abuse of dominant positions on the market; to preserve the revenue to protect public expenditure and therefore general services financed also from these resources. For these purposes, the rule provides for a period of time, fixed in 90 days, during which the concessionaire affected by definitive measures of revocation or revocation continues to operate in order to allow the takeover of the other concessionaires, in accordance with principles inspired by the conditio. In this way, the integrity of the legal network is preserved and the activities carried out by the outgoing dealer, divided among the other dealers present on the market, avoid that this can determine and create dominant positions. Moreover, the approved rule clearly establishing that the mechanism envisaged for the transfer of activities from the concessionaire affected by a withdrawal or revocation measure to other parties takes action in the event of an appeal against the measure only following the judgment in favour of the administration, protects the State from any major claims that would be made if the activity was blocked before the final judgment. In such cases, the deception could be added to the damage, as the State could be sentenced to compensation for the damage suffered by the concessionaire whose activity had been suspended. Obviously, in detail, the provision under consideration also provides for a different mechanism for 6B devices, covering VLTs and 6A devices, relating to AWPs. VLTs are game systems consisting of a series of video-terminals, with remote control of the game and are installed exclusively in dedicated rooms. The law provides that the installation of each video terminal is subject to authorization, subject to payment of a large amount. The provision under consideration therefore provides that the rights held by the outgoing concessionaire, which give rise to the possibility of installing such equipment, are conferred, subject to an option to be exercised freely, on the other concessionaires, in proportion to the number of equipment operated, thus ensuring that such equipment is carried out freely. As regards AWPs, since the operators are the owners of the equipment, the rule lays down the right to terminate, within the same 90-day period, the contracts between the outgoing concessionaire and the operators. The provision, therefore, is intended exclusively to regulate the crisis of the concessionary relationship in the event of revocation or revocation of the concessionaire, in order to protect the legal network and the related revenue, as well as the par condicio linked to the market; in this way, it is evidently prevented that the activities carried out by the concessionaire are transferred in the field of lawlessness. With regard to the other aspect expressly mentioned, regarding local authorities, provisions are introduced based on the principle of autonomy and self-responsibility between levels of government, within the framework of the existing reciprocal relations of the constitutional structure of Title V. Regional and local levels of government, within the framework of their prerogatives - which are based on Article 117 - may, as far as their competence is concerned, issue legislative and regulatory provisions which, moreover, may affect the general regulatory framework, in the field of public games, prepared with state legislation and according to which, over the years, it has been possible, through state concessions, to build the complex network of collecting the game throughout the territory. These interventions, if not coordinated with those already adopted at State level, are clearly likely to undermine the general regulatory framework and the whole of the state grantor relationships which are based on the latter. These possible aspects, however, are likely to put into question the overall holding of the game collection and its forecasts of revenue, as currently dropped in its trends. In addition, distonic choices made at regional and local level, from the point of view of concessionaires, linked to the State by contracts may constitute a violation of the fundamental principle underlying contract law, which can be summarized in the well-known term pacta sunt servanda. Such infringement may be prodromic to compensation actions which it is not allowed to exclude that the dealers concerned address directly to the State. In view of all this, the provisions introduce the general principle that, if negative financial effects were to be caused to the detriment of the State, also due to the fearful actions of compensation, due to regulatory initiatives by substate levels of government, the National Government takes, in order to neutralize such negative effects, appropriate measures to reduce correspondingly the state transfers to the aforementioned levels of substate government. In essence, from the point of view of the technical and financial assessment of the scope of the new provisions, it is possible to conclude that the new provisions are valid in the sense that they are valid in order to maintain the entire revenue forecasts - also contained in the trends - and to avoid, on the contrary, negative financial effects.
