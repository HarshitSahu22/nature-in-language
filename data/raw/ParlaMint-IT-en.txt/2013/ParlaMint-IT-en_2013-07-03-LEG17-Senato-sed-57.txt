As I have already said, it is essential that these measures, which have had a beneficial effect in both employment and economic terms, be made stable: that is what we are being asked for, in the end, also with the transposition of the directive. Among other things, in view of the scarcity of resources, the proposal that we are putting forward is to reduce it to perhaps 50%, while still giving stability. Another amendment, to which we attach a certain importance and to which we would like the Government and the rapporteur to pay particular attention, concerns the fact that heat pumps are excluded from tax relief in the current text, with the justification that they will then benefit from other tax reductions. I would like to mention two issues here: the energy efficiency of heat pumps is very, very high and is more than 50% higher than that of traditional boilers. Heat pumps use renewable sources and if we analyse the motivation for which they were excluded, i.e. the fact that they receive an incentive through the thermal energy account, we have to say that it is actually very small and covers only a small percentage of the investment supported. Thus, by comparing the different incentive systems, the 65% deduction for boilers and the thermal account, there is a decided disproportion that does not reflect the energy benefits that can be obtained. There are two variables for the amendments: those that exclude all the heat pumps tout court and those that include perhaps the heat pumps coming from geotechnical plants at low enthalpy. These are - in our opinion - the most important. In addition, I propose a question already posed before: that of including among the interventions that can benefit from the facilities also the substitution by the companies of the coverage in Eternit with photovoltaic plants (Brusio).
