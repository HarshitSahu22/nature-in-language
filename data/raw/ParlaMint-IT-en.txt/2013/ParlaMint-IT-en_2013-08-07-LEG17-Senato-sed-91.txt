ParlaMint-IT_2013-08-07-LEG17-Senato-sed-91.ana.u87	BONFRISCO (PdL). Mr President, as you can see, this amendment brings to our attention a subject which is particularly debated and heard by the public. As everyone knows, in our country, the threshold below which it is possible to use cash to make payments between individuals, or between individuals and companies, or between non-banking administrations was, a few years ago, lowered to the current limit of 1,000 euros. Despite this, further strategies are still required to discourage and reduce even more the use of cash with the aim, more or less explicit and conscious, to achieve in the future the total or almost total abolition of this method of payment. In support of the goodness of these theses the promoters and supporters of the so-called fight against cash claim the fact that all this is thought and studied in order to obtain greater degrees of general well-being, equity, progress and social justice. The truth, however, is unfortunately also another, namely that the fight against the use of cash does not include any noble purpose. The arguments for this support sometimes represent real mystifications of objective reality, because the only real objective that meets this rule is to equip the banks with an exclusive, absolute monopoly on money. There are therefore some "non-truths" or some lies that should be exposed; for example, the main one, the most important one, namely that the fight against cash is the fundamental tool for combating tax evasion. But this is not the case because the evasive and elusive phenomena are numerically more relevant, such as the concealment of revenues and compensations, or the undue deduction of costs are implemented with many other techniques and with the use of fictitious structures and behaviors that prescind from the use of cash. It is also said that the fight against cash is decisive in the fight against thefts and robberies, but it is also necessary in this case to be truthful to the end.
