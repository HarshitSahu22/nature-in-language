ParlaMint-IT_2013-07-29-LEG17-Senato-sed-81.ana.u29	Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, Minister, these reflections are in the margins of the debate and are in addition to those that have been put forward by the rapporteurs at the last sitting. I must thank all those who have spoken for the suggestions and criticisms that have been brought to the measure, because above all the constructive ones the Government is willing to take into account in the continuation of its activity that does not end with this decree-law that, to a large extent, concerns the fight against youth unemployment: other interventions are planned, and for these additional interventions will certainly take account also of the useful observations presented during the general discussion. I must add a few words to what the rapporteurs have already said regarding the content of the decree-law. As my colleagues know, it regulates various aspects of labour policies, and in particular active policies: this is the case for incentives to recruit young people with an indefinite contract. In this way, we want to promote stable employment and combat the precariousness that has often been the subject of considerations and observations during the general discussion. Of course, more could have been done by broadening the audience of beneficiaries: this will be done when additional resources are available for reducing labour costs, which is the crucial variable on which to intervene to facilitate employment, especially for young people. The decree-law also intervenes on the rules governing employment contracts: this is a complex and delicate matter, on which the greatest conflicts between political forces and social forces are manifested. In the course of the discussion, some senators criticized the decree-law because it would overly increase flexibility; other senators criticized it for the opposite reason, i.e. because it would not have increased enough flexibility in employment relations. It is true that some form of flexibility has been introduced, for example in fixed-term employment contracts, mostly by referring the subject to collective bargaining. It was considered that, at a time such as this, economic recession and falling employment levels, it is appropriate to extend the possibility for companies to activate contracts of this type, which, albeit temporary, offer workers and young people the guarantees of law and contract typical of contracts of indefinite duration and which, in these circumstances, could usefully play an important role: that of interrupting the long periods of unemployment and inactivity, which increasingly affect young people without work. On the other hand, incentives for the conversion of fixed-term contracts into fixed-term contracts - also provided for in the Decree-Law - should increase the likelihood of temporary employment becoming more stable. The decree-law then intervenes on other types of temporary employment contracts, such as intermittent work and project work, to remedy some shortcomings already demonstrated by Law No 92 of 2012. This did not mean to call into question the establishment of such a reform, despite the considerable dissatisfaction it has created, especially from some social partners. It was not intended to reopen the wide-ranging debate on this subject and it is not excluded that further details should be considered later, taking into account the fact that monitoring of the effects of the reform is still under way and the results are expected. Account will also be taken of the results of some important negotiations that are taking place between the social partners in the margins of the Milan Expo, which could lead to important results on both the issues of flexibility and the safety of workers; results that can give useful indications to be, at least in part, taken up and extended to other sectors and territorial areas of the country. The Government is committed to following closely these events in Milan and to making them the subject of comparison with the social partners at national level in order to identify possible evolutions and a strengthening of the rules included in this decree. But perhaps the most significant part of this decree, at least for the articles dealing with labour issues, consists in interventions in favour of the so-called employability of young people, that is, the ability of young people to deal with the search for a stable and skilled job more likely to be successful. Along these lines we observe the establishment of a structure that the Government wants to form to address the problem of employment services, a problem faced by the President of the Council in a question time held in the Senate a few days ago. We are very behind in this area: we must recover the lost ground quickly; it will not be easy, but we will have to make a huge effort, especially if we want to take advantage of that programme for young people which the European Commission recommends and which it wants to implement from next year. In addition, the decree also contains measures to combat poverty, especially in the Mezzogiorno. The rules on start-up and further facilitation for self-entrepreneurship should also be mentioned. Overall, these are a number of broad-spectrum measures: different aspects of legislation and labour policies are affected, because in each of them there have been shortcomings and deficiencies that needed to be remedied as a matter of urgency. Finally, the decree also enters into tax matters with the delay of payment of the IMU and the postponement of the increase of VAT. They are temporary and partial answers: the time will come to find more solid and shared solutions, which will have to find accommodation in the more general framework of a fiscal intervention.
