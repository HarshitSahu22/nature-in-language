This can also be called the draft ban on border trade. The point is that border trade is a strikingly serious problem, and it is the state, and more specifically the previous government itself, which has created an insane excise policy. Indeed, it is not only about the tax revenues that are being transported to the Latvian state budget, but it is about virtually all aspects of a completely failed excise policy. In the case of border trade, no positive aspects can be found. The 70% increase in excise duty on beer adopted at the end of 2016 and entered into force in 2017 gave a strong impetus to the development and growth of border trade. And the transfer of tax revenue is only one aspect. Failure is also that alcohol consumption has not actually decreased, as the studies show. In fact, there is a large quantity of alcohol on the market, which has been taxed at a lower rate than before the increase in excise duty in Estonia. Our draft is largely based on the KPMG study published last October. We have based ourselves on the activity rates proposed in this study. If this draft were to find support, it would be possible to withdraw at least 90% of the border trade and possibly receive an additional EUR 100 million in tax revenue from the state budget as of next year.
