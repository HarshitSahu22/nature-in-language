ParlaMint-EE_2012-11-20_U1	Dear Riigikogu, good morning! We will begin the 9th working week Tuesday session of the 4th plenary session of the Riigikogu. Ladies and gentlemen, do you want to pass on inquiries or drafts? Please, Kadri Simson!
ParlaMint-EE_2012-11-20_U2	Ladies and gentlemen, On behalf of eight members of the Riigikogu, I have an inquiry to address Prime Minister Andrus Ansip. This inquiry concerns the mitigation of the increase in electricity prices. As you understand, the government has several levers in its hands to mitigate the rise in electricity prices next year. It is possible to make this decision in the debate on the budget in the Riigikogu, but even if the second reading of the budget ends without it, the situation is not yet hopeless. To convey the message that, even after the second reading of the budget, we will not waive the requirement that the increase in electricity prices should be softened, we will pass on an inquiry and we will continue to demand a price reduction even after the second reading of the state budget has been completed. Indeed, rising electricity prices are not only difficult for households. Eesti Pank warns that the rise in electricity prices will have a dampening effect on economic growth, as it will in particular reduce the real income of households and thus increase consumption and domestic demand. The poorer outlook for the economy will lead to a reduction in both investment and demand for labour in the future. It is estimated that next year we will face a 20% increase in the price of electricity, which will lead to an increase in prices in all other areas as well. What the government has now offered, a slight reduction in the renewable energy fee, will reduce the 20% price increase by only 1.6%. That little isn't enough. At the same time, a reduction in electricity excise duty to the EU minimum rate of 4.5 times the increase in electricity prices would help to alleviate further 4.6%. Together, these two measures would already have a tangible impact. Thank you.
