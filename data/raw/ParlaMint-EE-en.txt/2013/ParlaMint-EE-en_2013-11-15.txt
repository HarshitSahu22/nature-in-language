ParlaMint-EE_2013-11-15_U561	Mr President, I would like to thank you very much. The Government of the Republic has prepared a three-and-a-half-hour presentation on this draft. So let me get started. I will begin by saying that this is a very good bill. The Ministry of Culture started the preparation of the Act amending the Creative Persons and Creative Unions Act as early as 2011, on the basis of the Government's Action Programme, which entrusts the Minister of Culture and the Minister of Finance, among other things, with the task of resolving the health insurance problems of freelance artists. A wide range of stakeholders were involved in the discussions, both at the level of artists and at the level of the authorities. The preliminary draft has already been drawn up at the end of 2012 and has now passed two rounds of coordination. It has been substantially improved in autumn 2013. The revised document was approved by the Government of the Republic on 24 October and sent to the Riigikogu for discussion. Today, the draft at first reading at the plenary session of the Riigikogu aims to alleviate the problems associated with the treatment and pension insurance of freelance artists due to the special nature of their income from creative activities. The world around us has changed, and stable from clock to clock relationships with monthly paychecks are no longer governed by many sectors. The uncertainty about social guarantees arising from irregular income is not just a problem for creators; it is a wider problem. The Ministry of Culture sees the first step in this amendment of the law, and I emphasise the word 'first' as an important step in opening this discussion, although no agreement has yet been reached on the summation of social tax during the preparation of the draft. I am pleased to note that today the first agreements have been reached in this sphere as well. In supplementing the Creative People's and Creative Unions Act, it did not pass the fact that the foundations for the recognition of creative associations laid down in this law and the arrangements for the allocation of creative grants, in which this draft provides important clarity, have been left behind. At the time of the adoption of the current law, there was still clarity about the nature of the creative alliances and the division of creative areas that was established during the Soviet era. These principles should now be modernised. I will explain the four major changes that the draft provides for. Firstly, simplifying the payment of creative support to the creative person. Creative support is temporary social tax-taxed support for creators who have had a longer break in their income. For a period of six months up to one year, a person can apply through the Creative Union for a monthly allowance of the minimum wage, accompanied by health insurance. In essence, this means a straw to overcome the creative low. To date, a freelance creative person from the Creative Union has had to register as a self-employed person in order to receive creative support. In practice, many of the beneficiaries of creative support have registered as self-employed only at the time of receiving the grant, since they are mostly overpaid to pay mandatory advance payments to the self-employed person after the grant has ended. In essence, we have put pressure on individuals to become entrepreneurs in order to obtain social guarantees, even though the lack of income is a condition for specific support. The possibility that the Creative Union will pay social tax for the person at the time of the payment of the creative grant by means of a legislative amendment will help to reduce excessive bureaucracy when applying for creative support. Secondly, changes in the calculation of creative support. According to the current rules, the state will count on multiple contributions from several Creative Unions, taking into account one fifth of the average monthly salary paid to each Creative Union in Estonia in the year preceding the filing of the application, although according to the law, the creative person can apply for creative support from only one association at a time. By means of a legislative amendment, the State will grant aid once on the basis of a single natural person. Where the same person belongs to several Creative Unions, the grant shall be distributed equally among the Unions, unless otherwise specified by the person in his application. At the same time, the restriction that only one Creative Union has so far been able to apply for creative support will be removed. The payment of the aid shall also take place either equally between the Unions or through a Union designated by the application. As this amendment results in the release of money that has now been paid 'double', the draft provides for a slight increase in the amount paid per creator: instead of 20%, 21.5% of the average monthly salary of the year preceding the application is paid. The draft will also increase the amount that creative associations can use to cover the costs associated with the implementation of the law. Of the current 10%, it will increase to 15% of the total amount of support allocated from the state budget. This change is particularly important from the point of view of the smaller creative federations, which have more difficulty in fulfilling the obligations relating to the administration of support. Thirdly, the foundations for the recognition of creative societies will be changed. Compared to the current law, the names of some creative areas have been modernised, bringing them into line with practice. Instead of performing art, the law now speaks of performing art, which also includes dance art. The stage design has been renamed scenery, musical art.
