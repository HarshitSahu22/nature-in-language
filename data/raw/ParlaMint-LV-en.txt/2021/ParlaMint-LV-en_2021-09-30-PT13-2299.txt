It is apparent from the Takeover Bids Directive that, where the State of incorporation of a public limited liability company differs from the Member State in which the shares are admitted to trading on a regulated market, the law of the State of incorporation of the company will regulate matters such as the provision of information intended for employees, the principle of management neutrality, exemptions from compulsory buy-back, as well as matters relating to commercial law. The legislation in force in the market in financial instruments does not provide for such an exception from the application of the law of the country of incorporation of the company provided for in the Takeover Bids Directive. The draft law fills this gap. The draft law regulates in case of acquisition of control of mandatory shares buy-back bids, establishing the amount of voting rights for expressing the mandatory buy-back offer, exemption from the mandatory offer, restrictions in case of non-expression of mandatory shares buy-back offer. The draft law also defines the types of remuneration, the maturity of the offer for the determination of the buy-back price of shares, as well as the methods of setting the buy-back price of shares. The draft law describes how the voluntary offer for the buy-back of shares takes place, including the minimum number of shares to which the offer relates, as well as the minimum and maximum number of shares to be acquired as a result of the offer. The draft law lays down the procedure for expressing the offer to buy back shares and the disclosure of information on the offer. The disclosure of information about the offer and the way in which it is done is essential for the shareholder to be able to make a balanced decision on the offer. The draft law allows for a distinction between times when this information is disclosed. The law lays down procedures for the final redemption of shares, including the setting of the repurchase price for the shares. In the event that the controlling shareholder of the company owns, directly or indirectly, at least 90 per cent of the shares in the company, the minority shareholders may require the controlling shareholder to buy back the shares held by the minority shareholders. This institution of law - unlike the final redemption of shares - provides for the right directly to minority shareholders. In particular, in a situation where the controlling shareholder has reached a level of holding where the other shareholders of the company are very difficult or impossible to influence the decisions of the company, they are actually entitled to withdraw from the company by receiving a fair remuneration for the shares. The Financial and Capital Market Commission has been established as the competent authority for the supervision of the norms included in the draft law.
