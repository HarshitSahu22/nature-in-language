The aim of the draft law is to ensure control of the aid for commercial activities in accordance with the current regulatory framework of the European Union and the conclusions of the Court of Justice of the European Union in the field of control of commercial activities at national level. In the light of the findings of the judgment of the Court of Justice of the European Union (C/349-17), it is necessary to supplement the provisions of the current Commercial Aid Control Act with provisions establishing an obligation to recover unlawful commercial aid in cases where the European Commission has not taken a decision to recover unlawful and incompatible aid from the internal market of the European Union. At the same time, the draft law provides that the aid provider is obliged to ensure the recovery of unlawful commercial aid, except where the aid provider ensures compliance with the conditions for compatibility of the aid with the internal market of the European Union with the aid scheme, individual aid or ad-hoc. This means that when assessing the legal and factual circumstances of the case, including the applicable conditions of the regulatory framework for commercial aid and other relevant aid measures, i.e. the conditions applicable to an aid scheme or ad-hoc aid, the aid provider can ensure the compatibility of the commercial aid granted with the internal market of the European Union, for example by notifying it to the European Commission. As I have already said, the Budget and Finance (Tax) Commission has supported this draft law and is asking the Saeima to support it for further progress.
