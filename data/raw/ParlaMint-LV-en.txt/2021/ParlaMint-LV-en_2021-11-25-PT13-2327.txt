Let me briefly outline the substance of these amendments. The draft law provides for clarification of the patient's ability to defend his rights and interests in the medical process, establishing the order of the patient's will and extending the possibilities of such will. As we know, the main objective of the Patient Rights Act is to promote a favourable relationship between patients and healthcare providers, thus contributing to the active participation of patients in their health care. The current law... Thus, Section 7(1) of the Law on Patients defines the procedure in which other persons may express the patient’s will in the medical process. In accordance with this provision, priority is given to deciding on the patient’s consent to treatment is given to the patient’s spouse and, if not, to the nearest relative. The existing regulation of Section 7(1) of the Law on Patients actually prevents the authorised person referred to in Section 6(7) of the Law on Patients from taking a decision on medical treatment if the patient has registered a marriage and the patient has the closest relatives as well. Thus, these amendments provide for the addition of Section 7(1) of the Law on the Rights of Patients, as provided for in the draft law, to the term “patient authorised person” by prioritising this person in the expression of the patient’s will over the spouse. In this way, the legal problem mentioned above will be resolved. That is, if the patient has authorized a person to take decisions on his treatment in his place, then this individual authorised person will have absolute priority in this... to ensure the patient’s will. Therefore, in general, the Social and Labour Commission discussed this draft law and, at the end, unanimously supported the orientation for further consideration to the Saeima at first reading. Therefore, colleagues, I call for support.
