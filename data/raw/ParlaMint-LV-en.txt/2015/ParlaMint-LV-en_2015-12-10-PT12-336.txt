In Latvia, one of the socially vulnerable groups is persons who were summoned from Latvia or sent to Afghanistan as members of the USSR Armed Forces during the period from December 1979 to March 1989. In Afghanistan, 3640 Latvians were caught up in the war, 63 of them have fallen in the war and 177 have been injured to varying degrees, which has made them disabled. I would very much like to hope that the participation of Latvians in this conflict will not be forgotten as a “inconvenient” page of history, which is divided and to which no one wants to return. 26 years have passed since this war ended. Currently, social and medical assistance, as well as rehabilitation, are needed by approximately 3000 Latvians, who have gone out of the horrors of this war and are socially vulnerable. That is why our group has prepared a bill on the protection of Afghan war veterans. The draft law is aimed at overcoming the existing social injustice and ensuring social protection measures for people forced to participate in the Afghan war. The draft law provides that Latvians who fought in Afghanistan between 1979 and 1989 should be formally granted the status of the Afghan War Veterans, as well as certain social protection measures. Latvia continues to send our NBS troops to participate in the NATO operation in Afghanistan, but these soldiers are protected by other laws. Meanwhile, about 3,000 people who, during the Soviet period, fought against their will in the Afghan war do not receive any support from the State. Therefore, the draft law provides that Afghan war veterans would be entitled to reimbursement of the cost of purchase of medicines, be exempted from the patient's contributions to medical institutions and be entitled to free social rehabilitation services once a year.
