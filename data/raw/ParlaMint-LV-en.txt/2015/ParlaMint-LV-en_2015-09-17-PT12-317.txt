For various reasons, a person may not wish to communicate the date of his birth to others. Therefore, it can be considered that the processing of personal data provided for by law is not always proportionate and consistent with the fundamental principles of the protection of personal data. In this context, it should be possible to identify natural persons using identification numbers, those which do not bear a reference to the date of birth. The draft law under consideration is aimed directly at establishing such a new system. According to the draft law, the personal code will be assigned by the Office for Citizenship and Migration Affairs. The personal identification number will, as it currently stands, be composed of 11 digits: the first two digits will be “32” and the other digits will be automatically generated by the system at the time the personal code is assigned. The draft law provides that the personal identification code under the new system will be assigned to persons who will be born from 1 January 2017. However, any person who has already been awarded a personal identification number with birth data from 1 January 2017 will be able to ask him to assign a new personal identification code without indicating birth data, as provided for in the draft law.
