On 19 October 2016, the Saeima received a collective application from 21 919 Latvian citizens “Undo the existing model of toll collection, put in place according to the example of Estonia”, in which the Saeima proposed: to abolish the current model of vehicle operation tax collection and to set up a tax collection according to the actual fuel consumption in Estonia and Lithuania. According to the first paragraph of Section 131.4 of the Parliament's Rules of Procedure, on 1 November 2016, the application was submitted to the Commission for initial evaluation of mandates, ethics and application. On 16 November 2016, the Commission held its first initial evaluation meeting, in which it heard an authorised representative of the applicants. He reported that people wanted to pay for what they were going and called on the Saeima to abolish the current model for collecting vehicle operating tax by setting the amount of this tax on fuel consumption in accordance with the example of Estonia or Lithuania. At this meeting, it was decided to continue the examination of the application by the Commission and to listen to the representatives of the institutions concerned by the request included in the collective submission at the subsequent meetings of the Commission. The Commission continued its assessment of the application on 11 January 2017. Representatives of the Ministry of Transport reported that overall the application was viewed positively. They informed that an action group had been set up in the Ministry to deal with this issue and that changes had been introduced as of 1 January 2017 in the procedure for collecting vehicle operating tax. The Ministry pointed out that there are some difficulties affecting the adoption of this decision and that they involve various reliefs for families, commercial transport, but they do allow the idea that they could gradually opt out of the vehicle operating tax. The representatives of the Ministry also pointed out that this group had developed the following possible lines of action: first, a joint assessment of the public and municipal road network; second, the renewal of the road fund as a special budget; third, a loan for the reconstruction and restoration of roads; fourthly, a financing model linked to the payments of road users; fifth, a revision of taxes and levies on vehicles; and sixthly, the transfer of profits from public limited liability companies to the delegated task of carrying out the national delegated task. The Action Group analysed the possibility of including vehicle operating tax in the excise duty on petroleum products. If a vehicle operating tax were to be included in an excise duty on petroleum products, there could be the following benefits: firstly, drivers would pay a proportion to how much fuel they consume; secondly, they would not have to pay a high amount of tax immediately because it would level up throughout the year; thirdly, they would not have to pay for the time when they do not drive (seasonal drivers, motorcyclists); fourthly, this would encourage the use of alternative modes of transport and reduce environmental pollution. The representatives of the Ministry of Transport continued to explain that income from vehicle operating tax amounted to approximately 80 million euros in 2015, including trucks with a mass above 12 tonnes, trailers and semi-trailers - 8 244 000 euros. In 2015, 203 tonnes of petrol were sold; 40 tonnes of marked diesel; 769 939 tonnes of diesel; 58 516 tonnes of liquefied petroleum gas. The total consumption of petroleum products in 2015 was 1 293 884 litres. The Ministry of Transport argued that the abolition of vehicle operating tax on passenger cars and motorcycles and the increase of excise duty on fuel should also take into account the following considerations: the increase in excise duty would also affect commercial vehicles with a relatively higher annual mileage, which would lead to cost-cost increases, as well as the maintenance of vehicle operating tax on goods vehicles with a gross mass above 12 tonnes, trailers and semi-trailers; the increase in excise duty would also affect other sectors such as rail transport, which consumes around 10 per cent of diesel. The Ministry of Transport took the view that, in view of the above considerations, the inclusion of the vehicle operating tax in the excise duty on petroleum products would not be eligible at present. The representative of the Association of Fuel Traders of Latvia reported that in order to reach the level of Estonia and Lithuania, the vehicle operating tax should be abolished. However, if the vehicle operating tax is abolished, the state budget will lose around €80 million. The representative of the Association indicated that the ‘problem’ of the excise duty is the insufficient amount of the excise duty to be passed on to roads. Calculations of the State Joint Stock Company “State Roads of Latvia” show that in 2016 only 19 percent of revenue from excise duty on fuel was diverted to roads. The rest shall go to other budgetary expenditure. The common risks will be price competition: if the price rises, the same thing will happen last year and this year between us and Estonia. Many Estonian transport companies are filling fuel in Latvia, and Estonia is about to raise this tax from 1 February. We get additional revenue for Latvia's budget. If we raise excise duty or shift it to vehicle operation tax, then our transport will be going to fill up fuel in Lithuania. The other thing. If this tax is levied in the form of an excise duty, that part, which is 10 per cent of illegal fuel, i.e. the smuggled product, will be exempt from another tax, and that would not be fair. The representative of the Association of Fuel Traders of Latvia expressed the view that the abolition of vehicle operating tax is a regressive increase in taxes, as some fuel users will be exempt from taxes, while others will have to pay more. Price increases can lead to negative public perceptions. Having assessed all the information received, the Commission prepared the Mandates, Ethics and Applications together with the Legal Bureau and supported the draft decision of the Saeima “On 21 919 Latvian citizens’ collective application “Delete the existing model of toll collection, implement the Estonian model”” and decided to instruct the Ministry of Finance to assess this collective application in 2017-2021.
