Taking into account that the Commission of the State Administration and Local Government has decided to reject the draft law “Amendments to the Law “On Local Government”, I will explain some reasons for this decision. The draft law “Amendments to the Law “On Local Government” presented by the Ministry of Environmental Protection and Regional Development and supported by the Cabinet of Ministers” was designed to ensure quality and data-based decision-making by local governments, which is necessary for preparation of local policy-making decisions and political decision-making. The draft law provided for the addition of Article 17.3 to Chapter 2 of the Law “On Local Governments”, establishing that local governments receive and process pseudonymised and anonymous data for the calculation of socio-economic indicator. The draft law also provided for the establishment of institutions which would provide data to the National Regional Development Agency for their processing, further ensuring the transfer of pseudonymised or anonymous data to municipalities. Let me explain. In order to ensure effective and high-quality services provided by local governments... For example, in order to identify the number of kindergartens required and to place them as easily as possible in the territory of the municipality, it is necessary to know the job placement, the location of the settlements of the people of the municipality who are actively employed, and to plan an effective transport route network and a timetable for the transport of the vehicles concerned. So there is a very large number of data, which are interrelated and which must also be obtained from public authorities in this way. In several meetings of the working group, the National Administration and Local Government Commission analysed possible further progress of the draft law and concluded that already now Section 10, Paragraph Two of the Personal Data Protection Law allows processing of personal data for initially unforeseen purposes, if it does not violate the rights of the data subject and is carried out for the purposes of scientific or statistical research. On the other hand, the relevant regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council - the regulation on the protection of individuals with regard to the processing of personal data - also allows for the free processing and circulation of such data. Thus, already now municipalities have the right to process data at their disposal, including personal data received from national information systems, for statistical purposes, subject to the requirements of regulatory enactments regulating the processing of personal data. The draft law also provided for a data pseudonymization solution, as well as provided an opportunity for municipalities to obtain information from information systems from which currently the municipality does not receive data (for example, data from the State Revenue Service). The solution to the functionality of data processing was intended to be carried out within the framework of the European Union Structural and Cohesion Funds Operational Programme for Growth and Jobs. The working group also discussed the development of pseudonymisation functionality. In an in-depth analysis of the technical solution and the initial design, it was concluded that the Single Data Area project did not provide sufficient funding. On this, the National Regional Development Agency provided information indicating that the development and implementation costs on the institutional side could exceed 795 thousand euros. Consequently, the pre-planned available funding of EUR 100 thousand in the Single Data Area project cannot cover the necessary costs. In view of the above, additional funding from the state budget would be necessary to ensure the functionality of the draft law. In assessing the potential benefits and costs and taking into account the principle of proportionality, the working group concluded that the development of pseudonymisation functionality is not appropriate. The conclusions of the working group were discussed and evaluated at the meeting of the Commission of the State Administration and Municipality on 21 November 2017. The Commission's unanimous decision is to reject that draft law. I ask Members of the Saeima to support the committee's decision.
