The Legal Commission also had for a long time a draft law submitted by the Ministry of Justice, which generally did not receive support, but which also contained some of these norms relating to strengthening the powers of the Council for the Judiciary. Thus, a new project was developed, which includes norms in order to strengthen the competence of the Council for the Judiciary in the procedure of appointment and appointment of the Chairmen of district (city) courts and the chairmen of regional courts, dismissal from office, transfer of judges to the vacant position of a judge in a higher or lower level court, as well as in determining the selection of candidates for the position of a district court, regional court, the position of a judge of the Supreme Court, traineeship, and the procedure of passing the qualification examination. This was also the subject of a discussion within the Legal Commission. This was not supported. At the moment this norm has remained in this project, but it is possible that there will be further discussions about it, as some of the judges who have participated in drafting this law have also indicated that perhaps it is not the right time to repeal this norm. An appeal procedure is also foreseen. The forms of order sought by the judge are also specified. It is also specified how judges – both in the Supreme Court and in other courts – are replacing one of the judges temporarily, and here also an open competition has been established as the most appropriate selection. It is a whole series of rules that will be discussed before the final adoption. Overall, the aim of this draft law is to strengthen the independence of the judiciary from the executive and legislative powers.
