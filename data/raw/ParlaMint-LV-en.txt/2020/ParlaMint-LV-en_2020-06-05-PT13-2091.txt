ParlaMint-LV_2020-06-05-PT13-2091-U2	Good afternoon, dear colleagues! I propose the draft law “Amendments to the Criminal Law” submitted by the Legal Commission for consideration (No. 712/Lp13). The draft law was designed to provide law enforcement authorities with criminal law tools to fight against illicit trade in cigarettes. I would remind you that this problem gained widespread public importance after last year's public media stories about the inability of the police to deal with illicit tobacco sales in the Central Market. It should be stressed that the long-standing illegal trade in tobacco products has caused and continues to cause significant damage to the public budget. I would like to remind you briefly of the progress of this draft law. The original proposal submitted by the Ministry of the Interior in December last year aimed to criminalise the offering or marketing of tobacco products which are not labelled with the stamps of excise duty of the Republic of Latvia, if it was done by a person who warned about the criminal responsibility for offering or marketing them. For the first time, the Ministry of the Interior offered to impose administrative responsibility rather than criminal responsibility, but the experts of the judiciary objected to this approach. The current draft law, which has received support from the Permanent Working Party of the Criminal Law and which has been supported by both the Ministry of the Interior and the State Police, envisages criminalising the implementation of tobacco products for the first time, with a stricter approach as originally proposed by the Ministry of the Interior. This version of the amendments to the Criminal Law has been carefully considered, also during discussions at sub-committee meetings of the Criminal Law Policy, in the presence of a wide range of experts. In order to avoid criminal liability for the implementation of one packet of cigarettes at the first time, amendments provide for the establishment of small amounts of the Law on the Application of Criminal Law, which will allow to exempt a person from liability in the relevant cases. The initial discussion was based on the proposal to criminalise any kind of tobacco activity, regardless of the purpose of the person, for example by providing for criminalisation of smuggled cigarettes. The proposed law provides for criminalisation of illicit activities in tobacco, when committed for a covetous purpose, i.e. by means of remuneration. I would also like to mention the debate on qualifications relating to the responsibility for offering tobacco products, which has been initiated in the range of experts. Such an offer is not separate from the criminal offence, as the offer of tobacco products should be recognised as an attempt to sell tobacco products. In turn, according to Section 15, Paragraph 5 of the Criminal Law, responsibility for preparing for a crime or attempting to commit a crime shall be established in accordance with the same Article of this Law, which provides for liability for the particular offence. In particular, the proposer of tobacco products will be held criminally liable according to Article 15, paragraph 5, of the Criminal Law after the new Article 221,6 of the Criminal Law, “Silence of Tobacco Products”, as well as the implementer of tobacco products. In addition to the changes already mentioned, these amendments to the Criminal Law also provide for the alignment of sanctions for similar compositions in the third part of Section 190 “Contraband” and Section 190.1 “Movement of goods and substances whose movement is prohibited or specially regulated across the state border of the Republic of Latvia” by providing for a maximum term of imprisonment of 10 years. It is important to point out that the planned changes to the Criminal Law call for responsibility for the illicit manufacture, manufacture of tobacco products, as is the case with the illicit manufacture of alcoholic beverages... The first paragraph of Article 221 of the Criminal Law provides for responsibility for such illicit production to a significant extent, but in the third paragraph of that Article if it is committed to a large extent or by an organised group. Finally, the proposed changes to Article 221.1 of the Criminal Law on the marketing of illicit alcoholic beverages and Article 221.2 on the production, storage and transfer of illicit alcoholic beverages include new qualifying features – significant amounts and an organised group – in the positions concerned. I call on Members to support the draft law on "Amendments to the Criminal Law" (No. 712/Lp13) at first reading.
