I would like to say that the draft law has been drafted in the light of the current situation in Ukraine, the unprovoked attacks of the Russian Federation, the ongoing wars, war crimes, human rights violations, genocide. The draft law has been created because currently the basis for the withdrawal of citizenship of Latvia is determined by Section 24(1) of the Citizenship Law, which lists the four grounds for the withdrawal of citizenship of Latvia. All these grounds for withdrawing citizenship are related to violations committed by a Latvian citizen in relation to the state of Latvia and thus undermine the principle of mutual loyalty between the state of Latvia and this citizen. Until now, the Citizenship Law did not provide for the basis for the deprivation of citizenship of Latvia, which is related to the recognised violations of international law against another democratic state, which would at the same time constitute a secondary threat to the democratic system and constitutional values of Latvia. The developers of the draft law believe that the Latvian citizen, who has substantially supported military aggression and crimes against another democratic state committed by a country, thus contributing to the threat of the democratic system of Latvia, has violated the obligation of the state and citizens to be loyal to each other. Therefore, the draft law provides for the addition to the former Section 24, paragraph 5, of the Law on Citizenship with a new paragraph, which provides for the possibility of subtracting Latvian citizenship to a person who “has provided substantial financial, material, propaganda, technology or other support to persons, states or other entities who have committed acts, including crimes against peace, genocide, crimes against humanity, war crimes that undermine or threaten the territorial integrity, sovereignty and independence of democratic states or constitutional facilities of democratic states, or who have participated in such activities themselves, and in the event of the deprivation of citizenship of Latvia this person does not become a stateless person”. The draft law also states: ‘The provision of such aid, the participation in the activities concerned, can be found in the opinion of the national safety authority. The decision to withdraw citizenship shall be taken by the Administrative District Court. The decision of the Administrative district court shall be appealed to the Supreme Court, the decision of which shall be final and not subject to appeal.” That is the wording at the moment. In addition, the draft law also provides for the addition of a new Article 26.1 of the law, which provides: “The information about the persons who are the subject of decision on acquisition and loss of citizenship of Latvia, as well as the progress of acquisition and loss of citizenship of Latvia, shall be recorded in the information system.” The draft law also provides for the delegation to the Cabinet of Ministers by 31 March 2023 to develop rules on the amount of information to be included in such information system, the procedure for including and deleting the information, the time limit for storing the information, as well as the institutions to which access to the information provided in the information system should be granted. Amendments to the law provide that the State Fire and Rescue Service will monitor the capacities of municipalities in providing support and, if necessary, direct Ukrainian refugees to those municipalities whose possibilities allow providing the necessary support. The DGT will maintain information about the number of Ukrainian civilians in municipalities and update it at least once a day according to information provided by municipalities. In order to reduce the administrative burden on Ukrainian civilians, amendments to the law provide that Ukrainian civilians will also be able to submit documents for applying for a visa or residence permit at the state and local government single customer service centres, from which they will be sent to the Office for Citizenship and Migration Affairs. Situations have been identified where Ukrainian refugees have to submit documents that need to be received from Ukraine, but due to the war caused by the Russian Federation it is impossible. In these cases, the documents will be accepted without certification and the authorities responsible will be able, on a case-by-case basis, to allow for exceptions and not to insist on the service or production of such documents. If a person has documents received during the USSR, it will be possible to use these documents as well. If there are reasonable doubts as to the authenticity of the document submitted or presented, the institution will have the right to refuse to accept such documents. In the field of employment, the amendments to the law provide that Ukrainian civilians who have received educational training will have the opportunity to work in the fields of vocational and interest education and foreign language learning. It will address the shortage of educators in educational institutions, and an adult Ukrainian citizen will have the right to continue acquiring vocational education and training in private institutions. The draft law also extends the right to minors of Ukrainian civilians to continue to receive education in municipal vocational institutions within the framework of municipal budget resources, thus giving them the right not to lose their professional knowledge and skills. Amendments to the law provide that the employer will be entitled to employ a Ukrainian civilian without performing the first mandatory health check for three months from the date of conclusion of the employment contract. This exception will not apply to the first health check of persons to be employed in jobs involving risks to the health of other persons. Those rules also provide for a minimum level of health inspection for tuberculosis in order to prevent cases of tuberculosis in persons who work with children. In order to ensure continuity of prescribed drug therapy for Ukrainian civilians based on the recommendations made by Latvian doctors, amendments to the law provide that Ukrainian civilians who are entitled to reimbursement of the cost of purchase of medicines or medical devices will be fully covered for six months starting from February 24, 2022, as well as for asylum seekers and deprived persons. The amendments also grant Ukrainian civilians the right to receive free of charge copies of the social legal certificates of the Latvian National Archives and related documents and, if necessary, translations of these documents into Russian.
