ParlaMint-LV_2016-12-08-PT12-391-U573	Thank you, Presidium. Honourable Members! On August 30th, 2016, the Saeima received a collective application of 10 thousand 570 Latvian citizens “Enterprise testing of vehicles every two years”, in which the Saeima proposed: to extend the deadline for carrying out technical inspection of vehicles to two years in order to alleviate the tax burden for Latvian citizens. In accordance with the first paragraph of Article 131 of the Saeima Rules of Procedure, the application was submitted on 20 September to the Commission for initial evaluation of mandates, ethics and application. On 28 September, the Commission held its first initial evaluation meeting, in which it decided to continue its examination of the application by the Commission, and representatives of the institutions concerned by the request included in the collective submission were heard at subsequent meetings of the Commission. The Commission continued its assessment of the application on 5 October. The Auto Association reported that approximately 17 thousand vehicles of categories M1 and N1 were sold in Latvia this year. In the age up to five years - about seven thousand. Auto Association informed that new cars have a guarantee, during which damage is eliminated free of charge. The guarantee for any car on the market is about three years, for a few - five to seven years. It is very unlikely that three- to five-year-old cars will be tested for technical inspection. It was also noted that at least one or a half changes in technology took place over a period of 10 years. In Latvia, the average used car imported is 10 years old. It can be said that after 10 years, cars need the first major repairs. The biggest problems are with cars in the secondary market. Road hauliers reported that in Latvia there is a regulation of the European Union, according to which trucks should undergo a technical inspection once a year, buses and taxis – twice a year. Since many work in Europe, it may be that the machine enters Latvia only some three times a year. Road hauliers reported that, for example, in Lithuania it has been ensured that roadworthiness tests can also be carried out in Germany. Estonia, on the other hand, has obtained that roadworthiness testing can be carried out at an authorized dealer. The Latvian Automoto Society reported that according to the data of the State Joint Stock Company Road Safety Directorate, the number of road accidents in 2015 was about 40 thousand. Of which 94 percent - driving fault, three percent - related to the technical condition of the car. On 19 October, the Commission heard the views of representatives of the Ministry of Transport, the Ministry of the Interior, the Ministry of Finance, the CSDD, the Association of Latvian Insurers. The Ministry of Transport expressed a positive attitude about the idea contained in the application and indicated that it was ready to discuss it. The Association of Latvian Insurers informed that insurers have the right to go the legal route and request that the perpetrator pay compensation if the car has not passed a technical inspection. Insurers shall not refuse and, if the car is insured, compensation shall be paid. They considered that giving the whole fleet the opportunity to pass the roadworthiness test every two years from a safety point of view is risky. Representatives of the Ministry of the Interior indicated that the State Police is a controlling institution and therefore it would not be as important for it as a technical inspection every year. The state police executes the law as well as provides statistical data. The number of cases when the driver was punished for not leaving the roadworthiness test was 2.9 per cent of the total number of drivers in 2011, and in 2015 - 1.75 per cent of the total number of drivers. CSDD representatives provided data that for the first time in Latvia 45 per cent of cars are unable to pass the roadworthiness test, and 18 per cent of cars are found to have significant defects. On average, 30 cars are banned from further traffic during the technical inspection. With regard to road surface in Latvia, information is as follows: 21 per cent are only black road, 76 per cent - snippet and gravel. Germany has 100 percent asphalt roads, Lithuania has 88 percent, Estonia has 60 percent. The CSDD representatives also reported on the intensity of use of cars: within five years 70 per cent of the car journeys exceed 100 thousand kilometres. The average take-off of a passenger car in Latvia is 70 thousand kilometres a year. CSDD also described the attitude of car owners - some drivers eliminate their defects only before or after a roadworthiness test. CSDD also provided a comparison of the average age of cars within the Baltic States. In Latvia it is 13 years old, in Lithuania - 11 years old, in Estonia - 10 years old. The periodicity of technical inspection of passenger cars in Latvia is as follows: first - after 24 months, second - after 12 months. in Lithuania: the first - after 36 months, the second - after 24 months, then every 24 months. in Estonia: the first - after 36 months, the second - after 24 months, then every 24 months, until the car reaches the age of nine years, then every year. Currently, the price of the roadworthiness test in Latvia is 22 euros, in Lithuania - 15 euros, in Estonia - 35 euros. Having assessed all the information received, the Commission together with the Legal Bureau prepared and unanimously supported the draft decision of the Saeima “On further progress of the 10 570 Latvian citizens’ collective application “Trafficing of Vehicle Testing Every Two Years”” and decided to instruct the Parliament’s Economic, Agricultural, Environmental and Regional Policy Commission by 1 July 2017 to consider and prepare amendments to the relevant laws in order to implement the principle that the frequency of vehicle roadworthiness testing in Latvia is comparable to that of Lithuania and Estonia on the basis of the 10 thousand 570 Latvian citizens’ collective application “Trafficing of Vehicle Testing Every Two Years”. Please support the draft decision prepared by the Saeima.
