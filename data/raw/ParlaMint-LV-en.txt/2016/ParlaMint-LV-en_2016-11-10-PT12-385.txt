The draft law, in turn, is intended to work in such a way that we can ensure the entry into force of these amendments by 1 January 2017, so that the first reforms bring results and can demonstrate that the area of insolvency, its adjustment to the legislator, is important, and that entrepreneurs see that we do not ignore this area. Provision is made for the establishment of a system of examination of administrators by transferring the function of management of qualifications, while also imposing stricter requirements on the qualification, reputation and professionalism of the administrator, as well as regular examination of the qualification of the administrator every two years. The right to train applicants for the function of administrator is intended to be transferred to insolvency administrations and other legal persons, without prejudice to continuing it also to the professional organisation of the administrator. It is also planned to introduce disciplinary responsibility of the administrator, establishing that the basis for initiating disciplinary proceedings may be a serious or systematic violation of laws and regulations or a serious violation of the norms of professional ethics. The Commission will be able to impose disciplinary action and propose the removal of the administrator. It is planned to strengthen the activities of the insolvency administration in the implementation of the supervisory function, for example by establishing a more extensive right to arrive at the place of the administrator’s practice and also to examine the insolvency proceedings and related documents in the course of the proceedings. The second direction of the reform is linked to the legal protection process. In particular, it is the rescue culture of businesses that is one of the main challenges that the insolvency process has been set up in general - so as not to be as it is at present, perhaps making the company empty, but rather to try to help it recover and return to economic life. The entrepreneurs themselves have concluded that the legal protection process currently in force does not contribute to the recovery of companies. Consequently, this draft law aims to strengthen the role of creditors themselves in legal protection proceedings, to waive the right of the administrator of insolvency proceedings as the sole supervision of possible legal protection proceedings, allowing the majority of creditors to choose freely for the purposes of the supervision of the legal protection proceedings, thus opening the free market. The draft law includes a wide range of persons who will be entitled to supervise the legal protection process. Conceptually, all the issues submitted in the draft law have also been agreed with the Council on Insolvency Issues and, as I said, with the non-governmental sector - Latvian Chamber of Commerce and Industry, the Latvian Employers' Confederation, the Association of Latvian Commercial Banks and the Council of Foreign Investors in Latvia. These organisations have expressed their support for this step to be taken at last. When looking at this draft law at the Legal Commission, because it has been created in the working group of the Ministry of Justice, when summarising the work of the working group, we have concluded that the draft law can be improved and supplemented between readings, and the commission will, of course, do this work (for example, will extend disciplinary responsibility or other state supervision mechanism to the person supervising the legal protection process).
