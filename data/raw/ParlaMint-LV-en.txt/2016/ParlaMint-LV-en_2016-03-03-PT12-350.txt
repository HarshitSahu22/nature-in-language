The draft law “Amendments to the Criminal Law”, undoubtedly concerns topical topics discussed in our society recently - action against the Republic of Latvia, spying, misappropriation of state secrets, criticism of state senior officials. It is certain that it is absolutely clear to all those present that the geopolitical situation in the world is constantly changing and that Latvia, like other countries, has to face new challenges. The authors of the draft law can agree that the existing regulation no longer ensures full protection of national security interests. We do not deny that there is a problem. It is a clear and consistent position of our group. However, the proposed solution raises a number of questions, so I would like to point out the potential risks contained in the draft law, including the application and interpretation of the rules. In our view, certain draft laws are scattered and unclear and too widely interpreted. In the statement, we do not see case-law, statistics, analysis of practice, applying the existing regulation. The concept of hybrid war has been widely used without providing a legal explanation for this concept. As a result, certain provisions may run counter to freedom of expression and human rights in general. Of course, freedom of expression is not absolute and states are entitled to impose restrictions if they comply with the requirements of the European Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms and with the case-law of the European Court of Human Rights. And indeed, in accordance with the European Convention for the Protection of Human Rights and Fundamental Freedoms, restrictions on freedom of expression are permitted if they are aimed at protecting the interests of national security, territorial unity or public security, preventing disorder or crime, protecting health, the dignity or the rights of others, preventing the disclosure of confidential information, preserving the authority and impartiality of the judiciary. However, I would like to remind you that freedom of expression includes the right not only to express opinions that are acceptable to society. The European Court of Human Rights has repeatedly pointed out in its judgments that the right to freedom of expression has very wide borders, and it also protects ideas that are unacceptable to the majority of society. Thus, in its judgments, the European Court of Human Rights points, for example, to the link between Article 10 of the Convention and democratic values, emphasising that it protects not only information and ideas that are received in a favourable way, but also those that offend, shock or alarm the public or any part of it. One of the most important shortcomings of the draft law is probably Article 81.1 of the new draft law, since the criticism of the state's senior officials, based on the text and the annotation, is not separated from harmful practices that are truly detrimental to the interests of national security. This is a very risky and slippery road, because the inclusion of general and unspecific articles into criminal law can, for example, take away almost any political competitor, as well as people who think otherwise, e.g. by populistly declaring his actions to be anti-state and threatening sovereignty. That is why we cannot support this wording of the bill and the Group will abstain from the amendment to the Criminal Law vote. Thank you for your attention.

ParlaMint-LV_2016-03-03-PT12-350-U301	Dear Members, In 2005, Latvia ratified the World Health Organisation Framework Convention on Tobacco Control. According to its Article 13, we are determined to ban advertising for all types of tobacco products, including any direct or indirect initiatives encouraging the public to buy tobacco products. The guidelines of Article 13 of the Convention state that the location of tobacco products at the point of sale is an advertising of products which encourages or stimulates the purchase of tobacco products. Positioning is a marketing tool which gives the impression that tobacco products are socially acceptable; this in turn does not stimulate smoking cessation. The most sensitive group regarding the promotion of the purchase and use of these products is precisely young people and children, because they are placed at the cash register - right next to sweets! - also tobacco products, electronic smoking devices, vegetable smoking products and similar products. In order to ensure the elimination of any incentive effects, States Parties to the Convention are invited to impose restrictions on product placement and visibility, recognising that only written information on these products and their prices is permitted at points of sale. The wording of the proposal put forward by Members is in line with practices in other countries as well. In Finland restrictions on the location of these products in trading venues have been in place since 2012, in Ireland since 2009, in Great Britain since 2012 for supermarkets and since 2015 for other trading venues. In Iceland such a ban has been in force since 2001 and in Norway since 2010. The proposal does not prohibit trade in tobacco and other such products, but sets out the form in which this trade is organised at national level. One of the main arguments used to prevent this proposal from currently being supported by the draft law is: it would be compatible with the European Commission and this would hinder the progress of the draft law. Colleagues! Let me explain why the proposal should not be considered as a draft technical regulation and does not require harmonisation within the European Commission. First. This is a trade restriction at national level, as is the ban on selling products at certain retail outlets or placing them in certain areas. In the context of Directive 2015/1535, harmonised standards are changes to the technical specification of a product, for example if Latvia introduced standardised packaging of products. It is essential that the proposal does not prohibit the marketing of products, but sets out the way in which certain products should be organised. Thus any product (regardless of where it is produced) we still... will be able to be placed on the market and will be made available to the consumer. Secondly. Such a restriction does not prevent producers from introducing certain brands or new products to consumers, as the proposal will allow products to be freely placed on shelves and will not cover them. The influence and powers of the Russian Federation's special services in Russia have increased considerably in recent years and continue to grow, increasing threats to both Latvia and Latvia's citizens. That is why the national safety authorities have been thinking about this for a long time - all together. In particular, both the SAB Director, the Security Police and the Military Intelligence and Security Service have jointly thought about how to improve the legislation so that we can feel secure in today's new threat and have prepared and jointly presented these amendments to the law to the Saeima. As I mentioned, speaking to you on the issue of putting the draft law on the agenda, the current framework of the Criminal Law has been developed in 1999 and no longer ensures that we are able to respond to the dangers that our country might have in today's circumstances. At the moment, new mechanisms are being used to influence what is happening in our country, to take over - perhaps - the actual control of the country. For this reason, both the hybrid war and the information war are being used and developed. The draft law is designed to provide an opportunity to address threats posed by hybrid war to Latvian security and to respond in a timely manner by criminal law to potential threats directed against the fundamental interests of the Republic of Latvia or other threats to the interests of Latvian national security. The draft law is also designed to improve the protection of the state secret and to prevent the entry of state secret or other information of importance to the state into foreign action, which may harm the internal and external security of Latvia. It is therefore envisaged to amend a number of articles in the relevant section of the law. First there is Article 80 – “Active against the Republic of Latvia”. This article is reworded in a new wording, which states that addressing any interests of national importance of the Republic of Latvia is, by their very nature, directed against the Republic of Latvia in general and therefore constitutes a threat to the state. The draft law provides for criminal responsibility for any activity that is incompatible with the Constitution of the Republic of Latvia and endangers important interests of the state of the Republic of Latvia. This provision of the law earlier... In the past, it was just - if with violent methods... As you understand, right now, in the 21st century, we... If we look back at the way events started in Ukraine, then we see that there was not only a violent method there; there were different methods used. Thus, the new regulation would already prevent the entry of the green people. Go on. This draft law also changes the wording. To date, only addressing the independence of the state of Latvia was a threat, whereas today this concept is broader. What is precisely defined in the Constitution is therefore an approach to national sovereignty. Addressing sovereignty is an attack on the power of the people, not of the state. The power of the nation can be exercised directly, as well as through state and municipal institutions. This means that the criminal law needs to establish clearly the responsibility for dealing with sovereignty. The threat of sovereignty has become particularly acute today, with the increasing use of hybrid war techniques. This law, too, not only in this Article, but in all of the law, has significantly changed sanctions, not only by increasing them, but also by excluding additional penalties - confiscation of property. Practically almost all sanctions have been accompanied by probation monitoring. These new amendments to the Criminal Law also contain Section 80.1 of the Criminal Law – “A merger in an organised group with the aim of carrying out an action against the Republic of Latvia”. That provision has therefore also been clarified. This provision is likely to be discussed with legal experts, i.e. whether a "organized group" has disappeared. But in any case, the “merger of a merger with the aim of taking action against the Republic of Latvia” is a new norm and I think it is very topical. The next article, which is in these amendments, is “A call directed against the Republic of Latvia”. The purpose of this amendment and the changes are analogous to the changes in Articles 80 and 80.1. In order to strike a balance between criminal law protection of the fundamental interests of the State provided for in the Constitution, the law has been supplemented by a new constituent of the crime – a public call to address the sovereignty of the Republic of Latvia. The draft law does not maintain a reference to the purpose. These rules provide that the fact that a person does so is considered to be criminal, because, as I said in the introduction, there is nothing more important than the security of our country. Consequently, addressing any fundamental national interest is considered to be a country as a whole and is criminally punishable. Articles 82 and 83 are excluded from the Criminal Law, but this does not mean that the content of these articles has disappeared. These rules are simply integrated in content in other articles of the Criminal Law. Another very important innovation is the article “Helping a foreign country in an action aimed at security interests of the Republic of Latvia”. This thus confirms once more that the threat to national security is an understanding of the situation in which the state is threatened, its territorial integrity and the functioning of the State’s facilities. This article is very important in this respect, and it will probably be a matter of debate for my colleagues from SASKAJA, because this article is a direct answer to what we have seen on television screens on several occasions - the misunderstanding stories about how Latvian young people who are studying in Latvian schools are recruited to participate in camps in Russia, where patriotism is raised not in relation to Latvia, but perhaps against what is happening in the neighbouring country. Whether such activities are carried out in the interests of the state of Latvia, so far, there was no criminal legal mechanism to influence such actions. This will be possible through this new framework. This draft law also amends the article of the Criminal Law on spying. So far it was necessary to prove that spying is done in the interests of a foreign country, whereas the new regulation with spying understands any interests... the collection of any information which any foreign country could use, irrespective of the purpose for which it was done. This project envisages that it could be a criminal offence in the future, which is also essential. Because it doesn't matter whether a person collects information that could be used against his country simply for a greedy purpose to figure out whether to live on it or to do good for someone, or whether he is self-governed by someone abroad. One of the new regulations is the illegal acquisition of state secrets. This article, too... Any national secret therefore deserves protection, and therefore the provisions of this article have been clarified, and these amendments have been tabled here. These amendments to the law, as I mentioned, have been developed by the national safety authorities. The norms of this draft law have been discussed in the working group of the Criminal Law, which operates in the Ministry of Justice; these norms have been agreed with both legal experts and law enforcement officers. ParlaMint-LV_2016-03-03-PT12-350-U415	On 5 November 2015, the Saeima received a collective application of 10 435 Latvian citizens “On roads without pits”, in which the Saeima made the following proposal: 1) to add annual transport charge to the fuel price and to shift excise duty to road repair and maintenance; 2) to create a separate fund where all transport tax money and fuel excise duty money enter; 3) to instruct the Ministry of Transport to manage this fund. In accordance with Section 131.4 first paragraph of the Saeima Rules of Procedure, on 19 November 2015 the application was submitted to the Commission for initial evaluation of mandates, ethics and application. On December 2, the Commission held its first initial evaluation meeting, to which the applicant was invited. The author justified the request made in the application to add to the price of fuel. This would result in a slight change in fuel price increases, but would improve the tax system. For example, there is a law in neighbouring countries that states that road taxes are based on the amount of fuel consumed in transport. It is advisable to shift excise duty towards a separate, road-based fund. If there were such a separate fund, the funds could be used for road repair. As one of the negative facts, the author mentioned that in Latvia, for example, all parking spaces in the sides of the road, or so-called pockets, are closed. There are no properly resting areas fitted with waste tanks. But if so, then they are usually full. The enclosures are owned by municipalities which do not have sufficient funding for waste disposal. The Commission continued its assessment of the application on 20 January. The meeting was attended by representatives of the Ministry of Transport, the Association “Latvian Road Builder”, the Association of Road Carriers “Latvijas auto”, the Association of the Latvian Motoclub, the State Joint Stock Company “Latvian Road Maintenance”, the State Joint Stock Company “Latvian Roads”, and the State Joint Stock Company “CSDD”. The Ministry of Transport compared the financing of roads in Lithuania, Estonia and Latvia with the funding of road user charges. They informed that road financing in Lithuania is granted from excise duty on fuel. Lithuania has registered truck toll, eurovignette revenue, radar tolls, unparalleled vehicle toll. In Estonia, however, by 2015 it accounted for 75 per cent of excise tax on fuel and European Union funding, 10 per cent, was available to municipalities. In Latvia, since 2004 the road foundation exists only as a name. The only legal source of funding is the Eurovignette. Its revenues amounted to EUR 18 million last year, but they are only allocated to the main roads of the country. According to the law, about 25 per cent is allocated as a targeted grant for the maintenance and construction of municipal roads and streets. In 2016, it is only 48 million euros. From 1994 to 2004, a national road fund existed, from which budgetary resources were allocated only to roads. In the last 20 years, there has been insufficient budget resources in the country for maintaining and repairing roads. At present, road surface is in a poor state and continues to deteriorate rapidly. The necessary funding for public roads is over €400 million, even 500 million per year. In 2015, 268 million euros of works were carried out. This year, around 320 million euros are planned, of which 49 million are a grant to municipal roads. The biggest problem is that the national road adjustment programme for 2014-2020 is not being implemented because public funding is insufficient. The deficit was €330 million in 2014 and 2015 and therefore the 1100 kilometres of roads included in the programme remained uncorrected. The state budget needs 151 million EUR 100 in order to implement the programme regarding the local roads of the country. In order to maintain the quality and safety of national road networks, an investment of EUR 673 million is needed every year to maintain them and to renovate them periodically. Since 1991, public roads have accumulated a repair deficit of around EUR 4 billion. ‘ The road builder of Latvia” expressed the opinion that it was necessary to renew the fund. The representatives recalled that before the elections, Members signed an agreement with the public organisations on the renewal of the National Road Fund, but this agreement was not fulfilled. The renewed fund would allow road users to pass on seven taxes and charges to repair their roads so that around EUR 860 million will no longer be lost every year on bad roads. According to the representative of the Road Safety Directorate (CSDD), the passenger car tax collected by the CSDD is transferred directly to the state budget. Latvia is the only country in which a system is in place, according to which when carrying out the roadworthiness test, the tax has been paid. The representatives of the Association of Latvian Road Carriers indicated that Latvia's transit routes are generally in poor condition, which has not improved in recent years compared to neighbouring countries. Rural roads are also in very poor condition. In addition, each truck pays about 10-15 euros per day in different taxes. The representatives of the association believe that the arrangement of roads should be a sustainable model and now it is high time to invest in the arrangement of roads, so that business in Latvia continues to take place in order to carry on freight transportation, and so on. Motoclubs’ representatives referred to road safety as one of the key factors to be addressed. The quality of the road is felt by motoring as no one else. They compared driving a motorbike on gravel roads in Latvia with participating in the Dakar Rally because the motorbike is very mixed. Motoclubs also pointed out that if one person owns two vehicles, such as a motorcycle and a car, the tax is due on both of them, although both cannot travel at the same time. They also pointed out, for example, that older people do not use the car as often as others do, but they also have to pay the tax all year round. It would be better to pay more who is driving rather than the other way around. In conclusion, they pointed out that it would be more appropriate to collect funding for roads in one place and then decide how much to spend. Having assessed the requirements of Latvian citizens and all the information received, the Commission concluded that there was a need for an in-depth study on this issue. As a result, on 24 February 2016, the Commission unanimously supported the prepared draft decision on the collective application submitted by 10 435 Latvian citizens and decided to instruct the Cabinet of Ministers to carry out a comparative assessment of the amount of taxes and duties payable on road use in the Baltic States and the legal framework, as well as to prepare and by 1 September 2016 submit to the Saeima an information report on the possibilities to amend the regulatory enactments in order to implement the principles that taxes and duties paid by road users are used for the repair and maintenance of roads and bridges in good technical condition, as well as to ensure that the amount of tax payments to road users would correspond to the intensity of road use. Please support the prepared draft decision.
