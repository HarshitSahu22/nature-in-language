The aim of the draft law is to implement Directive 2016/1148 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 6 July 2016 on measures to achieve an identical high level of security of network and information systems across the European Union. The draft law defines new entities - basic service providers and digital service providers - which have two main responsibilities: firstly, to comply with specific security requirements for information technologies, including network and information systems; and secondly, to report security incidents. In order to ensure the above responsibilities of basic service providers and digital service providers new tasks are set for the following institutions: Information Technology Security Incident Prevention Institution (CERT.LV), the Digital Security Monitoring Committee and the Ministry of Defence, as well as the ministries supervising certain sectors. The Directive had to be adopted by 9 May 2018, but the Law on Information Technology Security at national level already regulates part of the measures provided for in it (e.g., CERT.LV tasks and rights) and defines the nature of information technology security incidents. The second part of Section 6 of the Law already provides that state and municipal institutions, the owner of critical information technology infrastructure (e.g. Latvenergo, Baltic Grid, Latvijas Gāze) or the legal manager in the event of a security incident are obliged to inform about the competent security incident prevention institution immediately. State and municipal institutions must ensure compliance of information and communication technology systems with the minimum security requirements set by the Cabinet of Ministers. These requirements are to be maintained and the draft directive transposes provisions that are not yet in place at national level and clarifies existing legal provisions accordingly.
