ParlaMint-LV_2018-05-31-PT12-484-U266	Good morning, colleagues! These are amendments to the National Security Act, which is very important and is being led by the Ministry of Defence. The aim of the draft law is to improve the legal framework in the field of state defence, specifying the cases when the Cabinet of Ministers is entitled to take a decision on the mobilisation of landguards and reserve soldiers, as well as defining comprehensive fundamental principles of state defence in legal acts. The draft law provides for the possibility to announce mobilisation for up to 72 hours in the event of a state threat, as well as establishes the rights and obligations of the National Armed Forces and citizens in the event of war or military invasion. It is planned that the mobilisation of national guards and reserve soldiers to overcome the threat will be announced even before the state of emergency or the time of war enters into operation. Under the existing legal framework, the mobilisation of landguards and spare soldiers can now only be announced during an exceptional situation. Today’s military threat, however, is dynamic and can evolve without initially creating indicators for declaring a state of emergency. In order to ensure the fulfilment of the tasks set out in paragraph 18 of the State Defence Concept, it is necessary to promote and organise direct and as wide a range of volunteers' involvement in ensuring state defence, organise military and civil resistance, as well as ensure information of citizens about actions in case of military threats. The involvement of the population in national defence is primarily in the composition of the NBS, entering the professional service, the Earthguard, becoming a spare soldier, as well as fulfilling the letter of mobilisation. The implementation of the national defence objective requires a comprehensive national defence approach, which involves the involvement of the whole society, thus effectively addressing the new generation of troops. The concept determines that national protection is unconditional and it is the duty of every citizen to defend the state and actively or passively resist the aggressor. In the event of an imminent military threat, the national defence system must be able to implement protective measures by organising and directing military and civil resistance, the continued functioning of the state power and the protection of critical infrastructure. In the event of a military invasion or war, it is envisaged that not only the institutions of the State administration and local government, as well as natural and legal persons take measures for the protection of the state military and civil defence and implement armed resistance, civil disobedience and non-cooperation with illegal administrative institutions. The draft law states that citizens are entitled to implement civil disobedience in case of war or military invasion by acting against illegal administrative institutions and armed entities of aggressors, to show armed resistance to armed entities of aggressors and to provide all forms of support to participants of civil disobedience and armed resistance, as well as to units of NATO or the armed forces of the Member States of the European Union providing support for the execution of their tasks. Overcoming the military threat can only be achieved by the involvement of Latvian citizens in national defence, thus promoting the stability of the state and realizing the principle enshrined in the Preamble of the Satversme of the Republic of Latvia that the people of Latvia protect their sovereignty, independence of the state of Latvia, territory, its unity and democratic state system. The basic principle of comprehensive national defence, which states that every citizen of a country has the obligation and right to protect the country by all means available to him, is currently laid down in the national legislation of several North Atlantic Treaty organisations and the Member States of the European Union, such as Lithuania, Estonia and Finland. The Commission examined the draft law at its meeting on 21 May this year and supported it. On behalf of the Commission, I call for support.
