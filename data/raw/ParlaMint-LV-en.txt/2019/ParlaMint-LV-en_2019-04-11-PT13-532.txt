Is the government's desire to set cash limits favourable and conducive to the economic environment? Only two laws and regulations of the European Union impose obligations on the use of cash. The Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on cash control provides for a control system applicable to natural persons entering or leaving the European Union and holding a currency of EUR 10 000 or more. The Fourth Anti-Money Money Laundering Directive imposes due diligence obligations in relation to (underscore!) persons trading in goods, in so far as they make or receive cash payments amounting to EUR 10 000. According to the report of the European Commission of 12 June 2018 to the European Parliament and to the Council on restrictions on the movement of cash and the study carried out in this context, it is necessary to have equivalent competitive conditions in all Member States of the European Union and it is not possible to ensure them if there are very different laws at Member State level which not only distort fair competition but may lead to cross-border relocations and thus affect consumers who choose to buy goods at better prices in other countries. The study concludes that limiting the cash payments of the citizens of the European Union is a sensitive issue and that the possibility to pay cash is considered by the citizens of the European Union as one of the fundamental freedoms, which must not be disproportionately restricted. In the light of the study's data, the European Commission does not consider any legislative initiatives on this issue. As indicated in the study, different legislations distort fair competition and lead to the relocation of companies to countries with more favourable tax policies. The above clearly defines the European Union's approach to the restriction of cash transactions within the European Union and the principles of respect for the fundamental freedoms of natural persons who are citizens of the European Union.
