The proposals for amendments and additions to the Energy Act concern several directions: First of all, the liberalisation of the wholesale electricity market is continued, with producers with total installed capacity from 500 kilowatts to one megawatt offering their electricity on the free market. Secondly, proposals are made to remove the 5% contribution obligation on the revenues of renewable energy producers from 1 January 2021. The aim of this change is to stimulate the production of green energy by regulating measures to promote the production of electricity from renewable sources in relation to the implementation of the commitments of the Republic of Bulgaria as a Member State of the European Union resulting from the set objective of achieving a full carbon neutrality of the European Union by 2050, the so-called Green transaction, and in particular the achievement of the allocated share of renewable energy from Bulgaria in the total energy consumption in the country. The bill provides for the withdrawal of cold reserve purchase transactions, which will be achieved through the conclusion of transactions on a competitive basis for the purchase of availability for additional services, namely participation in primary frequency regulation, automatic secondary regulation and manual secondary control of frequency and exchange capacities in accordance with the provisions of Commission Regulation (EU) 2017/1485 of 2 August 2017 laying down guidelines on the operation of the electricity transmission system in the volume defined by that Regulation. Under the said Regulation, each transmission network operator manages its control area with sufficient reserves to increase and reduce active power so that it can deal with imbalances in its control area caused by a gap between demand and supply and emergency situations. This bill introduces the national law in accordance with the Regulation, expressly providing that the operator provides the necessary reserves by concluding ancillary services contracts on a market-based basis after a tender. The specific approach proposed to the cold reserve transactions is that the availability required for the security of the electricity system will be provided by active production capacities under Commission Regulation (EU) 2017/1485 of 2 August 2017. In this way, the balance, security and flexibility of the electricity system will be ensured in normal and emergency modes, while introducing additional incentives for producers to participate in the free electricity market. The removal of cold reserve availability transactions represents another step towards introducing a capacity mechanism. The proposed change aims to settle market relations in the transitional period before full liberalisation of the wholesale electricity market and to create a predictable environment for all market participants. Following the end of the discussion and on the basis of the 11-vote energy committee vote, 6 votes abstentioned and no votes against the House proposed to the National Assembly to adopt a bill amending and supplementing the Energy Act, No 054-01-103, submitted on 11 November 2020 by Valentin Nikolov and a group of MPs.
