The amendments resulting from Directive (EU) 2019/160 are targeted in those areas and are aimed at facilitating the cross-border offering of investment funds. Next, Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 introduces harmonised rules for the disclosure of information in relation to the sustainability of financial market participants and financial advisors established in the Regulation in order to ensure transparency for investors and to improve the comparability of financial products. The Regulation requires financial market participants and financial advisors to pre-negotiate and ongoing disclosure of information to final investors when they act as agents to those final investors. In this regard, the amendments to the Draft Law are proposed measures in the relevant sectoral national legislation which provide conditions for the effective implementation of Regulation (EU) 2019/2088 by financial market participants and financial advisors, setting out national authorities and providing for administrative penalties. The final provisions of the Draft Law complement the exceptions from the scope of the Financial Instruments Markets Act with a new point in relation to the figure of the collective funding service providers resulting from Directive (EU) 2020/1504 and in accordance with the definition in Regulation (EU) 2020/1503 on European undertakings' collective funding service providers. The introduction of uniform procedures for withdrawing the notification of supply, as well as the mechanisms provided to investors without providing physical presence, is expected to lead to the elimination of the inefficiency of the current regulatory framework and to have a positive impact on management companies originating in the Republic of Bulgaria. Easier cross-border distribution is expected to create prerequisites for the access of more shareholders, as well as for the growth of assets under the management of undertakings for collective investment in transferable securities and alternative investment funds. During the public discussion of the Draft Law Petko Krastev, Chairman of the Management Board of the Bulgarian Association of Management Companies stressed that the proposed amendments were discussed thoroughly in the framework of the public discussion, a large part of the proposals of the Association were supported, optimized the activities of management companies, collective investment schemes and alternative investment funds, and unnecessary administrative burdens for the business were dropped. The Financial Supervision Commission supported the Bill. During the discussion, Professor Rumen Gechev emphasized on the risk that alternative investment funds could diversify their investments into more different assets and in particular that in the preliminary study there is no mandatory form of notification to the Commission of financial supervision of the content of the preliminary offer and potential professional investors to which it is addressed. It also focused on the deadlines for notifying the Commission of financial supervision of any material change in data and documents, including unplanned ones, by expressing concern about the possibility for the Commission to suspend it in a timely manner. Professor Gechev also raised a question as to whether the fees to be collected for the operation of the Bulgarian market are proportionate to the European market and whether they are sufficient to cover the costs that the Financial Supervision Commission carries out in relation to its activities. He also paid attention to the reduction in the amount of sanctions and fines and asked what this required? In conclusion, Professor Gechev noted that the capital market is a key mechanism of any market economy, which is why it is important to analyze how the Bulgarian market will change in numerical terms. In response from the Ministry of Finance pointed out that the preliminary offer is a new institute, which is currently being introduced not only in our country and is derived from European legislation. The aim is to facilitate cross-border supply without launching the notification procedure and only for professional investors. Such a offer may only be made by authorised persons who manage alternative investment funds with assets over 500 million. EUR capital and which are entitled to offer such alternative funds in principle under a notification procedure in other Member States. They shall be given the opportunity to test the market in another Member State without addressing an official notification procedure. The provisions are recorded in such a way that persons may not be able to record actual shares within the pre-offeration but only after they have officially notified the Commission of financial supervision. As regards the question that persons are not obliged to comply with a form of notification to the Commission of financial supervision, it was answered that this is because they are not supervised by the Commission within the framework of the prior offer. They shall not also be obliged to notify the conduct of the offer itself, but shall maintain information at all times on the conduct of such and potential investors to whom it is addressed in the event that the Financial Supervision Commission checks or is referred to to verify, under what circumstances, to whom it has been addressed and whether all restrictions have been complied with. The main focus is the restriction on subscription of shares. With regard to penalties, a minimum level of the maximum sanction which the supervisory authority may impose for a specific infringement is laid down and the decision is left to the Member States.
