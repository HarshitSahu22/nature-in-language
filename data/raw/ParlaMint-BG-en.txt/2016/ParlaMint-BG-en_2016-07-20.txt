Art. 23a 23d: "Professional Colleges" Art. 23a. Professional colleges shall carry out vocational training for persons aged 16 to acquire a fourth degree of professional qualification under the framework programme D. Status and types of professional colleges Art. 23b. (1) Professional colleges are state, municipal and private. (2) State and municipal professional colleges shall be opened by order of the Minister of Education and Science and shall be financed by the State budget, respectively, through the budget of the Ministry of Education and Science or of another ministry or office or through the budget of the municipalities. (3) Professional colleges established by natural and/or legal persons such as commercial companies, non-profit legal entities or cooperatives or as companies under the legislation of a Member State shall be private. (4) The private professional colleges shall carry out their activities after entering the register of professional colleges under the conditions and under the terms of this law. (5) Private professional colleges shall be financed under the terms and conditions of the law under which they are established. (6) Professional colleges are legal entities. (7) State and municipal professional colleges shall acquire the quality of a legal person from the day of the promulgation of the act of opening them in the Official Journal of the European Union, unless otherwise provided. (8) Private professional colleges acquire the quality of a legal person under the terms and conditions of the Commercial Law, the Law on Non-Profit Legal Entities, the Law on Cooperatives or the Law of another Member State. (9) The headquarters of the professional college shall be the place where its management is located. (10) The official address of the professional college shall be the address at which the management of the institution is located. The official address shall correspond to the address of one of the buildings where vocational training or validation takes place. (11) The name of the professional college shall be written in Bulgarian literary language and may include an indication of the main professions in which training is carried out and/or the basic form of training. The name of a private professional college shall also include the designation of a private professional college. (12) Professional colleges hold a simple own stamp. State and municipal professional colleges also possess a stamp with an image of the state crest. Autonomy of professional colleges Art. 23c. Professional colleges shall carry out their activities on the basis of the principle of autonomy by: 1. define their development policies in accordance with the laws of the country; 2. establish their arrangements and activities in accordance with this law; 3. independently determine the faculty staff and additional conditions for acceptance of the students; 4. draw up curricula and curricula depending on the interests of learners, the needs of the labour market and in accordance with the requirements of this law; 5. determine the organisation, forms and methods of training; 6. carry out joint academic, project and innovation activities with vocational schools, universities, scientific organisations and individual employers; 7. participate in national and international programmes and projects supporting activities in the field of vocational training. Professional college management bodies Art. 23 years old. (1) The management and control body of state and municipal professional colleges shall be the director who shall be appointed by the funding body and shall be appointed on the basis of a contest held under the terms and conditions of the Labour Code. (2) The Director of para. 1 manages and represents the institution concerned. (3) The management and control bodies of private professional colleges shall be determined by the law under which they are established. (4) The activities of carrying out vocational training and validation of professional knowledge, skills and competences in private professional colleges shall be led by a director appointed by the company, by the non-profit legal person or by the cooperative. (5) The Director of a professional college is a person who is a Bulgarian citizen who has acquired an educational-qualification degree of higher education, who is not sentenced to imprisonment for crimes of a general nature and does not suffer from diseases threatening the life and health of the students. (6) Director of a professional college may also be: 1. citizen of another Member State; 2. a foreign citizen, if proposed under interstate agreements; 3. long-term, long-term or permanent resident foreign citizen. (7) The Director of State and Municipal Professional College organizes and controls the overall activity of the institution. (8) The Director of a private professional college shall manage the activities under para. 4 in the institution. On behalf of the importers, the bill was presented by Mrs. Diana Yordanova. The draft Law amending and supplementing the Law on Corporate Income Tax (CPA) was drawn up to ease the mechanism for taxation of personal use in the form of costs in kind related to own, employed or provided for use assets and/or personnel, from the point of view of the CPO and the Law on Income Tax on Natural Persons (CPA). This is achieved by granting the right of choice to employers, instead of tax under the Law on personal income taxes, to choose to tax income in kind, representing costs in kind related to own, employed or provided for use assets and/or personnel provided for the personal use of workers, employees and persons employed under a management and control contract, as well as persons exercising personal labour within the meaning of § 1, item 1. 26, point (d) of the Additional Provisions of the Law on Income Taxes of Individuals. The choice does not cover other categories of natural persons, since in such cases a substantial change in their tax regime would result, and the objective of the bill is not to change the order of taxation of the relevant categories of taxable persons, but to ease administration. The amount of assigned revenue in accordance with Article 21 (3) of the Financial Regulation is estimated at EUR 150000. This category does not include the expenses paid and/or incurred by the taxable person for goods and services provided to the natural persons concerned by third parties. In general, the costs associated with the use of assets or staff of a taxable person for the personal use of persons employed and by persons exercising personal labour bear the signs of remuneration for work rendered by those persons. The qualification of such costs as remuneration is a prerequisite for their recognition for tax purposes under the Law of the European Union, but leads to the determination of income tax on natural persons and compulsory social contributions as the natural person benefiting from these assets and/or personnel in practice acquires an additional income (in kind income). It is precisely the determination of the tax base for the purposes of personal income tax and the basis for the levying of compulsory social contributions that there is considerable effort involved in the administration of these obligations. For these reasons, in order to reduce the administrative burden, as well as the need to find a regime that is easier to implement, it is proposed to introduce a right to tax on costs in kind related to own, leased assets or provided for use assets and/or personnel provided for the personal use of employees and persons exercising personal labour. In the case of an employer's right of choice, non-cash income received by natural persons in the form of expenses relating to the use of assets and/or personnel of the company for private purposes will not be subject to tax under the Law on Income Taxes of Natural Persons, and in this regard the proposed changes to that Law are proposed. At the same time, it is proposed that these incomes are not subject to compulsory social and health insurance, and that the necessary changes to the relevant regulations will be made after the promulgation of the Law. The proposed tax regime provides for taxable persons to be all employers or contracting entities under management and control contracts as well as legal persons whose partners and shareholders engage in personal work when their income is equal to employment benefits for tax purposes. It is proposed that the tax base for determining the cost tax be the sum of the costs associated with the use of own, employed or provided assets and/or personnel for private use for the calendar year. As regards the assets provided for for personal use in the Draft Law, regulation is provided which distinguishes how the tax base is determined and the assets are grouped into the following three categories: vehicles, real estate and assets other than those mentioned in the first two categories. The purpose of the grouping is to create an appropriate mechanism for determining the tax base, which is both easy to apply and reflect the specifics of the assets. For this reason, in cases where the allocation of costs between activity and personal use is not carried out by measuring those costs, an approach is taken to use a ratio that is based on miles or time of use for vehicles or fixed percentage but on the property of an area or on time of use. This ratio shall be applied to the total cost and shall thus determine the tax base for the taxation of costs. For the third category, which includes a variety of types of assets, it is envisaged that the tax base will be 20 per cent of the total cost of the asset concerned, unless the taxable person justifies a documentary other amount on the basis. The rate for in-kind expenditure tax relating to own, employed or provided for use assets and/or personnel provided for personal use shall be 10%. The tax shall be final and shall be payable once a year by 31 March of the following calendar year. The bill provides for the cancellation of the tax on the costs associated with the operation of vehicles when they are operated. The proposed change aims again to simplify the cost tax mechanism, as well as to take account of economic realities, in so far as management is undoubtedly inherent and necessary for each company, and in the current dynamic business conditions it cannot be carried out without the use of vehicles. Changes are also proposed related to the indication of data on the owners of foreign persons in the annual tax return under the Corporate Income Tax Act.
