The Group of Members and Members tabled in the National Assembly of the Republic of Slovenia a proposal for a law on urgent recovery of the consequences of cross-border burdens in the Celjska Kolina area. According to the applicants, the basic purpose of the law submitted after almost 30 years is to ensure the implementation of measures to reduce the effects of environmental damage, in particular as a result of a long-standing metallurgical activity, and to improve the quality of the natural and built environment and the health status of the inhabitants in the Celje Valley area. The Government of the Republic of Slovenia is aware of the problems of the rehabilitation of the degraded environment, and believes that this problem needs to be addressed systematically in the whole of Slovenia, i.e. in the Celje Valley area, with clear final objectives, methodologies and procedures. The government supports the requirement to rehabilitate degraded areas while at the same time objecting to the adoption of the proposed law on a case-by-case basis. Such an approach undermines the current system regime in the area of the rehabilitation of the degraded environment and worsens it in important respects, with no new solutions and, of course, no systemic solutions for future recovery cases in the past of contaminated areas. The law is also non-executive in a number of proposed solutions or enforceability is linked to additional rules and measures which are not provided for by law. The Government is aware of some shortcomings in the current arrangement for the rehabilitation of degraded areas of the environment, particularly in the case of areas that have been contaminated in the past. As a result, the Ministry of Environment and Space has also included a specific chapter on rehabilitation in the past of contaminated areas in the draft revised Environmental Protection Act, which was already under public scrutiny. The new law will systematically address these issues, in particular the responsibility for pollution, the process of identifying the polluted area, the priority order of remediation and its implementation, and the systemic financial resources. The draft revised Environmental Protection Act between the previously polluted areas is also considered to be abandoned landfills and contaminated underground pits in addition to abandoned industrial areas. One of the shortcomings in the current regulation is precisely the prioritisation of the order of remediation in the past of contaminated areas. This is necessary in order to ensure equal treatment before the law and the equal use of limited public funds. The rehabilitation of a particular area is not intended to be subject to a non-systemic approach or pressure from different formal or informal groups. In this regard, the Government recalls the Resolution on the National Environmental Protection Programme, which also contains a chapter on the rehabilitation of degraded areas, including, for example, the urban area of Celje, the Shale Valley, Zasavje, Idrija, Anhovega and some other smaller environmental areas. Other available data and documents also show that other areas have been contaminated in the Republic of Slovenia in the past. It is therefore necessary to address the area in a systematic manner, since the following logic of the applicants for each further case of individual rehabilitation should be adopted by specific laws in the past of the polluted area. Comparative arrangements in some Member States, Germany, Austria, Poland have also been taken into account in the preparation of the revised Environmental Protection Act. In these countries, too, a system-wide rehabilitation system is in place in the past of contaminated areas. The bill also vaguely defines the individual measures envisaged, since it deals only with soil rehabilitation, but not with other over-polluted environmental components, which also adversely affect the environment and human health. The most important part of the bill's proposal, the implementation of the action programme, is irrelevant, as a specific protocol that will define the procedure for selecting the most appropriate technology and how to implement the action programme in terms of feasibility, efficiency, price and sustainable environmental protection has not yet been developed. Such a protocol should be prepared at the time of adoption of the law in order to ensure transparency in the adoption of the law. Environmental and health measures should be coordinated in advance and the specific tasks of the competent ministries should be specified in detail. In the event that the law is adopted, two different legal arrangements will be established in the Republic of Slovenia for the same cases. The rehabilitation of the degraded environment in some cases is governed by a government regulation issued under the Environmental Protection Act and, in other cases, a separate law. In doing so, it remains completely open whether, with the adoption of the proposed law in the future, specific laws will have to be adopted for all other cases of the rehabilitation of the polluted environment. The problem of dual regulation is particularly evident in the case of Celje. The improvement of air quality in the area of the Municipality of Celje is the subject of a government decree and soil rehabilitation should be regulated by the proposed law. Thank you.
