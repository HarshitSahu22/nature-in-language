With regard to your question concerning taxation of residents of the Republic of Slovenia with regard to the ratification of the US-Slovenia Convention on the avoidance of double taxation of income and property, we forward the following. The conclusion of the Convention on the avoidance of double taxation of income and property is a result of international transactions in which persons participating in them fall under the tax rules of several countries. The Conventions are based on the laws of the countries relating to taxation of income and property, which are based on two internationally accepted taxation standards: taxation of residents on incomes achieved in the country and abroad and taxation of non-residents on incomes originating in the country. These starting points are also based on the tax laws of the Republic of Slovenia, after which the residents of the Republic of Slovenia are taxed both on income received in Slovenia and on income received from abroad. The Conventions, for example, agree on a way to avoid double taxation resulting from the above-mentioned international principles. The basis of the negotiations of the Republic of Slovenia is the OECD Model Convention, which is also the basis for the conventions of the developed OECD Member States and some other less developed countries, as well as the specific US-Slovenian Convention. Under this type convention, where the Republic of Slovenia is negotiated, and which has also been approved by the Committee on International Relations of the National Assembly, the right to tax the State of residence shall be vested in it. The country of residence for an individual means the country to which he or she is connected, mainly based on the criterion of permanent residence of an individual. The State of residence is also obliged to abolish double taxation in accordance with the method normally laid down in Article 24 of the Convention. Slovenia, when negotiating with the United States of America, relied on a typical model of the Convention and adequately represented the interests of the Republic of Slovenia in this area. With regard to the taxation of pensions in Croatia, we have received information today confirming that the Republic of Croatia has not yet concluded a Convention on the avoidance of double taxation with the United States of America and that, in accordance with Croatian regulations, the pensions of residents they receive from abroad are fully exempt from taxation in Croatia. In Slovenia it is stipulated by the Income Act that in Slovenia such pensions are received in Slovenia and pensions received abroad. Thus, the Republic of Croatia unilaterally exempt pensions from abroad from taxation, while in Slovenia there is no such arrangement. The circumvention conventions do not achieve exemption or taxation of certain income in the country of residence, but are in fact subject to the tax laws of individual countries. Thank you very much.
