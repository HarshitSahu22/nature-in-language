ParlaMint-NO_2003-03-18-lower.ana.ud1932e49	What this is all about is a proposal to tighten up the penalties from two to six years for a particularly serious violation of customs law. In order for this penalty to be used, the offence must be ‘wilful or grossly negligent’. This increase would be particularly relevant in the case of large quantities of alcohol, tobacco and meat smuggling. The Ministry points out that Norway has lower penalties for smuggling than other countries with which it is natural to compare. The Ministry also points out that Norway has a higher charge for current contraband than our neighbouring countries, and that these two conditions in combination mean that Norway is particularly vulnerable to smuggling. Of course, high levels of service and low penalties make it more appropriate to smuggle than if the service was lower and the penalty was higher. In this proposition, however, one will not do anything in relation to the service of smuggling – only in relation to the level of punishment. None of the hearing authorities have responded to this change. Those who may be opposed to this criminal aggravation have not come to their point of view, cork to the ministry or to the committee. In view of this, a co-consultant committee is behind this change. Quite rightly, the Progress Party and the Coastal Party have their own comments, where the high level of fees is taken up as a significant element and basis for smuggling, and where a line points out that it is also important to reduce these strange charges in order to bring down the smuggling to Norway. But when it comes to the tightening of penalties, the committee is united.
