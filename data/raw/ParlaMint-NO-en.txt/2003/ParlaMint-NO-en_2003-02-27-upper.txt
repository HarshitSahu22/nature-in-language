ParlaMint-NO_2003-02-27-upper.ana.ud2262e122	I am not going to make any proposals, but in the public debate on the Mehamn Act after the Odelstinget took its decision, it has been pointed out that there may seem to be two possible interpretations of Section 2 of the Act that relate to explanation of closed doors in cases where the Commission uses its ability to impose a charge on witnesses to explain itself. In order to be sure that afterwards there will be no doubt as to what has been the intention of the legislator, I will allow myself to quote from the Commission's director's letter to the Presidentship of Storting on 19 February of this year, in which he explains his interpretation as follows: ‘I believe that the law aims at exceptional cases, for use where a witness refuses to meet the Commission and to provide voluntary explanations, and that the Commission may otherwise allow the hearings to be opened in accordance with Section 124 of the Court’s Law. § 125.’ The Presidency of the Grand Duchy responded to this enquiry at its meeting on 21 February, and I quote from the Presidency's reply: ‘The Presidency subscribes to the interpretation of the law on which the Commission’s Chairman has relied with regard to the submission of explanations to the Commission, and considers this to be a consequence of Section 1 of the Act.’ With this, I would like to take the opportunity at the Lamentations meeting, and therefore during the formal discussion of this law by the Stortinge, to stress that the interpretation of a single presidency has formed, of course, is also the interpretation that is in line with the intention of the legislator – that is, the Stortinges.
