ParlaMint-NO_2003-01-23.ana.ud1153e29	Honourable co-representatives! Former prime minister and councillor Kaare Meland is dead – almost 88 years old. Kaare Meland was born on February 22, 1915, in Bergen, but grew up in the neighbouring municipality of Fana. After graduating artium in 1933 and commercial gymnasium in 1935 he took the diploma at the Norwegian School of Economics in 1939, followed by the examination as state-authorised accountant in 1945. Throughout the post-graduate period, Meland combined the full-time studies with work in different positions in the business community. Kaare Meland took part in the Western campaign in April 1940 and, in the fall of 1940, became involved in the construction of a secret military organization in the Bergen region — one of the precursors to Milorg. Already in 1946, Kaare Meland became a member of the Fana Government – until 1951 and then from 1956 to 1959, when he was also the municipality's rapporteur. He was a representative of the Stortinget from 1954 to 1961 and met for shorter periods from 1959 to 1961. Meland also had more partisan jobs in these years, both locally and centrally. But he was more concerned with his business tasks, including: as Head of the Office of the Bergen Steam Ship Company and as Director of Johan C. Martens A/S, “Tranmartens”. He was more concerned with these duties than with politics and was probably as surprised as most others when he got the demanding assignment to take over the management of the Ministry of Industry after the Kings Bay crisis in 1963. However, both Højre’s President Sjur Lindebräk and Prime Minister Jon Lyng were convinced that Kaare Meland had all the necessary qualifications to be able to lead the very department that had been most central in the development that led to the government change. Although the term of office as an industrial minister was short, Kaare Meland was nevertheless able to show that he was an adult. He himself gave a vivid description of the situation he encountered in the crisis-affected department, and of the persons and matters he had to deal with during his brief Council of State. During the state council period, Meland saw as a temporary interruption in the business effort, and in 1964 he was appointed as Managing Director of Bergen's Sparebank. He therefore reacted very negatively when Hordaland Right wanted him to be the first to be on the Grand Prix list in 1965. However, the protests were not followed, and Kaare Meland was very contradictoryly nominated in the first place. It turned out that Hordaland Right had assessed the situation correctly. Not least thanks to Kaare Meland, the Right made an even better choice in Hordaland than on a national basis. Meland naturally became a member of the financial committee, where he with normal intensity engaged himself with life and lust. At the same time, he was firmly convinced that there were four years in Stortinget, and in order to keep in touch with his civil profession he chose to carry out his duties in Bergen's Sparebank next to the landfill work. Consistently he phrased himself re-election in 1969. Kaare Meland led the Bergen Savings Bank up to 1980 and spent the last two years before he left for the 1982 age limit, to finish the work of collecting savings banks in the West in the Savings Bank West. Meland was the chairman of the Sparebank Association and had a number of other positions in different business organisations and employers’ organisations. In 1978 he was appointed knights of the 1st class of the Order of St. Olav for his all-round social effort. Kaare Meland combined great professional skills and great work with a strong sense that the individual citizen also has clear obligations to engage in the big society, even when it comes across the personal desires and interests of the moment. His great efforts in Norwegian policy are the result of this very sense of duty. He did not engage in politics to promote his own interests, but because others thought he had an unusual amount to contribute. In order to avoid creating the impression that Kaare Meland was solely a sense of duty and work, it is close to borrowing a few words from the necrologist over Kaare Meland in the Official Journal of Bergen: ‘Kaare Meland was a raw and warm personality with a large team of friends in very different environments. He liked himself in a good way, and cultivated the narrator's joy even with a few listeners. He had a lot on his heart, and there was a lot he burned for." That's how Kaare Meland will also be remembered in Stortinget. We thank Kaare Meland for his great work and government and bring peace to his memory. The representatives heard the President's commemorative speech.
