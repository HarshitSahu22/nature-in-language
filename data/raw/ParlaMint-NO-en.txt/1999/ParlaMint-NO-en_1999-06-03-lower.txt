ParlaMint-NO_1999-06-03-lower.ana.ud1370e129	The amendment to the Product Control Act we are dealing with today facilitates more environmentally friendly use of chemicals. The substitution principle requires that the person dealing with hazardous chemicals assess whether one can achieve its objective equally with less hazardous chemicals or with another method. The principle complements the work of the authorities on limiting and prohibiting the most dangerous chemicals in society and aims to reduce the unnecessary risks associated with the use of hazardous chemicals. Furthermore, the UN Action Plan for the 21st century, Agenda 21, highlights substitution as a classic method of risk reduction. The same approach has been developed nationally and internationally through the principle of the best available technology, or rather the concept of best available techniques, which are used to create a concession for polluting activity, and in the EU Integrated Pollution Control Directive, to which Norway is obliged by the EEA Agreement. In Norwegian law, the principle of substitution has been part of older and more recent legislation on the protection of the health of workers. It can be said that there has been a lack of Norwegian environmental legislation that the principle has not been statutory in terms of damage to the external environment. This is now being changed. It is underlined that a significant increase in financial burden is not expected as a result of the introduction of the substitution principle. The rule does not require hazard or risk assessment, or the implementation of substitution where this entails unreasonable costs or disadvantages. It is gratifying that there is a broad majority supporting the change. The Progress Party is solely concerned with its amendment.
