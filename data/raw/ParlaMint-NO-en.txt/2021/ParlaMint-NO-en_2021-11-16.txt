ParlaMint-NO_2021-11-16.ana.ud163e24	Honourable co-representatives! Mr. Astrid Nøklebye Heiberg passed away on 2 April last year, 83 years old. We have lost a good friend and colleague and a role model for many both inside and outside this house. Astrid was a wise lady who generously east of her knowledge of younger staging representatives. She fought all her life against injustice and discrimination and was a pioneer in many areas. As a young psychiatrist, she stood in the first place in the fight to remove the prejudices of society against people who met condemnation and exclusion solely because of sexual orientation. In the process of removing homophilia as a diagnosis, she played a leading role. As State Secretary for the second time from 2013 to 2016, long after the ordinary retirement age, and later as the oldest representative of the Stortinge, she both symbolized and fought for active older people and against all forms of age discrimination. Astrid said often that she became a politician by chance. It was hardly by chance, but she herself has said that it was during a cinema visit with the incoming social minister in the Willoch government, Leif Arne Heløe, with whom she wrote the doctoral thesis, that she was asked to become his State Secretary in 1981. With sound professional background, sound discretion and eyes and interest in people, she was noticed as a State Secretary. The road went naturally to Stortinget as representative of Oslo Right from 1985 to 1989. She was given a central position in the Right, as the leader of the Hojrekvinners Landsförbund 1985–1989 and as the party's first Vice-Chairman 1990–1991. She was also Minister of Consumers and Administration in 1986 and Oslo Højre's rapporteur's candidate in 1991, followed by a period in Oslo City Council. Astrid was always concerned with people. It was perhaps this that led her to undertake the central tasks of the Red Cross. She was president of Norway's Red Cross 1993-1999 and president of the International Association of Red Cross and Red Crescent Unions 1997-2001. For her great efforts here, she was awarded the highest distinction of the International Red Cross movement, the medal named after the founder of the Red Cross, Henry Dunant. Titles of books she wrote – The precious hope, Change and wonder – or edited with others – So much solitude: Fate, crises, opportunities – are in many ways eloquent for her commitment. It also appeared in the board of directors she assumed, for example. University of Oslo Prize for Human Rights, Lisl and Leo Etingers Foundation, Norsk Gerontologisk Institutt, Statens Institutt für Alcoholforschung and Zenter für Medizin Ethikk. In her own field of study, she was also a pioneer. as the first Norwegian woman with a doctorate in psychiatry and later a professor in the subject. Her humanistic overall exposure was internationally recognised, including: as Honorary Member of the American College of Psychiatry, an honorary membership granted to very few outside the United States. We light peace over Astrid Noklebye Heiberg's memory.
