ParlaMint-NO_2000-02-08-lower.ana.ud639e50	I have reason to believe that this will be a relatively short debate. In the 2000 budget, the Government agreed to launch various measures to reverse population trends in six municipalities in Inner Namdal, North-Trondelag. Inner Namdal has been designated as a test area for people to settle in District Norway. A number of projects have been launched for this purpose and one of them is to give up training loans for a period of three years for borrowers residing or pursuing a profession in Inner Namdal. There were no comments on this pilot project during the budgetary procedure before Christmas. The Government is now proposing to legislate on the pilot project, and the majority of the Committee agrees with such a legalisation of a time-limited cancellation. Should the experiment have any value, it must work over three years and not depend on annual budget debates. So a law shelter is right. I am pleased that we can now try out the depreciation of student loans as an instrument of district policy. The scheme for Inner Namdal is going to be equal to the scheme for Nord-Troms and Finnmark. We need more instruments in rural policy, and the experiment will clarify whether this is an appropriate instrument.
ParlaMint-NO_2000-02-08-lower.ana.ud639e65	We have encouraged our remarks that state that we do not want to subscribe to the law of the trial system in Inner Namdal. However, we have tacitly joined the appropriations in budgetary proceedings in the municipal committee and in the church, education and research committee, but the principles that are moving when it comes to legislation cannot therefore be accepted. We do not believe that municipalities will be guaranteed permanent residence over time if they give up e.g. study loans — in whole or in part — because they are very likely to attract these municipalities to be students with a particularly weak economy, and who, when they have been there for some time, have been depreciated from their loans and have developed the practices necessary to become even more attractive in the labour market, will leave these municipalities again. That is why, in our opinion, this is a blow in the loose air, and it is more appropriate to say something about the possibilities of six municipalities to be attractive in relation to a general housing problem. I therefore recommend that the Group of the Party of European Socialists vote against the Committee's position.
