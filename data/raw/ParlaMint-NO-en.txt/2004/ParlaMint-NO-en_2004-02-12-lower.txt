ParlaMint-NO_2004-02-12-lower.ana.ud2549e75	The Financial Collateral Act is the Government’s proposal for implementing the EU Directive on Financial Collateral Agreements in Norwegian law. A unanimous committee supports the Government’s choice of method of implementation with its own special law. The Committee has pointed out that such a legal solution could create ambiguities, in particular if it is chosen in many cases. Such confusion has been pointed out in a letter dated 30 January of this year from the Financial Industry’s Main Organisation and the Reserve Bank Association to the Judicial Committee, which are currently amending the law on mortgages for consideration. The Financial Committee has received a copy of this letter after our suspension has been issued. I believe that the Minister of Finance's letter to the Finance Committee dated 20 January this year provides sufficient clarification that a legal pledge will not interfere with security rights established on the basis of the Financial Collateral Act. I refer to the answer to question 1 in that letter from the Minister of Finance. The Committee supports the choices made by the Ministry when the minimum rules of the Directive have been waived in some respects. In particular, the fact that own shares are not exempted from the financial instruments eligible for financial collateral is further that the scope of the law has been extended in relation to the Directive by the inclusion of an agreement between a financial corporation and an ordinary limited liability company and that the company is considered a financial institution when carrying out a type of activity involving the widespread use of cross-border financial collateral. The Committee has noted that Sweden, Denmark and Finland have chosen to extend the scope further so that natural persons may also be a contracting party. Such an extension, given similar rules as neighbouring countries, I expect the ministry to assess. The purpose of the law and the directive is, inter alia, to: clarifying an uncertain legal situation and creating better predictability. The security officer shall be provided with better protection in the event of payment problems. In the long term, this will reduce risks and improve access to risk capital at lower cost. It's positive. I would like to see a broader legislative review with the same objectives to make it safer and less unpredictable to contribute risk capital to business. We see clearly the need for such a review. The number of private shareholders in Oslo Stock Exchange is reduced by 30,000 in a few years. It is clear that the small shareholders have been alarmed by unforeseeable and high risks. The situation of the coercive shareholders in the Aker Kværner system is a sad example. Most of them are small and experience, among other things. that the shares in which they have been pledged after winning the court have been rendered non-voting by the pledger while an appeal is waiting to be dealt with. This will reduce the value of the mortgage. It is highly desirable for the Government to take the initiative to bring about the changes in the law that the ongoing conflict has shown to be lacking. The Minister of Finance, as already mentioned, pointed out in his letter that it is the general mortgage law that is the right place. I see that the law we are adopting today does not prevent the situation of minority shareholders from being improved in relation to a large owner and in relation to the dominant creditors. It must be possible to improve the law without the EU imposing it on us. I expect the government to give a signal that this will happen. The last points of view, on getting a legal review, are also supported by the SV and the Coastal Party.
