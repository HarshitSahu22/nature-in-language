ParlaMint-NO_2004-10-19.ana.ud1022e42	Honourable co-representatives! The former deposit representatives Tore A. Liltved, Oddbjørn Langlo and Sigurd Holemark have died. Former poacher Tore A. Liltved died in July, 65 years old. Tore A. Liltved was born on May 8, 1939 in Arendal. After graduating artium in 1959 he became cand.mag. with the subjects English, Norwegian and history in 1965. In 1966, following a pedagogical seminar, he was employed at Moland Secondary School, which was to become Liltved's job until he arrived at the Stortinget in 1981. He had then become a teacher at the school. Tore A. Liltved became a member of Moland Municipal Government in 1971. From 1975 to 1979 he was a member of the Presidency, and from 1979 to 1981 he was a rapporteur in the municipality. At the same time he had a number of jobs both in the local Right Association and in the East-Agder Right. Tore A. Liltved became alternate to Stortinget from East Agder at the 1977 Grand Prix and met for a short time both in 1977/78 and in 1979/80. At the formation of the government of Willoch in October 1981, Astrid Gjertsen was the Council of State, and Tore A. Liltved moved up as a permanent representative. From April 1986 and beyond, he met continuously under Astrid Gjertsen's leave. Tore A. Liltved was elected as permanent representative in the 1989 election and was re-elected in 1993. During his 16 years at Stortinget, Tore A. Liltved had ample opportunity to show his broad political commitment. He was a member of several trade unions and distinguished himself well in them all. He was a member of the Consumer and Administrative Committee, of the Social Committee, of the Church and Education Committee and of the Municipal Committee. He was for a period of time a whipper and a member of the group board in the Right and was engaged in the Nordic Council and the Stortinget Group of the IPU. If we're just going to draw one case out of the many that Tore A. Liltved engaged in, there has to be work for the best possible education for students with different disabilities. This was not least the work on better education opportunities for deaf and hard-to-heard people. Former Puppy Representative Oddbjørn Langlo passed away at the end of July of this year, 69 years old. Oddbjørn Sverre Langlo was born on 20 April 1935 in Stranda. He graduated artium in 1954, followed by commercial high school in 1955 and the Defense Russian course in 1956. Oddbjørn Langlo became a civil economist from Norway's School of Economics in 1961. He was hired by Ringstads A/S in Stranda soon after the exam and was going to stay here – in different economic positions – until he was elected to the Stortinget in 1973. In 1973, as a co-owner, he participated in the establishment of Stranda Spekemat A/S. Until 1985 he was chairman of this company. Oddbjørn Langlo came early into political work and became head of Stranda Right in 1964, only 29 years old. From 1967 he was a member of Stranda Municipal Government. From 1971 to 1975 he was also a member of the Presidency. At the election in 1973, Oddbjørn Langlo was elected as a representative from More and Romsdal – a position he held until he made his pre-election in 1985. On Stortinget, Oddbjørn Langlo was first a member of the Transport Committee. In the next period he moved to the Industry Committee and then continued in the new Energy and Industry Committee. These committee positions were in the middle of the spotlight for Oddbjørn Langlo, both as district representative and as an industrial man. He was particularly engaged in power supply and had a number of trusts in local power companies alongside the big business. In Stortinget, he was Højre's chief spokesman in a series of major power building cases at the end of the 1970s. It was a task he solved with great thoroughness and great cause. Between 1981 and 1985, Oddbjørn Langlo was Chairman of the Stortinget Group of the Interparliamentary Union. Former PD Sigurd Holemark passed away on 12 August, at 69 years of age, after a long period of illness. Sigurd Holemark was born on September 8, 1934 in Norderhov. He graduated from Artium in 1954. After high school in trade in 1955 he became an intern at Borregaard Fabrikker A/S. It was the start of a long career in Borregaard. Sigurd Holemark had several different positions in the group. When he arrived at Stortinget in 1981, he was head of office, and in the years after Stortingen, he worked with information and social contacts in the same group. Sigurd Holemark had a long line of jobs in the Right. He was president of Sarpsborg Right in 1965–1967 and again in the period 1998–1999. He was chairman of the Eastfold Right in 1974–1978 and in 1980–1982. In the same periods he was also a member of the Right Central Government. During the peak periods 1973-1977 and 1977-1981, Sigurd Holemark met several times as an alternate for Eastfold County. He was elected representative in 1981 and was re-elected in the 1985 and 1989 elections. In the first period he was a member of the Defence Committee and an alternate of the enlarged Foreign and Constitutional Committee. From 1985 to 1989 he was first a member of the Financial Committee, then of the Committee on Agriculture. In the last period he was a member of the Energy and Industry Committee. Sigbjørn Holemark was a member of the Great Britain delegation to the NATO Parliamentary Assembly for a period of time and was head of the Interparliamentary Union Group from 1989 to 1993. Sigurd Holemark was a thorough and solid representative, who always went completely up in the fields for which he was responsible in the various professional committees. At the same time, however, he was always the industrial man — livingly concerned with the working conditions of industry and industry. He was always aware that he represented the industrial district of Eastfold. Sigurd Holemark also had a strong interest in foreign policy issues. He was also a member of the Defence Committee at a time when defence and foreign policy were very central to the current Norwegian domestic policy. He was also a hot follower of Norwegian membership of the European Union. He was a member of the EC Right-wing Committee until 1992 and took an active part in the work towards the 1994 referendum. These three representatives agreed that they were all very accessible and easy to deal with, with a large contact surface across all the lot boundaries. There are still many in the Storting that have positive memories of all three of them, although some years have passed since we had them among us to daily. We remember Tore A. Liltved, Oddbjørn Langlo and Sigurd Holemark for their work in Stortinget and brighten peace over their memory. The representatives heard the President's commemorative speech.
