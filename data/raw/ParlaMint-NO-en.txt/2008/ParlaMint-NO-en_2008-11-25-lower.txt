ParlaMint-NO_2008-11-25-lower.ana.ud1880e109	The background to this case is that in recent years there has been a sharp increase in the use of legal aid in patients' injuries, and thus a large increase in the costs of Norwegian patient damages to cover legal assistance. From 2003 to 2007, there was an increase of around 90 pst. In this proposition, the Government proposes changes to the Patient Injury Act so that measures can be taken to improve the control of these costs. There is consensus in the Committee on the need for action and the proposals bring together broad support within the Committee. The changes now proposed in the Patient Injury Act involve essentially three things: Firstly, it is proposed that the Patient Injury Act be amended to allow the King to provide detailed rules on the cover of legal expenses, including: to use the public remuneration rates of the State. Secondly, a change is proposed in such a way that a lawyer who undertakes a task for a client who wishes to promote a claim against Norwegian patient injury compensation under the Patient Injury Act or who complains of a decision to the Patient Injury Subject cannot claim or accept payment at higher hourly rates than the public salary. Thirdly, the time limit for bringing legal proceedings is extended from four to six months in order to give the patient a slightly better time to consider whether or not to bring an action. The Committee supports both the proposal to extend the time limit for actions and the proposal to allow the King to lay down detailed rules on the cover of legal expenses. A broad majority also supports the proposal to amend the law so that a lawyer cannot demand or receive additional payment from the patient in addition to the public salary. I think this will ensure non-discrimination, and that all patients are set on the same line in such cases.
ParlaMint-NO_2008-11-25-lower.ana.ud1880e137	The progress party sees that there is a need to control the costs of legal assistance. We will therefore support the proposal for a flat-rate pay-as-you-go rate. But we also see that there may be a danger that patients' injuries can be compromised by experienced lawyers. We believe that the public salary will lead to equal treatment in relation to what is covered after the right to cover legal expenses has been granted by Norwegian patients' compensation. In those cases where the right to use a lawyer is granted, there are complex issues that require a high level of acute competence, and it has to be emphasised. The Progress Party therefore considers it important that the level of the salary rate itself be assessed and, where appropriate, changed, in order to ensure the assistance of good lawyers. In view of this, we will support the proposal that has been submitted. On the other hand, the progress party cannot see that the imposition of a pay-as-you-go fee paid by Norwegian patients’ compensation should limit the patient’s choice of available lawyers and the right to use own funds at their own discretion in cases where they choose to use a lawyer who requires a higher hourly rate. There is still a possibility of leaving parts of the procedure outside the area authorised by Norwegian patients’ compensation and subject of the patient’s injury, which are not covered by the regulation. However, this could lead to a higher bill for the patient. The Party considers that the patient should be able to require in advance a decision on the extent of the legal fee to be covered and has the opportunity to make a decision on the choice of lawyer based on his own financial situation. Therefore, the Progress Party will not support the proposal for a new third paragraph of Section 11 of the Patient Damage Act that no additional remuneration may be required from the lawyer by the introduction of the public pay-as-you-go. The Progress Party will support the proposal to extend the time limit for judicial review from four to six months, which we think is very good.

ParlaMint-NO_2008-11-25-lower.ana.ud1880e250	As is known in this proposition, the government has submitted proposals for extending the right of asylum for chiropractors and manual therapists. The proposal is an adaptation to the asylum rules of 1 March 2007 adopted in the light of Ot.prp. No 6 for 2006-2007, which means that a dialogue meeting between the employer and the employee will take place within 12 weeks. If the employee so wishes, the doctor or other infirmary shall attend the meeting. Today, chiropractors and manual therapists have the right to apply for up to eight weeks. They will therefore not be able to attend meetings held closer to the 12-week deadline. An extension of the right to seek medical attention facilitates the participation of chiropractors and manual therapists in meetings held after eight weeks. The introduction of dialogue meetings changes focus from emphasis on evidence of lack of medical ability and what cannot be done to focus on functionality and what can actually be done. It is therefore more appropriate for chiropractors or manual therapists to be the ones attending the dialogue meeting, if there are no other conditions than those they treat, which are the basis for the search message. In such cases, health care professionals are more likely to be the one who continues to treat the sick person physically, and to be closer to assessing the functioning of a dialogue meeting. On this basis, I am pleased that, apart from the members of the Committee of the Progressive Party, everyone agrees with the proposal. It may appear that the Progress Party questions whether chiropractors and manual therapists should at all be entitled to search. I am pleased to point out here that the current health care system for up to eight weeks is not a probationary system, but was made permanent and nationwide in 2006 after a thorough evaluation of a trial scheme in three test cells. I also refer to the assessments in the proposition.
