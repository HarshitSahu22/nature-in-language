ParlaMint-NO_2008-06-04-lower.ana.ud248e81	In the autumn of 2007, it was found that a few Norwegian municipalities had sold future power revenues and invested the money in risk-filled bonds provided by companies in the Terra system. The municipalities lost huge sums on these investments. At the request of the Ministry of Local and Regional Affairs, the counties of Sogn and Fjordane, Nordland, Vest-Agder and Rogaland carried out legal checks on the financial management regulations of the municipalities concerned in their counties and assessed whether the financial agreements concluded by the municipalities were in line with the regulations. The statutory checks revealed that all municipalities had breached the municipal law on several points. In my view, the violation of the municipal law is very serious. In addition, the economic situation of these municipalities is now very serious. The municipalities themselves are responsible for the investments they make. It may not be possible for municipalities to place money in risky investments in order to make the profit themselves if it goes well, but government authorities must set up if the investment results in losses. It would be wrong if the State intervened and covered the municipalities' losses in such investments. This must be our main point of view. At the same time, it is important to remember that it is the municipalities that provide many of our most important welfare services. The population of the municipalities in question must be minimised in this situation. Like all of us, they have other demands for basic welfare services. The children may go to school and kindergarten. The parents must have the security and dignity they require, and the citizens must receive the service they require. That is why, on 25 April, the Ministry of Local and Regional Affairs promoted the proposal we are now considering. A unanimous committee agrees with the Government's proposal for changes to the municipal law on the extension of the deficit coverage period. This is a necessary step, so that the municipalities that have faced disproportionate challenges have the opportunity to clean up their financial obligations without overstepping the population of the municipalities concerned. That is, after all, the most important thing in this case.
