ParlaMint-NL_2016-12-01-tweedekamer-8.u12	Mr. Chairman. The first two motions, the motions on the items Nos. 6 and 7 do not concern me, because they are a matter between Parliament and the European Commission. I'm keeping an appropriate distance from that. In the motion in part No. Mr Van Vliet asked the government not to accept the proposals, even after any substantive changes. As far as I'm concerned, that's a turnaround. The cabinet has said that it is in no way in favour of basic harmonisation as an end in itself. However, it has always been prepared to address, in a sensible way, harmful differences between systems where necessary. This motion will block it. I must therefore advise against this motion. I've been clear enough about this. The cabinet is very negative about these two proposals, but now to say that they must now be rejected for years and always... no one can say how they are adjusted. Then I come to the motion in part No. 9 by Mr Merkies. It calls on the government not to advocate a long delay in the proposed implementation date at European level. I have just had a detailed exchange of views with the Chamber on the postponement. I have to discourage this motion. In the motion in part No. 10 The Government is invited to make efforts to add a minimum rate as a criterion for assessing whether countries are included in the list of non-cooperative jurisdictions. The rate is included in the blacklist as an indicator. That is exactly what needs to be done. However, it is not in line with the OECD spirit and it is also not in line with the sovereignty of countries to set minimum rates. I therefore advise against this motion. I come to the motion in part No. 11, by Mr Groot, calling for talks to be held on the gradual harmonization of the basis for European taxation of profits. Again, I say that I do not think harmonisation is an end in itself, but that the cabinet has always been prepared to eliminate harmful differences. Staying in conversation, however, seems to me to be a productive appeal. Again, it is not an end in itself, but it is not an end in itself either. If it is possible to remain in conversation in a sensible way, I would like to leave the ruling on this motion to the Chamber.
