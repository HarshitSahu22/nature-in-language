ParlaMint-NL_2016-12-08-tweedekamer-4.u2	Mr. Chairman. We've had a debate about signal animals. It's never good when you're called a signal animal. The meadow birds, the farmland birds are drastically decreasing in number, which is an important signal for the state of our ecosystems and the robustness of our nature. That is a very, very bad situation. We have asked various questions about the agricultural budget and during this general consultation. In fact, the most important thing is to stop intensive farming. Food has disappeared from the countryside. Pre-food and herbal pastures have now become monocultures, to flattened billiard sheets on which many fertilizers and pesticides are used. Chicks of meadow birds born in such an environment come from the egg in a place where there are no insects and no food. If they're unlucky, they'll end up in the mowers. That is the structural situation that needs to change. The Chamber isn't ready for that yet. The Labour Party has asked a few questions, but motions have also been rejected asking for a real transition to nature-inclusive agriculture. As long as this intensive farming does not end, attempts to save the meadow birds are described as "mopping with the tap open'. We certainly support GroenLinks' own-initiative paper and we must do everything we can, but we insist that intensive agriculture makes it impossible to protect nature sufficiently in the Netherlands. That remains the plea and commitment of the Party to the Animals. Anyone who believes that the status of meadow birds should be improved would do well to abandon intensive farming.
