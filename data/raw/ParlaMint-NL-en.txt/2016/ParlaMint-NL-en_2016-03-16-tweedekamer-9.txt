ParlaMint-NL_2016-03-16-tweedekamer-9.u6	Mr. Chairman. I can only respond to the three motions. I'll start with the motion in part No. 167 by Mrs De Vries, who rightly states that transparency about the functioning of banks, for example when it comes to interest rate derivatives, is very important to put the quality in order and also to protect the image of the others, who hopefully are doing well. In good Dutch: naming-and-shaming. I consider that motion to be a policy support, and I leave it to the Chamber to decide. We are going to try, of course, within the limits of the law and the European framework, but within it there are certainly possibilities to regulate this as quickly as possible. We are going to try, as requested in the motion, to submit a small and targeted bill to the Chamber by this autumn at the latest. The motion-Ronnes c.s. in piece No. 168 is about Deutsche Bank. There are two elements in it. The motion calls on the government to commit itself to Deutsche Bank's participation. That's what we did. The AFM has done so and from the Ministry of Finance it has done so. On that point, I leave the judgment to the Chamber. If the motion is adopted, I will bring it to the attention of Deutsche Bank again and I hope that this signal from Parliament will also be taken seriously. As I have said, I will at least ensure that the outcome of the process at Deutsche Bank, which is therefore going to set up its own process, does not deviate qualitatively from the outcome of the recovery framework for the other banks. If that's the case, we're really gonna have a serious conversation. This would mean that entrepreneurs would be treated unequally and that should be avoided. Secondly, this motion calls on the government to shape the powers of the AFM in such a way that participation in recovery actions is no longer voluntary. We are going to look at the legal ins and outs of them. You must realise that this is an interface with the discussion on the extent to which we protect small businesses and/orzzp'ers when it comes to financial services. SME organisations have expressed views on this issue. As we provide greater protection for entrepreneurs with the best intentions, financial lenders will make more demands and apply higher risk surcharges. That is exactly what SMEs are not waiting for. With that remark, we will look at this when the motion is adopted, but that does not, in my view, necessarily lead to a firm conclusion. So I cannot promise that we will then carry out or commit to such a thing. We really need to look at the legal aspects and the advantages and disadvantages for small and medium-sized enterprises. Having said that, let me make the judgment on the motion in part No. 168 to the Chamber. The motion-Ronnes c.s. in piece No. 169 is about prioritization. Let me say first of all that I very much regret with the Chamber that there has been another delay in this project. The new way in which we shape this is much more compelling. This concerns the way in which the review is now to take place, with a pre-established recovery framework which prescribes fairly precisely what decisions are to be taken and what compensation may result from it, but also how it is to be implemented. I call it hiring external people and so on. We're gonna roll it out very compellingly now. In the meantime, people can get into trouble. As we give this broad priority, the whole project is, of course, delayed. You can't give everyone priority. We will ask external experts to provide an adequate solution to the really poignant cases as quickly as possible. That's what I said in the AO. I think that's the way forward. I am prepared to inform every six months, but I cannot, of course, go into detail on how far we are. I will inform the Chamber in six months' time whether things are going as planned and whether urgent cases have been taken into account where necessary. I will do so in general terms in six months' time, so that the Chamber will not be surprised in a year's time that there will be another delay. Of course, that's the concern. With that remark, I can also leave the third motion to the Court's judgement.
