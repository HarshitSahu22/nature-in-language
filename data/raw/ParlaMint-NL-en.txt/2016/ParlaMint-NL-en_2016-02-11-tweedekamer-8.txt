ParlaMint-NL_2016-02-11-tweedekamer-8.u1	Mr. Chairman. Scientists determine the fishing pressure that allows you to achieve the maximum sustainable quantity from the sea. That's the FMSY. On that basis, quotas are agreed. A range has now been proposed in the Council to implement a mixed fishing plan. The negotiations are now examining whether this range can also be above the established FMSY. While there is already a scientific basis, the Council is now trying to extend the standard. It is no longer the science that determines how much can be caught without endangering populations, but it is the interests of the fishing industry that determine it. That's why I'm filing the next motion. ParlaMint-NL_2016-02-11-tweedekamer-8.u5	Mr. Chairman. Three motions have been tabled for discussion in the Agriculture Council next Monday. In the motion in part No. 894 Mr Wassenberg and Mr Koşer Kaya refer to the maximum sustainable yield, the question of what is a responsible quantity to catch from a particular species of fish. The cabinet's position is clear. We take the scientific advice as our starting point. That's the basis for us. In his motion, Mr Wassenberg seems to point out the repeated discussion on whether the MSY should be determined at one point or whether it should be based on a range. Such a range does more justice to the way in which fishing is carried out, particularly in mixed fisheries, in order to be able to reach responsible quantities. That is why the position of the Council and the Dutch cabinet is that it is justified to assume the range as long as we follow the scientific advice. The latter is an important starting point for us. This is a way of doing more justice to the reality of fishing. That's why this motion is detachment. In the motion in part No. Mr Koşer Kaya and Mr Dikkers asked the government not to agree to the expiry of the European Commission's right of withdrawal. That's not exactly how it is. It is the other way round: the European Commission is proposing to grant itself a right of withdrawal. This competence now rests with the Member States. If the proposers say that the Member States will have to comply with the agreement that they themselves exercise sufficient control over compliance with the agreements, I understand that commitment, but I do not share the consequence. So I advise against this motion. However, I would like to refer to the motion that I shall now deal with. The motion in part No. 896 of Mr Dikkers, Mr Koşer Kaya and Mr Van Gerven are somewhat premature, because the recommendations of the European Court of Auditors need to be taken into account. I have indicated, in general consultation we have just had, that we share and support the recommendations of the European Court of Auditors, and that the Commission wants to accept all these recommendations. All this still needs to be implemented. It is therefore a little premature to say that if what everyone wants to do is not to happen, then in general Brussels should not be allowed to accept new negotiating mandates. Since it is the cabinet's commitment to adopt the recommendations, I can leave the vote on the motion to the Chamber, because the motion is in fact policy support.
