ParlaMint-NL_2016-03-22-tweedekamer-3.u1	Mr. Chairman. The current of news reports and the penetrating images that have been reaching us from Belgium all day are shocking and sad. So many innocent people suddenly ripped away from life; so many wounded and so many affected families and families. Deeds like this are inhumane. We are now thinking primarily of the victims and their families. They are in our hearts. The grief, the loss and the uncertainty are almost impossible to imagine; yet completely unreal actually. What I want to say here in the heart of our democracy is that we stand beside them and mourn with them. Because the Netherlands and Belgium share more than one border and more than just a friendship. We now feel that special band extra strong. We are deeply connected with you, our friends in Belgium, and we hold on to each other well. Involuntarily, we are currently reminiscent of previous attacks in Brussels, Madrid, Istanbul, London, Paris, Ankara and Copenhagen. We need to be real: our way of life is under attack. That is precisely why it is so important that democratic societies stand firm for the values of freedom, civilisation and openness. We need to be vigilant and sharp, and we are taking additional measures if necessary. But the fear may and will not govern us. Our society, our way of life, is too strong for that. Madam President, Brussels, Belgium and the whole of Europe have been heartbroken today. So soon after the attacks, we are silent and stand by the victims and relatives; vigilant and determined to protect everything that is good, what we believe in and what we stand for.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-03-22-tweedekamer-3.u2	I thank the Prime Minister for coming to the Chamber.
