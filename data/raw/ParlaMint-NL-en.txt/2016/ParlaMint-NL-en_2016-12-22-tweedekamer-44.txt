I come to the second motion by Mr Hoogland and Mrs Van Veldhoven, on item No. 222. There are three elements to be addressed: firstly, a scan of white spots in the loading infrastructure and its completion; secondly, a covenant with local authorities on procurement; and thirdly, a request for harmonisation at European level. I'm gonna say something about those white spots first. At the moment there are about 100,000 cars in the Netherlands that can drive electric in one way or another, a small part or a longer part. We have 85,000 charging points. That is why we are one of the countries in the world with the largest load pole density. Maybe it's even the biggest one with us. In the Netherlands so many people live close together and we have so many loading posts, so in our country it is already filled in for a very large part. Of course, we work with the market model, because it best meets needs. Loading infrastructure is only set up on the basis of the need for it, and not on the basis of white spots. There may be a white spot somewhere, but it may also be that there is no need for it, so there is no need to set up a loading pole. So I do not think this appeal in the motion makes any sense. The second point is the agreement with the local authorities. The National Knowledge Platform Load Infrastructure was established by the Reich in 2014. It plays a supporting role in the regional development of loading infrastructure. It is already working with the knowledge institutions to standardise the loading infrastructure. A broad-based basic set of agreements for loading posts is available via the NKL knowledge desk. There is also a practical starting document for future calls for tenders and for permits, so we have already prepared and set up all of this, together with the other governments, the National Knowledge Platform Load Infrastructure and also our own knowledge centre for municipalities for infrastructure. Therefore, I do not think that there is a need for a covenant in which all this is repeated. The third point in the motion concerns the standardisation of loading infrastructure. I am already in the process of implementing the directive in this area. At the beginning of next year I will come to the House of Representatives. Furthermore, I am working with our European partners in the Sustainable Transport Forum to standardise that charging infrastructure. I am even going one step further: I am also working on the standardisation of payment. This means that people with a pass for a charging station or charging point in the Netherlands, can also go abroad. I recently made agreements on this in North Rhine-Westphalia. So I am already working on all three points in this motion, or they have no added value. For that reason, I advise against this motion. In the third motion, which in part No. 223, members Van Veldhoven and Hoogland argue that it should be transparent for the critical consumer what the costs are when loading at a charging station. These members ask the government to require such transparency. I sympathise with this motion because I believe that transparency is important. I did inform the Chamber about this recently. I have said that it is of course up to the market to make prices clear and that, for our part, we will take responsibility for developing ICT protocols through the NKL, which I have just mentioned, so that all this can be easily understood. I think we need a supplement. It must be very simple: someone goes to a loading post and if he uses it, he must be able to see what it costs. I think such a thing can have real added value. If, in drawing up what is mentioned in this motion, I am given some room to consider how best to fill it in, whether in consultation or not, and if, in addition, I can consider whether this can be required, I have no objection to this motion. In that case, I shall leave this motion to the Chamber.
