ParlaMint-NL_2016-12-15-tweedekamer-9.u6	Mr. Chairman. I'll walk by the motions. In his motion in part No. 61 Mr Krol asked the government to continue to pay special attention to older people and their specific needs in its approach to low literacy in the broad sense. I advise against this motion because I want to pay attention to the entire group of low-lying people. In the summer of 2017, the results of a number of studies on the scope and impact of policy in the field of low literacy will be published. I am prepared to consider, on the basis of these investigations, whether there is a need to change or change the policy. One of these studies deals with the scope and use of educational money by municipalities. Another research is about the impact of the policy on individual students. Another research is about the midterm evaluation of the entire program Tel along with Taal. In particular, the latter research is crucial. If it turns out that certain groups do not receive sufficient attention, that would be the argument for adjusting the policy at that time. In the motion in part No. 61 and the motion in part No. 62 the government is also invited to make additional funds available. First of all, I do not have enough arguments to make an extra budget special for this group. Secondly, I do not know where I should get that extra budget, because that would be at the expense of something else. I'm always willing, if I have to, to adjust the policy, but right now I'm not going to do that, bearing in mind the data we have now. I am prepared to discuss this further, on the basis of the research results. I therefore advise against the motion in part No. 61 and the motion in part No. 62. In the Asante/Van Meenen motion to item No. 63 the government is invited, in consultation with the VNG, to make it clear at the beginning of 2017 how it can be realised that at least one third of the low-literate students have been reached with language courses by 2022. Let me say first of all that I share the sense of urgency of the petitioners of the motion. With the program Tel along with Language I took the first steps. No sooner did we reach as many participants as with this program: there were 18,000 in the first nine months of this year. That is also beyond our expectations. I'm very happy about this. If we continue in this way, we can increase our ambitions. The more people we reach, the better it's me. So I see opportunities, also in cooperation with municipalities. We know that the municipalities that are supported for the longest time with an integrated approach to low literacy reach most participants and achieve the best results. So permanent wagering pays off. So I understand the ambition of the proposers. In addition, next year the compulsory use of the education budget will end in the case of roc's, allowing municipalities to purchase a more diverse course offering, which is better suited to the different target groups. However, Mr Krol's motions also apply to this motion. I would remind you once again of the three studies whose results are yet to be published. Regionplan researches the scope and use of educational money by municipalities. The University of Maastricht is researching the impact of policies on individual students. Finally, there is the mid-term evaluation of the entire program Tel along with Language. All these research results will be published in the summer of 2017, between June and August or September. I would like to take the results of these investigations with me and have the conversation with the VNG. If I can understand the motion in such a way that "beginning 2017" is easy, so that in the summer I can check whether the policy adjustment is necessary on the basis of the research results and the results that are then known, I leave the assessment of this motion to the Chamber. In the motion in part No. 64 member Straus invites the Government to come forward with a proposal before 1 May 2017 on how to reduce the number of pupils entering the primary school as functional illiterate. We're talking about a functional illiterate level 1F. A recent report on school year 2015-1016 shows that 98% of boys and 99% of girls at the end of group 8 for reading reach the required reference level. The number of children leaving primary school as functional illiterate is therefore very limited. If children do not have that level, it often also has to do with cognitive limitations. So the question is whether we can achieve 100%. However, I think that 98% and 99% are extremely good results. I am also saying here that I am always willing to look at policy adjustment, but I really wonder if this is the most worrying problem we have to address, even if I look at what is still to be improved. I therefore advise against the motion in part No. 64 as it is now, but I would like to add that if future measurements show that we are not doing well with the results we are achieving or that more is needed, then of course I am prepared to adjust the policy. In this case, I would also like to include the internationally comparative PIRLS research. That's an investigation into reading skills among primary school students. I therefore advise against the motion in this form.
