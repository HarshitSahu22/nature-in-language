During the general consultation on pensions, the CDA raised the question of the IORP II Directive, the new Directive on pension funds, which makes it easier for pension assets to leave the Netherlands. This amounts to more than EUR 1,300 billion of pension assets. It is about the pension of all Dutch people. This is twice as much money as we make in the Netherlands each year. It is by far the largest savings bank in the entire European continent. The House of Representatives issued a yellow card against this draft directive. The CDA asked to disagree with that directive. In the end, the House of Representatives made a parliamentary reservation. This was lifted in June 2014 under the agreement that the government would inform the Chamber more frequently each quarter and if necessary, and that the House of Representatives could receive a final text in due time, together with an appreciation of the government before the government agreed to any compromise that would be reached in the trialogue phase. Despite repeated questions from the CDA Group, little has come of the first. There has been only one formal report on IORP in two years. However, six rounds of negotiations appear to have taken place. Can the government explain why it takes agreements with Parliament so little seriously? Now the Chamber has only been informed orally confidentially about the progress. That is rather unsatisfactory, especially since this was an official briefing. We didn't get an insight into a number of cases during that briefing. That is why, first of all, we would like to hear a clear explanation of where we are now at all points in the negotiations. We also ask for the texts that are now before us. Secondly, my group is asking the government to give us an overview of the state of play. Is there an agreement now, or is there no agreement on the final text? Thirdly, the CDA insists on receiving the final texts with an appreciation and having the opportunity to debate them before the government takes a position in Coreper or in the European Council. That is why we would also like to receive an indicative timeline for the discussion, especially if the government is committed to adopting this directive by 30 June, as the Minister for Finance announced on 15 June. Finally, my group asks the government, the CDA's parliamentary questions on this subject, to answer them carefully and before next Thursday at 12 noon.
