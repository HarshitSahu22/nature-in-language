ParlaMint-NL_2016-11-09-tweedekamer-6.u2	Mr. Chairman. The PVV believes that the Netherlands should supply arms exclusively to decent and democratic constitutional states. Last year's democracy index of The Economist shows that the first 48 most democratic countries are none of the OIC countries (Organisation of the Islamic Conference). Only in place 49 Indonesia appears. That says enough about the democratic content in most Islamic countries. In addition, there is a high risk that weapons will fall into the hands of fundamentalists in these countries. The situation in many of these countries is also unstable. This is also the case in some countries, as we see in Turkey. In other countries, instability is clearly visible in the longer term. Let me give you just one example: Egypt. The Cairo Declaration of Human Rights, signed by the Member States of the Organisation of the Islamic Conference, states that human rights are subject to Sharia. None of that looks good, and that's why I'm filing the next motion. ParlaMint-NL_2016-11-09-tweedekamer-6.u3	Mr. Chairman. I thank Mr De Roon for his motion. Export control on weapons and weapons systems, but also on dual-use goods, is very strict. It is subject to all kinds of review criteria. That is also a good thing. The Netherlands is not only a very careful test of these criteria but is also a very active player in the gremia in which these criteria are defined, adjusted and sometimes increased. This means that we apply a very strict test for each individual licence application. We do so on a case-by-case approach, which means as much as that we consider both the licence application and the sender and the final destination, so where it ends up. Experience has shown that, in practice, we are strictly enforcing the EU's eight criteria. That's why I'd like to dissuade the motion. The Netherlands has strict policies. In my opinion, the case-by-case review is much more purer and the problem is much broader than Mr De Roon describes. I believe that with the case-by-case review we can do a much wider review.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-11-09-tweedekamer-6.u4	Still, it went wrong. Islamic spring has become an Islamic nightmare. It has already killed hundreds of thousands of people, in different countries. Islam is just a big disaster in the world. The risks are very high. What happens now can happen in any Islamic country. There's tension everywhere. My proposal would be: no more weapons to Islamic countries. Why doesn't the Minister agree to that? This will prevent all risks in that region.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-11-09-tweedekamer-6.u5	Quite apart from the fact that we could have a debate on why Mr De Roon is so qualified and separate to this group of countries, I do not agree that we should confine ourselves to strict control over a particular group of countries. I want strict control of all countries except the allies. We have also talked about this. This means that, in Dutch practice, which we are also tightening up in the light of current events, we are asking for advice from the security services, checking with other EU countries and having in broad terms the strict application of the criteria as Mr De Roon is referring to.
