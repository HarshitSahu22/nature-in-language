ParlaMint-NL_2016-07-07-tweedekamer-48.u6	Mr. Chairman. I haven't received the fourth motion yet, but I'm sure it'll follow. So I guess we can get started. First of all, I will deal with the motion in part No. 410 over the dollar rate. We've talked a lot about this. The General Court has also expressed its views on this matter. In a discussion with the Court of Auditors, I said that I understood the request for scenarios. However, we must be careful that these will not be based on many arbitrary assumptions, for example on the development of exchange rates, cost developments and the possible release of risk reservations. After all, it produces all kinds of uncertain and therefore few useful results and then we get discussions like we had in the purchase decision. At that time there was a quantity of money left over by the dollar rate; now we have a certain deficit. Then we were all fantasizing about what we would do with the remaining budget; now we are all concerned about the possible deficits. It is not for nothing that I, together with Mr Dijsselbloem, had to send a note to your Chamber last Friday: a letter; I am sorry, we call it a note, but it is just a whole letter about the Study Group on Budgets. The cabinet is very aware of the exchange rate changes and their impact on the defence budget and, above all, on planning stability. We have already indicated that we will return to your Chamber, if I am keen on the General Financial Reviews. We will, of course, always pay attention to currency exchange issues in the progress reports, and we will make the impact of the different rates on the cost estimates for members clear. I would therefore strongly advise against this motion. I'll come to the motion in part No. 411 on hearing damage in children. I'm glad Mrs. Eijsink said we have a Steering Group sound. It works very well, and in it there is close cooperation between the various parties involved. Mrs Eijsink was so attentive to complimenting Defence, but also all residents and the organisations involved. So let me say to her the following: all this arose on the basis of a motion by Mrs Eijsink. Since that motion was tabled, cooperation has been given a tremendous boost and all noses are moving in the same direction. The steering committee will continue to play a crucial role and be given every opportunity to address any issue at random. It is therefore far too far for me to talk here about monitoring hearing damage in children. Let that steering committee do its job and give him the space he needs. I am sure that this will bring us to the best possible noise reduction measures that we could not have invented with each other in the Binnenhof. This motion, too, would therefore be dissuaded. The motion in part No. 412 by Mr De Roon is about the block buy. It's like I don't want to embrace any motion. That is by no means my intention. However, a block buy aims at lower unit prices. Left or right, we all benefit from lower prices. So I'm also going to dissuade this motion. Finally, the motion in part No. 413. Here too: give it space. We have already talked about standards for aviation, in the sense that it is an average and that you have to operate within the sound contours anyway. So this motion, too, I regret to say, will have to be discouraged.
