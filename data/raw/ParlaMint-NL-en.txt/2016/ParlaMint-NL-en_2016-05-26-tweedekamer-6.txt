ParlaMint-NL_2016-05-26-tweedekamer-6.u1	I would like to welcome the Minister for Infrastructure and the Environment. I understand that there is one speaker, and that is Mr Van Helvert. Mr Van Helvert, you will have two minutes for your speech, including the tabling of motions.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-05-26-tweedekamer-6.u2	Mr. Chairman. It is nice that everyone could make time to deal with this, because it is an important subject. We have talked about competition and alleged unfair competition from the Gulf States in the field of aviation. A firm competition is good. You have to be able to handle that, but there's got to be a fair playing field. The EU and the Gulf States are negotiating a treaty to put an end to unfair competition. The Secretary of State has said in the past that the Netherlands does not grant new landing rights to carriers from the Gulf States as long as this treaty is being negotiated. That is the last statement the Secretary of State has made on this matter. Now that negotiation is taking place. After the February 2015 Dutch Parliamentary Motion, the cabinet asked the European Commission to investigate the massive subsidies granted by the Gulf States. According to an American investigation, 39 billion euros have been granted to Gulf States. So it's all not clear yet. In short, until it has been clarified how the Gulf States operate unfair competition, they should not be granted fifth-freedom rights for passengers and cargo. Can the Minister confirm that the Netherlands will use this, as will Germany and France? Is that still true? I'll file the next motion. [[Motion]] [[The Chamber,]] [[heard the debate,]] [[noting that there are signs of unfair competition in Europe by states and airlines from outside Europe;]] [[noting that research has not yet been completed while a level playing field is necessary;]] [[calls on the Government to promote the inclusion in the negotiating mandates for the Gulf States of as few fifth-freedom rights as possible for passengers and cargo as possible and only on condition that firm agreements are also made on fair competition and the fight against state aid,]] [[and goes to the order of the day.]] [[This motion was tabled by Mr Van Helvert. ParlaMint-NL_2016-05-26-tweedekamer-6.u3	Mr. Chairman. I can answer directly because we have had a recent debate on this. In the interests of the negotiations, I cannot go into the specific terms of reference, including the rights of freedom. They are confidential; Mr Van Helvert knows that. However, our commitment is to keep market access for the United Arab Emirates and Qatar as limited as possible in exchange for the strongest possible provisions on fair competition. I think that's important. So that's access in exchange for fair competition. The Netherlands, of course, has a double role. As President, the Netherlands has the task of achieving a good compromise between the 28 Member States, but we will certainly not forget our own interests. If I may read the motion in such a way that I will continue to argue for limited market access in exchange for strong provisions on fair competition, then I see the motion as supporting my policy.
