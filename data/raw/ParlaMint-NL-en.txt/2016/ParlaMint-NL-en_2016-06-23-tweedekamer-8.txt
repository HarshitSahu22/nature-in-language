ParlaMint-NL_2016-06-23-tweedekamer-8.u9	Mr. Chairman. In the motion in part No. 40 member Lucas is asked to examine whether the term "costed educational institutions" could be replaced by "costed educational activities." I advise against this motion. Mr Kamp recently reviewed the Market and Government Act. The reaction was sent to the Chamber on 3 June. The policy response of the Minister for Economic Affairs shows that there is sufficient sector-specific regulation applicable to all educational institutions. I therefore believe that the motion is neither necessary nor undesirable. Following the inspection report "Private activities of funded educational institutions" and my policy response of 13 August 2015, I am investigating a clarification and/or tightening of the sector-specific rules. I will discuss the findings in consultation with all relevant educational sectors, including non-cost education. However, we do not need to change this with an investigation in relation to the Market and Government Act. If there were no such exception, only a very limited part of the educational institutions would be covered by the Market and Government Act. After Mr Kamp's good evaluation, I think it is wise to follow his line. This line applies not only to education but also to all government authorities, i.e. all public institutions, including education.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-06-23-tweedekamer-8.u10	The same assessment also shows that additional efforts should be made to prevent market disruption. This is explicitly mentioned in the evaluation. This would be a very simple but very nice opportunity to do so. We can then say that the activities financed are still excluded, but the private activities of the same institutions are no longer excluded. In this way, I believe you will ensure that attention is still paid to the task, but that the whole institution is no longer excluded if the same institution also carries out private activities.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-06-23-tweedekamer-8.u11	That is why I am investigating clarification and tightening up of sector-specific rules. It is then very doubtful whether this is the most desirable way, because it does throw away the starting point of the Market and Government Act. The same applies to municipalities and provinces. They're basically excluded. I attach great importance to that clarity. So I don't want to interfere with that either. That's why I keep dissuading the motion.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-06-23-tweedekamer-8.u12	I think it would be a good thing if the Minister were to look at the possibilities, she would take this opportunity because it is a very elegant solution. So I'm maintaining my motion.
ParlaMint-NL_2016-06-23-tweedekamer-8.u13	Mrs Lucas' second motion asks the government to explicitly invest in the ACM in the enhanced note Brightness on the supervision of market effects of activities by funded educational institutions. I advise against this motion because there is already independent supervision by the ACM and the Education Inspectorate. If an educational institution with non-cost activities distorts the market and thus infringes the Competition Act, then the ACM can intervene. Action can then be taken either on its own initiative or in response to complaints. I know that's what the ACM does in education. The Education Inspectorate oversees the public tasks for which institutions receive funding and these tasks are not covered by the Competition Act but by the sectoral rules. Here too, the evaluation of the Market and Government Act confirms this position of education. I do not wish to interfere with the clarity of where the market has to work and where the inspection has to do its work, because I fear that otherwise it will lead to shadowiness and grey areas, which I would like to avoid at all times.
