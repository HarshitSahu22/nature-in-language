ParlaMint-NL_2019-02-20-tweedekamer-15.u2	Mr. Chairman. We've been working on transparency for two years now and we're making some progress, but it's still not good in the EU. That is not just what the parliaments say, but also the European Ombudsman, the European Court of Justice and the European Parliament. I want to make two points in this VAO. The EU rightly calls into question the rule of law of Member States when freedoms are violated. Indeed, transparency is an important guarantee of freedoms. If a citizen cannot follow the legislative process, then we have a problem. That is why I want it to be above all doubt within the Union. I also think that the Union should do this: practice what you preach. That is to say, open and transparent, not secret documents and clarity about who controls. Transparency International writes strikingly about the euro group: what exactly is the euro group, what decisions it makes if there are.................................................................................................................................................................................................. It is this lack of clarity that has a profound impact on accountability and therefore its legitimacy. The eurogroup is probably one of the least transparent pieces of the European Union. In fact, strictly speaking, it is not even part of the European Union, because it does not appear in the Treaties. The eurogroup has no internal rules. Parliaments cannot control exactly what is happening and therefore the Eurogroup has not laid down its procedures either. Mr. Chairman. With these matters in mind, I would like to table a motion asking for an investigation by the Venice Commission. That's a figure we haven't had yet, and of course I like it.
