ParlaMint-NL_2019-07-04-tweedekamer-44.u12	Mr. Chairman. First, the motion in part No. 281 by Mr Voordewind, Mr Groothuizen, Mrs Kuik, Mr Alkaya and Mrs Diks. I understand, like everyone else, the poignant suffering, also on Lesbos and Samos. I also know that a huge amount of money, especially through the EU, went to the Greek Government for these islands. The big problem isn't money, it's capacity and management. There is also an intra-European political debate behind it. I really want this to be said formally: it is not about money at all. What I do recognise in the motion is that we can continue to insist, both within the EU and at the United Nations. This is particularly true within the EU, Mrs Broekers-Knol. At the UN, we can urge to see how reception can be improved, what the UN organisations can do and where relevant channeling can take place through NGOs, for example, to speed up aid even more. But that's a management issue. I would also like to make the point that we always look at relevance, capacity and who can deliver what. When it comes to NGOs, we don't necessarily focus on Dutch NGOs. The best NGO must be deployed when it comes to the needs of the people. I shall leave this motion to the House's judgement, but I would like to stress that the many funds that have gone to Greece must be spent better and more quickly. Then the motion to part No. 282 by Mr Bouali, Mrs Kuik, Mr Voordewind, Mr Alkaya, Mrs Diks, Mrs Van den Hul, Mr Stoffer and Mrs Ouwehand. I am happy to give this motion to the Court of Justice, provided that I can interpret the motion in such a way that aid, decisions on aid and the forms of aid take place only on the basis of the humanitarian imperative. A possible additional Dutch contribution has therefore not yet been decided by means of this motion, but it simply takes place on the basis of an assessment of the needs, the number of people affected, funds already made available and, of course, the expendable within the emergency budget. On the basis of this remark, I give this motion judgment Chamber.
ParlaMint-NL_2019-07-04-tweedekamer-44.u13	I note that Mr Bouali nods; so he agrees. Then the motion to part No. 283.
ParlaMint-NL_2019-07-04-tweedekamer-44.u14	Then the motion to part No. 283. It identifies a number of themes: the group of migrants and chronic diseases. These are usually excluded from the emergency aid package, because the costs and size are often too high. These costs are always considered to be borne by the host country, as happens in Lebanon and Jordan, for example, but it remains very difficult. It is asked to investigate this, in consultation with the NGOs and the Colombian government. That really does not fall under the emergency aid budget. That is poignant, but this is the reality. On that basis, I advise against this motion. Then the motion to part No. 284, by Mrs Diks. I wonder why we need to talk about this. Vulnerability is always included in emergency aid, as well as in our development cooperation programme. Vulnerable people can be people who live with disabilities. I have also recently written a letter to the Chamber about this, so I do not see the urgency of this motion, because it is work in progress, but on this basis I leave the judgment to the Chamber.
