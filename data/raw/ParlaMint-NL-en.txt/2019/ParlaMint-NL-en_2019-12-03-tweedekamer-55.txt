ParlaMint-NL_2019-12-03-tweedekamer-55.u12	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. First of all, I respond to Mrs Özütok's motion on item No. 12, in which the government is invited, together with the municipalities and the Election Board, to arrive at a clear way of communicating the results and to ensure that this unambiguous manner of disclosure is clearly communicated to the citizens. I see this proposal, this motion, as a support for policy, which aims at greater transparency in the process of establishing the election result. Since 1 January 2019, municipalities have already been obliged by law to publish records of their polling offices on their website. This did not go well in the elections in 2019. Not all municipalities have acted in accordance with the law, but I expect this to improve quickly. Together with the VNG, we will actively highlight the importance of a clear presentation of the results. Furthermore, in the second quarter of next year, your Chamber may expect a bill that requires municipalities to also publish the files with results generated by the use of digital tools in the result calculation. I am also working on a central digital location that provides access to all municipal results. For example, interested parties do not need to search the websites of all individual municipalities, which is often, I can say from experience, an actual process. So I can leave the verdict on this motion to the Chamber.

ParlaMint-NL_2019-12-03-tweedekamer-55.u16	Then I come to the motion on part No. 14 by Mrs Den Boer, in which the government is asked to investigate how feasible and practicable it is to make it possible to vote for voters in the Lower House and European elections anywhere in the Netherlands, without having to apply to another municipality. Of course, that's the ultimate service, but there's a bit of a way to figure it out. At the moment, a voter in the Netherlands can, as a result of a previous legislative amendment from 2010, go to any voting room in his municipality. There's a lot of them already. It is already quite extraordinary that this can be done. You don't see that much, not even in the countries around us. But these polling stations have a negative list, invalid voting cards from the register, with numbers of voting cards that cannot be voted on. If you were to vote nationwide without a voter pass, municipalities would have to exchange the numbers of all invalid voting cards the day before the election and distribute them to all polling stations. This is an administratively complex but also error-prone operation, which also creates an irresistible large register for the polling office. So I must discourage this motion.
