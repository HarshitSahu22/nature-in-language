ParlaMint-NL_2017-07-06-tweedekamer-40.u5	Mr. Chairman. In the debate, we have paid a great deal of attention to how we work together to ensure that the purchase process of health insurers and healthcare providers is carried out in a way that is sufficiently contracted within the budgets. I have told you that the Dutch Healthcare Authority will act as an activist, so that the care purchase takes place. This motion reflects what I mentioned in the debate. On one point, as far as I am concerned, the motion goes too far. The government must ensure that sufficient purchases are made so that waiting lists and patient stops are prevented. Patients stoppages can sometimes not be prevented, because one provider has a budget ceiling, while the other provider has sufficient space. I also think that we have exchanged enough in the debate that we agree that there is a sufficient supply of care and that we have made agreements with the Healthcare Authority, in particular with regard to case management dementia, including additional transparency arrangements. On the one hand, this motion reflects exactly what we have said, but on the other hand, we are asking the government for something that we cannot guarantee. That's why I'm dissuading the motion. Then it's not about the content. It's about the content. In the motion in part No. 839 the government is asked to provide insight into the obligation to transmit. The motion contains a number of statements about the way the contract is put together, with budget ceilings. Also, it actually says whose fault it is. I don't think that's a good idea. The debate has exchanged the view that there are contractual arrangements between health insurers and healthcare providers and that sometimes the question of what to do in the event of budget overrun and budget ceilings is part of those contractual agreements. This may lead to agreements being made on the consequences of the contract. This motion favours either one party or the other in advance in the contracting process. That's why I'm dissuading the motion. In the motion in part No. 840 Ms Ellemeet asked the government to actively involve and inform the profession and healthcare training on the development of e-health. I consider this motion to support policy and leave it to the Chamber.
