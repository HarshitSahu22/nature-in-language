ParlaMint-NL_2017-05-09-tweedekamer-17.u5	Mr. Chairman. Five motions have been tabled today on the evaluation of the Natural Law. As Mr De Groot said, this took place mainly on the occasion of Mr Van Veldhoven's motion (33400-XIII, No. 90) and focused in particular on golf courses. That is also the subject of the first motion. The Chamber has seen that, on the basis of the evaluation, the Cabinet has decided to tighten up a number of rules on the ranking of golf courses, in order to ensure that there is indeed a robust nature on the golf courses, so that the tax, financial support provided does not benefit the golf courses, but actually the nature on the golf courses. In his motion in part No. Mr De Groot asked the government to indicate how the NSW ranking could be removed in the long term for existing solitaire golf courses which did not keep the desired quality. This motion seems superfluous to me. If the desired quality is not achieved or is maintained, this must also be demonstrated by checks, which will be carried out on the basis of these tightening-up measures, the NSW classification will be abolished in any case. I therefore reject this motion because it is unnecessary in my opinion. In the motion-De Groot in part no. 102 The government is invited to investigate how to regulate the opening up of estates to cyclists without unreasonable cost increases in managers or obstacles for hikers. I'll leave the verdict on this motion to the Chamber. If the Chamber needs it, I'm willing to have that investigation carried out. In the motion-De Groot in part no. 103 the Government is invited to specify in the Nature Conservation Act or underlying regulation the coordination of controls, including feedback to ministers. I myself have no indication that there are problems in this area. I would point out that directors are naturally responsible, so I would advise against this motion. In the Geurts/Lodders motion in part No. 104 the government is invited to seek solutions for customisation for economic carriers on estates in consultation with stakeholders. Although I do not see any problem with this customization for economic carriers on estates, I am always prepared to consult with stakeholders and, if problems are experienced, to examine whether they can be removed. I shall therefore leave this motion to the Chamber. I would point out here that the more you regulate, the more obstacles there are. Customisation can therefore be counterproductive in this case. However, I do have the willingness to consult. In the Lodders/Geurts motion in part No. 105 the government is asked to consider a minimum area of half a hectare and to release the minimum size of 30 metres. These are natural beauty on golf courses. That motion is contrary to the request made by the Chamber to ensure at golf courses that nature on golf courses, supported by the Natural Law, is robust enough; it must be serious nature. We shouldn't be subsidising golf courses on a detour. We do not do that with, for example, complexes where football is played. Because of the objectives of the NSW we have decided that nature on the estates must consist of robust units. The limits that have been drawn do not fall from the sky. They are the same limits as those used in other environmental regulations. For example, the limit of half a hectare is based on the threshold set by provinces under nature subsidies. The minimum width of 30 metres is an ecological criterion used in transition environments, such as eco-passages and ecologically valuable nature lanes. So it's a generally accepted value. Since it is also contrary to what the Chamber has previously requested, I would strongly advise her against the motion.
ParlaMint-NL_2017-05-09-tweedekamer-17.u6	That, of course, requires a response. When we talk about customization, it could also be about the deletion of rules. That is what this motion calls for. We are concerned with the minimum width. My group considers the area of half a hectare to be important. So that cut is inserted into this motion. However, I do not draw from the evaluation on which this minimum width of 30 metres is based when it comes to whether or not there is natural beauty. Perhaps the Secretary of State will be able to comment on that. It doesn't just have to do with the layout of sites. You can see in narrow strips where there are different transitions, more biodiversity. So my question is: where can I find in the evaluation why the width of 30 metres is so important?
ParlaMint-NL_2017-05-09-tweedekamer-17.u7	The Chamber has previously asked to take a critical look at the ranking of golf courses under the Natural Law. I couldn't help but imagine. After all, as a government, we should not subsidise golf courses by means of a detour. What we subsidise is nature. We're subsidising actual nature. Nature is a little bit more than building a strip of trees along the entire golf course. This way you could get to half a hectare, but it has to be really robust nature that is part of the golf course. That's why I made the choice to handle the limit of 30 meters. As I have said, that is a limit that we apply elsewhere as well. This also ensures that this nature is actually nature, and that it can't just be a strip of trees along the golf course. That's what it has to be about. So I'm sticking with the dissuading of this motion.
