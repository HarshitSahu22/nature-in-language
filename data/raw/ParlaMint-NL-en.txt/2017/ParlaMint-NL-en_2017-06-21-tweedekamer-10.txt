ParlaMint-NL_2017-06-21-tweedekamer-10.u7	Mr. Chairman. Mr Hijink's two motions concern the cost of pumping money around. The latter is a somewhat general and popular expression and does not indicate exactly what it is about. In the AO, Mr Hijink mentioned at least European grants from the Structural Fund, the Cohesion Fund, the Social Fund and so on. My question to the author would therefore be how we should read this. Mr Hijink also asked in the AO what costs were generally incurred. At that time, I had all sorts of reservations about these subsidies being applied for by companies and organisations, by all kinds of social players. I didn't feel like doing a wide-ranging, unfocused investigation. Mr Hijink's motion to paragraph No. 1243 is much more precise. This concerns the implementation costs of Dutch authorities. What we can do in any case is to make that widely understandable to the government. We can also make clear the costs of the implementation organisations we have in the Netherlands for European grants. With this explanation, including the definition of the exact means involved, as I have just given it, I would like to leave the opinion on this motion to the House. In his motion in part No. 1244 asks the government to examine the amount of EU spending on the implementation costs of the EU budget. I think we can do this. I believe that the European Commission can give them access. So I'll leave the verdict on this motion to the Chamber, too. In the motion in part No. 1245 by Mrs Maeijer and Mr Van Dijck, the government is invited not to agree to the European Multiannual Financial Framework if the Netherlands will be net contributor again for the next seven years. I would like to pass on all the text above, which I do not agree with, but this is not a reasonable task. The Netherlands is one of the most prosperous countries in the European Union. The fact that we are contributing to the European Union is therefore not an objection. We want us to be just and comparable with countries that are close to us in terms of prosperity. That's what we do. So I really shouldn't recommend this motion. Mr Bouali asked a question about the deadline. The whole process of achieving multiannual financial agreements in Brussels is a huge hassle, as Mr Bouali well knows. One of the big benefits of the seven-year term is that you only have that thing once every seven years. At the same time, I understand that there is a certain logic, given the responsibility of the European Parliament, to join that five-year period of the European Parliament. So I have no objection in principle. However, I would like to point out to Mr Bouali that we often have the whole fight about who pays the bill and who gets what subsidies and money back. I believe that the debate is moving towards the European Parliament's five-year term, which in itself is a logical development on the basis of democratic legitimacy.
