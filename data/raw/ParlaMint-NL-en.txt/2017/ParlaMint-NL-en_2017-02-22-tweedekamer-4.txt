ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-22-tweedekamer-4.u3	Mr. Chairman. I'll start with the motion in part No. 130 on the limitation of mileage pricing. We have discussed this in detail in the debate. Now the situation is that, after a discussion of years on how to organise Valys transport, we have always been discussing budget overruns and the question of whether the budget was sufficient has a limit of 600 kilometres. That figure is sufficient for 99% of people. Where appropriate, a higher mileage allowance shall be granted. This is working well now. The lifting of the mileage limit and the adjustment of the rates would again lead to an open-ended arrangement, on which we have to talk each time to answer the question of whether adjustments should be made. I'm not for that. However, we have agreed that we will see if we can further optimize the system. The prices are between the prices of public transport and those of taxi transport. That is certainly reasonable. I therefore advise the Chamber not to take the motion.
ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-22-tweedekamer-4.u4	It is a pity that the Secretary of State says this, especially for those who want to make use of the mileage budget. The Secretary of State states that only 1% of people would like to use more kilometres. For that group, a whole administration would be set up that would cost about that amount. The Secretary of State is very much afraid of an open-ended scheme of EUR 1 million, while I believe that the only open-ended scheme in this country is the influx of asylum seekers.
ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-22-tweedekamer-4.u5	The budgetary impact of an open-ended scheme cannot be estimated. The observation is that everyone now has about their budget, with the exception of 1.5% of the group. And if you leave the boundaries open, it's hard for us to assess its effects. Also on the motion on item No. 131 in the debate. I have argued in favour of not looking at any part of the rates but at the whole rate. As far as public transport tariffs are concerned, substitution is also taking place in the current situation: institutions are left on one part of the tariff and they are short on the other part of the tariff. That is why it is necessary to look at the total and that is what is happening. The NZa is doing research into the wide rate. The Chamber will be informed of the results in due course. I therefore advise the Chamber not to adopt this motion. In the motion in part No. 132 we are called upon to include student transport and thus the Secretary of State for Education, Culture and Science. It is indeed my intention to look at the integrality of transport as widely as possible, including the contribution that the Ministry of Education, Culture and Science could make to this. I therefore consider the motion to support policy and leave it to the Chamber to give an opinion on it.
