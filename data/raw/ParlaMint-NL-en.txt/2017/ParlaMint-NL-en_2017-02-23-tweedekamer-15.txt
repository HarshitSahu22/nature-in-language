ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-23-tweedekamer-15.u12	Mr. Chairman. The motion in part No. 631 by Mr Bosman, on cross-border cooperation and the compulsory opening up of subsidy systems. It states that it should not be accepted. I have the same view as the view expressed in this motion. I do not think that this is yet ripe to take a positive view in Europe. I will therefore oppose it. We will look at how this further argument is from the European Commission and, if necessary, exchange it with the Chamber. I do not see any advantages in this proposal from the European Commission at the moment. I do see the logic of the line that also speaks out of this motion. I shall leave the judgment on the motion in part No. 631 please to the Chamber. In the motion in part No. 632 members of Agnes Mulder and Van Veldhoven state that the government must work again with three national goals. We have discussed this extensively in the consultations. I have indicated that the Paris agreement is about solving the global climate problem. That means we have to limit the temperature increase. The temperature increase should not exceed 2 ̊C, but it would be even better if that increase were limited to 1,5 ̊C. It is also clear that this ambition of the Paris agreement can only be achieved through a drastic reduction of greenhouse gases, in particular CO. The Netherlands believes that what is expressed globally should also be done. The Netherlands believes that there should be an ambitious European framework allowing European countries to all contribute to the transition to a low-carbon economy and thus to implement the Paris agreement properly. We believe that, in order to support all the measures that are being taken, it is absolutely necessary to have cost-effectiveness. Major investments will have to be made to ensure that we make the transition to another economy and that we emit much less CO2 in that new economy than is currently the case, namely as much as is necessary to implement the Paris Agreement. However, in order to maintain support for the very large investments, it is necessary to achieve CO2 reduction as cost-effectively as possible. The Netherlands wants to tighten up the ETS, the trading system with regard to CO. This has to happen in Europe, but unfortunately, at present, the system does not provide sufficient incentives for a gradual reduction of CO emissions in the Netherlands. That is why the cabinet comes up with additional national policies, at the top of the ETS, to ensure that we go in a straight line towards the desired situation in the Netherlands. So we come up with additional policies aimed at even more CO reduction. This will require a very strong commitment, in particular and mainly in the areas of energy saving and renewable energy. Therefore, the Chamber and I do not disagree on the view that there is a great deal to be done about energy saving and renewable energy. We just need to find the optimal mix for that. If you set goals in terms of energy saving and renewable energy, you make it harder to find the optimal mix. You make it harder on yourself, you spend too much money. That money must ultimately be raised by the citizens. That's very unwelcome. The cabinet discussed this and adopted the Energy Agenda for the period after 2023. There is no difference of opinion on the period up to 2023. Until 2023, we are carrying out what is stated in the coalition agreement and in the energy agreement. This means that, until then, three objectives will be worked out. The cabinet believes that after 2023, we should focus on CO reduction. This view of the cabinet is set out in the Energy Agenda for the period 2023-2050. I can't take the liberty of departing from that. We will see how the next cabinet and the next chamber will assess it. There'll be a lot of talk about that. New conclusions will also be drawn. That's up to the next cabinet. I cannot reasonably deviate from what we, as a cabinet, have set out in the longer-term energy agenda. That is why I advise against the Agnes Mulder/Van Veldhoven motion in part No. 632.
