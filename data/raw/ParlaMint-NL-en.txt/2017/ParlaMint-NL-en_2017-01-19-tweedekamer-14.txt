ParlaMint-NL_2017-01-19-tweedekamer-14.u6	Mr. Chairman. Perhaps I could start by addressing two specific questions that have been raised. Mr Van Gerven asked about the aftercare allowance. Aftercare is indeed very important, I agree. We have also exchanged this with each other in the debate. The Dutch Healthcare Authority has included aftercare in the overhead component of the rate for the district care. In addition, the question is whether the fare is adequate is part of the research that the Dutch Healthcare Authority is currently doing on palliative care. My proposal to Mr Van Gerven would be that I should include this point in the letter on palliative care that will come before the summer. So the Chamber knows what the latest state of affairs is. Mrs Volp asked a specific question about the signal that false reports are being made at Safe Home. I've already indicated that I'm taking that signal very seriously. The Intensive Child Care Interest Association has indeed contacted me about this. As Mrs Volp said, consultations have taken place with the Safe Home Network and the Interest Association. We're still in the middle of this meeting. Based on anonymized casusism, we look at exactly what is going on. What improvements are being made? In particular, the consultation also showed that parents may be left unsatisfied if child abuse has not been confirmed after examination. We want to see if it is possible to offer these parents a form of aftercare. If analysis shows that false reports have been made, I would like to discuss with the inspections what role they can play in this. I propose to include this point in the progress report Violence in dependency relationships and to further inform the Chamber of the state of play. Then I'll come to the motions. In the motion in part No. 160 we are asked to launch experiments for transmural work. I would point out to Mr Van Meenen that a number of experiments are already under way in the context of child care. That means I consider this motion to be a policy support. I'll leave the judgment to the Chamber. In the motion in part No. 161 we are asked to investigate how often a child with an intensive care demand is discharged from the hospital but access is not going well, and what is the reason for that. It seems to me appropriate to see if we can see what the causes are. I take it there are specific signals that give rise to this motion. Moreover, we are now looking at the problem of demarcation, to put it that way, also in response to a request made by Mrs Bergkamp in the debate. The question is whether it is true that intensive child care would work better if we chose a different classification. We have agreed with parents and parent organisations to look very closely at whether a new classification will lead to better results in 2017. I can imagine taking this with me. I have already promised the Chamber to send a letter on this, so I can imagine that Mr Van Meenen is considering holding this motion until we have sent the promised letter to the Chamber. The motion in part No. 162 is about the minute registration. The government doesn't file minutes. If it takes place, parties have agreed to it themselves. I myself am in favour of a simple one-rate system. I would like to assure Mr Van Gerven that I will address the parties to this matter wherever it occurs. We can't lift something we're not imposing. If that is done, it is something of parties. I reject the motion, but say to Mr Van Gerven that I will address the parties where it occurs.
