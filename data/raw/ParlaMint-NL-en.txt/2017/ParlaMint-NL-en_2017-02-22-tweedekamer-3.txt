ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-22-tweedekamer-3.u1	Good morning. I would like to welcome colleagues in the Chamber, the people in the public gallery and those who follow the sitting by other means. I also welcome the Secretary of State of VWS. There are currently three VAOs planned with him, and we begin with the VAO Elder Abuse. I call Mrs van der Burg on behalf of the VVD Group.
ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-22-tweedekamer-3.u2	Mr. Chairman. We all want our elders and our parents and grandparents to have a nice and safe old age. Unfortunately, this is sometimes not the case, and often parents or older people are abused or financially exploited from their own immediate environment. It is therefore very important for the elderly, before they can no longer do so, to think about who they give the confidence to make important decisions for them, if they can no longer do so themselves. They can capture that in advance in a life testament. However, this must also be known when a judge appoints a director, mentor or curator. That's why I'm filing the next motion. [[Motion]] [[The Chamber,]] [[heard the debate,]] [[Whereas it is very important that the wishes of the notary in a life testament be respected and known to canton judges before deciding on the appointment of a director, mentor or curator, in respect of the person or persons who may or may act on their behalf if they are no longer able to do so;]] [[noting that after more than a year of questions and commitments on this matter, it is still unclear whether canton courts actually have digital access to the Central Life Testament Register (CLTR);]] [[noting that the Expert Group on Curatele, Board of Directors and Mentoring (CBM expert group) has informed the Royal Notarial Professional Organisation (KNB) that there is no legal basis for automatic control of the CLTR in the context of the examination of petitions for the appointment of a director, mentor or curator;]] [[noting that the Expert Group also informed the KNB that standard checking of this register would be too high a workload;]] [[Considers that the established wishes regarding the person or persons who may decide on crucial confidential and/or financial matters on their behalf should, unfortunately, be respected if they themselves are no longer able to do so, and it is therefore necessary that the canton courts have digital access to the CLTR and, in their working methods, consult this register by default before deciding on the designation of a particular director, mentor or curator;]] [[Calls on the Government, with the Court of Justice and the Royal Notarial Professional Organisation, to discuss how canton judges, where appropriate, become aware of the contents of a life testament before deciding on a director, mentor or curator;]] [[invites the Government to inform the House of Representatives of the state of play before 1 June 2017,]] [[and goes to the order of the day.]] [[This motion has been proposed by Mr Van der Burg. It seems to me that their submission is sufficiently supported.]] [[She's getting No. kst-28345-175]]

ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-22-tweedekamer-3.u5	I give the floor to the Secretary of State for Health, Welfare and Sport for his opinion on the motions tabled.
ParlaMint-NL_2017-02-22-tweedekamer-3.u6	Mr. Chairman. First of all, the motion in part No. 175 by Mrs van der Burg. Justice and notarial law are indeed the responsibility of the colleague of Security and Justice. He sent a letter to the Chamber on this subject last week. It is good to say that I am also speaking on his behalf on this point. The testament to life is indeed a good instrument. It provides a need. In this way, people can express their will for the moment when they are no longer able to do so. As Mrs van der Burg also said, consultations have been held with the Royal Notarial Professional Organisation to examine, together with the Judicial Council, how consultation by the canton courts can take place. That is, as is the reasoning of the motion, to prevent a decision from being made in spite of the choice that people themselves have made. I think it is important to hear the ideas of the case-law on this before a decision is taken in a particular direction, because, as the Chamber knows, the Minister for Security and Justice does not have any direct powers in the area of justice. Justice is independent. I understand that at the end of this week the Justice Council will be discussing this issue with the canton judges and that the Minister for Security and Justice will be happy to discuss it with the Council for Justice and the Royal Notarial Professional Organisation on this issue and on what is requested in the motion. On that basis, choices can be made. I read the motion in such a way that we are encouraged to go further into it now and to agree on it. That is why I see the motion as a support for the policy. The House of Representatives will of course be informed about the outcome of the consultation.
