ParlaMint-NL_2017-03-28-tweedekamer-6.u1	Madam President. Exactly one year after the attack on Brussels airport Zaventem, another neighbour was struck in the heart last Wednesday. London mourns and we mourn. A cowardly act cost innocent victims their lives and the consequences for a large number of wounded are profound. It can't be a coincidence that Westminster has now been chosen as a target: not only as the location of one of the oldest democracies in the world, but also as a place where the whole world comes together. People of different nationalities were affected by this attack: from Great Britain, Germany, Italy and other European countries. But also people from South Korea and Australia, for example. Thus Westminster stands for everything that extremists hate and everything they try to destroy with such attacks: democracy and justice, openness and freedom. A world in which people come together peacefully and work together. It is the values for which we went to the ballot box only two weeks ago in our country. It is precisely those values that are becoming stronger as a result of these attacks. Humanity is always stronger than hate and violence. At times like this, we choose a position. At times like this, we realize what is at stake. At times like these, therefore, we express our support and assistance to a country and a people with whom we feel closely connected through friendship, a shared history and a shared tradition. That is exactly what I did last week in the direction of my colleague Theresa May. The London mayor Sadiq Khan said in response to the attack: "London is the greatest city in the world and we stand together in the face of those who see to harm us and destroy our way of life." Let our message today be that we stand alongside the British in the defence of all that is dear to us. Our hearts and our condolences go out to the victims and their next of kin.
