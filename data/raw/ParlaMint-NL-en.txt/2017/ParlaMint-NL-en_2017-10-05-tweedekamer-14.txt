ParlaMint-NL_2017-10-05-tweedekamer-14.u14	Thank you, Madam President. I apologize for the fact that I needed a moment. I had to take a look at that law in France. Let me make the motions on the items Nos. 174 and 178 together. The motion in part No. 174 notes that the Dutch Government has still not formally apologized and calls on the government to apologize formally and openly for our colonial past. I would like to point out that in 2013 the government expressed deep regret and repentance about the past of Dutch slavery. I have also said this in general consultation. Later governments, including these, have repeated this on several occasions. This cabinet and I are in the same position. I believe that this has been the most genuine way of showing human commitment and the deep sadness of the suffering that has been done to others. The cabinet chooses its own words. It is precisely by talking about formal excuses that I think we are going in a very different direction, namely: to degrade this to a formality. I would also point out that this position is no different from that in France, for example. Slavery is and will continue to be characterized as a black page in Dutch history that we do not like and want to forget. The Dutch Government is not concerned about this either. I would like to point out that this year, too, I believe, at the time we speak and tomorrow in the Tropenmuseum, there will be an extensive discussion of the history of slavery: what exactly has been the discussion and how has that happened? The suffering suffered by the Netherlands in the past has played an important role in this regard. The government supports the importance of commemorating and keeping alive the history of Dutch slavery history. In 2017, we will continue to work on human rights and the fight against racism and discrimination. I am also in conversation with the Caribbean leaders on this issue. They have made a request to the Netherlands. Then there's the situation in France. Part of the law you spoke about is that 10 May is the official anniversary of slavery and that the European slave trade is a mandatory part of history education. That is already the case in the Netherlands, and rightly so. As I said, the suffering suffered by the Netherlands in the past has been recognised by the Dutch Government and therefore a new law is not obvious. That means that I have both the motion in part no. 174 if the motion in part No. 178 wants to dissuade. In the motion in part No. 175 it is noted that there was 11.7 million for the first Twinning Facility and that 6.5 million is now estimated. Mr Kuzu is actually asking for that to be another 11.7 million and to find cover for it in the budget surplus. I would advise against that motion. For good reason, I have chosen, within the priorities of the Foreign Affairs budget, to continue the twinning facility. This is a catalyzing fund. That's always been the case. It can never be a development fund to solve the major problems that exist in Suriname society; there was no such illusion. I'd like to watch for that. However, I have promised to look more closely at a number of points which he considers important, namely the relationship between urban and rural areas and a number of aspects relating to ecology. Mr. Chairman. I'll deal with the motion on part number one. 176. I sent you a letter today. In all honesty, I must say that the Minister of SoZaWe did so on my behalf. The letter deals with the importance of the AOW deficit and explains precisely what can and cannot be done legally in so far as residents whose income from the AOW age is lower than the social minimum. I think we should pay particular attention to this. We should not discriminate, but rather give those people the attention they deserve. They can supplement income to the minimum income by means of supplementary income provision for older people. In the light of this, I would advise against this motion. Then I come to the motion on part No. 177. I would also advise against this, because the Dutch Government always looks at proportionality and the need for measures to control flights from the Caribbean and Suriname. Mr President, that brings me to the end of the answer to the questions and the assessment of the motions.
