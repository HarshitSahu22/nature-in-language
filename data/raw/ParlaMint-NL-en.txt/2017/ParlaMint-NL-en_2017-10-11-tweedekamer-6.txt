ParlaMint-NL_2017-10-11-tweedekamer-6.u4	Thank you, Mr President, and thank you to Mrs Leijten for her contribution. It shows her professionalism as a Member of Parliament that she wants to complete these proposals in this way, in a VSO. Why do I say that? This is an important subject. Yes, it is technical, and so you can think "forget it," but she is also right that at the start of the financial crisis, particularly in the United States, an unhealthy form of securitisation has contributed to the mortgage crisis and later the financial crisis. That was really different in Europe, by the way. In the case of securitisations in Europe, the same losses have not been incurred. That's a really big difference. Securitisation means that the pooling of loans that can be sold to other financial parties, which are going to win out these loans, will start to exploit. Having said that, we have to understand that what we are doing here is to make sure that there is a much better view of those risks. Then what kind of loans are they? What kind of risks are there? We're gonna make sure that the selling party continues to bear part of the risk. That too is an important guarantee to prevent bad stuff being sold or a false representation of risk being given. The new legislation further provides for the implementation of part of the new Basel framework for capital requirements, thus increasing on average the capital requirements of financial institutions, for example to banks investing in securitisations. A distinction is also made between complex securitisations on the one hand and more simple transparent or standardised securitisations on the other. There are therefore also different requirements. So all this contributes to a better understanding of these risks. Securitisation in itself is not the problem, but it is a problem if risks are not recognized, hidden, mixed, polluted, etc. So you have to make it transparent so that risks can also be priced and carried where they can be carried. That is exactly what this legislation is doing. With these arguments, I advise against both motions. The first motion, the motion in part No. 2401, calls for an impact analysis of the formation of the Capital Markets Union for small and medium-sized enterprises. Of course, small and medium-sized enterprises have two aspects to do with this. Firstly, loans to small and medium-sized enterprises may be involved. However, whether these loans are granted by one or the other financial institution is not in itself relevant. It doesn't change the position of the person who took the loan. In fact, it can promote loans to small and medium-sized enterprises if the capital market is working properly. This brings me to the second way in which small and medium-sized enterprises benefit from the capital market union. The Dutch SMEs are in fact dependent on bank financing, on bank loans, on companies across Europe. And we saw during the crisis how vulnerable that is. It is therefore important that this capital market union also leads to differentiation, to more different providers of financial products and loans for SMEs. I therefore believe that the capital market union is good for SMEs. I think that the securitisation proposals are really a great improvement over the pre-crisis years. The motion in part No. 2402 calls on the government not to explicitly agree to accelerate the Capital Markets Union as long as there is insufficient clarity as to the risks of securitisation. We have, of course, outlined these risks in the correspondence with the Chamber and I have mentioned it here once again. The financing of loans is accompanied by risk. That's not the point at all. Securitisation itself is not a problem either. What we are doing is ensuring that the risks are transparent and that they are priced and borne by those who can carry them. That's what the proposals do. That is why I would advise against the motion.
