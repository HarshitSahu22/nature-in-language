First of all, I should like to comment on the motion on part No. Mr Verhoeven, in which the government is asked to ensure that Internet service providers, in the context of combating illegal downloading, are not forced to play an active, enforcing role, for example by monitoring or prioritising Internet traffic or blocking websites. I would point out that the Court of Justice has already banned general monitoring. Mr Verhoeven knows that, of course. The Sabam/Scarlet judgment also shows that this is not possible. As far as Mr Verhoeven's motion relates to net neutrality, the position of the cabinet is that the motion is in line with existing policies. According to the Telecom Act, there is net neutrality and an internet service provider cannot be forced to impose blockages, delay or prioritize internet without judicial intervention or legal exception. That is actually a bit of the discussion that has already taken place between Mr Verhoeven and Mrs Oosenbrug in the event of interruption. This also applies to the private law enforcement of copyright. I can therefore support that part of the motion because that is the implementation of existing policies. I do not see any reason for the concern that has apparently arisen in the final stage of general consultation among the honourable Members. I hope to remove that concern with my explanatory statement. Mr Verhoeven's motion, as it now stands, is in fact the implementation of the current policy. I see the motion as supporting the policy advocated by the cabinet. We will, of course, have a discussion with each other, against the background of this vigilant motion by Mr Verhoeven.
