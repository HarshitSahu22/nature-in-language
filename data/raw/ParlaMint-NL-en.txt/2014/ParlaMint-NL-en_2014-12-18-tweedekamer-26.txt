ParlaMint-NL_2014-12-18-tweedekamer-26.u3	Mr. Chairman. I can judge immediately, because we talked about this last night. Like my colleague Mr Koenders of Foreign Affairs, I indicated that the current EU mandate for Atalanta was adopted very recently for the next two years, so until the end of 2016. This has been preceded by a very long period of negotiations. That's how it works in Europe; you can't always get what you want. In those negotiations, the Netherlands once again made no effort with all Member States to extend the mandate for Atalanta with supporting tasks. Those supporting tasks shall aim at combating or combating as much as possible other criminal activities at sea. The mandate for Ocean Shield and Atalanta does not allow action against human trafficking, charcoal smuggling or illegal fishing, but it is specifically aimed at combating piracy. As I said last night, it is possible to share information about observed activities with partners or other organisations, allowing them to act again. Here, of course, is the motto: more parties see more than one. At that time, other parties also need to be activated to tackle their own tasks. We don't all have to do that ourselves. I am sure that no agreement will be reached in the near future as Mr Van Dijk asks. We will draw attention to this in the appropriate organisations and put it in a position to further the information they receive from us and to tackle, for example, illegal fishing, as I said last night. What Mr Van Dijk and Mrs Hachchi are asking of me is not feasible because it would make Atalanta responsible for tackling illegal dumping and fishing. In short, I will continue to draw attention to it, so I am partly in favour of Mr Van Dijk, but I do not recommend the motion as such.
