ParlaMint-NL_2014-12-18-tweedekamer-16.u35	Mr Bashir and Mr Van Gerven asked the government in their motion for a resolution on item No. 444, to have the government examine what standard can be set for enterococci above which companies are obliged to act. There is no standard, but the level of enterobacteria is an indication of the hygiene in the production process and thus also an indicator of a possible risk. High values are a reason for companies to look critically at the process and to adapt it where necessary. The breach of the standard for enterococci is not a direct reason to reject the final product. There is also no direct link between the amount of enterococci and the presence of harmful bacteria. There is therefore no point in reducing this standard in relation to food safety control. The indication value is generally considered to be manageable in order to make an accurate assessment of the hygiene within a holding. I would therefore advise against the motion. In the motion in part No. Mr Bashir and Mr Van Gerven asked the government to publish the names of the companies concerned immediately in the event of a recall. ParlaMint-NL_2014-12-18-tweedekamer-16.u41	Mr. Chairman. I will judge three motions on behalf of the cabinet. First of all, I come to the motion on part No. 441, inviting the government to improve communication and data exchange between the Dutch Food and War Authority and the Security Research Council. I think the exchange of data is going very well. The Research Council also has access to all data of the NVWA. For that reason, I advise against this motion. In the motion in part No. 448 the government is invited to examine the risks of the farming industry in the risk analysis. The NVWA checks compliance with the approval of the fish farms and also investigates contaminants and microbiological infections in farmed fish. We already control a lot of imported fish from Asia. This year 237 samples of fish from third countries were taken for monitoring under the National Plan Residues. It shall examine whether there is no excess in the framework of, for example, veterinary medicinal products or contaminants. If there are exceedances, there will also be action. The risk analyses carried out by the Agency in the various chains will also further examine the fish chain. The results may lead to further prioritisation and intervention. The NVWA will also further investigate the presence of resistant bacteria from 2015 onwards. As far as animal welfare is concerned, there are now several studies on which we would like to inform the Chamber in due course. That is why I would like to discourage the motion as it has now been formulated. I hope I have been able to show that many different actions are already being taken. I now come to the motion for a resolution on part No. 449, also tabled by Mrs Ouwehand and Mrs Thieme. I would also advise against this motion. The medicine endosulfan has not been authorised in the European Union since 2005, but the historical use of endosulfan may still allow residues to be present in feed raw materials. We know fish are sensitive to endosulfan. For this reason, the standards for fish feed are also lower than for feeds for other animal species. The standards were also raised in 2012, specifically for salmon. This has been done by means of a European regulation. We are not aware that this medicine is still being used in Norway. Norway is part of the European Economic Area. The same bans apply to the countries that are members of it as to EU countries. For that reason, I would like to discourage this motion.
