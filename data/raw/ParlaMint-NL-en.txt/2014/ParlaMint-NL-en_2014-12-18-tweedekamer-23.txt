ParlaMint-NL_2014-12-18-tweedekamer-23.u3	Mr. Chairman. According to the government, the Dutch military mission in Mali is a stabilisation mission. So it is not a mission that is going to solve the problem there, but a mission that must create stability. If this stability exists, the solution to the situation in Mali must take place in a political negotiation process between the parties there. These negotiations were initially scheduled to take place in Algiers in mid-October, but they did not continue. A reason for not continuing these negotiations has never been announced. The negotiations would then be resumed on 20 November, but we have never heard from them again until today. My question to the government is therefore whether the negotiations have actually resumed. If so, what is the progress? Is a political solution in sight? If that is not the case, then the question is how long the government intends to continue with this Party of the Labour Problem Project, which I always call the Coenders' Mission for the sake of clarity. According to Mr Koenders' temporary successor as a UN Special Envoy for Mali, the solution to the Malian problem will last for 15 to 20 years. I hope the government does not intend to spend so much money on Mali for so long. As far as PVV is concerned, we should stop depositing money into the bottomless Malian well today rather than tomorrow.

ParlaMint-NL_2014-12-18-tweedekamer-23.u5	Mr. Chairman. D66 has filed a motion on item No. 271. It goes without saying, and I do not make that argument, that there is much to improve on the position of women in Mali. That's what we talk about on a regular basis. There should certainly be more women at the table at the peace talks in Algiers. I therefore very much agree with everything that is said about this in the motion. I do not agree at all with the assumption in the motion that MINUSMA and the Netherlands are not sufficiently committed to this. That's exactly what we're doing. Mr Koenders also mentioned this during the general consultation. Partly on the initiative of the Netherlands, Malian civil society is involved in the discussions in Algiers this autumn through a consultation. I was in Mali myself recently, and then I talked about it. Through the bilateral development cooperation programme, we support UN Women's projects in Mali. I really do not agree with what is being said in the motion, namely that the Netherlands would not do enough to date. This also applies to MINUSMA, because we also have a share in this mission. Nevertheless, I agree with the statement that it is good to keep tambourizing on that. That's what I'm happy to do. All in all, I see the motion as supporting policy and leave the verdict on the motion to the Chamber. Mr De Roon asked a question on the state of play of the negotiations in Algiers on Mali. Negotiations took place both in October and November. Algeria submitted a draft agreement after that last round in November. The parties taking part in the negotiations probe their supporters of this. I noticed that myself when I was in Mali last week. Discussions will resume in January.
ParlaMint-NL_2014-12-18-tweedekamer-23.u6	We are still missing an opinion on Mr Jasper van Dijk's motion. I understand that the Secretary of Defense will provide an opinion on that motion.
ParlaMint-NL_2014-12-18-tweedekamer-23.u7	Mr. Chairman. Mr Van Dijk's motion for a resolution 272 seems sympathetic, but Mr Van Dijk's motion also suggests a contradiction between management and the workplace. There isn't one. Complaints can be dealt with very quickly by the commander, who receives the complaints. There are also the unions and of course they always have a listening ear. In short, setting a separate hotline is not necessary. Such a hotline is unnecessary and unnecessary, as there are sufficient channels through which the complaints of the staff can be promptly addressed. There is no contradiction between the workplace and the management, because it is also in the interest of management to address and remove discontent among the staff as quickly as possible. I would therefore like to discourage the motion, however sympathetic it may be.
