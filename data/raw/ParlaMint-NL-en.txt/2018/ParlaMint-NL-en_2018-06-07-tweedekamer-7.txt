ParlaMint-NL_2018-06-07-tweedekamer-7.u13	Mr. Chairman. The motions on the documents Nos. 707 and 708 are specific to palm oil. The Dutch policy is that we focus on generic provisions to ensure a net effect of CO reduction. This makes demands on the ILUC risk and emissions. But we are not focusing specifically on a special fuel. So we're doing it raw material neutral. In this sense, there is no specific policy on palm oil. There is a specific policy on biofuels. As in the motion in part No. 707 'palm oil' is replaced by 'biofuels', so I can live well with it, but otherwise I have to advise her not to. The second motion, the one in part No. 708, suffers from the same but also from something else, namely that it is not possible, even technically not, to have the Dutch position adopted in this way. I must therefore reject this motion for technical reasons. Then the motion to part No. 709 on the formula. Look, we're saying here we're in danger of heading for binding sub-targets. The Netherlands has a very clear strategy with an ambitious main objective, with some degrees of freedom on sub-targets. Those sub-objectives should not therefore be increased nationally, because otherwise we will lose our degrees of freedom and come up with the argument that Mr Dijkstra has just maintained, because then we will no longer have the most cost-effective mix. Because the costs are very important for the support base, I cannot agree on national binding sub-targets. With that, I must advise against this motion. At the motion in part No. Mr Jetten's 710 plays the opposite. His motion concerns European goals without national commitment. This is helping again, because it means that we hold degrees of freedom for the most cost-effective commitment, but that Europe as a whole needs to become more ambitious. This brings us not only closer to the 55%, as stated in the motion, but also closer to a level playing field. This is of particular benefit to us. I should therefore like to leave the judgment on this motion to the Chamber.
