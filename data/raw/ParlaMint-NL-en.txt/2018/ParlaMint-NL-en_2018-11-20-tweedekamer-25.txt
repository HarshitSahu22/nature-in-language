ParlaMint-NL_2018-11-20-tweedekamer-25.u9	Mr. Chairman. I shall begin immediately with the motion by Mr Van Nispen and Mr Van Toorenburg on part No. 2724. Thank you for the explanation of that motion. The regulation is indeed based on a cross-border system of jurisdiction. I say'regulation', but the draft regulation under discussion means that any competent authority can issue a removal order against a hosting service provider, regardless of where that hosting service provider is located in the EU. I have already pointed out during the AO I would like to repeat here aloud that it is a very important topic for me in the negotiations to bet on a system in which the test could take place at least in the country where the hosting service provider is located. In that connection, I shall leave the motion to the House's judgement. Mrs Maeijer's motion on item No. 2725, which contains the request to oppose a cybersecurity knowledge center, I must advise against. We need more European cooperation on these kinds of issues. It is not so much a question of whether or not there is an agency that an agency would be too much. It is a very important issue in which we need cooperation, precisely because of the cross-border aspects of cybersecurity, by definition. So I'm gonna have to dissuade that motion. Also the motion in part No. 2726, on behalf of the Dutch Government, on the basis of fundamental objections to a regulation that would deal with terrorist content online, I really have to advise against. At this point, we really need to get on with each other. By definition, the Internet is cross-border. If we want to have a good approach to terrorist content online, we need to do so in good cooperation and in alignment with each other's legal systems. I have already indicated in the debate on Mr Van Nispen's and Mrs Van Toorenburg's motion that, of course, we are very clear about the element of the possibility of legal proceedings in the country of establishment. By the way, we are also looking at other constitutional principles; let me emphasise that. The motion in part No. 2726 I therefore advise against. Then there is the motion from Mr Laan to item No. 2727. It was good to hear Mrs Laan's explanation of the motion. The draft regulation now makes it possible to issue a cross-border removal order to a terrorist content host provider. Then that content must be removed within one hour. In addition, we can see that some of the larger Internet companies, such as Facebook, Google and Twitter, are already relatively advanced in the development and deployment of proactive measures. They have proved to be very effective so far. We also see a lot of expertise being developed in industry. If I understand the motion in such a way that I am closely monitoring market developments on behalf of the government and that we are also monitoring them in terms of removal time, then I will leave the judgement to the Chamber.
