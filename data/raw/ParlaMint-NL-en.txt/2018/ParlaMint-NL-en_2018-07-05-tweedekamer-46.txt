ParlaMint-NL_2018-07-05-tweedekamer-46.u10	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. I come first to Mrs Ploumen's motion. 200 calling on the government to examine the possibilities for a UN mission in Myanmar at international level. I agree with Mrs Ploumen that this would be highly desirable. Mrs Ploumen also knows that it will not be easy, but I am happy to work for it. So I'll leave the motion to the House's judgment. In the motion by Mr Sjoerdsma, Mr Ten Broeke, Mr Van Helvert and Mr Voordewind, on part No. 201, the government is invited to advocate, at European level, the extension of the EU sanctions list. I think it is an important achievement that we got a number of Myanmari government leaders on the penalty list last week, and I agree with the authors that it is desirable to add more people to that list as soon as there is sufficient support for that. So I would also like to leave this motion to the judgement of the Chamber. In their motion in part No. 202 Mr Kuzu and Mrs Ploumen asked the government to seek support in the UN Security Council for the UN call on the Myanmari government to grant the majority of the Rohingya population full and equivalent civil rights and to draw up a plan of action to that end. The desirability of granting civil rights is part of Mr Annan's report and is fully endorsed by the Dutch Government. The motion calls for support in the Security Council. That's not real. A number of permanent members will not support that in any case. Their names are also well known here. That does not make the operative part of the motion real. That's why I have to discourage this motion. The motion in part No. 203 calls on the Government, in bilateral contacts and through international gremia, to grant the Myanmari Government membership of the International Criminal Court, and also calls on the Government to make the necessary preparations to submit the situation in Myanmar to the International Criminal Court. I would very much like Myanmar to become a member of the International Criminal Court, but precisely because of what the motion says, the Myanmari Government will not do so, because it will realise that it will immediately come before the Criminal Court. So here too, I do share the wish, but the call in the motion is not real. That's why I have to dissuade this one. In the last motion, in part No. 204, the cabinet is invited to encourage the Israeli Government in bilateral contacts to stop arms exports to Myanmar. I have also indicated in the AO that I agree that it is undesirable to provide arms to the regime in Myanmar. I have also indicated that when I speak to members of the Israeli Government, I would also like to address them. But this motion suggests that only the Israeli government is involved in arms supplies. That is an incorrect picture of the situation. So since this motion focuses on one country, I must also discourage it.
