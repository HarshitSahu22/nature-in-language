ParlaMint-NL_2018-09-25-tweedekamer-24.u3	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. The Netherlands has already argued in the European Council on this subject for the early adoption of the regulation on the basis of the text of the Estonian Presidency, which has also been communicated to the Chamber. Now we are going to argue for a postponement of this, making the Netherlands unbelievable. An impact assessment has already been carried out by the European Commission. This is comparable to the SME test, which is what the consequences are for small and medium-sized enterprises. In addition, there seems to be some confusion here, because the e-Privacy Regulation really regulates something completely different from the General Data Protection Regulation. The SME peloton, but also football associations, which are sometimes discussed in the context of the General Data Protection Regulation, are not experiencing any new effects from the e-Privacy Regulation, except for the rules already in force. Only small and medium-sized enterprises that provide inter-human communication services via the Internet, such as Messenger or WhatsApp, will be subject to new rules. But that is also a good thing, because at the moment, communication secrecy applies only to the classic telecommunications providers, and not to these kinds of services, which we are all going to use very much. So I dissuade the motion and it is also good to realize that the e-Privacy Regulation will also regulate the great irritation that exists in the field of telemarketing. So I understand Mr Wörsdörfer's concerns. The General Data Protection Regulation is a good new regulation for small and medium-sized enterprises that can have an impact. But that does not apply to most SMEs, except for those providing so-called inter-human communications services. In doing so, this motion goes far beyond its purpose. Thank you.
