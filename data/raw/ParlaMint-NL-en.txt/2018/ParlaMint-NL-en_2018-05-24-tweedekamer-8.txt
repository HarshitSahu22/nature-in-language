ParlaMint-NL_2018-05-24-tweedekamer-8.u5	I give the floor to the Secretary of State.
ParlaMint-NL_2018-05-24-tweedekamer-8.u6	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. The Van der Berg/Van der Lee motion calls on the government to promote equal rules in the field of guarantee periods in consumer protection in Europe. I'll leave the verdict on this motion to the Chamber. She's sympathetic. In the case of equal rules in Europe, both consumers and entrepreneurs know better where they stand. This could benefit trade. The motion follows on from the Vos/Jacobi motion previously adopted by the Chamber in which the government was called upon to ensure an equal level of consumer protection. The adoption of this motion will result in me taking a position that will ensure a minimum level of consumer protection as currently applicable in the Netherlands and at the same time ensure an equal level of protection in Europe. I think it is right to point out here, just as I did in the AA, that as it stands today, most European Member States seem to advocate minimum harmonisation. The second motion is the Paternotte/Wörsdörfer motion in which the government is invited to express the view during the Council that the draft regulation should not have undue negative effects on the relationship between digital platforms and entrepreneurs. This motion is also sympathetic. It is important that we keep an eye on possible negative side effects in new regulations. This draft regulation on fair trade between platforms and entrepreneurs is on the Council's agenda. This Regulation regulates transparency and dispute resolution. The aim of the motion is to avoid unnecessary regulatory intervention in innovation and consumers' freedom of choice, insofar as that balance in the relationship between these two types of entrepreneurs is not necessary for fair trade. There will be another BNC sheet, but this motion supports the policy and that is why I leave the judgment to the Chamber. Then I have a question and a motion from Mr Alkaya on the same subject. The question and the motion are about the notification procedure based on the Services Directive when governments take decisions that affect it. This has already been discussed in the Council in May 2017. A general approach has already been adopted at that time which shows that the Dutch Government has agreed to this. The House of Representatives was informed at that time. This notification procedure is now in the final phase of the trilogue. There is one change in content: from aft notification to prior notification. This does not mean that permission is requested. Local authorities are going to report to the European Commission that they are making proposals that could affect the free movement of services. It's considered proportional to do it this way. The European Parliament is expected to vote in favour of it. With this argument, I agree with you that I would therefore advise against the motion.
ParlaMint-NL_2018-05-24-tweedekamer-8.u7	I understand the Secretary of State's argument when she says that it has already been in the Council once. But the outcome of the trialogue must also be approved by the Council. We must agree to that. It's often a hammer piece. On the contrary, I would ask the Secretary of State not to treat it as a hammerpiece, because I would like to discuss this final proposal, which she now indicates will be a major improvement. Isn't the Secretary of State wondering what the final proposal says before she reacts so positively to it?
ParlaMint-NL_2018-05-24-tweedekamer-8.u8	There is already a notification procedure. Even now, a government that lays down rules affecting the free movement of services must publish them to the Commission only afterwards. The only thing that changes now is that this has to be done in advance. It's a change, but it's not so big that I'm worried about big administrative burdens for those governments. This is also the reason why the Netherlands already agreed to this proposal in the Council in May 2017. I see no reason to take a different view now, because, as we have already exchanged, the free movement of services is particularly important for Dutch entrepreneurs, so that they can offer their services and earn money in other European countries. It is also in our interest that other countries should stick to it and not create unnecessary barriers. That is why we have agreed and I see no reason to do so differently now.
