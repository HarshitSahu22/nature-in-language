ParlaMint-NL_2018-01-23-tweedekamer-4.u4	Thank you, Mr President. I thank Mr Grothuizen for his questions. Mr. Chairman. I recognise the impact that long lead times can have on the applicant and, above all, on his family members. The long wait and the insecurity are of course always annoying to those involved. Therefore, the IND's commitment is to decide on an application for family reunification as soon as possible, without prejudice to due diligence. The core of the cause of the waiting period is in the period of large influx in 2015 and 2016. This led, especially around the turn of the year 2016-2017, to a large number of applications that still had to be dealt with. For this reason, last year the formation of the IND was temporarily extended to catch up. This was also largely achieved, from around 20,000 remaining applications in early 2017 to over 6,000, 6,100, applications at the end of 2017. For this purpose, the formation of the IND, at least the people who have been deployed for this task, has been extended from 180 in the beginning of the year to 220 in April, for a number of months in 2017. After September, that number was reduced to 185 people. We did it. In every month of 2017 you can see that the outflow, so the cases that have been dealt with, is two to three times the amount of the items that are inflowing. So we've been able to catch up to a large extent, but we're not there yet. In the written preparation for the budget debate, I have already informed the Chamber that the initial promise of catching up in the summer of 2017 would not be met. It's gonna be spring 2018. This means that everything is intended to be able to maintain normal levels again this spring. This means that the inflow can be eliminated within the legal deadline, given the expected composition of difficult and easy matters. Mr Groothuizen also asked about the reasons for the long lead times. Most of the delay is due to the fact that the cases are very complex, where new facts and circumstances can be presented at a late stage, or where there are no documents to prove the family relationship and/or identity. The relatively simple requests have been dealt with, but the settlement of these complex applications often requires further research, DNA research and/or an on-the-spot hearing at an embassy. So that makes these cases take longer than we were used to in the past.
