ParlaMint-NL_2018-11-08-tweedekamer-3.u5	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. The motion in part No. 1917, Mrs Leijten's first motion, calls on the government to extend the time period for the yellow card procedure from eight to twelve weeks. I said during the AO that the Dutch Government has sympathy for a longer period and that this requires a treaty change. The motion calls for a government commitment. I'd like to do that. So I would like to leave this motion to the House's judgement. The second motion by Mrs Leijten, which was tabled in part No. 1918, calls on the government to guarantee the right of residence for all the British currently residing in the Netherlands, including in the event of a lack of agreement on UK exit from the European Union. I have pointed out to the Chamber that I think it is very important that the British who are in the Netherlands should be given a proper regime with regard to their right of residence. By the way, I've brought that out before. The wording that has been chosen here, namely 'guarding', creates a certainty which cannot be realised at the moment. Moreover, that is not what the British Government has said towards European citizens. As far as they are concerned, too, the British Government has indicated that it wants to ensure careful regulation, without talking about guarantees or the implementation of very important issues such as social security, access to training and its financing. So this wording, which goes far beyond what I myself have said, means that I must discourage this motion. Then the motion to part No. 1919, also by Mrs Leijten. It calls on the government to start the debate on a reverse approach, in which the European Commission must demonstrate that a solution needs European direction rather than national governments and/or parliaments having to show that something is their competence and inform the Chamber about it. The principle of subsidiarity is very important; I agree entirely with Mrs Leijten, and I agree with the whole Chamber. But the suggestion in this motion that the European Commission does not take any subsidiarity test is incorrect. It is not the case that the Dutch Government or the House of Representatives, but that is what she herself is about, always agrees with that subsidiarity test, but it is made. Since that assumption is already incorrect, I must also advise against this motion.
