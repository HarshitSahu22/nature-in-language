ParlaMint-NL_2020-03-12-tweedekamer-3.u22	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. I shall begin with the first motion by Mr Laçin and Mr Van Gerven, in respect of item No. 54. The whole system of the law is, of course, aimed at protecting nature. I share that with the authors of this motion. That's why the law is so important. But this motion says that it must always be the only interest that you should be the highest. We've already totally lifted the regime. The system of the Birds and Habitats Directives is also fully applicable to Natura 2000 sites and species protection. This law doesn't change that. With the supplement decision it is converted into the Quality of Living Environment Decision. In addition, the general duty of care referred to by the author of the motion also applies. So, in fact, this motion is unnecessary, and that's why I'm not advising him. Then the motion to part No. 55 on legal protection and codes of conduct. Under the Environmental Act, the adoption of codes of conduct takes place through a ministerial arrangement rather than a separate decision. In this way, the code of conduct also fits within the instruments available in the Environmental Act. This ministerial arrangement is open to participation. That's what's happening here. That's what's coming through here. On the basis of the Awb, there is no objection and appeal against a ministerial arrangement, but stakeholders can influence the establishment of such a code of conduct. So he's not just coming through here. It is also possible to influence the development of the Code of Conduct. Stakeholders are involved in the preparation and there is the possibility of participation in the internet consultation. So we secured it all in advance. At present, the "code of conduct" instrument is under review. This involves IPO, VNG and the Union of Water Councils, as well as other users of the Code of Conduct. The issue of legal protection will also be taken into account in this context. Any changes to the system surrounding the codes of conduct may be carried out by means of a separate modification trail. Quite a story, but on the basis of this statement of reasons, I would advise against this motion. Then the motion to part No. 56 on the Digital System Environment Act and the disclosure of information. This motion I can give judgment Chamber. I can also clarify the rules of the instruments mentioned by GroenLinks, which become visible with a click on the map. All the rules in the environmental regulation, the environmental plan and the watership regulation will be visible in the Digital System Environment Act with that click on the map. Those rules are thus always linked to an area of operation and provided with geo-information, including for nature. So this motion gets judgment, Chamber. Mr von Martels asks me with a motion on item No. 57 to talk to the province about the actualization of the exemption list. The evaluation of the exemption list is a provincial matter. If there is national damage and nuisance, the Empire can place species on the national exemption list. I am prepared to discuss with the provinces what the problem is and how best to deal with it at the national or provincial level. I'm giving this motion judgment room. Mr. Chairman. For the motion in part No. 58 I need a few extra words, because there is a difference between the legal word "priority statement" and how I read it in this motion. So pay attention, Mr. Stoffer. It's gonna be a treatise. It is true that the Chamber of the Bishop's motion of 17 October 2019 (32670, no. 188) on a better definition of priorities in Natura 2000 policy. I am carrying out that motion with the actualisation of the Natura 2000 target system. That's what we're doing right now. It also looks at how the Natura 2000 targets have been formulated and whether a different target formulation is possible. The results will be incorporated into the designation decisions for the different areas. That is what happens when the updating of the designation decisions is at issue. We don't do that in between. We just have times when we update those designation decisions and then we look at what we need to do. This motion suggests, at least as I read it, that an immediate priority should also be put in place and that some sort of ranking is going to be made between targets in a Natura 2000 site. We cannot just say: we are going to make a sort of ranking of goals that must now be served first in one area, and the other objectives, which are also pursued in that area, we are going to do last. We always look closely at those goals within ourselves. For example, if there is climate change that means that you no longer have a particular species in an area, you need to be able to adapt it. That is what we are doing at the appropriate time. It's just that it's not like I'm just gonna be able to make in-between rankings between the targets right now. On the basis of that explanation, I must discourage the motion. But we are doing the actualization of that target system, which Mr Bishop has actually asked for. It's a fairly secure treatment, but as the motion is now formulated, I have to dissuade her. The motion in part No. 59 by Mr Wassenberg asks for nature policy to be transferred to the Empire. I also had a lively discussion with Mr Futselaar on this subject in another debate. In that debate, I was already opposed to it, and here I also advise against the motion. The motion in part No. 60 of Mr Weverling's calls for talks with the provinces on the matter of stone martens. Once again, the provinces of course have the power to see what they are doing against the stone martyr, but I am prepared to discuss this with the provinces. With that I can give this motion judgment Chamber. I think that's it, Mr. Chairman.
