However, the operative part of the motion calls very strongly for this to be done before the directive enters into force. I can't live up to that. So, if that motion isn't modified, it's not recommended. Then the motion to part No. 161 by Mr Koerhuis, in which the government is invited to make use of the deferment option for building applications until 10 March 2021 for the mandatory construction of loading infrastructure. The possibility offered by the Directive to postpone construction applications until 10 March 2021 has not been used, as no need has been found to do so. The obligation applies only to new buildings with more than ten parking spaces. So these are not all categories. For utility construction it is one charging point per parking space and one in five parking spaces must be provided with empty pipes. In the case of housing, all parking spaces must be provided with empty pipes. Loading points and pipeline throughput are minor modifications to the design of a building. So I do not see that as an insurmountable objection. Interested parties have been aware of the introduction of this obligation for a long time and have been regularly informed about it in the past year. During the internet consultation, only one party made a comment about this, which has also been able to inform its constituents. I must therefore discourage this motion. In the motion in part No. 162 by Mr Koerhuis, the government is invited to make use of the derogation, so that SMEs do not have to build a loading infrastructure. The Directive lays down two obligations for the construction of loading infrastructure. The Directive provides for the possibility of excluding SMEs from these obligations. This was not chosen in the implementation for a number of reasons. The obligations apply only to more than 10 or 20 parking spaces. Small entrepreneurs are therefore usually not covered by these obligations. National policies on the roll-out of charging points are also aimed at SMEs. It would then be strange to exclude SMEs from this. The exception is also expected to be difficult to implement for supervision and enforcement, as the concept of SMEs is defined in the European Directive on the basis of numbers of employees and turnover. That's why I advise against this motion.
