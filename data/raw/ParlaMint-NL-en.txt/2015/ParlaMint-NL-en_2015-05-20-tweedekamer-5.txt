ParlaMint-NL_2015-05-20-tweedekamer-5.u6	Mr. Chairman. The AO took place following an own-initiative paper by Mr Nijboer. Many of the action points he has put forward have either been taken up or can be taken up. There are still a few points that have now led to motions. I shall begin with the motion by Mr Merkies and Mr Nijboer on item No. 60. It invites the Minister to examine the extent to which privacy is affected by new developments in the payment market, in particular in the area of internet payments. In the AO, I have already said that I want to remove the suggestion, should it already exist, that new payment service providers, such as Google and Apple, are not subject to privacy regulations that apply throughout the European Union. That is clearly the case. At the moment, the privacy regulation is being amended, with all kinds of new rules for the gaps that remain. Since it is a regulation, there is in principle no room for specific national regulation on top of that, except where such space is explicitly provided for in the regulation. It is therefore important that we take up European legislation. From the Netherlands, the Minister of V and J is, of course, responsible for this. He shall conduct the debate with the Chamber on such legislation and inform the Chamber thereof. In the light of this motion, I can inform the College of Protection of personal data about the concerns of the proposers on this point. I could suggest to the college that it is, of course, independent to check whether there is sufficient regulation for consumers and whether it is sufficiently transparent what happens to their data. If the College is willing to do so, we can use it to make improvements if necessary. The Social Dialogue Payment Traffic (MOB) has decided to conduct an independent investigation, including into the views of customers. What degree of protection or protection do we want? After all, there are also advantages if your payment behaviour is known to providers. It's just what you want with that. The MOB will have this investigated by clients. It is specifically aimed at the use of data for commercial purposes by the banks of those clients. That investigation was completed before the end of the year. We can get that involved. There are therefore three elements: European developments, the involvement of the College on personal data protection and the exploitation of the research already started by the Social Dialogue Payment traffic. If I may consider these three elements together as the elaboration of what the motion asks me to do, I shall inform the Chamber as soon as possible and I shall leave the opinion on the motion to the Chamber. Then I come to the motion on part No. 61, by Mr Nijboer. This motion concerns the fact that the automatic conversion of old bank account numbers to the new IBAN numbers has been terminated by banks. Of course, I keep saying that it is in everyone's interest, both from companies and from consumers, that we all get used to the new numbers and get used to them. This can also be expected from companies. They have been given some extra time, so they can be expected to have this in order. Now there is a need for automatic renumbering, especially among individuals. I am willing to talk to the banks to see if they can continue their service or otherwise find a solution. I can promise that I will. I shall make this motion to mine and I would like to leave it to the Chamber to decide whether the motion will be put to the vote. Otherwise, I shall leave this motion to the House's discretion. The third motion is the motion in part No. 62 by Mrs De Vries and Mr Nijboer and Mr Koolmees. This motion is about the 24/7 processing of payments, so also on weekends and evenings. Denmark shows that this is possible. That country has a different payment system. That is not new information for the Chamber. We also discussed the advantages and disadvantages of the solutions in the AO. Banks themselves have indicated that they have now started working on this subject, including with stakeholders. They are looking for a structural solution and it is an urgent matter for them. I shall therefore leave this motion to the House to decide. The sector itself, in consultation with DNB and, of course, the Ministry of Finance, is looking for a solution. We are working constructively on this, so I can also get out of the way with this motion. I'll leave the verdict on the motion to the Chamber. Mrs De Vries asked when the examination of the switching service and number portability could be completed. The subject has been taken up by now. I expect to be able to present the results by the end of this year at the earliest.
