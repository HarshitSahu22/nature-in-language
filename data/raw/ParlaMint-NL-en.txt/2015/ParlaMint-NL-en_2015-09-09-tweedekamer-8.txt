ParlaMint-NL_2015-09-09-tweedekamer-8.u7	Mr. Chairman. I can respond briefly and forcefully, because only motions have been tabled. There have been very few questions asked. The first motion is that in part No. 40 by Mr Geurts. He would like us to have the predicted salination above the legal standard of 150 mg/litres, he said 150 mg, but meant "per litre" because otherwise it would be very little kept above the table and that we would inform the Chamber, should anything else arise. With the Deltaplan Freshwater and the instruments being developed in it, it is also explicitly intended that we comply with the legal standards for drinking water. I do not want to commit to the instrument mix yet, but there is talk here about, among other things, thrusts and locks. So, if I can understand the motion in such a way that there are various instruments available, I see it as supporting policy. If those instruments were limited, I would have to discourage the motion, but that's not how I read her. I see the petitioner nodding, so I think we're good on that. I shall now come to Mrs Hachchi and Mrs Jacobi's motion on item No. 41 about the Great Waters Natural Ambition and the reaction I gave to it in general consultation. Let's just say that with the indication that it's "a dream," I don't mean that a dream is also a deception, as some songs say, but that it's something you can keep an eye on for the future. If you do an intervention somewhere, you can then also consider target images. That's how the Chamber should read that report. The report on nature ambition is about targets and not about concrete goals that will be achieved in the short term. I do not want to turn the Great Waters Natural Ambition into concrete actions and indicate how these actions are linked to policy. First of all, it's not my wallet. It is Mrs Dijksma's portfolio, and I understand that the provinces also play a leading role in this. In that respect, I am sitting on someone else's chair. This is without prejudice to the fact that it is just interesting for us to see what is possible in the future. Sometimes you choose to do it, and sometimes you don't. It depends entirely on which issue is on the table. I therefore advise against this motion. Then I come to the motion on part No. 42 by Mrs Jacobi and Mrs Hachchi on the Water Framework Directive implementation programme; are we going to achieve the targets in time? This motion requests that the Jacobi c.s. motion be followed in the light of the National Water Plan. and the recommendations from the publications of the PBL, the European Commission and the Water Advisory Committee and to establish the relationship with the implementation programme of the Drinking Water Policy Note. In order to achieve clean water, I opt for a coherent approach, as laid down in the June opinion of the Water Advisory Committee. The Advisory Committee recommended three steps to implement this. Firstly, to appoint the joint task, then to appoint the joint action and finally to make joint agreements on the necessary resources. At the moment I am working with the partners on steps one and two: mapping the task and mapping the measures. After that, I would like to talk to them about how we are going to finance it. The Chamber knows that I have also made available my own budget for this and the partners know that they will match it. From this point of view, the question contained in the motion comes too early. I therefore call for the motion to be retained so that we can discuss it once the package of measures has been clarified.
