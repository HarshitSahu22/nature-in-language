ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-02-tweedekamer-3.u11	Mr. Chairman. I would like to thank the members of your Chamber for their contributions to this VAO. I'd better get right on the motions. The first three motions are by Mr Ulenbelt, along with Mrs Karabulut. I want his first motion on part number one. 154 in conjunction with that of Mrs Vermeij and Mr Kerstens in part No. 157. I too am very interested in the way in which municipalities shape sheltered work. Municipalities are busy making their regulations and completing them. There are good examples of this. We have agreed with this Chamber that the Participation Act will be monitored; we are doing so. In the autumn we will give the House of Representatives a very good insight into the number of places for sheltered work. For the record, these 30,000 places are, of course, in the future. For the 2015 annual album we are talking about 800 places, so we have to see if they have been realized in the autumn. It shall be reported by means of statistics. Of course I'm prepared to talk to this Chamber about that. The thrust of the motion of the SP is just a little too enthusiastic. It asks whether measures can already be taken to ensure the realisation of sheltered workplaces in each municipality. I think that at this stage is too far-reaching, because we have said that until 1 July municipalities have time to make regulations. Furthermore, we consider the policy freedom of municipalities to be a great asset. So what is being asked in the motion of the SP goes a little too far for us. I need the motion in part no. 154 therefore, it is not recommended. In the motion in part No. 157 of Ms Vermeij and Ms Kerstens asked the government to inform the Chamber in autumn 2015 of the way and extent to which the sheltered work facility is being implemented. I can leave that motion to the House's judgement, because I think it is very worthwhile. In fact, I want to know myself. Then I come to the motion on part No. 155 over, if I may say so, "the nanny's and grandma's." I have answered some of the questions put by Mr Karabulut. If it is correct, they have landed yesterday morning, but I can imagine that not everyone has been able to read the answer. In that I have said that it is natural that grandpas and grandmothers should be able to look after their grandchildren. I think that's as ready as a whistle. So I would like to underline that. I am not acting in individual cases, as the Chamber will understand, but it is of course a question of being open to the labour market if assistance is granted. It is therefore up to the municipality to see whether it is doing enough. That is also what I said in my written reply. I must therefore advise against this motion because it states that the care of grandchildren by grandparents in the assistance should not be regarded as a work worthy of money. In most cases, of course, it is, but not always. So I can't leave this motion to judge Chamber. It is really the municipalities that have to take the lead in this. Then I come to the third and final motion on the part of the SP, the motion on part No. 156. It's about people with mental or psychiatric disabilities. I recognize a great deal in this motion. In fact, I am incredibly busy with my colleague from VWS to raise the promotion of the participation of people with mental illness to spearhead numero one. In this respect, we already have all kinds of things on track, such as the project People with Possibilities. Divosa also has a method for customer managers of municipalities. I think it is a good idea to inform the Chamber after the summer about the progress of the activities and the actions that have been initiated and will be launched. So I'm leaving this motion to judge Chamber.
