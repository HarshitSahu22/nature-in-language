ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-29-tweedekamer-7.u5	Mr. Chairman. First of all, let me say that during the general consultation we had a good exchange of views on organised crime, and in particular on its handling, which we have been very strongly encouraging in recent years, particularly under the leadership of my predecessor in office. In the letters I have sent to your Chamber in recent days, I have drawn attention to the successes that have been achieved. Today, too, the good news has come that parts of organised crime continue to decline in the Netherlands. The Netherlands is becoming structurally safer. First of all, I would like to answer the question raised by Mr Van Oosten as to the extent to which the prosecution is faced with a situation involving firearms. I've had to observe that I haven't talked to the D.A. about that lately. I will do so in the short term. I assure Mr Van Oosten that I will raise this issue at my next meeting with the Public Prosecutor and that I will inform the Chamber by letter of the answer to his question. Then I come to Mr Van Nispen's motion, co-signed by Mrs Berndsen-Jansen. This motion sees the need to increase the capacity of detectives in a quantitative and qualitative manner. It is not news that there is currently a major reorganisation of staff within the National Police. This means that a job vacancy summit has been put in place for ordinary jobs, but that there is explicitly no job vacancy summit with regard to the detective capacity, because additional resources have been made available for this in the past. Right now, we're talking about a capacity of 840 detectives. This capacity is expected to be increased to 1140 in the short term. Yesterday, by letter, I showed the Chamber the results of what is going well in the South of the Netherlands, where we are also tackling organised crime more firmly by means of an extra effort by detectives. The National Police are currently working on a strategic staff planning, which I hope to be able to take into account in the review of the reorganisation of the National Police which I have pledged to your Chamber. All in all, therefore, there is no reason at the moment to worry about the detective capacity and its strengthening, because that is existing policy. That is why I propose that I abstain from this motion. Then I come to the motion by Mr Van Nispen and Mr Kooiman asking for an inventory of how many cases there are waiting for treatment and how long it takes for the treatment to take place. I informed your Chamber in the letter I sent yesterday about the development in the South of the Netherlands about the lead times there. It is precisely because of the extra attention we give to this that these lead times are decreasing. It is of course intended that this will be the case throughout the Netherlands. For this purpose, complete steering and weighting committees have been set up, which, precisely for this reason, consult each other on how we can ensure that these matters are handled as efficiently as possible and that, in particular, the working stock is reduced as quickly as possible. Of course, you can always discuss board business, but it's probably better to talk about the work stock. It is a policy of standing to ensure that this situation does not occur. That is why I do not think the motion is useful, because it is a policy that is already in place. One could say that there is no point in doing additional research now. I suggest we just let the National Police go to work. That's why I have to discourage the motion. The third motion, tabled by Mr Van Nispen and Mrs Kooiman, calls for agents dealing with synthetic drugs of protective clothing to be provided with protective shoes at least. I am glad that this motion is there, because this is the commitment I made explicitly to Mrs Kooiman in general consultation, that I would consider the LFO at my next working visit. I haven't had the opportunity yet, but to my great joy I was invited by Mayor Jorritsma of Almere to open the National Safety Day. There the LFO was present with an extensive presentation of his stand. I have been able to discuss this issue very extensively with the LFO. The answer of the LFO spokesperson was clear and quite simple: 'Dwelling agents should not be allowed to enter drugs laboratories, they are not equipped for that, and the equipment and the suits we use for that cannot be made available to ordinary agents for all kinds of reasons, I will not go too far in technical terms'. For this reason, the policy is and will continue to be that only the members of the LFO will have their consent to enter a drug laboratory if they are sufficiently protected. So I do not want to give the impression that it might be the intention to enter such a laboratory without protective footwear, because that is dangerous. That simply must not happen, and that is why I must advise against this motion.

ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-29-tweedekamer-7.u9	Then I come to the motion on part No. 104 by Mr Oskam. In this motion, the government is asked not to give the municipalities any room to actually implement in practice controlled pot growing plans. Municipalities as independent administrative bodies in the Netherlands are free to conduct research. In this context, municipalities have the complete freedom to conduct investigations, including on regulated growing of weed, if one so wishes. Another problem is when one would decide to implement that regulated growing of weed. This is not allowed under the Opium Act. This is also not permitted under the international conventions to which the Netherlands is a party. I was recently at a meeting with the Association of Dutch Municipalities, which set up a committee to examine how to look at the growing of weed in the Netherlands. That is where I understand explicitly that there is no intention whatsoever to go against any legislation to implement this weed-growing regulation. The same applies to the plans in Rotterdam, which Mr Oskam mentioned. I have now received confirmation that there is no intention to implement these plans either, as they are simply contrary to the law. This means that, like my predecessor, I still assume that, if municipalities were allowed to act in breach of Dutch law, the Public Prosecutor would then prosecute. That is why the policy has not changed. I can do the Oskam motion in part number. 104 in this respect. On the other hand, I can see the motion as a policy support. I shall therefore leave the motion to the House's judgement. In the motion-Oskam on part No. 105 the government is invited to shorten municipalities if they have investigations carried out into the possibilities of regulated hemp cultivation.
