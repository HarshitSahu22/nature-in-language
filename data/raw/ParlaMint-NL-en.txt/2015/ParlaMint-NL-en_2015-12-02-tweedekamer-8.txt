This may lead to immediate discussion, but not to negotiations, let alone decision-making. So it is a real misunderstanding that the Luxembourg Presidency wants to reach an agreement on this before the end of the year. In principle, the securitisation proposal has now been agreed at Council level. Perhaps that is the reason for your confusion, Mr Merkies. In the Merkies motion in part No. 1326 the complainant is already taking a negative view of the European Commission's proposal for the European Deposit Guarantee Scheme. In the cabinet's view, the European deposit guarantee scheme is the final piece of the banking union. It is absolutely a part of it, but it is a conclusion when all the other legs of the banking union are set up and started, when funds are filled and remaining undesirable risks are reduced. All this must be done in the next eight-year transition period. The European Commission's proposal provides strictly for the gradual establishment of the European deposit scheme. Only in the final phase, so in eight years' time, should there be a fully-fledged European system. I would like to make a procedural comment and a substantive one. The procedural remark is that it is crazy to present chamberal motions which simply say "refusal; point." Not even a cabinet position has been sent to the Chamber. The Dutch Government does not yet have to take a position in Brussels. The care may require that the cabinet be asked for a position first. That is what we are having a debate on and then the Chamber takes up a position. In substance, I find it strange to close the door on the basis of a proposal alone, which also refers to a final introduction in 2024. Our approach is to talk about this only if it does not come into effect until 2024 and if in the meantime all kinds of risks that banks in Europe still have are cleared up. This requires a whole agenda that we must draw up with each other. We have to agree on that. Then we can also keep track of whether all the steps are being taken, whether the funds are being set up, whether they are being filled and whether the implementation is taking place. On this basis, we can then consider whether we are going to take steps in the future to eventually, as a final step, move to a dgs. I therefore consider the motion to be premature and not sensible in terms of content. That's why I have to dissuade her. Furthermore, I would prefer Mr Merkies to continue the motion pending the cabinet's position on this Commission proposal.
