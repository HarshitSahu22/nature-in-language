ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-23-tweedekamer-12.u2	Mr. Chairman. We do not often talk about nuclear weapons, but we do when that is relevant. It is relevant on the eve of a review conference on the Non-Proliferation Treaty, which should ultimately lead to the elimination of nuclear weapons. It would be a good thing for the SP Group if the government were to carry out previously adopted motions in this area. I will continue to say that until all the motions in this area, such as the van Velzen/Azough motion, have been put into effect. The debate on nuclear weapons always focuses in the Lower House on at least two issues. Firstly, that is transparency. I have signed a motion on this subject, which states that the Netherlands must strive for the same way of openness about nuclear weapons as in Germany. We may not have to talk about locations and numbers, but we can at least confirm that nuclear weapons are present on Dutch territory. That would be a huge step forward in relation to the current situation. Secondly, the ultimate aim is to achieve a global ban on nuclear weapons, as we know about the possession and use of chemical weapons. That should also apply to nuclear weapons. That's why I'm filing the next motion. ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-23-tweedekamer-12.u6	Madam President. I'd like to discuss the motions immediately. We have just discussed in detail the various options for next week's important conference. I shall begin with the motion by Mr Van Bommel/Van Ojik on part No. 17. I indicated in the debate that I find the Austrian pludge sympathetic. We share a number of objectives, namely the commitment to Global Zero, a world without nuclear weapons. I have also said that I support a different strategy. I do not see a treaty banning nuclear weapons as a starting point for disarmament as a sensible line, given the coalition which the Netherlands has now entered into. A rather progressive strategy has been chosen in the framework of the Non-Proliferation and Disarmament Initiative (NPDI) to make progress with other countries. In our opinion, the Austrian pledge is not the most preferred, that should be Australia's. In that light, I advise against this motion. Then I come to the motion-Knops c.s. in piece No. 18. I see this motion as a policy support. I think it is important to achieve greater transparency. There has also been a tightening-up today. Because the roads we have always been on have not actually led to results, you should consider whether we should achieve transparency in the same way as with Germany. According to the official line of NATO, it is no secret that American nuclear weapons have been or have been present in Europe, namely in Germany and the United Kingdom. Other NATO countries are not specifically mentioned. This situation has existed for many years and is mainly due to the fact that the German plain was considered most likely to be a battlefield between East and West. There was therefore a specific historical reason for this scheme. Nevertheless, I think it is absolutely worth a serious try. That is why I would like to underline this motion with a positive opinion. Then I come to the motion on part No. 19, by Mr Sjoerdsma, Mr Servaes and Mr Van Bommel. I have just said that the most important thing now is to move forward towards negotiations in order to reach a treaty on a nuclear ban, but I do not see it as a starting point. I am asked to take part in the substantive negotiations, which I do not think should begin now. That's why I advise against this motion.
