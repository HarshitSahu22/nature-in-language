ParlaMint-NL_2015-06-10-tweedekamer-11.u7	Mr. Chairman. We talk about class size and group size almost every year. In February 2015, I sent a letter to the Chamber about the average group size in primary education. The average group size of 23.3 pupils remained approximately the same as that of the previous year. At the same time, it is sometimes the conversation of the day in schools and also in parents. Let me say a few things about it. Does group size matter? I think so, but it's too easy to say that a group of 28 or 29 is too big or too small. I come in groups where 30 students are fine and I come in groups where 20 students are already very busy. I am therefore not in favour of taking an arbitrary standard of 28 or 29 as a maximum. That is why I would advise against Mrs Siderius' motion in respect of item No. 261. As a result, I would also advise against Mrs Ypma's motion on item No. 263. Indeed, it implicitly contains the same kind of standard, namely that, although less binding than in Mrs Siderius' motion, you must state why you may have classes larger than 29. I believe that class size should be a subject of conversation in the mr-en, but as part of the discussion on total quality and not as a specific theme. Why? With regard to the quality of education, you can ask yourself how decisive group size really is. An interesting study from the OECD has been published very recently, covering the seven myths of well-performing schools. This shows that class size is not decisive, unless it concerns very large or very small classes. Then it's a different story. With a class size of 15 you have a very different discussion with a very different price tag. The investment we are making now in education offers a wide range of choices. You have to choose whether to bet on something smaller classes or keep what you have, or whether to bet on more professionalization of the teacher. The latter, the investment in powerful teachers for the class, more after- and further training, space and time for class preparation, is, in my opinion, many times more effective than steering by class size.
