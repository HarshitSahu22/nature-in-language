ParlaMint-NL_2015-12-10-tweedekamer-8.u9	Mr. Chairman. First of all, there is the motion on part No. 44 tabled by Mr De Vries, together with Mr Ronnes and Mr Koolmees. That motion responds to the task for Rotterdam-Zuid. The government is invited to make proposals, in the event of persistently inadequate own resources, which can make an additional contribution to enable the Empire to implement the plan as soon as possible. The Rijk committed itself in 2011 to help solve the problems in Rotterdam-Zuid. We have taken a number of measures to this end. We are now getting first experience of these measures and I think it is very important that they work well. That's what they're meant for. The assignment in the motion to discuss with Rotterdam about the operation of the instruments and to make any adjustments, I am happy to take on board. I would like to leave the motion itself to the House's judgement. In his motion, Mr Bashir goes to item No. 45 about the unclearness that has arisen about the money for livingability. It is good to recall that quality of life was one of the subjects for which the Committee of Inquiry called attention, because there were also many projects funded under the title of viability which were very out of control and where there was in fact no link to the core task of housing corporations. The law initially stated that living expenses are permitted in so far as they are aimed at the own residents of the corporation. The House has extended this amendment to activities that also take place in the vicinity of complexes, but at the same time we add a maximum amount of €125. Linking an amount inevitably means defining which costs are to be booked under "liviability," otherwise that amount cannot be verified and maintained. I have also said in debates and in public that the classic housekeeper's task in and directly adjacent to a complex is, of course, simply part of the work of a housing corporation. That's not life. However, if it goes much further than that, there will soon be discussion. I have therefore also promised that in the short term I will come up with a clarification of what is or is not included in the viability budget. The way in which Mr Bashir is looking for this, namely by never taking up staff expenditure under any living conditions, seems to me to be a bit blunt. The contribution to quality of life can indeed be achieved through the use of staff. The Court will therefore be given the clarification it has asked for. However, I think that the motion tabled is too short. That's why I'm betraying her. Mr Van der Linde has not tabled a motion but has asked how I look at buying up private property by a corporation in Rotterdam-Zuid. It's allowed. In any case, it is allowed to buy up houses that are intended to be "social housing." It has also been explicitly included in the Housing Act that, in the context of the restructuring of one-sided neighbourhoods, activities may also take place outside social housing, if the market does not take them on board. The activity itself is allowed. It is of course up to the corporation together with the municipality and the tenants, in the triangle of the local performance agreements, to assess whether and how much resources are released for this purpose. Resources devoted to this purpose cannot be spent on new social housing, improving stocks, saving energy or limiting rent increases. Legally, there is no prohibition, but whether and to what extent it should be done must be decided locally. I hope I have answered the motions and the questions sufficiently.
