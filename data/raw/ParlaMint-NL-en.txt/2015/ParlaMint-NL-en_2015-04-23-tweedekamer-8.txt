ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-23-tweedekamer-8.u4	Mr. Chairman. I congratulate you on the fact that you have planned this VAO today. Today is World Books Day. This is celebrated in more than 100 countries. We have a Book Week and therefore this day may get relatively less attention with us, but it remains World Books Day. Mr Van Veen has tabled three motions. I shall begin with the first motion, the motion in part No. 7 (32300) requesting an evaluation by an independent organisation. It is also stated that the conclusions lead to the answer to the question whether the fixed book price should be maintained. I said in the general consultation that the mid-term review after two years consists of three parts: the research on cross-subsidisation, the state of play on digitisation and the implementation of the innovation agenda. The research into cross-pollination is being thoroughly addressed. I'm in consultation with the Ministry of EZ on the investigation setup. We are looking for an independent agency. I also said this during the general consultation. I don't want to presort on the results of that investigation. The study will be an important part of the broad review of the law in four years' time. I did not save against the first part of the operative part of the motion asking for an evaluation by an independent organisation aimed at the positive and negative effects of the fixed book price. However, I have difficulty with the last part of the sentence, namely that the conclusions lead to the answer to the question of whether the fixed book price should continue. If Mr Van Veen changed the last part of that sentence to 'and that the conclusions are taken into account in the wider assessment in four years', I could leave the opinion on this motion to the Chamber. If not, I'm afraid this will cause noise. If the motion isn't changed, I'll advise her. In the motion in part No. 8 (32300) the Government is invited to adjust the decision to the fixed book price in such a way as to increase the discount to individual students enrolled in a higher education institution and the discount to educational institutions or individuals or organisations mandated by them to 10% and 15% respectively. I note that this motion is actually a follow-up to the long and complicated discussion on the Love/Vordewind amendment. Unlike the amendment, the motion is in any case technically feasible. A definition of the scientific book is no longer required or the textbook as requested in the amendment. Now the user criterion is chosen. The discount refers only to prescribed literature purchased by students. I am in principle in favour of this, because for me too, it is difficult to reduce the cost of books for students. Here, however, is a warning in place. The law now provides for a discount scheme for students with a maximum discount of 5% for individual purchases and 10% for collective purchases through student associations. The current discounts already leave very little trade margin. I think the proposed 5% increase, i.e. 10% and 15% respectively, as proposed in the motion, is just right. So I leave the judgment to the Chamber. The legal assessment in four years will show whether the increase in discounts has had an impact on the offer and whether those effects are positive or negative. Finally, in Mr Van Veen's motion and Mr Bergkamp's motion, the government is to be found in item No. 9 (32300) called on the Commission to consider the possibilities for individual booksellers to expand their combined sales so that entrepreneurship can be promoted and the book promoted. I consider this motion to support policy. I have already indicated during the general consultation that I will examine, in consultation with the Media Commissioner, what individual discounts from local booksellers are possible. If they are not possible within the limits of the law, we will consider whether a change of law will help. However, it is essential that an amendment does not affect the basis of the law. The right size is therefore of great importance and also the question where it can and where not. In general consultation we have seen several examples of this. Think of the discount for a concert or a theater ticket. Then culture remains in a narrower sense. Offering a discount on handcuffs when buying Fifty shades of grey is another variant. Then we are not talking about the cultural sector in a narrow sense. In that case, I will have to expand the cultural sector very much. We also had a discussion about parking rates. So these things really need to be measured. There's quite a difference. I also said that after the summer recess I would report to the Chamber on the results of the investigation. All in all, I therefore consider the motion to support policy and leave it to the Chamber.
