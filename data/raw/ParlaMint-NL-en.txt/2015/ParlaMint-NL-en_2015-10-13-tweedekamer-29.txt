I have one motion to comment on, the motion for a resolution by Mr Agenma on part No. 491. I will say a few things about this in general. Then I will ask Mrs Agema to hold that motion so that I can give the Chamber some further information on where we stand with the vog. The following is what I say in general terms about the vog. Everyone in the youth services sector is currently obliged to have a vog. That is what we agreed after, and following that horrible tragedy in Amsterdam. At that time steps have been taken to this end and the vote has been made compulsory. Mrs Agema is now asking in her motion to take far more far-reaching steps and to use them much wider. In addition, it is good to realize that the vog gives a snapshot of course. It is issued on the basis of what police data are available on the person concerned at the time of application. A person who has not yet bumped into the lamp but is still not good, will thus get a vog. Someone can also, after a vog has been delivered, do something that is not in the hook. I have for a long time warned, including in other areas, that the vog is a snapshot. It does not offer 100% certainty that there is nothing wrong with the person concerned. I would have liked to say that, because there is always a tendency to see that vog as a kind of panacea for all problems. That really isn't the vog. Nor does it relieve institutions and voluntary organisations of their obligation to constantly look at their people and, for example, to ensure that there is good control by allowing people to act in pairs if possible. They must continue to follow their instincts in a very sensible way. At the moment we are talking about whether the vog can be used more widely. Of course, there are many advantages and disadvantages. The vog is free for volunteers. That's what Mrs. Agema says. For non-volunteers, however, the vog is not free. So that's what costs come into play. I also believe that it is the responsibility of the organisations themselves to make that duty applicable in principle where necessary. I would ask Mrs Agema to allow me to discuss the subject with the departments and with the profession. In the short term, I will come back to this. Then I will inform the Chamber as to whether I think it is wise to extend that Vog obligation. I would be very grateful if Mrs Agema would be prepared to hold on to her motion until then.
