ParlaMint-NL_2015-03-03-tweedekamer-19.u13	Mr. Chairman. During the general consultation I did not get the impression that there would be a VAO and that motions would be tabled. I see these motions for the first time now. I will try to respond as best I can on the basis of the sometimes limited explanations given by the spokesmen. I come first to Mrs van Veldhoven's motion, on item No. 527. She talks about emissions and the ETS, and she also says that we have made emission requirements for our coal plants in the Netherlands. In my view, we should first note that it is expressly excluded that emission requirements should be imposed on companies covered by the ETS, including electricity generation companies. Article 9 of the Industrial Emissions Directive excludes emission requirements, so we have not done so in the design we have chosen in the Netherlands. This provision is contained in the Industrial Emissions Directive because the European Commission wants to prevent the emission trading system from being undermined. The European Commission wants the ETS to work properly. The Commission has therefore taken a number of measures. These measures are expected to provide a price for CO of €40 per tonne in 2030. If that is the case, coal plants are automatically being pushed out of the market and cleaner forms of energy production are being brought forward. In this way, the impact envisaged will gradually occur between now and 2030. But we cannot set emission standards at the moment.
ParlaMint-NL_2015-03-03-tweedekamer-19.u14	The Minister is, of course, quite right about that. That's why it's not in the motion. The motion states: more stringent requirements for coal-fired power stations throughout Europe. That can therefore also be efficiency requirements, as we also put them in the Netherlands.
ParlaMint-NL_2015-03-03-tweedekamer-19.u15	The motion calls on the government to call for stricter requirements for coal plants in the review of the ETS in Europe. When you talk about the ETS, you talk about CO and so you talk about emissions. In her explanatory statement, Mrs Van Veldhoven also said once again that we would have made emission requirements for coal plants in the Netherlands. That's not true; we didn't. We've set yield requirements. Let us assume that Mrs Van Veldhoven meant yield requirements. Then my reaction to that is that we have indeed done so in the Netherlands. As of 1 January 2016, that requirement for efficiency for power plants shall be set at 38%. Three existing coal plants are affected by this. As of 1 July 2017, the efficiency requirement will be tightened to 40%. Two more coal plants are expected to be hit. So you reach a total of five coal plants, corresponding to the agreement we made in the energy agreement. At the moment we are meeting the efficiency requirements set by the European Commission for Europe as a whole. The European Commission is already in the process of tightening these efficiency requirements. The Netherlands is already going to comply with the tightening of efficiency requirements, as they now have in mind in the European Commission. So with our 38% and 40% standards, we are also going to meet those possibly stricter efficiency requirements. I regret to say that I do not see at all that this can be achieved. At the moment, I believe that there is really not enough support in Europe to strengthen these efficiency requirements. I shall be happy to argue for this on occasion. My associates will do the same. In the short term, however, we should not have too high expectations about this.
