ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-08-tweedekamer-7.u19	I now come to Mr Schouw's motion for a resolution. 1972 on children's interests. Storage of children is an ultimum remedium. We are developing it from the cabinet. We're not doing exactly the same thing as before. There are now child-friendly locations. We are increasingly looking at how we can do this better. We always respect the judgments of the judges. I believe that the conclusion that the motion brings to that conclusion gives the impression that we are doing something different than we are doing now. That's why I don't recommend the motion. Mr Schouw's motion for a resolution (Doc. 1973 is about the Committee of Wise Men. The same applies to the content as what I said at Mrs Voortman's motion for a resolution on item No. 1971. The review is the work of the IND and is subject to judicial review. I want to continue that policy. That's why I advise against this motion. Then I come to the motion on part No. 1974 by Mrs Gesthuizen in which she is asked to limit isolation. We deal with it reluctantly. It's the last resort. It's not a jig and bust that it happens. I find it difficult to link some of them with a view to reducing the relative percentage, precisely because the policy is not often used. I sincerely hope that it will fall, because it will not be necessary to apply these measures. That is better for everyone, but if in one year certain incidents happen that required more isolation in foreign detention, that will prove to be the case. The aim is to use all other options first. So it is not a policy on which you can steer in numbers. That's why I don't recommend the motion. Mrs Gesthuizen's other motion, which she tabled together with Mrs Voortman, calls on us to stop deporting people to Afghanistan. I would advise against this, but I would like to say that we are, of course, constantly in consultation with the Afghan authorities. There are also successfully evictions taking place at the moment. I certainly do not want to put an end to this, but I certainly do not want to leave the discussion, with the room for improvement that is still there. I now come to Mr Fritsma's motion. Cabinet policy is to limit foreign detention where possible. This motion is contrary to that, and that is why I should like to advise against it.
