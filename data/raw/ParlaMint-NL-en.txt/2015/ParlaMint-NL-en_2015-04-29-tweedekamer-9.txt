ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-29-tweedekamer-9.u15	I come to Mrs Wolbert's motion on item No. 529. Perhaps it would be nice to make a comment in that context as a result of a discussion I had this week with a mayor from the East of the Netherlands. He told me how clumsy the sirens are these days. No one knows what to do when the siren goes. What happened in his congregation? There was a big fire. The sirens have gone, but no one knew what to do. A whole group of kids cycled through the smoke. It was precisely the intention not to do that to school. When they arrived at school, it was said: the sirens have gone; we will not let you in; you must return home. And the group of children cycled home again through the same smoke. That is exactly what I have exchanged with Mrs Wolbert in general consultation: the system that we are now unleashing on crises is much better and more informative than the old alarm signals. Due to the system we now have, with NL-Alert, the regional calamity transmitter, social media, www.crisis.nl, the websites of the security regions, possibly supplemented by other resources developed by the security regions themselves, and the development of NL-Alert in the coming years, with 4G messages being received, the number of people receiving messages will increase enormously. It is already clear that more than 80% of people are informed about reports from NL-Alert via content, so that they know exactly what is going on and what they need to do: whether they should close windows and doors and stay inside or whether they should go outside because of the disaster that is happening. So I see no room or reason to change the policy that my predecessor has put in place, namely ending the alert on 1 January 2018. I therefore advise against the motion.
