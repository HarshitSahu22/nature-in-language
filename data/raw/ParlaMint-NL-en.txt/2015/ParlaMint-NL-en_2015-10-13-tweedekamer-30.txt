It has been discussed for a short time in the debate: the basic principle of the Council for the Protection of Children, which seeks analogy, is that children under 18 years of age are not able to act. There is then a relationship of authority with the parents. The Council of Children's Protection then makes a ruling on that relationship of authority. This is fundamentally different from the problem that could arise in the elderly. Then there are a number of possibilities. If, for example, someone can no longer see the consequences of his actions as a whole, then there is a curate through the canton court. If there is a reduced capacity of the interests, a director may be appointed. And if, for example, someone cannot take care of himself financially, there is the possibility to appoint a mentor. I note that the analogy with the Council on Children's Protection may be less significant and that there are already a number of measures that provide for the situation where an older person is in trouble, such as an act-making decision, a curator or a mentor. In short, I do not see any added value in re-examination of the possibility of anything similar to that of the Council for the Protection of the Child. That's why I don't recommend the motion.
