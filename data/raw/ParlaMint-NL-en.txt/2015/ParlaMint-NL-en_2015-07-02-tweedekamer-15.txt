ParlaMint-NL_2015-07-02-tweedekamer-15.u6	Mr. Chairman. I will get an answer to the last motion, but it will probably come naturally during my speech. I'll start with the motion in part No. 83 by Mrs Jacobi and Mr Van Helvert, on the speed limiters on the water. It calls for research into whether water enforcement can be made smarter and simpler, thereby achieving an alternative to red tape. I agree that smarter and more efficient enforcement will be investigated. There are also a number of tests with laser and other systems. I'll inform the Chamber about the results. So I see the motion as a policy support. Then I come to the motion on part No. 84 by Mrs Jacobi and Mrs De Vries on the boat from Holwerd to Nes and the delays that are taking place there. The Secretary of State's letter offers a comprehensive comprehensive approach. I cannot speed up the open planning process in such a way that everything is known on 1 September, but I am certainly prepared to give the Chamber a first look at the solutions that are feasible and realistic for the shorter term, including an initial indication of the costs of breakthrough and PIANC standards, which Mrs Jacobi asked for. These do not apply to all waterways. The Wadden Sea does not have an explicit guideline system. However, there are limits in the PKB (planological core decision). I'm willing to include the modification of the PKB in the evaluation. So I see the motion as a policy support. In the motion in part No. Mr Bashir asked me to take part in the removal of unnecessary requirements in the CCNR and CESNI and to inform the Chamber twice a year. I'm also against unnecessary demands. Mr Bashir knows that. I am also in constant conversation with other countries in the CCNR and CESNI and I am prepared to inform the Chamber regularly. Half-yearly is often; this is not the case internationally. However, I believe that we are also having a six-monthly discussion on shipping. So I can always take the state of affairs with me in that consultation. So I see the motion as a policy support. Then I come to the motion on part No. 86 by Mrs De Boer and Mrs Jacobi on the subscriptions for the collection of household waste. The cost of subscriptions can indeed be reduced if more steps are taken. I hold on to volunteering, also because this is required by the Mannheim Act. That is what I said in the debate. However, I am certainly prepared to look with the sector at how we can stimulate the reduction of subscriptions. I will speak with a small delegation shortly, with the women, but I will also speak with the other larger interest groups. I propose that this point be included in the evaluation I promised the Chamber for June 2016. I shall therefore leave the motion to the House's judgement. The motion in part No. 87 by Mrs De Boer and Mrs Jacobi are concerned with the competitiveness of the Netherlands and inspection tariffs. As a result of this motion, I would like to stress once again that there is no such thing as a shift in load flow due to higher inspection rates at the moment. I have already indicated in general consultation that I am in favour of a pragmatic approach. I would like to look, together with my colleague from EZ and the business community, at concrete situations in which costs lead to poor situations. For those specific cases, we will come up with a solution. It is a state policy to keep the cost of tariffs for business as low as possible and to constantly think about alternatives. So I see the motion as supporting that government policy and I agree with it. If the motion is adopted, I will pass it on to the person responsible for the NVWA, so that it can also be further developed. I'll leave the verdict on the motion to the Chamber. Finally, I have the motion in part No. 88 by Mr Van Helvert. It calls on the government to draw up an action plan to promote the transport of goods by water. I would like to discourage this motion, not because I do not think such a plan is important, but because with the elaboration of the maritime strategy, the MIRT and the Better Benutten programme we already have an action programme for promoting the transport of goods by water and by waterway. As far as I am concerned, a new action plan is merely an administrative act of bringing together all the plans we have here and there. I hereby advise against this motion.
