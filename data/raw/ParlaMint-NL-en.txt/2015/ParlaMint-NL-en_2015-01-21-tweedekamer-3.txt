ParlaMint-NL_2015-01-21-tweedekamer-3.u4	Mr. Chairman. I dissuade both motions. In the motion in part No. 126 the Government is invited not to include financial frameworks in the request for an opinion to the Culture Council. That's not one bridge too far, but a lot of bridges too far, as far as I'm concerned. I have agreed in general consultation to ask the Culture Council to reflect on the post-2017 period. That's an open question. Why did I do that? Well, because I realize that the council is going to advise on the period 2017-2020, which is scheduled to fall under a subsequent cabinet. So we have no idea. It can go either way, I say. There is no expectation that it will, of course, provide more financial space for culture. During the period of preparation, I can only rely on the resources set aside in the coalition agreement. There are no additional resources for culture. If I did go into that, I'd sign a blank check. How's the procedure going? Before 1 April 2015, the Culture Council will deliver an opinion; at the end of May I will send a letter of principle with a draft regulation; in June a debate may take place with the Chamber and in October 2015 the arrangement will be final. On the basis of that arrangement, institutions shall apply between 1 November 2015 and 1 February 2016. The Culture Council advises on this in May 2016 and the decisions fall on Princess Day in September 2016. Within this framework, the agreements of the government agreement apply. I will give the Council of Culture the opportunity to form its own opinion about the period thereafter on the basis of an open question. The second motion, the motion in part No. 127, I also advise against. This calls for an understanding of the impact of the cuts and what is needed for a future-proof cultural infrastructure. We already have a lot of information on this, so I think this motion is really superfluous. In general, we have discussed in depth the issue of culture in the Beeld, the exploration of the Council for Culture and other insights that we already have and will get. In short, we have a lot of information and it is now important to continue to monitor how the cultural sector is doing. Next year we do this again with Culture in Beeld and in the meantime we ask the Council for Culture to think about the contours of a new BIS period. I say to Mr Monasch that the question of regionalisation is included in the request for an opinion to the Council on Culture.
