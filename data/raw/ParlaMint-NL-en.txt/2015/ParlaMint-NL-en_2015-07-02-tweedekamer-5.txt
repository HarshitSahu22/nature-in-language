ParlaMint-NL_2015-07-02-tweedekamer-5.u6	Mr. Chairman. Mrs Gesthuizen has in part no. 2014 tabled a motion asking the government to be extremely reluctant to object to an alternative location elsewhere in Russia. In certain cases, the conclusion is indeed drawn that the person concerned can live safely in Russia if that person chooses another place of residence. That is an individual test, so it is not categorically said to everyone: there are places where it can be done, go there. The motion calls for restraint in the object of this. I think that is a bit double, because in my view we are currently reluctant to apply this policy. So I think the motion calls for even more restraint. As long as we are working under the current post, I intend to do it as we do it now: to the extent that we are now reluctant to object to it, and to review this element at the next post of office. However, I do not agree to increase the restraint in the meantime, and I therefore advise against this motion. The same applies to the motion in part No. 2015, which calls for the designation of Russian lhbt as a vulnerable minority group. I'm acting on the basis of the post I have now, and on the basis of that information, I don't think it's necessary to do so. If in a subsequent letter of office it is noted that the situation has deteriorated, I will, of course, determine what is needed on the basis of that letter of office. However, I would advise against amending it at this time. The motion in part No. 2016 requests to register. I can imagine that need in itself, but the IND doesn't send by numbers. If there are ten people coming, who are all entitled to stay, then there is a 100% score. If there's no one on the next ten with the right to stay, you have a 0% score. In either case, there can be no consequences. The percentage does not say anything about what happens in policy, because each individual case is considered conscientiously. So I'm not a big fan of registration, because that's more than just filling in and getting out of the computer. This is all very intensive work that costs a lot of money. If the majority of the House appreciates it, I will of course simply carry it out, but I myself do not think it is proportionate to spend all this time and all that money on it with the aim of getting numbers, of which it is still the question of whether I can make them public. In fact, I need a substantial number to prevent it from being traced back to individuals; this could cause a problem. In addition, I do not think it says anything about effectiveness or how the policy is implemented. That's why I advise against this motion.
