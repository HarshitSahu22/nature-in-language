ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-23-tweedekamer-9.u2	Mr. Chairman. The VVD is concerned about the decline in the number of pupils trained in the workplace, the so-called bbl'ers. In four years' time, the number of students who choose this combination of work and learning has fallen from 170,000 to 100,000. Although the number of pupils in the full-time version of the MBO, the sphere, has increased in recent years, it has not happened to the same extent. This means that students will refrain from MBO training if they do not find a bbl-place. That's a shame. For pupils, but also for employers, because the link between education and the labour market is nowhere more good than for these pupils. 95% of them find a job within a year of graduating. We therefore want to reverse the decline in the number of bbl-spots. In the AO we have made several proposals to make it more attractive for employers to make workplaces available. Fortunately, the Minister has also made several commitments. On one important point, however, we still see an opportunity to achieve more for pupils looking for a b.B. place, namely at the collective bargaining table. In 14 collective agreements, additional agreements have been made on the salary of pupils in the b.b. These agreements were made at a time when the sector was doing well and the students wanted to take advantage of them. That was right, but in the meantime these agreements are the main reason for employers to refrain from offering learning places. That is why we are tabling the next motion. ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-23-tweedekamer-9.u9	Mr. Chairman. I'll start with the motion in part No. 43 by Mrs Siderius. It calls on the government to set up a training quota as a pilot scheme for companies with more than 150 employees. I'm deceiving that motion. We have discussed this widely with each other. I am not in favour of taking responsibility unilaterally with the business community. There have also been proposals from the Chamber to unilaterally transfer responsibility to the MBO schools. I've always dissuaded those proposals, because that's how I make the other party lazy. He can sit back and say, "They must do it." The beauty but also the difficulty of the MBO is that these traineeships are an essential part of cooperation and exchange between business and schools. There are SMEs that have internships and also large companies that have internships. In fact, many rocks like to do business with large companies because they have multiple internships. So there too, I would not find it right to do this only for large companies. I am therefore not convinced that imposing an obligation on one party will help. Also as a test, this goes too far for me. I therefore advise against this motion. Then the motion by Mr Lucas, who asks the government to talk to employers' organizations and trade unions to see, in the interests of pupils and their job opportunities, whether new agreements can be made that will increase the opportunities for young people at a job place and thus in a job. In the current wording, I advise against this motion, because the recitals place great emphasis on the agreements in the collective agreement and, in particular, on the minimum youth wage. If I read the motion correctly, Mrs Lucas concludes that these agreements have reduced the number of bbl-places. Now we know that the number of bbl-places has fallen and that that is worrying, but also that there are several causes for this. I also note that employers believe that the minimum wage provisions are a problem. Secondly, I note that the trade unions have sent a letter in which they deny it and indicate that they are looking at it differently. Thirdly, that is perhaps the most complicated thing about a collective agreement between the two sides of industry. I do not want to be a third party in this, because then I will break through the way in which we have arranged the discussion between the government and the social partners in the Netherlands. I assume that social partners will take into account both the interests of current workers and the need for new influxes in order to meet the demand for replacement and enlargement. What I can do is, in a discussion on this point, to convey the concerns of the Chamber to Mr Asscher of Social Affairs, in which I ask him to bring this point to the attention of the Labour Foundation. If Mrs Lucas wishes to reformulate her motion in this way, then I would leave it to the Chamber, but also with the considerations of both sides, both employers and trade unions.

ParlaMint-NL_2015-04-23-tweedekamer-9.u15	Mr Van Meenen's motion for a resolution 46 to offer flexibility in the curriculum and to explore the possibility of a tailor-made course for re-enterers and cormorants, I consider to support policy. It is already possible, for example, to follow through the route of competences acquired elsewhere and by granting exemptions an education programme, whether funded or not, aimed at certification. In addition, the cabinet is currently conducting two reconnaissances. In order to strengthen the position of adults with MBO levels 2 and 3 on the labour market, the Cabinet explores, in cooperation with the branches, the usefulness, necessity and potential of the expansion of reduced diploma-oriented training pathways at level 4. In this exploration, given the importance attached to language and arithmetic, or adults may participate only if they master the language and calculation requirements at least the level of the initial qualification. We are also exploring the possibilities to make it possible, on an experimental basis, for students who have taken part in the third learning pathway and who have achieved all the elements during this course, except for language and/or arithmetic requirements, to issue a certificate recognized by the government. The requirement of citizenship and English will also be taken into account. I intend to include the results of both explorations in my response to the Lucas-Jadnansing motion on strengthening the flexibility of the MBO system. I expect to be able to send the reaction to that motion to the Chamber in June of this year. In view of all this, I consider this motion to support policy and leave it to the Chamber to decide.
