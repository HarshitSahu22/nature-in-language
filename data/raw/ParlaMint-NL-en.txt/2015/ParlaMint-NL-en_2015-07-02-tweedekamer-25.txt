There is new dynamism through these agreements in higher education: more excellence, more profiling and higher student satisfaction. I look forward to the evaluation at the end of the experiment and also to the answer to my question on how to ensure greater acceptance in future quality agreements. That is why I find the motions calling for the right of assent very premature, even though I am entirely in favour of a great deal of support. I find the motions very easy, too. It is also a question of involving more stakeholders, more stakeholders in these agreements. We discussed it in the debate. It's not just one group. For example, the Minister himself mentioned regional interests, business and teachers. So it's not just about the students. In addition, participation already has instruments, but they are not yet fully exploited. That could be much better than it is now. That is why it is very premature to draw it all quickly to this new instrument. Moreover, the turnout in the elections of co-determination councils does not exceed 10%. In short, I think that the parties who are submitting these motions are getting out of hand very quickly. I would rather wait for the evaluation, because then we can really make new agreements with broad support. The VVD will certainly vote against the motions.

ParlaMint-NL_2015-07-02-tweedekamer-25.u7	In the motion in part No. 478 Mr Mohandis and Mr Rog asked the government to limit the binding study opinion to the first year of the Bachelor's term of office in the forthcoming quality agreements. I have to discourage this motion. The binding study advice in later years................................................................................................................................................................... One purpose of an experiment is to learn. Then you check if there's any reason to go through with it. We are going to evaluate the experiment in 2018, and that is, as far as I am concerned, the time to see if it is an instrument to promote study success or not. I do not think it is desirable to anticipate that. Furthermore, I do not want to anticipate the question of what quality agreements should look like. I will also say this when I see Mr Van Meenen's motion for a response. In that sense, I agree with Mr Duisenberg. Let us also learn from the current experiments: performance agreements are an experiment how we can achieve better agreements in a next round. I therefore advise against this motion. In the motion by Mr Mohandis and, among other things, Mr Van Meenen in part No. 479 the government is invited to come forward with proposals to give training committees the status of co-determination with corresponding powers. I would like to ask the petitioner to hold this motion until we discuss the Act on strengthening the administrative power of educational institutions, which will shortly be coming to the Chamber. I have already promised that this law will also strengthen the position of the training committees. I therefore believe that the treatment of this law is the right time to discuss such wishes. I have said before that I consider the training committees to be very important, as do participation, but we must be careful not to treat the training committees in the same way as participation. Contrary to the chosen participation, a training committee consists of experts. From this expert role, a training committee can advise par excellence. It does, however, have a different role to that of an elected representative. The training committees have an important role to play in accreditation and in training evaluations, and I must say that I agree with Mr Duisenberg that there are more ways of strengthening this role than mere formalisation. I happened to talk to students from Utrecht this morning who had good ideas about this. Let us also discuss them with each other. I understand the desire to strengthen the position of training committees. I think that we should have a good discussion on this, also in view of the legal aspects in relation to participation. I therefore ask the author to hold that motion. If he does not wish to do so, I would advise against the motion, especially on procedural grounds. Mr Mohandis asked a question about the experiment on tuition fees differentiation and its relationship with the strategic agenda. The strategic agenda will have the Chamber very soon. I have already promised that I will return to the decision on the difference in tuition fees in that strategic agenda. That was a proposed experiment that we maintained in the context of the study advance. I will explain more in the strategic agenda how I intend to proceed with this. In their motion in part No. 480 Mr Van Meenen and Mr Jasper van Dijk ask me to give formal consent to participation councils on quality agreements. The same is true of Mr Mohandis' second motion for a resolution. I think it would be a good time to discuss the wishes of training committees in the Act on strengthening administrative power, and I also believe that it is not desirable to anticipate quality agreements at this stage, because we are involved in a number of experiments, namely the experiment performance agreements, but also the experiment binding study advice. I therefore call for this motion to be retained. If Mr Van Meenen does not wish to do so, then I can only advise against this motion.
