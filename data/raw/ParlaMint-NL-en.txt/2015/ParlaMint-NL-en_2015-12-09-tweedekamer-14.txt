ParlaMint-NL_2015-12-09-tweedekamer-14.u5	Mr. Chairman. In the motion in part No. 95 the government is asked to appeal to all municipalities to purchase sufficient grace care at a reasonable rate. It is indeed already the case that grace is part of the Wmo 2015. This is also mentioned in the motion for a resolution. Municipalities therefore have a duty to ensure that sufficient grace care is purchased. They shall provide appropriate facilities. The College of B and W is accountable to the city council. That's settled, too. I do not think there is any reason at the moment for further regulation on this point. I would also like to mention the hand-pick-up which has become available to all municipalities in the last summer. This assistance is specific to the grace provision and has been drawn up for the purposes of the implementation of the grace policy by the municipalities. I therefore advise against this motion. In the motion in part No. 96 the government is asked to develop the programme around the dementia-friendly society, to roll it out and to formulate objectives. The Dementia Friends of Alzheimer's Netherlands programme, drawn up as part of a sub-plan of dementia, is indeed an essential part of the policy of achieving a more dementia-friendly society. It will indeed continue to be filled in. As far as I am concerned, this is also the case in the light of the opinion of the working group on dementia. I hope to receive that advice next week. I will send that to the Chamber as soon as possible. I will inform the Chamber about this policy before 1 April. I see the motion as a support for policy, and I leave it to the Chamber to judge it. Then I come to the motion on part No. 97. That motion is about case management. The Care Standard Dementia, signed by all parties, states that a case manager should be available to everyone from the moment the diagnosis of dementia has been made or that there is a suspicion of dementia. We have so arranged this in the Health Insurance Act under the claim "neighborhood care." I do not think it is right to include a separate "neighborhood care" claim because we have already arranged that anyone who is entitled to that claim can also assert a right to case management. Sometimes it's better to turn on a specialised case manager. In other cases, there is a district nurse with an additional specialization for case management in dementia. Appropriate arrangements should be made locally between insurers and suppliers. I want to keep that space for that, because in practice you can see that they are sometimes case managers and sometimes district nurses with a specialization. Sometimes there are also combinations throughout the chain. I think it's good to keep that space local. As far as I'm concerned, that claim, including case management, remains in the forefront of the Health Insurance Act. I want insurers and providers to stick to that, too. That is why I would advise against this motion calling for a separate claim.
ParlaMint-NL_2015-12-09-tweedekamer-14.u6	Until we changed the law, the case manager was actually doing really well. ParlaMint-NL_2015-12-09-tweedekamer-14.u7	A separate "case management" claim is in fact already settled. Mrs Bruins Slot distinguishes between "case manager" and "case management." I think it is very important to make it possible, depending on the growing situation in a city or with providers, where sometimes there are case managers and sometimes district nurses or combinations, that you sometimes choose for the case manager and sometimes for the district nurse or for other arrangements in the chain. I note that the claim is settled and that the way you organize it can be a little different, depending on the existing offer and what you really want to renew. I think you're gonna put the horse behind the cart if you set up a separate "case manager" claim now, because you're gonna send it to a position and not a claim. That's why I'm sticking to my judgment.
