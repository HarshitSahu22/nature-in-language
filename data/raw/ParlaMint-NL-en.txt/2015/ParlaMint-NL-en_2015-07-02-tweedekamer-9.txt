I indicated that it could not have been a policy choice when we did the legal treatment here, because I was not aware of that choice at the time. It was a technical choice and therefore not a policy choice. As a result of my defense of the bill in the First Chamber, some have made a different judgment. But to the spokesperson of the VVD Group, I expressly say that we must not interfere directly or indirectly with ongoing procedures in any way. In fact, if we were to do so, we would again cause a great deal of legal uncertainty in relation to other judgments. Nevertheless, I think it is a good thing that this motion expresses the nature of the legal process at the time. I hope that this will help with future interpretations of this part of our legislation. In her motion in part No. 35 Ms Siderius asked the government to come up with different scenarios to optimise the impact on the size of child support and to submit them to the Chamber. I have difficulty with this motion because it contains a number of assumptions. One is that we would know whether we would agree on the optimal effects on the size of child support. And, of course, that's not true. This ultimately depends very much on the circumstances of the case and the relationship between those who have to agree on child support or come to a judge. Finally, if you do not find these two arguments sufficient, another argument is that in this case it would also be a cross-section of a lot of pending proceedings, including with the Supreme Court. This could have negative consequences. So if you didn't find the first two arguments convincing, another argument would be that I don't think this is the right time. That's why I don't recommend the motion.
