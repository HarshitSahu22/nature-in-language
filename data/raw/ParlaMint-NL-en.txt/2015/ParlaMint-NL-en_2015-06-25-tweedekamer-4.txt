The motion in part No. Mr Van Meenen asked the government to clarify the fight against digital literacy as part of the action programme Tel with Language. I'll leave the verdict on this motion to the Chamber. I agree with Mr Van Meenen that digital skills are important and closely linked to low literacy, because in practice, reading, writing, calculating and digital skills are also done in conjunction with internet banking or filling in the tax return. At the same time, a computer course only makes sense if students have sufficient language and writing skills. This coherence is part of the approach Tel along with Language. It is also the basis of the local course offer. Moreover, it is also a particular part of the ambitious pilot project on education for women, which aims to combine digital skills and language and computing skills, but I recognise that it could have been given even more explicit attention in the action programme. So in that respect, I recognize the motion and leave it to the Chamber. In the motion in part No. 41, also by Mr Van Meenen, is the request to develop a long-term approach to combating low literacy, including digital low literacy. The program Tel met Taal is a program for the next three years, in which we have tried to combine everything that happens within my department in relation to language, but also to work together with other departments, with SZW and VWS, and thus to support local and regional initiatives as best as possible. After three years, the municipalities are supposed to have secured the approach much more structurally. After 2018, attention will always be paid to language promotion from my department. Funds have also been earmarked for this purpose, i.e. 10 million for future national support. These resources are structural. The resources of the other departments for this action programme are occasional over the next three years. If Mr Van Meenen means that we must always pay attention to low-literacy, my response is that we will continue to do so. Then I would see this motion as a policy support. If he says that we must now also make the resources of other departments structural, I cannot speak for my colleagues. Let us then first see what the present action programme delivers. In that case, I must discourage the motion.
