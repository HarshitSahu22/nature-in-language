The Senate of the Republic of Poland honors the memory of St. Brother Albert Chmielowski January insurgent, artist and protector of the poor On the 100th anniversary of his death. Albert Chmielowski was born in 1845 in Igołomi near Krakow. At the age of only 17 at the outbreak of the January Uprising in 1863, he entered the fight for his homeland's freedom. He was badly wounded during the fights, which paid with amputation and Russian slavery. Thanks to his family's efforts he got out of captivity and went to Paris in 1864 and then, after the amnesty of 1865, to Warsaw. Albert Chmielowski studied at the Academy of Fine Arts in Munich, where he became friends with Stanisław Witkiewicz, Józef Chełmoński, Aleksandr Gierymski, Leon Wyczółkowski and many other outstanding artists. From this period came his religious paintings, including the most famous Ecca Homo, depicting Christ the Suffering. He gradually abandoned painting to care for those in need. He lived in the city heating room in Krakow with homeless, alcoholics and poor people, as their brother. On 25 August 1887 he put on the habit of the third order of St. Franciszka in the Chapel of the Loreta Church of Capuchin Fathers in Cracow and since then was known as Brother Albert. He gave rise to the Albertine Brothers' Assembly and the Albertine Sisters' Assembly, which he founded. The most famous and visited are the hermitage of both these conventions in Kalatówki in Zakopane, there is also a chamber commemorating the life of Albert Chmielowski, and the church of the Albertinek Sisters in Cracow, where there is a painting of.Ecce Homo. Brother Albert Chmielowski's life motto was: "Be good as bread." Brother Albert's great influence and moral authority gave his friends the impulse to follow in his footsteps. Only Brother Albert's insistence and reminding them that they had a different calling changed their intentions. For outstanding merits for artistic activities and in the field of social work, President Ignacy Mościcki, on 10 November 1938, posthumously awarded Albert Chmielowski the Grand Vest of the Order of the Rebirth of Poland. The character of Brother Albert Chmielowski inspired the young Karol Wojtyla to write the drama "The Brother of Our God," which was founded in 1944 and 1950. Brother Albert Chmielowski literally understood the call of Christ to be merciful toward another man and, having abandoned everything, to follow Christ. Sharing the fate of the disinherited, homeless, and poor, did not consider charity a real help. In charity he saw only a calming conscience of the rich, not real help. In recognition of the heroic virtues of Brother Albert Chmielowski and confirming his cult, Pope John Paul II announced him on June 22, 1983 on the blessed Błonia in Cracow, and on November 12, 1989 in Rome. I'm a saint. On December 25, 2016, the death of Saint Brother Albert Chmielowski coincides with the end of 2016 announced by Pope Francis the Year of Mercy.
