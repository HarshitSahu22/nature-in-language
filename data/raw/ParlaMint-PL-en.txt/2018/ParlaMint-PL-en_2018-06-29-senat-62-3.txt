ParlaMint-PL_2018-06-29-senat-62-3.u87	Mr. Marshal! High Izbo! I address my statement to the Vice-President of the Council of Ministers, Minister of Culture and National Heritage, Mr Piotr Gliński. Dear Prime Minister, In the last six months we have seen an increased attack of international, foreign forces on Poland and Poles in terms of the cause and responsibility for the Holocaust of Jews during World War II. In order to resist this attack, the Polish authorities should act both outside the country and against indifferent or anti-patriotic environments in Poland by more offensive methods. As for the historical policy of our country, we have the comfort that, like none of the other nations, we need not be ashamed of the actions of our ancestors during World War II. So all you have to do is objectively describe the events, the history of World War II, and pass on this knowledge to our fellow countrymen and abroad by various means, including from the sphere of culture. Three members of the government have a special responsibility in this regard: the Minister for Foreign Affairs, the Minister for National Education and the Minister for Culture and National Heritage. Your position in the government, your position as Deputy Prime Minister, and the diversity of methods at your disposal, make it your responsibility to conduct an effective Polish historical policy. I will, of course, not instruct you on how to implement it, but I would like to draw attention to one aspect that has not yet been present in a broad political and public debate. Well, I think that it is essential that Poland and Poles are blamed for their accomplice with the Holocaust. Along with evil will and hatred for us, of course. It suffers far-reaching ignorance, even ignorance about the war fate of Poland and Poles, in particular the great loss suffered by our nation and state as a result of the aggression of the Third Reich and the Soviet Union, as a result of the occupation, changes in borders and consequences of the delayed indirect consequences of World War II. In reducing the area of this ignorance, various means that you already have can be helpful, but in my opinion it might be useful to create a museum of Polish war losses in Warsaw, in a symbolic place by its own history, in the capital city of Poland. It would document, describe and expose the martyrdom of the Polish people, losses suffered by the Polish state And the most important ones, the human ones, and the material ones. The seat of the museum should be the capital of the state, the representative place most often visited by foreign delegations. A visit to this museum should be included in the programme of any such visit. Warsaw is also a tourist town, the purpose of foreign trips, as well as trips of Polish children and young people, which facilitates the achievement of the educational objectives of the museum. Currently there is no museum of such character in Poland, centrally located and easily accessible. Numerous monuments of history, museums in places of extermination scattered in Poland, for obvious reasons cannot fully picture the total war losses of Poland. Nor does the Museum of the Second World War in Gdansk, presenting a broader geographical theme and subject matter. The decision to set up a museum of Polish war losses is of course a secondary issue, it would be particularly symbolic in 2018, the year of Poland's regaining independence. Of these 100 years, we survived equally half, from 1939 to 1989, being the subject of aggression, under occupation or as a non-sovereign state. The museum exhibition presenting our war losses and their effect, which is the lost benefits of this half century, would explain our role during World War II, but also the current geopolitical, economic and demographic situation of Poland. Dear Prime Minister, the suggestion that it is necessary to set up a museum of Polish war losses was already formulated in September 2017 in a statement addressed to the President of the Council of Ministers. Unfortunately, the laconic response to this statement received from the Foreign Minister was not relevant to this subject. All the more so, I ask for a clear and concrete response from the Prime Minister. With respect, Jerzy Czerwiński, Senator of the Republic of Poland.
