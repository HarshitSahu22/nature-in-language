ParlaMint-BE_2016-09-28-54-commissie-ic498x.u25	Mr Nollet, the climate accountability mechanism, established by the special financing law, has not yet entered its operational phase. All the conditions necessary for its effective implementation have not yet been met. As a reminder, the special law sets multi-year trajectories of greenhouse gas emissions in buildings in the residential and tertiary sectors in kilotonnes of CO2 equivalent for each of the Regions, starting in 2015. The difference between actual greenhouse gas emissions for these sectors and its emission trajectories shall be converted into a bonus or malus, depending on whether the actual emissions are lower or exceed the emission ceilings on the basis of the average price of emission allowances. The corresponding amount is allocated to the Region or the Federal State, respectively in bonus or malus situations. The bonuses to be paid to the Regions will be financed from the federal share of Belgian revenues from the auctioning of emission allowances under the European ETS. Any mali will have to be used to finance the federal climate policy. From an accounting point of view, the calculation of these annual differences between actual emissions and ceilings established by emission trajectories can only be achieved once the data, i.e. greenhouse gas emission inventories, are available and formally validated. These inventories are carried out with a two-year lag. Emissions data for 2015 will therefore not be available until 2017. Emission inventories are notified in the first half of the year, but are only formally validated once the review process, organised at European and international level, is completed in the last quarter. Another condition to be met with regard to the payment of bonuses by the federal State is that the federal share of revenues in the auctioning of ETS allowances, which the special law identifies as the sole source for the payment of these bonuses, be released. These revenues are, until further notice, still blocked in an account pending the conclusion and entry into force of the cooperation agreement on burden sharing. In addition, a series of questions have been raised by lawyers in my administration, as well as by regional administrations, regarding the implementation of the accountability mechanism. These issues, which have been the subject of a note communicated to the members of the National Climate Commission, concern the definition of multi-year trajectories, the scope of application – which are the buildings concerned – and some inconsistencies in the law concerning the timing of the determination of deviations, the payment of bonuses and mali – the timing provided by the law which does not correspond to the availability of emission data – and, finally, the role and composition of the advisory committee provided for by the law. Some leads are proposed in this note, particularly with respect to a revision of trajectories, and still need to be discussed within the CNC. Several steps therefore remain to be taken before the climate accountability mechanism can be made operational. However, I have taken the necessary steps to ensure that, as soon as the conditions are met, this mechanism can enter into force by establishing an organic budget fund for that fund.
