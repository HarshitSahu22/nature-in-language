ParlaMint-BE_2022-06-29-voorlopig-55-commissie-ic837x.u1	Mr Secretary of State, my question has already been raised, for which I apologise. The Federal Planning Office estimates the total cost of the administrative burden for Belgian companies and self-employed persons at about EUR 5 billion. This is a decrease of 27% compared to 2016 and even of 42% compared to 2000. However, we note that the costs for large companies increased by 22% between 2016 and 2020 and that the costs for self-employed people even increased by 32% over the same period. A survey shows that very few companies and self-employed persons consider that administrative costs have decreased over the period between 2019 and 2020. A very large majority of companies and self-employed people even consider that administrative costs have increased significantly. The survey also looked at the assessment by companies and self-employed persons of the quality of legislation in the areas of taxation, employment and the environment. The quality of the regulation is apparently the most problematic in the tax field. Companies and self-employed are critical of the intelligibility of the regulation and its adaptation to all situations. The High Council for Self-employed and SMEs has put forward a number of proposals which could lead to fiscal administrative simplification. I have the following questions, Mr Secretary of State. Can you explain the increase in costs for large companies and for the self-employed between 2016 and 2020? Can you explain the significant gap between the evolution of the estimated administrative costs and their assessment by enterprises and the self-employed? What measures do you or will you take to improve the quality of the regulation, particularly in the field of taxation? What is your opinion on the proposals of the Supreme Court for the Self-employed and SMEs?
ParlaMint-BE_2022-06-29-voorlopig-55-commissie-ic837x.u2	Madam President, Mrs Goethals, I would like to start by reminding you that the evolution of the costs of administrative burdens analysed in the report you mentioned relates to a period of four years, from 2016 to 2020, while the perception of companies and self-employed persons referred to refers to a period of two years, 2019 and 2020. There is no estimate of the costs of administrative burdens in 2018 and it is therefore not possible to know how the costs of administrative burdens developed from 2018 to 2020. I would also draw your attention to the fact that 2020 was a particularly difficult year for both self-employed and businesses and that these difficulties have had an impact on the perception of administrative burdens. To answer your questions with figures, the increase in the administrative burden for large companies is almost exclusively explained by the increase in the number of large companies between 2016 and 2020. On the basis of the information provided by the DAV, the number of large companies increased from 1,381 to 1,765, an increase of 28%. It concerns both industrial and service activities. If the population of the large companies had remained the same in 2020 as in 2016 in terms of number of enterprises and activity structures, the increase in the total cost of administrative burdens for that category of companies would have been only 0.03 %, i.e. almost stable. Also, the self-employed population increased by 11.5 % between 2016 and 2020, from 411.000 self-employed to 459.000 self-employed. This increase concerned all sectors of activity, except the agricultural sector. If the self-employed population had remained the same in 2020 as in 2016 in terms of the number of self-employed and activity structures, the increase in the total cost of the administrative burden of the self-employed would have been 19.7 %. As far as the regulatory areas with the greatest impact are concerned, it is, in fact, mainly taxation regulation which is the main cause of the increase observed. 92 percent of the increase is explained by the increase in administrative costs related to taxes. In view of the nature of the costs, the increase in external costs is mainly responsible for increasing the costs of administrative burdens for self-employed workers. Indeed, between 2016 and 2020, the latter increased by 74 percent, while internal costs increased by only 10 percent. In particular, the cost of services purchased by the self-employed to help them complete the administrative formalities has thus increased. Finally, the DAV took note of the report of the High Council for Self-employed and SMEs (HRZKMO). This will be examined in the update extension of the Federal Action Plan on Administrative Simplification 2022-2024. For my part, a number of instruments have been introduced, such as the digital portfolio, the virtual identity card and the eBox. A major regulatory project is also under way to facilitate the exchange. All these processes aim to further reduce administrative burdens, in particular for SMEs and large companies as well as for the self-employed. For the specific processes for the other ministers, and in particular the Minister of Finance, I invite you to address your question to them.
