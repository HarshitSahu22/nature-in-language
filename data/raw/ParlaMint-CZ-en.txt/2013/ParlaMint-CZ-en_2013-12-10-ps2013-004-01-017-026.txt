Madam President, ladies and gentlemen, we have one of the biggest amendments to foreign law in the last three years, since Amendment No. 427/2010 Coll., which transferred decision-making powers from the Services of Foreign Police to the Ministry of the Interior of the Czech Republic. It is therefore no longer appropriate for such an important law not to be discussed by the Chamber of Deputies in standard procedure and to be approved at first reading. This amendment is intended to implement a Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on a uniform procedure for the processing of applications for uniform residence permits and work in the territory of a Member State for third-country nationals and on a common set of rights of third-country workers legally residing in a Member State. As is already apparent from the title of this Directive, it provides for two basic areas, namely the way in which this single residence and work permit is to be issued, the purpose of the directive being to simplify or mitigate the whole process by bureaucracy and, secondly, the rights to be granted to holders of this single authorisation. The first area is proposed by the Ministry of the Interior of the Czech Republic to address the issue of the so-called employee cards, under conditions that need to be discussed. This is a completely new setting of the system of working migration of foreigners to the Czech Republic. At the same time, it is another extension of the power of officials of the Ministry of the Interior of the Czech Republic who will now decide not only on residential matters, but also on what specific work foreigners will be allowed to carry out in businesses, which may mean another significant administrative burden. As regards the implementation of the rights conferred on foreigners by the directive, in the opinion of some, the government proposal is in direct conflict with the directive, namely with Art. Having regard to the proposal from the High Representative of the Union for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy, Article 4 (1) (a) (e) and (e) Article 4 (1) (a) (b) the Directive. However, a government proposal does not confer on foreigners the right to enter the employment applicant's register and receive unemployment support and the right to participate in public health insurance. The fact that the government's proposal is contrary to the directive, in vain, reminded the government of the Ombudsman and the Human Rights Commissioner. The conflict with the directive also results from an expert opinion from the Department of Labour Law and Social Security Law Faculty of Charles University. The government's proposal is also contrary to elementary justice. Although foreigners, like other employees, make a contribution to the state's employment policy and public health insurance policy, they cannot benefit from these schemes in case of loss of employment. This situation is unfair to all working foreigners, but it is particularly scandalous in the case of foreign women who become pregnant and for this reason the employer will not extend their employment contract. These foreigners must then pay for the birth and related postpartum and neonatal care themselves. It is true that the deadline for implementing the directive ends already 25. 12. 2013, but implementation must not be detrimental to the quality of this bill. When discussing the law, it is necessary and appropriate to resolve the problem of commercial health insurance for foreigners as a whole, not only in relation to labour migrants and their family members, as required by the directive. As the professional public, the Ombudsman and the Human Rights Council have long pointed out, last in the October 2013 recommendation, the only systemic solution to this problem is the inclusion of foreigners with a long-term stay in public health insurance, but officials of the Ministry of the Interior of the Czech Republic and the Ministry of Health of the Czech Republic are not willing to do so for many reasons. The problem lasts for more than 20 years, and by 2007 at the latest, the inclusion of foreigners in public health insurance is on the table in all negotiations on foreign law. In doing so, only commercial insurance companies and other health insurance traders benefit from the delaying of the solution, while, on the contrary, they are running public budgets and medical facilities.
