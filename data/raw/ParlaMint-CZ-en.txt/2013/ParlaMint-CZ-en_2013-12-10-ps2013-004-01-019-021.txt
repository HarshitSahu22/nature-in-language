ParlaMint-CZ_2013-12-10-ps2013-004-01-019-021.u2	Madam President, ladies and gentlemen, allow me to introduce the proposed bill amending the Act on Public Health Insurance on Hospitalization Regulatory Charges. It is a brief amendment, which is limited solely to addressing the effects of the part of the Constitutional Court's decision, the PLUS 36/11 file, which, by 31 December of this year, cancels the regulatory fee for bed care, and aims to maintain the balance of the bed care system even after that date, taking into account the requirements of the Constitutional Court. The regulatory fee of CZK 100 is a significant income of some health service providers and often accounts for more than 10 percent of their total revenue. This is particularly the case in the case of providers of post- and long-term bed care or, for example, a spa. It is therefore necessary to maintain this fee to an extent that does not lead to significant existential difficulties for such entities. The proposal is therefore new or old-fashioned, since the fee used to be CZK 60, sets the regulatory fee for bed care of CZK 60 per calendar day, with the obligation to pay this regulatory fee exempting children until the date of completion of 18 years of age inclusive. No other restrictions, no time, no age, we don't suggest. This solution is not intended to address comprehensively the issue of regulatory fees, which will undoubtedly be the subject of your further discussions, but it is merely a question of meeting the conclusions of the Constitutional Court's ruling in order not to jeopardise the provision of bed care in the case of those health services providers who specialise in these services and are often given the deficit status of the entire public health insurance system in existential problems even at present. A systematic solution to regulatory charges must be the subject of a longer-term expert discussion, in which it is also possible to revise the bed-care fee and compensate for its failure in the public health insurance system in another way. However, in view of the short legisvacation period of the Constitutional Court's finding, there is not more scope for a detailed analysis and assessment of the economic impact of several options. In accordance with the Constitutional Court's view, the primary task of the legislature is to ensure that the failure of the regulatory fee for bed care does not affect health service providers for no reason and unfairly economically. Madam President, ladies and gentlemen, it is absolutely necessary that the draft law be discussed without undue delay. It is therefore also proposed that the Chamber of Deputies of the Parliament of the Czech Republic give its consent to the draft law at first reading, and I am asking you to support it. Thank you.
