Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, let me present here at first reading the main changes brought by the amendment to Act No. 170/2002 Coll., on war veterans, which have been sent to you as House press number 32. First of all, the amendment wants to tighten up and unify the conditions for the status of a war veteran so as to preserve his moral value. Tightening will occur as a result of the distinction between military missions according to their level of hazard and the setting of a different period of service that will be necessary to obtain the status of war veteran. Newly it will be set that it is necessary to perform the service continuously for at least 90 calendar days in a foreign mission in a place of armed conflict or in a place with a significantly impaired security situation or for a total of at least 360 calendar days in other foreign missions which took place on the basis of a decision of an international organisation of which the Czech Republic is a member. However, the shortest estimated period under this resolution shall be at least 90 days of continuous service. Individual points can be added to each other, while a new institute is introduced to eliminate the hardness of the law, on the basis of which it will be possible to reflect some exceptional situations, when, for example, the duration of the service at the place of armed conflict is shorter due to the injury of the soldier or due to the premature termination of the mission atp. In addition, tightening is driven by an effort to eliminate the current unequal condition, where it is sufficient for modern-day veterans to participate in the mission for 30 days, while second-year veterans who have often participated in combat conflicts directly in war operations had to meet the condition of at least 90 days of continuous active combat activity. Another objective is also to extend the circle of persons entitled to the status of war veteran. This will be available to all members of the Security Corps, members of the Fire Rescue Corps, Customs Administration and others, and not only members of the Army and the Police of the Czech Republic, as has been the case so far. In addition, the aim is to extend the scope of authorised persons also to persons civilian, respectively. staff members of the organisational units of the State who are being sent to missions and who are also at risk of endangering or losing their lives. The possibility of using the capacity of home for war veterans will also be adapted. It is proposed that, in exceptional cases, the Ministry may, for a temporary period, accept another so-called third party who would be provided for a full remuneration. However, it is proposed that the war veteran should always take precedence over this other person and that, if necessary, the release of the veteran's bed may be unilaterally terminated by that other person's stay in the home. The bill was submitted to the Chamber of Deputies in the previous parliamentary term and was discussed in May of this year by the Defence Committee which recommended it for approval. However, the legislative process has not been completed as a result of the dissolution of the Chamber of Deputies. Now the law is after it 6. 11. 2013 discussed its resolution No. 836 government, presented again. Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, thank you for your attention and I call for this law to be discussed in a classic manner, not in accordance with Section 90 at first reading. As a result, this prestige, or moral value of the status of a war veteran, could be completely devalued in the future in view of the enormous number of persons meeting the slightly set conditions for the emergence of this position. In addition, state citizens of the Czech Republic are currently being sent abroad not only as members of the armed forces or the Police of the Czech Republic for the purpose of defending or forcing peace, defending and restoring democracy, freedoms and democratic values, but also as employees of the organisational units of the state or other security forces, in order to provide assistance in the reconstruction of structures of state administration and economy in unstable areas abroad or in observation missions in designated areas with a higher risk of injury to health. The original aim of the War Veterans Act, which took effect on 30 April 2002, was to morally appreciate the citizens of the Czech Republic who fulfilled the conditions for obtaining the status of a war veteran, to support the activities of civil associations bringing together war veterans and to provide them with care and to establish homes for war veterans. The experience gained in the implementation of the War Veterans Act confirms that its adoption was very necessary, because, in addition to the moral appreciation given to war veterans, the law also allows the Ministry to provide them with appropriate care. In its current form, however, the War Veterans Act does not fully reflect the fact that the Czech Army is fully professionalised and there is a regular rotation of its members sent into operations to force peace or to reach other agreements in foreign missions. Ladies and gentlemen, if I sum up the basic objectives of this law, in addition to tightening the assumptions for obtaining the status of a war veteran in order to preserve his moral value, it is a distinction between missions according to their level of hazard and, in that context, the determination of a different period of service necessary for the possibility of obtaining the status of a veteran, as well as the extension of the circle of nationals of the Czech Republic who can obtain the status of a war veteran, including persons of civil and the extension of the circle of persons who can be admitted to the home for war veterans. Ladies and gentlemen, allow me to recommend a further continuation of this law through the transition to second reading. Thank you.
