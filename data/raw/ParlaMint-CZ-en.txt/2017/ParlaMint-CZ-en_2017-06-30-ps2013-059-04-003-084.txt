ParlaMint-CZ_2017-06-30-ps2013-059-04-003-084.u2	Thank you, Mr President. Ladies and gentlemen, the aim of the proposal is to increase the social sensitivity of the public health insurance system, by reducing the participation of insured persons belonging to socially vulnerable groups of the population in the supplement to medicinal products partly covered by public health insurance. Specifically, it is proposed to reduce the annual limit to so-called deductible surcharges for children under the age of 18, elderly from 65 years of age, from CZK 2,500 to CZK 1,000, with this limit being further reduced to CZK 500 per year for seniors who have reached at least 70 years of age. After the insured person reaches them, the health insurance undertaking shall pay him the amount by which the limit on these supplements has been exceeded. As part of the second reading, a number of amendments have been read, and I would like to comment on some of them for the Ministry of Health. First of all, allow me a few words on Mrs Hnyková's amendment, which is referred to in letter B. This proposal extends the obligation to publish contracts under the Public Health Insurance Act as well as on health insurance contracts with social services providers, while it also includes an amendment to the register of contracts. At this point in time, I think it will not be very good to reopen the register of contracts, because this law already contains an appropriate exception to the publication of contracts for the provision and reimbursement of health services covered by public health insurance, which are published under the Public Health Insurance Act. The Public Health Insurance Act distinguishes, on the one hand, the contracts concluded between health insurance companies and service providers and, on the other hand, the so-called. specific contracts concluded between health insurance undertakings and social services providers. Both types of contracts are covered by the Act on the Register of Contracts for the provision and reimbursement of health services covered by public health insurance. The Act on the Register of Contracts therefore leaves to the Public Health Insurance Act what extent of the contracts will be published under this Act and how many contracts will be published under the Act on the Register of Contracts. If, therefore, the amendment in question were to be divided into two parts under the voting procedure, namely part B1 to part B3 and part B4, I could support those points B1 to part B3 under the Ministry of Health, i.e. the regulation of the publication of contracts under the Public Health Insurance Act, unlike point B4, which amends the Act on the Register of Contracts - I am able to support those points B1 to part B3. Ladies and gentlemen, thank you for your attention.
