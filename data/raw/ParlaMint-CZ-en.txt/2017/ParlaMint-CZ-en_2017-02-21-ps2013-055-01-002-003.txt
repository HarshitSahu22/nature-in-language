ParlaMint-CZ_2017-02-21-ps2013-055-01-002-003.u2	Thank you for the floor. Mr President, fellow Members, ladies and gentlemen, Madam Senator, in brief, the reasons for the presentation, the content of the bill. The draft law adapts the Czech rule of law to the relevant regulation of the European Parliament on fluorinated greenhouse gases and the aim of this regulation is to gradually reduce emissions of fluorinated greenhouse gases by limiting the quantities of these gases in installations placed on the market. The bill focuses on three fundamental areas of regulation. And all I have to say is that the Ministry of the Environment agrees with all the amendments that have been adopted in the Senate. Thank you.

ParlaMint-CZ_2017-02-21-ps2013-055-01-002-003.u4	Thank you for the floor. Good afternoon, esteemed colleagues. I'll basically follow up on the minister. I believe that the changes that have taken place in the Senate are acceptable within the framework of an amendment that has already passed through our diaper. There's a total of three minor modifications. One concerns the evaluation bodies, which have so far had an obligation to organise training. They will now be given the opportunity to organise training for authorised persons if they are interested. The second amendment concerns the specification in the list of certified persons by the Ministry, where persons who are certified from other EU countries may also be newly included. Thirdly, there is a minor adjustment to the clarification in Section 18b, when "at least" is inserted after the word "quantity." I believe that these changes are acceptable and therefore there is no problem on my part that we should also vote in favour of the Senate version. Thank you for your attention.

The Chamber of Deputies passed a draft amendment to the Senate Act on 20. 12. 2016 and the Senate discussed it at its 4th meeting, and by their resolution number 83 in voting number fifty-three, they agreed to return the draft amendment to the Chamber of Deputies with amendments. 48 of the 58 senators voted to return the bill, no one opposed it. As has already been said, these are three amendments. They concern, on the one hand, that the obligation of an evaluator would be completely nonsensical, i.e. To arrange training for a licensed person, though no one would be interested in him. In the explanatory memorandum, it was said that the EU wanted us to do so. She really doesn't want us to do this, so we fixed this with the Ministry's agreement. The second issue concerned certified users who would come forward before those handling fluorinated gases or regulated gases, and people would hardly look in foreign lists for the fact that they are indeed the certificater who is justified. This means that, in this case, the Ministry again wants to meet users in agreement with the amendment that the Ministry supports. And the third one corrects an obvious mistake that would be contrary to EU legislation. So thank you for your attention, thank you and the Ministry, and I believe that in this case, there will be agreement with the Senate to amend the law so that it serves as needed to all users. Thank you.
