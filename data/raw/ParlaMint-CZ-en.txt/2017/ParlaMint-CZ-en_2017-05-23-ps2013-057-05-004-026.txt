ParlaMint-CZ_2017-05-23-ps2013-057-05-004-026.u2	Mr President, dear colleagues, let me introduce the Government's bill amending Act No. 166/1999 Coll., on Veterinary Care and amending some related laws, as amended, and some related laws. The aim of the draft law is to ensure greater protection of the health of persons and animals and to reduce the administrative burden for breeders in cases where this is possible while maintaining the necessary animal health and animal health requirements. In particular, in order to ensure greater protection of persons and animals, the bill transfers certain obligations from livestock farmers to breeders, irrespective of whether they keep farm animals or pet animals in order to maintain or improve the favourable health status of farmed and interested animals and wild game. It sets out the actions to which the State budget grants a contribution to their implementation, e.g. Allergological test etc. It complements the so-called law in accordance with the requirements of the Czech Federation of Beekeepers. a bee-viewer who will be entitled to inspect the hives in the protection zones in the event of a confirmed dangerous disease of bees. The bee-breeder will be able to entrust the bee-keeping work to bee-seekers or to inspect his bees himself as before. It specifies the sale of small quantities of own products from primary production, extending the possibility to deliver small quantities of nutria to the market. This adjustment complies with the directly applicable EU legislation on the hygiene of foodstuffs of animal origin. It specifies the heading of the persons for whom the meat and the organs of domestic slaughter animals are intended, the meat and the authorities of domestic slaughter are intended for consumption by the breeder's household, in the case of meat and organs of bovine animals, or by the household of the breeder or for consumption by the nearer person, in the case of meat and organs of other animals. The age for domestic slaughter of bovine animals is increased from 24 months to 72 months, reducing domestic slaughter of bovine animals over 24 months and under 72 months to three pieces per year. It introduces an obligation to register shelters for stray animals so that such establishments can be kept under veterinary supervision. It introduces the obligation to notify breeding of five or more female dogs over 12 months of age. In order to reduce the administrative burden, the draft law replaces, in particular, the obligation of the breeder to apply for the authorisation of domestic slaughter of bovine animals and farmed cervids to report such slaughter and reduces the period for notification of such slaughter to three days from the previous seven. It allows livestock farmers to carry out certain veterinary tasks on their holdings, such as: administration of medicines. The Government Bill was discussed by the Agricultural Committee of the Chamber of Deputies of the Parliament of the Czech Republic at its 42nd meeting on 3 May 2017 and recommended by its resolution to the Chamber of Deputies of the Parliament of the Czech Republic to approve the Government Bill as amended by the amendments adopted. Thank you for your attention.
