The same thing happened at the December and January meeting of the Economic Committee. The reason was simple. In agreement with the Ministry of Transport, we were waiting for a comprehensive amendment to be prepared to respond to the comments of this department. This comprehensive amendment, which is based on the results of negotiations between the Ministry of Transport, representatives of the Union of Towns and Municipalities, and last but not least as representatives of the petitioners, was actually presented at the end of January. Since I consider the compromise to be acceptable in principle, I have adopted this amendment. It was then adopted by the Committee on Public Administration and Regional Development. Like the original draft, this comprehensive amendment has a simple objective - to make life easier for applicants for a driving licence. In particular, those applicants who study or travel for work, or for other reasons, stay out of their place of residence. Let me give you an example from my e-mail, which I think is eloquent. After all, I introduced him at first reading. A student from Moravia wrote to me a while ago - I quote: "I have a permanent residence in Znojemsko, but I stay in Prague for a long time because of my work at my doctorate. This year my driving licence issued at the Znojmo Municipal Authority will expire. However, its renewal cannot be carried out through another city office without a change in permanent residence. It is also not possible to have a completed driver's licence sent to another city office as opposed to the ID card. I therefore have to take two days' vacation to change my driving licence and travel 200 km there and back twice with a cost of CZK 900 only for a fare, which is responsible for a student like me, for example, for a weekly food budget." [[10.40 hours]] Again, I stress that it is the amendment that gives these people a helping hand when it allows them to apply for a driving licence, not in a community with extended scope in a place of residence, as envisaged by the current regulation, but in any community with extended scope. The issue of driving licences will also be facilitated. In addition, as submitters, we strive to ensure that, where the Authority already has a photograph of an applicant fulfilling certain parameters, it is not obliged to require it from the applicant again. The proposal also assumes that photos can be taken on the spot. That, I think, is very helpful and sensible. Let me reiterate my belief that this is what the public administration should look like in the 21st century. Thank you for your attention.
