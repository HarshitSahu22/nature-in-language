ParlaMint-CZ_2017-04-05-ps2013-056-02-005-035.u2	Thank you again for the floor, Mr President. Ladies and gentlemen, may I present to you the bill amending Act No. 201/2012 Coll., on Air Protection. The aim of the draft law is to implement a partial transposition of Council Directive 2015/652/EU laying down the calculation methods and reporting requirements under Directive 98/70/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council on the quality of petrol and diesel fuels and partly the transposition of Directive 2015/1513/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council amending Directive 98/70/EC on the quality of petrol and diesel fuels. By amending legislation, the European Union favours the use of biofuels produced from waste fats, such as: used kitchen oils and rendering fats, and biofuels having a low impact in relation to indirect land use change, so-called Advanced fuel. These are biofuels produced from waste and non-food biomass, e.g. Straw biofuels, glycerol, algae and others, by adding these biofuels to fuel, their contribution to the obligation to ensure a minimum share of biofuels by a specific fuel supplier is counted twice as much. Furthermore, European legislation allows fuel suppliers to meet their commitments in reducing greenhouse gas emissions from fuels through the so-called "fuel'. alternative fuels and electricity. The proposal therefore gives suppliers of diesel and diesel fuels the possibility to take into account twice the quantity of biofuels produced from the raw materials contained in Annex 9 (A) and (B) of the Directive in the minimum mandatory proportion of biofuels, i.e. biofuels from used kitchen oil, rendering fats and advanced biofuels. The proposal complements the provisions on the basis of which suppliers of diesel and diesel fuels will also be able to take account of CNG natural gas or LNG or LPG liquefied petroleum gas, pure and high-percent biofuels, electricity and, where appropriate, hydrogen which they have delivered for transport purposes, or supplied by another person with whom they conclude a joint performance contract. Furthermore, the proposal provides for penalties for failure to comply with the greenhouse gas emission reduction obligation. The valid wording of the Act allows to impose a fine only up to 10 million. CZK, which would not, however, be dissuasive for large suppliers of diesel and diesel fuel, would therefore not be met by the requirement of European Union legislation and, on the contrary, for small suppliers such a high fine would be completely liquidation. A new method of sanctioning, which sets the amount of the fine on the basis of a really unabated amount of greenhouse gas emissions, will be fairer compared to the current situation. A similar method is used in Germany. In particular, it is actually the multiple kilograms of CO2 where the required reduction in the amount of emission gases multiplied by ten crowns did not occur. That's ten kroner for each kilo of CO2 that hasn't been reduced as required. In view of this - then I will comment on this in the general debate - that the deadline for the first transposition directive will expire on 21 April this year and the second directive then on 10 September this year, I would also like to ask you to shorten the deadline for committee discussions to 30 days. Thank you.
