Government bill on European political parties and European political talents and on the amendment of certain laws /German press 1110/ - first reading according to § 90 para. 2 Please note that it is proposed that we give our consent to the bill at first reading. On behalf of the Government, the proposal submitted will be made by the Minister of the Interior Milan Chovanec. Please, Minister, you have the floor.
ParlaMint-CZ_2017-05-24-ps2013-057-06-004-066.u2	Mr President, honourable Members, let me put forward a draft law on European political parties and political talents and on the amendment of certain laws. This proposal has already been discussed by Parliament as House Press 876. On 4 April 2017 The Chamber of Deputies voted on the bill returned to the Senate and did not adopt the law. However, the adoption of the draft law is necessary because of the adaptation of the rule of law to Regulation (EC) No 659/1999 of the European Parliament and of the Council. 1141/2014 on the status and financing of European political parties and political foundations governing their activities. The proposal is re-submitted to the Chamber of Deputies in a similar text which Parliament has already discussed. However, changes are reflected in the proposal initiated by the Senate by the amendment. An unnecessary amendment to the Act on Association in Political Parties and Political Movements has been launched here. Following consultation with the Ministry of Finance, there was also the launch of an unnecessary amendment to the Accounting Act. The Government proposes to the Chamber of Deputies that the bill be approved in the first reading under Section 90 of the Rules of Procedure. This is because the European regulation on which the rule of law is to be adapted has been effective since 1 January 2017 and is also close to the end of the term of office. Thank you for your attention.

ParlaMint-CZ_2017-05-24-ps2013-057-06-004-066.u6	Mr Vice-President, Prime Minister, Minister, ladies and gentlemen, I shall perhaps just briefly repeat my slight reservations which I have and which lead me not to support this bill on European political parties. I think, unfortunately, that is one of the typical elements of violent European federalization, that there is pressure on the European Commission and the European Parliament to create a single European party, not as we know it today, when it is the conglomerates of individual national parties gathered here in the People's Party, here in the Socialist International Office, here in other, here in conservative formations, more or less on the basis of the European Parliament, or on the basis of some international groups where they communicate with each other. And here is the effort, and the effort will obviously be supported in the coming years, it will already be and will be strongly supported by the finances, so that European parties will start to emerge and thus deepen European federalization. I think it's a step that's not right. I even think that, in today's situation, we should think about whether the path of European unification should be more slow and let us say normalise whether we should insist much more on the principle of subsidiarity than on these EU principles of ever closer and closer unification. And I fear that European political parties are precisely a step towards this closer and closer unification, to creating a kind of Evropan, a European person, to creating a kind of European elite even more detached from ordinary citizens of the Czech Republic, but also from all other states than the elites of the past. That is why I will not support the bill on European political parties. Thank you for your attention.
