Mr Vice-President, honourable Members, I hereby submit, before first reading, a Government Bill amending Act No. 281/2002 Coll. on certain measures relating to the prohibition of biological and toxin weapons. The Law was drawn up by experts from the State Nuclear Safety Authority and was approved by the Government at its meeting on 27 February this year. It's the House Press 1055. With this amendment, the government responds to the fact that our existing legislation does not reflect the requirements of certain UN Security Council resolutions, particularly in the area of the security of certain biological and toxin elements before they are stolen, as well as in the area of the ban on financing activities for development, production, possession and further handling of biological and toxin weapons. It is therefore an implementation of the requirements of UN Security Council Resolutions, their precise description can be found in the explanatory memorandum. The common denominator is the effort to increase biosecurity, which means security in the area of the handling of this type of weapon. The law also responds to some experience from the practical application of existing legislation. The proposed wording of the Act clearly defines the conceptual apparatus, increases the formulation and legislative level of a number of provisions of the Act. At the same time, as petitioners, we believe that it will lead to a reduction in the administrative burden on all the entities concerned due to the abolition of declarations of planned activities. The amendment also simplifies the handling of vaccines and diagnostic kits containing high-risk biological elements. The amendment also lays down the lack of requirements for the security of biological elements and toxins against alienation and possible misuse. The requirements for the disposal of these substances, both for the authorisation holder and for persons dealing with risk biological elements and toxins, are further clarified. The amendment thus contributes to the protection of the environment, as it minimises possible consequences for their potential escape. Mr Vice-President, honourable Members, thank you for your attention and I would like to ask for the support of this Government proposal and its commandments to the committee. Thank you.
