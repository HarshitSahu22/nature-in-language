So, for the approval of the Government's proposal to terminate the Treaty between the Czech Republic and the Kingdom of the Netherlands, with regard to the Netherlands Antilles as well as the automatic exchange of information on savings income in the form of interest payments. On 10 April 2017, the Government adopted Government Resolution 279 on its resignation, together with seven other international treaties of the same type. The contracts in question were negotiated in 2004 and 2005 with the overseas countries and territories of the United Kingdom or with the Kingdom of the Netherlands, taking into account its overseas countries and territories. The purpose of these contracts was to allow savings income in the form of interest payments paid in one State to their beneficial owners - natural persons residing for tax purposes in the other Contracting State, to be subject to effective taxation in that other State. The Treaties followed the directive of the Council of the European Union on taxation of savings income and were intended to lead to the gradual onset of automatic exchange of information. In the light of international developments, when the European Union adopted a common standard for reporting information on financial accounts created under the OECD into its legislation, the Directive on taxation of savings income in the European Union was repealed in order to avoid double reporting. The Czech Republic and all those overseas countries and territories have signed relevant international documents with the intention of automatically exchanging information according to the Common Standard for the notification of the OECD EU. In view of the replacement of the notification by the common standard, it is necessary to terminate the automatic exchange of information contracts, savings income in the form of interest payments and the taxation of savings income in respect of those territories. The termination at the outset of that Treaty does not conflict with international law or with European Union law and will not affect the state budget.
