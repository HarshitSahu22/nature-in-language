In fact, my amendment proposes to change this in two matters. The first concerns the obligation to own a data box. When data boxes were established, the obligation to establish them was given to all legal entities registered in the Commercial Register. Since then, however, a public register has been established, which includes other organisations that belong there under the register law. They are a federal register, a foundation register, a register of institutes, a register of community owners of units and a register of generally beneficial companies. My proposal therefore aims to ensure that the obligation to maintain these data boxes is also addressed to these entities, i.e. all entities which are registered in a public register. It can be said that this partial change will have a major impact on the promotion of eGovernment and the electronicisation of private contacts with public authorities. It can be estimated that this change will save between 50 and 100 million public funds spent annually on paper mail printing and recommended delivery. It is possible that, as was the case with the introduction of data boxes, there may be some temporary resistance in that first phase to the mandatory establishment of data boxes. But on the case of lawyers who at the time opposed the requirement to receive data boxes, and today they can't even imagine their lives without them, I think that this change is very beneficial, and the arguments I put here are valid.
