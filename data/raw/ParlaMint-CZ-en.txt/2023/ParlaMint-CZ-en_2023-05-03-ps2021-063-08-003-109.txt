ParlaMint-CZ_2023-05-03-ps2021-063-08-003-109.u2	Madam President, ladies and gentlemen, the proposal for an amendment to the Road Act has one fundamental objective, namely to ensure the transformation of the Road and Motorway Directorate as an existing contribution organisation into a future state enterprise. It's a process that's been going on for years. The basic purpose of the transformation is to make clear the relationship between the State and the Road and Motorway Directorate to the extent that it will also be clear in every particular step, for what it is worth, the Road and Motorway Directorate, the standard of regular maintenance of roads, first class roads and motorways, applies at the time of summer maintenance and in the winter maintenance house. This is one of the fundamental key objectives of the entire transformation. At the same time, it will allow the Road and Motorway Directorate to compete better in the labour market with commercial entities, design offices and the like, because the state urgently needs top experts to carry out its investment activities in the field of transport infrastructure. Unfortunately, the current legal form of the contribution organisation in this sense has significant limits and, in a situation where we do not have enough technicians, when we do not have enough designers, we often find ourselves in a very difficult situation to deal with the lack of quality professional engineers. At the same time, transformation should bring simpler conditions for the acquisition of new people. Another key thing that is connected with the transformation is, of course, what I mentioned at the beginning, clarification of relations in ordering the service by the State towards the Road and Motorway Directorate. However, this should not change the way the Road and Motorway Directorate provides public contracts, how they compete, and the number of things that the Road and Motorway Directorate takes up in the form of outsourcing, however, in the basic tasks, in control and preparation of individual buildings, will certainly not change in a fundamental way. At the same time, several amendments have appeared to this bill as part of the second reading. The first two are affected by the conditions of control and all of them are agreed by the Ministry of Transport. The third concerns a derogation from the general inhibitory regime. Finally, the fourth concerns the price cap on rents from land and buildings where they are necessary for the construction or administration of motorways and first-class roads, or where such communications are established. Finally, the last amendment makes the process of removing illegal advertising equipment more effective. I am convinced that the bill really brings a significant positive shift to the administration of state roads and motorways, ensuring the further functioning of the Road and Motorway Directorate in a legal form that is close to the Railway Administration. The unification of legal personality in this respect is also a positive step. I would therefore like to ask you to support this proposal as a whole and to support individual amendments. Thank you for your attention.
