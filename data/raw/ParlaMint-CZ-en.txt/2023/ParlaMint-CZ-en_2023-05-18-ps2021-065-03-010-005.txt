[[12.30 hours]] The content of the first part of the proposal is the amendment of the trade law or its annexes. The reason for this is the new definition of the trade in the field of public auctions. As has already been said, public auctions on the owner's proposal will be made easier, and this is important, less administratively demanding rules. For this reason, the implementation of these auctions is proposed to move from the existing concession scheme to a more moderate trade-related scheme under the name of the execution of public auctions, with the exception of compulsory auctions. Forced auctions will continue to remain under the strictest regime, i.e. concessions. In addition, the current trade in the conduct of voluntary auctions of movable goods under the Act on Public Auctions will be abolished, which, in view of the aforementioned liberalisation of the rules, will in some way render practical sense no longer available. The other parts of the proposal contain only minor changes to the tax rules, which are needed because of the new concept of public auction as a special way of concluding the contract. The amendment of the provisions of the Capital Market Business Act is proposed in relation to public securities auctions. The amendment to the cadastral law then reflects the change in terminology, instead of the concept of involuntary auction the concept of auction forced or forced auction will be introduced. As well as the draft law on public auctions, this draft accompanying amendment law was adopted at first reading across the political parties, the constitutional-legal committee also recommended its approval to the Chamber of Deputies, and in this case I also note that in a detailed debate I would apply to an amendment which, as was the case with the previous proposal, moves the effectiveness of the Act by one year.
