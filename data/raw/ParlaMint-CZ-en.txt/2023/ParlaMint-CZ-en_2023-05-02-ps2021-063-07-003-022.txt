ParlaMint-CZ_2023-05-02-ps2021-063-07-003-022.u2	Thank you for the floor. The reason for submitting this draft law is the obligation of the Czech Republic to implement the EU 2020/284 Council Directive of 18 February 2020 amending the 2006 Directive as regards the introduction of certain requirements on payment service providers. The bill is of a purely transpositional nature, there is nothing more than mandatory implementation. It shall contain only the changes contained in Union legislation and the necessary procedural adaptations to those changes following it. The draft law contains amendments to the Value Added Tax Act, the Bank Act, the Savings Bank Act and credit cooperatives. The new regulation follows the already adopted e-commerce regulation, which has brought a number of simplifications to entrepreneurs. With the expansion of e-commerce, new opportunities for fraudulent VAT negotiations have also emerged and the detection of such fraud poses a major challenge at this time for tax administrators. One of the tools to detect these frauds will be a pan-European EURES system, which will contain data obtained by Member States' tax authorities from payment service providers. In accordance with Union law, payment service providers are required to provide tax authorities with data on incoming payments from other Member States and outgoing payments to third countries. This data will be inserted by the tax administrator into the European system CESOP. The effectiveness of the bill is proposed in accordance with the transposition directive for 1 January next year. In view of the need to adapt the internal systems of payment service providers, it is desirable to allow sufficient legisvakance. For this reason, and for reasons of the purely transpositional nature of the proposals, I will then propose a reduction in the deadline for discussing the draft law by committees of 30 days to 30 days. Thank you.

ParlaMint-CZ_2023-05-02-ps2021-063-07-003-022.u4	Thank you for the floor, Madam President. Ladies and gentlemen, I will just briefly add to what the Minister said. The reason for the proposal is the obligation of the Czech Republic to implement the EU 2020/284 Directive of 18 February 2022 amending Directive 2006/112/S as regards the introduction of certain requirements for payment service providers. The amendment concerns the following laws: the Value Added Tax Act, the Bank Act, the Savings Act and the Credit Cooperatives Act and the Income Tax Act. While the expansion of e-commerce greatly simplifies interconnection and purchase, this method is also widely used in fraudulent activities, thus obtaining an unfair advantage on the market by failing to fulfil its VAT obligations. The proposed bill obliges payment service providers to keep a register of cross-border payments and beneficiaries in which they keep data on cross-border payments, i.e. payments made between Member States and, where appropriate, third countries, payments received in the case of payments from other Member States and payments outgoing in the case of payments to third countries and beneficiaries of such payments. This obligation will not be activated until 25 cross-border payments relating to one beneficiary per calendar quarter have been exceeded. At the same time, the obligation to provide data from this register electronically to the tax administrator is laid down until the end of the month following the end of the calendar quarter. I propose that the bill be ordered to the Guarantee Budget Committee for discussion. Thank you for your attention.

ParlaMint-CZ_2023-05-02-ps2021-063-07-003-022.u6	Thank you for the floor. Ladies and gentlemen, I would like to address the amendment to the Government Bill amending Act No. 235/2004 Coll., on Value Added Tax. In a nutshell it is the implementation of the European directive and it brings new obligations for payment service providers in the Czech Republic when this directive aims to combat border fraud in the area of VAT caused by fraudulent conduct by certain businesses in the field of cross-border e-commerce. Online shopping in electronic stores is in the world, in Europe and, of course, also in the Czech Republic an increasingly common phenomenon of customer behaviour and it is not really a problem for you right now, during vacations in Austria, to buy a phone or tablet from a Spanish e-shop and to have it delivered home to the Czech Republic, where we will be waiting for our return from vacation. [[18.20 hours]] Cross-border entities can thus make use of differences in tax rates between countries and cheat by not collecting or not paying the VAT they should. This can lead to significant revenue losses for state budgets and an unequal economic environment for legal business. The European Union has thus noted the problems and risks of cross-border VAT fraud and is trying to respond to them. To that end, it has issued this Directive laying down an accounting obligation for payment service providers. These obligations were chosen because, in particular, payment service providers have access to the information necessary to identify the payee, including the amount, date of payment, the Member State of origin of the payment and information on where the payment was initiated. The disclosure of this information would help the competent tax authorities in each Member State to check compliance with VAT obligations and to combat border fraud in this area. The amendment therefore obliges payment service providers to keep a register of cross-border payments and beneficiaries of such payments, the cross-border payment being to that effect the payment where the payer is located in one Member State and the payee in another Member State, in the third territory or in a third country. We support this amendment as an ANO parliamentary club. Thank you for your attention.
