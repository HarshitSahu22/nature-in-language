ParlaMint-CZ_2023-05-18-ps2021-065-03-002-096.u8	Thank you for the floor. Ladies and gentlemen, I would like to introduce you to the position of the ANO MP. We are in favour of this government proposal by which Parliament of the Czech Republic is to approve and agree to ratify the amendment resolution on the International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers 1978, as amended in 2010, for three reasons. The first reason is maritime safety. In essence, we are presented with a set of resolutions that have been adopted in the foreseeable way over the last almost ten years and which update the international convention. The Czech Republic is a Member State of the International Maritime Organisation as well as a party to the International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers. Their common denominator is maritime safety. Navy crews are trained in different states and according to different teaching and training systems. In order to ensure maritime safety, Member States of the IMO adopted in 1987 the International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers. This was revised mainly in 1995 and 2010. In view of developments in maritime transport, in particular in the field of technology, six amendments to the Convention have been adopted during the years 2014 to 2021, which are now submitted together for the thematic link and substantive coherence for approval, including one correction of the administrative error in the English version of the amending Resolution. Resolutions MSC 373 93 and MSC 374 93 introduce mandatory audits by IMO Member States to analyse the correctness and completeness of implementation and implementation of the Convention at national level. The audit shall be repeated regularly at a seven-year interval. Amendment Resolutions MSC 396 and 397 introduce requirements for the new type of training of marine crew members using low-flash propulsion or transporting fuels. It is a rapidly expanding segment of ships that are driven by very light ignition engines such as compressed or liquefied LPG or LNG gases in order to reduce harmful emissions. These fuels represent a significant increase in the fire risk on these ships in the event of incorrect technological procedures in the operation of or supply of marine machinery by the crew, and therefore special training of crew members is required. Amendment Resolutions MSC 416 and MSC 417 introduce requirements for the new type of training of marine crew members sailing in polar waters. The reason for the other is our membership of the European Union. Europe's legislation also follows the international regulation of the training standards of crew members. The adoption of the proposed amendments is therefore also necessary because of the fulfilment of the commitments of the Czech Republic arising from its membership of the European Union. The third reason is the positive impact on Czech sailors. Yes, the Czech Republic has a Naval office located in the Ministry of Transport, which, among other things, exhibits internationally valid certificates and certificates of competence and Navy books based on certified courses. The adoption of the proposed amendments therefore also ultimately has an impact on the possibility of applying Czech seafarers to the international labour market. Thank you for your attention.
