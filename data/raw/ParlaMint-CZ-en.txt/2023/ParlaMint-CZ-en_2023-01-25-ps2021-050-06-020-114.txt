ParlaMint-CZ_2023-01-25-ps2021-050-06-020-114.u2	Thank you. Madam President, ladies and gentlemen. The House press 122 deals with the annual report on the activities of Czech Television in 2018. I'm just gonna poke a few of the most important things out of the report. As has already been done here, 2018 was in a way a turning point for Czech TV because it started to switch to a new digital broadcasting standard DVB-T2, it had to deal with wage growth on the Czech labour market and also with the long-term increase in prices for all inputs. At the same time, prices in the field of purchasing foreign programmes, broadcasting rights and the like have also been increased on the international market. [[12.40 hours]] The concession fee was unchanged after ten years, it was actually set in 2008. The TV fee, which amounted to CZK 135 in 2018, was used as follows. The largest part, CZK 95, was used to produce content, signal distribution 8 CZK, infrastructure, production support 12 CZK, overhead costs 10 CZK, selection of TV fees 4 CZK, VAT without the right to deduct 3 CZK, conversion to DVB-T2 3 CZK. The total current income of Czech Television amounted to CZK 6.2 billion in 2018, and the main source of income was television fees of CZK 5.64 billion. In 2018, Czech Television managed in line with the budget and with long-term plans approved by the Council of Czech Television. The Czech Television budget for 2018 has been respected. According to the independent auditor of Ernst & Young Audit, the attached financial statements provide a true and fair picture of the Czech Television's assets and liabilities as at 31 December 2018 as well as an overview of the costs and revenues and results of the management for 2018. The Election Committee dealt with House Press 122 at its 4th meeting on 3 February 2022 and adopted its 29th resolution: "The election committee of the Chamber of Deputies after hearing the introductory words of the representative of the Czech Television Council, the report by Mr .
