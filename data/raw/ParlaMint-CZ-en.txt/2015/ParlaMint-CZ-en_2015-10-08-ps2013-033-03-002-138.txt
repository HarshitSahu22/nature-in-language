ParlaMint-CZ_2015-10-08-ps2013-033-03-002-138.u2	Madam President, honourable Members, may I present the Government's proposal to the Parliament of the Czech Republic to approve the ratification of the amendment to Annex I and Annexes II to the International Convention against Doping in Sport. The International Convention against Doping in Sport is a multilateral treaty which entered into force on 1 February 2007. For the Czech Republic it entered into force on 1 June 2007. The Convention belongs to the category of contracts whose acceptance or acceptance of their amendments is subject to the approval of the Parliament of the Czech Republic and the ratification of the President of the Republic. Annex I - List of prohibited substances and methods and Annex II - international standard for therapeutic derogations are part of the Convention. The purpose of the Convention is to take account of current circumstances in the fight against doping, to strengthen the activities of national anti-doping organisations and to establish a worldwide contractual framework for the joint fight against doping by governmental and non-governmental organisations. To date, approximately 197 countries have acceded to the Convention. The World Anti-Doping Agency, in accordance with Article 34 of the Convention, has prepared amendments to Annexes I and Annex II for 2015, which have been unanimously adopted following written consultations. This is, in principle, a procedural issue, and these changes are of a technical and complementary nature. The Government of the Czech Republic agreed to accept the amendment Annex I and Annexes II to the International Convention against Doping in Sport on 15 April 2015 by Resolution 275. In the Senate on the submitted material, they recommended that both committees agree to the ratification and that the material was approved by the Senate on 23 September 2015. Amendment to the Annex I update and supplement this Annex on newly classified substances and methods with doping effect. These are the following adaptations: change and clarification of the nomenclature of certain substances, minor changes in the distribution of the group stimulant, change of wording and arrangement of the group hormones including new examples. Amendment to Annex II simplifies the use of some of the least serious doping substances and doping methods for athletes and doctors. These are the following adjustments: change in the procedure of individual anti-doping organisations in the approval and mutual recognition of therapeutic exceptions, possibility of retrospective application by athletes of lower performance and age categories following possible positive findings of prohibited substance in urine, innovative form of application for therapeutic derogations. So much for the brief introduction of the government proposal. Thank you for your attention.
