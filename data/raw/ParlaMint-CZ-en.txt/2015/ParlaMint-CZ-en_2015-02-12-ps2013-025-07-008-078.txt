Allow me to mention this government proposal and note that training of marine crew members plays an important role in maritime safety. For this reason, the Member States of the International Maritime Organisation adopted in 1978 the International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers. The Convention entered into force on 28 April 1984 and began to apply to the Czechoslovak Republic - then also the Socialist Republic. In view of developments in technology in particular, a comprehensive revision of the present Convention was decided and the Conference of the Parties held in June 2010 adopted extensive amendments to the Annex and regulations in Manila, Filipino. Manila changes. The adoption of Manila changes is necessary for us in order to strengthen safety, life and property at sea, to protect the marine environment and, last but not least, to ensure better employment opportunities for 1,200 Czech sailors and marine crew members. At the same time, the transposition of the Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on the minimum level of training of seafarers will be ensured. The negotiation process submitted to the Convention should be aligned with the current constitutional requirements. I therefore submit, together with Manila's amendments, the original text of the Convention for ratification. Manila changes are in line with constitutional order and other parts of the legal order of the Czech Republic, with obligations arising from membership of the Czech Republic in the European Union and obligations arising from international law. The implementation of Manila changes will have no impact on state budget expenditure. I would therefore like to ask you, ladies and gentlemen, to discuss this material in the House Committees. Thank you for your attention.
