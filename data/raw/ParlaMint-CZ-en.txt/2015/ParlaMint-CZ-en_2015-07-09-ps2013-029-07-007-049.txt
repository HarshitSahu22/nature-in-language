ParlaMint-CZ_2015-07-09-ps2013-029-07-007-049.u2	Mr President, colleagues, let me briefly justify the Government's proposal for a transposition amendment to the Act on the return of illegally exported cultural goods. The main reason for the proposed legislation is the adoption of a new Directive of the European Parliament and of the Council on the return of cultural goods unlawfully exported from the territory of a Member State which will replace the existing Directive with effect from 19 December 2015. The directive does not contain fundamental changes compared to the existing regulation. The present procedures remain maintained and the proposed amendment therefore contains only the necessary interventions. Among the changes proposed by the proposed legislation is incorporated into Act No. 101/2001 Coll., includes, in particular, a new, broader definition of cultural goods, which will be part of the national cultural treasure of a Member State for the future, without any other restrictive criteria. In the future, it is sufficient that the subject matter, the cultural property, is identified or defined by the Member States as a national cultural treasure of artistic, historical or archaeological value in accordance with Art. 36 Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union. ParlaMint-CZ_2015-07-09-ps2013-029-07-007-049.u6	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. Dear colleagues, the proposed amendment to the Act submitted by the Ministry of Culture is intended to implement Directive 2014/60/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 15 May 2014 into the legal order of the Czech Republic. The amendment to the law brings the following changes. First of all, it simplifies the definition of a national cultural property, by reference to the national cultural treasure of the Czech Republic, thereby extending the scope of the subject matter under this law compared to the current legislation. In the context of this amendment, Annex No 1, which is a list of categories considered as a national cultural property, is deleted from the law. It also establishes communication between Member States through a so-called system for the exchange of information on the internal market. This information system is already in place in the legal order of the Czech Republic, in the framework of Act No. 222/2009 Coll., on the freedom to provide services. However, this amendment still leaves the possibility of communication in other ways than through this system. The amendment further extends the limitation period from one to three years, which positively affects the likelihood of returning illegally exported cultural goods to the country of origin. Furthermore, the time limit for the central authority of the requesting State to participate in the examination of illegally exported cultural goods shall be extended from two to six months. In the course of legal proceedings, the amendment to the Act then transfers the burden of proof to the owner or holder of an illegally exported cultural property. In order for such an owner or holder to be granted compensation for the withdrawal of an illegally exported cultural property, he must demonstrate that he has taken the necessary care to acquire such a good. The law then sets out the facts to be examined by the court. Since the text of the amendment to this Act No. 101/2001 Coll., on the return of illegally exported cultural goods, is dedicated solely to the implementation of the directive, whose deadline will run on 18 December of this year, I recommend that the Chamber of Deputies allow the amendment to second reading. Thank you.
