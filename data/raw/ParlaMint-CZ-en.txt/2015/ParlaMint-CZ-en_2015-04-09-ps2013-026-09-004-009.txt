ParlaMint-CZ_2015-04-09-ps2013-026-09-004-009.u10	Thank you for the floor. I would merely sum up some basic information on the House's press in a nutshell. The proposed amendment to the Local Charges Act aims to eliminate a long-term negative situation where children are already debtors at the time of their birth by the law itself in conjunction with generally binding municipal decrees. When introducing a local fee for the operation of the system of collection, collection, transport, sorting, recovery and disposal of municipal waste in 2002, the legislator neglected to address the situation of the local fee for minors by express provisions of the law. As a result of the failure to pay this fee, children become debtors without having children cause this situation and have any possibility to influence it. In a number of cases, the amendment to the Local Charges Act, which has allowed the municipal authority to charge a fee to a legal representative of a minor taxpayer since 2013, has not helped to improve, but some municipal authorities are still charging local charges directly to children. We consider this to be unbearable, and we believe that it is necessary to make it clear by law that parents pay for children. Another objective of the proposed amendment is to exempt certain groups of persons from paying a local fee for the operation of the system of collection, collection, transport, sorting, recovery and disposal of municipal waste. They are persons who are clearly not detained at the place of their registered permanent residence with which there is a fee obligation and, on the contrary, are located in a facility which pays the costs associated with the disposal of municipal waste. They are children in children's homes and similar facilities and persons located e.g. in homes for people with disabilities, homes for seniors, etc. Therefore, there is no justified reason why these persons should pay local fees at their registered permanent residence, where they are not staying at all, and at the same time the costs of the disposal of municipal waste produced by them are covered by the establishment where they are located. In addition, they are the most defenseless members of society, i.e. children whom their parents were unable to care for, people with disabilities and seniors. Since the proposed exemption from the local fee for the operation of the system of collection, collection, transport, sorting, recovery and disposal of municipal waste cannot logically affect all individual cases where there are serious reasons not to pay this fee, we propose to allow the municipality to waive this fee on the basis of an individual request from the taxpayer. In the same way, the municipality should be able to forgive the payment of all local executor fees to all taxpayers concerned during emergencies. They already had the power to waive local charges, but they were withdrawn in connection with the adoption of the tax rules. However, there is no reason why the municipal council, i.e. an authority that decides to introduce local charges and the details of their collection, including exemption by generally binding decrees, could not decide to waive the local fee. The text of the accompanying proposal was prepared by the lawyers of the Ombudsman's Office. During its discussion in the Committee on Budgets with the representatives of the Ministry of Finance, there were serious legislative shortcomings in this proposal, and therefore a comprehensive amendment under number 1821 was prepared for which the legislative department of the Ministry of Finance, which maintains the substantive framework of the original proposal, was involved, and the objectives of the Senate proposal submitted as press 287 were also incorporated into it, namely the principle that a taxpayer with limited jurisdiction must pay a fee, as well as the liberation of persons with disabilities, in homes for seniors, etc. The petitioners believe that the proposed change corrects the current situation, where the state's imperfect legislative activity has caused an extremely unfair state to children, i.e. directly by legal standards, debt is incurred at the moment of their birth. The effect of these legal standards thus stands in sharp contradiction with the fundamental generally binding principles of humanity, which, according to the Constitutional Court's case law, would justify the use of direct retroactive effects of the proposed changes. However, since this proposal was raised by the Ministry of Finance with fundamental objections, stating that this would interfere with the legitimate expectations of municipalities and other entities, this comprehensive amendment was abandoned from the retroactive effect of the proposed changes. However, since the intention of the petitioners is to remedy as far as possible the injustices that have occurred as a result of the current legislation, it is proposed, at least in a transitional provision, that the possibility of a fee waiver should also apply to fees incurred in the past. Thank you for your attention. I have tried to sum up everything contained in the comprehensive amendment, and I will pass on the floor to the next party to the debate. Thank you.
