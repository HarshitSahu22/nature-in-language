Czech National Bank report on inflation - July 2015 (The Monetary Development Report for the first half of 2015) /German press 573/ According to Law No. 6/1993 Coll., on the Czech National Bank, as amended, the Czech National Bank is obliged to report to the Chamber of Deputies at least twice a year on monetary developments. I now ask the Governor of the Czech National Bank, Mr Miroslav Singer, to present this report. Please, Mr. Governor.
ParlaMint-CZ_2015-12-11-ps2013-036-08-003-201.u2	Thank you for your word. Given that this is the inflation report No. 3/2015, which was published in August, and given the time gap as we come to discuss, is also available this year's latest report, or inflation report 4/2015, which was taken into account last week by the House Budget Committee at its meeting, so maybe I would rather mention some new data than repeat the facts in the report. First of all, the Czech economy accelerated its growth in the first half of this year. The pace remained high even in the third quarter when it reached 4,5 percent. What is extremely gratifying is that all components of domestic demand, especially fixed investment and household consumption, contribute to the growth of gross domestic product. Or maybe we're not just being driven by exports anymore, but we're being drawn by the domestic demand component. Of course, robust economic growth continues to improve favorably in the labour market. Annual growth in total employment continued in the third quarter of this year. The general unemployment rate and the share of unemployed people are falling. In recent months, the authorities have recorded the least job seekers in the last almost seven years, or since the beginning of a period that will go into history as a major recession. In particular, in November, the labour authorities registered some 430,000 applicants, which is about 86,000 less than in the same period last year. The number of job vacancies increased almost twice year to 105,000 job vacancies. This, of course, has to do with the start of wage growth, in the national economy in the third quarter, with the contribution of the business sector to 3.8 percent, and with a rapid increase in average wages in the non-business sector, namely by 3,5 percent. Inflation remains very low. There are two factors colliding. External, when oil prices fall, and weather, a factor that nobody affects, but which then manifests itself on a good crop and de facto stagnation of food prices. But against this, the revival of the economy is beginning to manifest itself in core inflation, which reflects demand in our country. On the contrary, it begins to grow slowly and the last number was, I think, 1.3 percent. As far as the macroeconomic forecast is concerned, we expect not as amazing times as it was this year, but very decent times in the next two years, growth somewhere below 3%, further decline in unemployment and, of course, no more wage increases. As far as our current less standard instrument is concerned, this means the ceiling for strengthening the koruna, we assume that the conditions will allow the end of this non-standard instrument sometime at the end of next year. This is how the Bank Board expressed itself at its last November meeting. I remind you that the next monetary meeting will be next week on Wednesday, which limits my ability to communicate our immediate views a little bit. I think I'd like to put an end to that, and I'll wait for your questions again.
