ParlaMint-CZ_2015-12-15-ps2013-036-09-002-063.u4	In accordance with the Government's legislative work plan for 2015, and in the context of the adoption of Directive 2013/55/EU of the European Parliament and of the Council of 20 November 2013, the Ministry started work on the implementation of this Directive at the beginning of 2014. The proposed legislation aims to ensure full harmonisation of national rules on the recognition of professional qualifications acquired in other Member States with the provisions of the Directive to be introduced by the Member States of the European Union in accordance with Article three into their respective legal systems by 18 January 2016 and to apply from 19 January 2016. I'm just stating the closeness of the implementation deadline. The proposed legislation maintains the existing classification of the law on the recognition of professional qualifications, which is a general regulation governing the concept and the process of recognition of professional qualifications in the Czech Republic. The main innovation is the institutes of the European Professional Card and the warning mechanism, which I would like to present more closely. The European Professional Card will be an electronic document which the applicant will be able to print out and which will be an alternative to the existing decision on recognition of professional qualifications. The purpose of its introduction is, in particular, to shorten recognition deadlines and facilitate the whole process for applicants. I would just like to report here that this is, therefore, a medical profession and a non-medical profession of health workers and then a trade licence issue. The purpose of the alert mechanism shall be to inform the competent authorities of the Member States without delay of persons who have been prohibited or restricted by the profession I have mentioned, including temporarily. This institute concerns only occupations affecting the health and safety of the population, i.e. health professions and professions related to the education of minors. Member States are newly required to send a warning via an electronic system for the exchange of information on the internal market within three days from the date of the effective decision to prohibit or restrict the pursuit of those professions. The authorities which issue the received and sent warning alerts shall be determined by the Member States. Due to the short deadline for sending an alert, the draft amendment is determined by the Ministry of Education, Youth and Sports as the responsible body. The draft amendment contains two new institutes which contribute to simplifying the free movement of persons and access to the pursuit of regulated professions. It is also a question of the obligation imposed on recognition bodies to recognise professional training carried out in another State of the European Union and it is a matter of recognition based on a common framework of training and recognition on the basis of a joint final examination of training. The amendment also allows partial access to the professional activity to which the applicant is qualified in the State of origin and which is part of a regulated profession in the Czech Republic. In total, the proposed amendment concerns nine laws, with the fact that the proposed draft law was prepared in cooperation with the Ministry of Education, Youth and Sports with representatives of the relevant departments, whose scope was affected by the amendment. Taking effect of the amendment is proposed for 1 January 2016 due to the need to comply with the transposition deadline. So here I am just saying that the transposition term has drawn so close that it is clear that this efficiency will have to be shifted in committee discussions.
