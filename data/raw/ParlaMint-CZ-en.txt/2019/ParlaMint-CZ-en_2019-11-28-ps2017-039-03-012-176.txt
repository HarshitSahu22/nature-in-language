On the second point, we will address the so-called Berne Convention, which concerns the protection of European flora, wildlife and habitats. There are two stages of what we're going to decide today. One is purely legal, the other is factual. In fact, this legal issue is an additional ratification of the Convention, and it is submitted because the Czech Republic acceded to the Berne Convention in 1998. It was actually understood as a government contract at the time, because this occurred before the 2001 amendment of the Constitution, which was more clearly adapted to the criteria defining the treaties of the so-called presidential category. According to these criteria, the Convention falls within the category of presidential treaties in accordance with Article 49 (b). (e) the Constitution. Therefore, in order to formally align the process of negotiating the Convention with the current constitutional requirements, the Czech Parliament is presented to express its consent to ratification in addition to the amendment of Annex II, also the text of the Convention and its annexes in its up-to-date version, so that the Convention is subsequently ratified by the President of the Republic. Additional ratification of the Convention has no impact on international obligations of the Czech Republic or national legislation. And the second relevant part concerns the already mentioned amendment to Annex II, which contains a list of strictly protected species of animals. It was agreed in Strasbourg that this Annex would be extended to a new species. This is a feature of the Balkan, but this species is not present in the Czech Republic, and therefore this change has no impact on the commitments of the Czech Republic towards the Berne Convention. In this case, I would also like to ask you to support this proposal. Thank you.
