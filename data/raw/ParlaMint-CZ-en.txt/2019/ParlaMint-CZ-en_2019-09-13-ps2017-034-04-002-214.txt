The main objective of the proposed bill is to implement the relevant provisions of the European Union, in particular Directive 2016/2370 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2016 as regards the opening up of the market for national passenger transport services by rail and the management and management of railway infrastructure into national law. In addition, Regulation 2016/2338 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 14 December 2016 on the opening up of the market for national passenger transport services by rail and, last but not least, Regulation 2016/424 of the European Parliament and of the Council of 9 March 2016 on cable lines. In addition to the implementation of the above-mentioned European Union regulations, it is also proposed to remedy several shortcomings in the existing legislation, which is contained both in the Railway Act and in the Public Services Act. These changes are also relevant from the point of view of European Union legislation. As you can see, the Ministry of Transport is mainly based on implementing European directives, European regulations. As part of the discussion of the bill in the guaranteeing economic committee and subsequently at second reading, a number of amendments were applied, of which the large majority goes in substance beyond the framework of the Government-approved draft law and the need for the implementation of European Union rules. The Ministry of Transport has agreed on nine of them, with a neutral position on five of them, and with a negative opinion on the remaining ones. From the point of view of the Ministry of Transport, the amendment affecting the definition of a publicly inaccessible train and the amendment intervening in the regulation of mobility of persons entrusted with major functions must be identified as the most problematic. The Railway Act distinguishes the lifts on publicly accessible and non-accessible trains. The existing definition of these two categories of lifts is inappropriate from the point of view of the limits of European Union law, while the government bill seeks to remedy this unsatisfactory situation. However, the adoption of the amendment would deepen the current unsatisfactory situation even further, since it is proposed to define a publicly inaccessible train as a train used by virtually any person for any needs. However, the law of the European Union exempts only trains serving exclusively their owners for their own freight transport. The Ministry of Transport is consistently of the opinion that trains cannot be excluded on a flat-rate basis from the scope of the European Union rules and insists on defining a publicly inaccessible train in the Ministry of Transport in its proposed form. [[9.40 hours]] In relation to the amendment intervening in the regulation of mobility of persons entrusted with the main functions, it can be noted that European Union law requires that mobility of persons entrusted with the main functions does not create a conflict of interest. It is clear from that text that it must be ensured that the mobility of all persons in charge of the main functions, i.e. not only members of statutory bodies, to which the amendment narrows the initial adjustment, but also of ordinary employees, must not create a conflict of interest. Although both amendments were recommended by the Economic Committee, they certainly cannot be identified. Their adoption would make the new legislation informal with the law of the European Union, in which case the Czech Republic would be in real danger of initiating a infringement procedure by the European Commission, which could result in the imposition of sanctions which can be imposed in the form of a lump sum of EUR 1,310 000, and at the same time in the form of a penalty for every day of delay, which may also amount to several tens of thousands of euros. The Ministry of Transport, which is legally responsible for the compliance of national legislation with European Union rules and for the proper implementation of these regulations into national law, therefore reiterates its fundamental opposition to these amendments. In this context, I would strongly ask all Members not to support the adoption of these amendments, for the reasons that I have mentioned above.
