Ladies and gentlemen, let me briefly respond to some of the misinformation that occurred yesterday in connection with my amendment to the amendment to the veterinary law on social networks and are also sent by e-mail. Whether this is a disinformation of purpose or ignorance, I will certainly not discuss it here. I would just like to comment on those points in my amendment that have just been misinterpreted. The definition of shelter is based on the relevant provisions of the Civil Code, which govern the legal regime of stray and abandoned animals. This scheme has been in force since 1964 and clearly follows that the municipality, not the finder, may entrust the animal to the shelter operator and that the local municipality concerned cannot be excluded from the process of placing the animal in the shelter under any circumstances. This is a very common regulation, which also applies in other European countries. The proposed definition thus fully corresponds to the doctrinal disappearance of the concept of shelters and in this context I refer to the opinion of the Central Commission for Animal Protection, which is the advisory body of the Ministry of Agriculture on the animal section according to which the shelter is understood: the shelter is established by a municipality or shelter established by a private person who cooperates with the municipality in ensuring the care of abandoned and stray animals, and it is not possible for there to be a private shelter for animals in which private persons would gather abandoned and stray animals without cooperation with the municipality. According to Czech law it is not possible for such care to take place without the knowledge and cooperation with the municipality. I also refer to the very definition contained in the Parliamentary Institute study. Accent on the necessary cooperation with municipalities also sounds in the long term from the head of the State Veterinary Administration, which is also evident from the media outputs. Further disinformation concerns the obligation to conclude contracts with municipalities. It is certainly not true that my proposal introduces an obligation to conclude contracts with municipalities. From what I have already said, it is clear that municipalities have a major say in the process of caring for stray and abandoned animals. That is precisely why my proposal contains the phrase "after agreement" which implies a single obligation, namely to inform the municipalities of the animals found, as stipulated by the current 1964 law. The issues of the agreement are a matter of agreement between two parties. This can happen during personal negotiations, in the form of electronic or e-mail communication. Municipalities that do not have sufficient capacity to take care of the animals, respectively. do not have the funds to pay for this care, nothing prevents animals from continuing to direct legal associations. After all, this model is also preferred in practice by legal associations. I would like to stress that my amendment only affects shelters, this is for facilities that take care of animals wandering or abandoned. My proposal does not concern other categories of animals, e.g. animals donated or purchased from troubled breeding, imported from abroad, whether legally or illegally, or officially collected, for which the regime defined by the generally binding legislation, in particular by the Civil Code, the Law on the Protection of Animals against Abuse and the Criminal Code applies. Of course, legal associations that care for other categories of animals beyond stray or abandoned animals will be able to continue to operate. As I have already said, my proposal really only affects shelters and animals roamed or abandoned, in full compliance with the legal regime that has been in force since 1964 when Civil Code No 40 was adopted. Any reports that other facilities other than shelters should be banned are completely odd and unsubstantiated. Finally, I would like to point out that the points contained in my amendment concerning the shelter regime are not points of change. Therefore, there is no change in the state in force. The aim is only to formalise the regime of stray and abandoned animals in accordance with the Civil Code in order to avoid circumvention of legal obligations. The reason for the proposal was numerous complaints by both citizens and, above all, local authorities. The problem with some shelters is the fact that, in addition to real stray or abandoned animals, they also take care of animals of unclear origin and, in one case, require municipalities to pay for care costs. The proposal was, of course, consulted with a number of protective facilities and shelters headed by the largest network of Czech shelters, a renowned organization called the Animal Protection League. As regards the clear rules for the operation of breeding shelters, I refer to the text of my colleague Zahradník's amendment. My amendment does not address this. Thank you for your attention.
