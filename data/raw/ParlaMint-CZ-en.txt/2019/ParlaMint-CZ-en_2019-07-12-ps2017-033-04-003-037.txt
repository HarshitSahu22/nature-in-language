ParlaMint-CZ_2019-07-12-ps2017-033-04-003-037.u2	Thank you, Mr. Vice-President. Colleagues, let me briefly remind you of the Government's bill, which changes some of the laws in the internal administration section. I have presented this press in detail during the first reading which took place in January of this year at the 26th meeting and also during the second reading which took place at the 28th meeting. The main reason for submitting this proposal is the need to adapt certain regulations within the competence of the Ministry of the Interior to the Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on the promotion of the free movement of citizens by simplifying the requirements for the submission of certain authentic instruments in the European Union. This directly applicable European Union Regulation allows for the replacement of a requirement for the official translation of certain documents, such as matriculation documents, by a multilingual standard form issued by the competent authority together with its own document. Following this, the Czech law requires the proper powers of the authorities to issue these forms. This change brings simplification of administration to Czech citizens if they submit documents in a foreign language to the Czech authorities, as well as when they submit Czech documents abroad. Furthermore, the proposal includes changes that respond to practical knowledge, in particular in relation to the verification of copies and signatures, in relation to the Basic Registers Administration or changes concerning the introduction of the possibility to establish and manage data boxes exclusively electronically, for example, using an electronic ID card, that is, without the need for a visit by the competent authority.

ParlaMint-CZ_2019-07-12-ps2017-033-04-003-037.u4	Thank you. With regard to the amendments, I support the amendments proposed by the Committee on Public Administration and Regional Development, in particular the extension of the date of entry into force of the Act following the current legislative process. However, I cannot identify with Mr Čižinský's amendment. The aim of this proposal is to shorten the time of the death book at the registry office from the current 75 years to 50. First of all, it is not clear whether the problem described in the reasoning of the amendment is too intense to justify the intended change. The Ministry of the Interior has submitted to the Government an extensive amendment to the Act on Matrices, in which it responds to the amount of knowledge that results from application practice and addresses a number of topics that are not sufficiently covered by the existing text of the Act on Matrices. This proposal went through an inter-departmental process in which it was expressed by the number of entities applying the Matrics Act and which therefore contributed to a number of practical knowledge. The problem associated with the length of time periods for which the matriculation books are kept at the registry offices was not open at all. The adoption of Mr Čižinský's proposal would be accompanied by significant complications and costs on the side of archives, which would have to accept unexpectedly and shockingly 25 years of death books. This would represent a considerable burden both for archives and for the hand-over authorities. Significant costs would also require the digitisation of such a number of documents. The matrice authorities do not digitize their matrice books, while without the implementation of archive processing and digitization, the accessibility of matrice books for searching would be more difficult, as the demand for archives would be concentrated between an disproportionately larger number of matrice offices. It should also be said that, in the event of a shortening of the time of deposit of death books at the registry offices, and thus speeding up their transfer to archiving will increase the number of cases where data on still-living persons will be kept in the death books. For example, if a man aged 30 died in 1960, the surviving widow may still be alive today, and it would therefore be necessary to ensure proper protection of her personal data. In view of what I said, the Ministry of the Interior cannot support Mr Čižinský's amendment. And for the record, I'm just saying that the guarantee committee has not taken any position on this proposal. Thank you.
