ParlaMint-CZ_2019-06-18-ps2017-030-09-007-004.u4	Hello, hello, everyone. Excuse my indisposition, but I'm a little rusty waiting. But I'll be in a hurry to make it to 6:30. Mr President, honourable Members, I was assigned to the 8th meeting of the Senate on 2 May 1919 to justify the Senate resolution in the Chamber of Deputies on the return of the Chamber of Deputies to the draft law amending Act No. 114/1995 Coll. on Inland Navigation, as amended. The draft law was discussed at that Senate meeting as Senate press number 66. During the meeting of the Senate, the votes were expressed in favour of both the approval of the draft inland navigation law, as approved by the Chamber of Deputies, and the prevailing votes to extend the bill so that under conditions similar to those proposed for watermen, it would allow the use of alcoholic beverages also for cyclists. The main reason adopted by the Senate resolution on the return of the draft with amendments changing also Act No. 361/2000 Coll., on road traffic, is therefore the interest of the senators to set not only for watermen but also for cyclists similar conditions for tolerating the consumption of small quantities of alcohol, i.e. the extension of the Senate parliamentary initiative already approved by the amendment to the Road Traffic Act, Senate press No. 261, approved by the Senate on 15 November 2018. In the Chamber of Deputies, the printing number is currently 338. Respective of the first Senate initiative discussed in 2016, which arose from citizens from the Zlín Region and South Moravian Region and signed by more than 10,000 people. The Senate therefore proposes that the law should not only be applied to watermen in the direction of small non-motor vessels on selected streams, but also to cyclists under clearly defined conditions, where they do not transport children in a child's transport seat, in a trailer or in a children's bicycle on cycle paths, special-purpose or local roads or on roads III. the class admitted to consume less alcohol. In both cases tolerance of 0,5 per cent of the alcohol content found is proposed. The Senate is convinced that there is no significant reason for the law to approach cyclists differently from watermen when it comes to their activities on waterways and roads which are not associated with an increased risk to the life and health of persons. Liberalization of rules not only for watermen, but also for cyclists will help promote further development of tourism in our country. It will help hundreds of small businessmen who develop their business activities along a dense network of watercourses. For similar reasons, the Senate is proposed to reduce the amount of the fine for the infringement of a cyclist in the management of a one-seat round after the unacceptable consumption of alcoholic beverages. [[18.30 hours]] In the debate at the Senate plenary, the question was also discussed as to whether the amendments were a so-called label. The Senate is of the opinion, given the definition of the so-called label in the Constitutional Court's findings, that in this case it is not the legislation of another legislative material, but that there is a close relationship between the content and purpose of the original bill and the content and purpose of the amendment under consideration.
