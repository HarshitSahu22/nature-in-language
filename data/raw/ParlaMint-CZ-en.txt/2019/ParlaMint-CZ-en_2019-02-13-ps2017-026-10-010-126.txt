ParlaMint-CZ_2019-02-13-ps2017-026-10-010-126.u2	Thank you, Mr President. The submitted material was prepared on the basis of Article 19 of the Constitution of the International Labour Organisation. It provides for the Government to submit to Parliament every new convention and recommendation of the International Labour Organisation, including its opinion on them. The purpose of this commitment is, above all, to inform the legislator about the current development of international law in the field of labour and social affairs. In order to fulfil this obligation, two documents were submitted to the Senate and the Chamber of Deputies adopted in 2016 concerning two different conventions governing the work of seafarers. [[11.50 hours]] The first document amends the 2006 Maritime Labour Convention. Parliament agreed to ratify this Convention in the autumn of last year. The submitted amendments shall contain procedural rules concerning the extension of the relevant maritime certificate issued under the Convention for individual ships. It is actually a reconciliation of this procedure with the modification applied to other certificates, which are in the sense of the International Maritime Organisation. Since the existing national regulation in the Maritime Navigation Act fully complies with these vessels, it is proposed to ratify these changes in order to maintain the commitments of the Czech Republic in this area in line with international developments. The second document deals with the technical specifications of the issue of Navy books. The relevant Convention of the International Labour Organisation was not ratified by the Czech Republic and dealt with in a different way. Therefore, the government only took note of this second document without the need for any changes. Thank you for your attention.

ParlaMint-CZ_2019-02-13-ps2017-026-10-010-126.u4	Thank you. Mr President, honourable members of the government, colleagues, colleagues, I would like to repeat this a bit. On 7 September 2017, at its 60th meeting, the Chamber of Deputies adopted a government proposal to approve the ratification of the International Labour Organisation Convention at sea, which was adopted in 2006. The aim of this Convention is to harmonise and update the system of international labour standards in this sector adopted over the past 80 years. It lays down minimum requirements for seafarers working on a ship and contains basic rules on conditions of employment, working time and rest, accommodation, catering, health protection and medical care, as well as social security. Basic principles are therefore already adopted by the Czech Republic. Now the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs has tabled a proposal in which the amendments, which were approved at the 105th session of the International Labour Conference in 2016, were no longer incorporated into the 2006 version of the Convention, which was approved by the House, as I said, in September last year. Specifically, it is to supplement the issue of harassment and bullying into the non-binding part of the Convention, dedicated to safety and health protection at work, to allow for an exceptional extension of the so-called 'obligatory'. a certificate for work at sea and a declaration of compliance with the conditions of work at sea if, in the context of a renewal inspection, the ship continues to comply with national legislation or other measures implementing the requirements of the Convention, but a new certificate cannot be issued immediately and made available on board that ship. This is about aligning the procedures for certification of work at sea and the procedures for other certificates issued under the conventions adopted under the International Maritime Organisation. In 2016, there was also an amendment to the Annex to the Convention number 185 on the identity cards of seafarers, revised in 2003. The change has responded to the development of biometric data processing since 2003. Although the Czech Republic is not a sea state, many citizens of the Czech Republic work at sea in employment. Further amendments to the Convention that protect their interests should therefore be adopted by the Czech Republic, and I therefore recommend that the Chamber of Deputies agree to this proposal. Thank you for your attention.
