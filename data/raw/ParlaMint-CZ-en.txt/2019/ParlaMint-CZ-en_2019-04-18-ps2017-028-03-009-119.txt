ParlaMint-CZ_2019-04-18-ps2017-028-03-009-119.u6	Thank you, Mr President. I'd just like to clarify what's really going on with this contract. This concerns sport, therefore the International Convention against Doping was approved in Paris on 19 October 2005 and the following annexes are an integral part of it, a list of prohibited substances and doping methods and an international standard for therapeutic derogations. Both Annexes shall always be valid from 1 January of the calendar year concerned. The abovementioned Annexes are annexes to the international treaty of a presidential nature and, as such, must be discussed in the same legislative manner at the level of the Chamber of Deputies, the Senate and the President of the Republic. Practice so far, virtually. the procedure is as follows. The list of prohibited substances is issued by the WADA, the World Anti-Doping Agency, and is sent through UNESCO for comments by individual signatories to the International Convention against Doping on 1 October of the calendar year. Thus, individual countries have time for possible comments from 1 in theory. 10 to 31. The 12th year in question, because, as already indicated, the new list applies from 1 January of the following year. In fact, however, comments are merely typos in names or changes of prohibited substances in groups or omissions of a substance, otherwise nothing can be changed on the list in question. Unfortunately, the whole legislative process of approval takes place in the Czech Republic so that in the three months in question, which is October to December, the list cannot be discussed and approved. As we know, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs has international agreements, but the issue of anti-doping is addressed by the Ministry of Education. This means that the process in question should coordinate these two ministries. Due to anti-doping practice, the list of prohibited substances has already been published on its website by the WADA and the Czech Anti-Doping Committee. These lists are binding for all athletes as they must follow the world anti-doping code. Sports associations in the Czech Republic and their members of the Czech Republic of course committed to respect the code, so from this point of view there has not yet been a situation that in the Czech Republic, although the list of prohibited substances has not been legislatively approved, the athletes did not respect it. [[12.00 hours]] The aim of the National Sports Agency, which should have the issue of anti-doping in its strategy, should be to discuss the above two annexes in the Parliament of the Czech Republic and the President of the Czech Republic in the three-month period of the relevant year, i.e. from 1. 10 to 31. 12. both annexes should be valid ideally from 1 January of the following calendar year. The aim of the National Sports Agency should be to avoid situations currently occurring, i.e. that the list of prohibited substances and doping methods as an annex to the international treaty in force from 1 January 2018 is to be approved for second reading in the Chamber of Deputies of Parliament in April 2019. I know that there will be a major improvement in this situation within the National Sports Agency in the future. Thank you. I just wanted to shed some light on this situation.
