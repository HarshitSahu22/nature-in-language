So to get to know the whole of this position, it is because this amendment allows us to calculate the assessment base of insurance premiums for insured persons, for which insurance premiums are paid by the State through the state budget under Act No. 48/1997 Coll., on Public Health Insurance. In particular, it is proposed that the basis for the calculation should be 25 per cent of the average wage. The similar system already worked in our Republic between 2006 and 2009. This proposed change allows the assessment base to be changed by state insured people depending on the current development of the average wage. The proposed amendment thus removes, according to the proponents, the schizophrenic situation established by an effective legal standard, where the level of assessment for state insured persons is currently laid down by law, but the current amount depends on the government's regulation as a legal act. From this point of view, the proposed change in the method of calculating the assessment base in question brings elements of stability, not only in legal terms, but also in practical terms and in particular in the form of foreseeable development of insurance premiums, transparency, which also implies a certain presumption of transparency for the state budget. ParlaMint-CZ_2019-11-29-ps2017-039-04-010-019.u10	Ladies and gentlemen, let me present to you our amendment. The reason for its submission is, on the one hand, a long-term shortage of blood donors who go to donate blood without a contribution, so that they are not provided with a financial reimbursement of expenditure related to blood collection or its components under the law governing specific health services, with the exception of the reimbursement of proven travel costs associated with collection, as well as the pursuit of a fairer arrangement of relations between blood donors and their employers, if the donors are employees and the State. At present, blood-donor employers bear on their shoulders the costs associated with the participation of their employees - donors for collection. It is the employers who are obliged to work on the basis of Section 203 of Law No. 262/2006 Coll., Labour Code, provide work leave and compensation for their income. However, this is very problematic in view of the fact that blood collection is an act in the public interest in the case of non-sponsored blood donors. According to our conviction, these costs should not be borne by employers, but by the state, or by public budgets, namely the public health insurance system. Inside it employers should pay insurance premiums, the amount of which will be reduced by the amount of compensation paid to employees - non-profit donors. The advantage of this arrangement is, in addition to a fairer distribution of the economic burden among the various actors, strengthening motivational elements. During the existence of the current regulation, we have noted cases where employers have been very reluctant to participate in their blood collection, for obvious reasons. They did not consider it fair that this act in the public interest should be paid for by their private means. The proposed proposal addresses this deficiency and can thus contribute to increasing the number of blood donors in the Czech Republic. In a detailed debate, I will sign up for this amendment. Thank you.
