Although the Regulation on supervision is directly applicable, it is necessary to transpose certain provisions into national law. The reason for this is that market surveillance is within the exclusive competence of EU Member States and only Member States can establish their supervisory authorities, grant them appropriate powers and lay down obligations. In the course of the preparation of the implementation of the Regulation on supervision, it was decided, following consultations with the departments concerned and the supervisory authorities and representatives of economic operators, to process a new legislation which would unite the market surveillance of products by the amended EU rules set out in Annex 1 to the Regulation on supervision. The adoption of the new legislation has been assessed as the most appropriate version of implementation with a view to ensuring clarity and clarity of legislation and taking into account the scope of the Regulation on supervision. The proposed bill is of a purely procedural nature. As the most important aspects, the determination of the powers of the market surveillance authorities in products may be indicated in such a way that all supervisory authorities have the same minimum set of powers in the market surveillance of products falling within the harmonised sphere which comply with the powers of the supervisory authorities in the other Member States, the establishment of measures which the supervisory authorities may adopt in the event of infringements and which are uniform for all supervisory authorities and, last but not least, the establishment of penalties for non-compliance with the obligations imposed by the Regulation on supervision. Product safety and compliance with legal requirements is one of the main priorities listed in the strategic material of consumer policy priority 2015/2020, which was adopted by the Government of the Czech Republic in 2015. In order to ensure product safety and the consistency of products with the required legislation, it is necessary to ensure effective market surveillance in products, paying attention to close cooperation and cooperation between supervisory authorities at both national and European level. Involvement in information and communication systems at European level, monitoring market developments, application of the principle of risk analysis and transparency vis-à-vis consumers and economic operators and their information - in addition to consumer protection, product market surveillance also plays an important role in the field of free movement of goods and also market competitiveness, as it affects those economic operators who do not respect the requirements of the legislation on the marketing of products. In doing so, such economic operators may have an unjustified competitive advantage over economic operators which consistently comply with the legislation and cooperate with the supervisory authorities. In the Czech Republic, there is a system of market surveillance authorities with products whose supervisory powers and powers are laid down in specific regulations. These are, for example, the Czech Trade Inspection Authority, the Czech Office for Arms and Ammunition Testing, the Czech Mining Authority, the State Energy Inspection Authority, the Punctual Office, the Office for Technical Standardization, Metrology and State Testing or the Czech Telecommunications Office. [[15.50 hours]] The Czech Republic as an EU Member State is part of the EU internal market. The most developed and best established of the four fundamental freedoms is the free movement of goods. For the vast majority of products legal requirements are laid down at European Union level, that is the so-called harmonisation sphere, and for all products the general requirements for product safety apply. Products falling within the non-harmonic sphere shall be subject to general product safety requirements which are supplemented by a principle of mutual recognition. One of the essential aspects of ensuring effective market surveillance for products which must enable dangerous or non-conformity products to be identified and removed from the market or from the regulation of remedial measures and sanctioning those economic operators who place products which do not comply with harmonisation legislation on the market. Supervision of the product market is the most important means of ensuring consumer protection as regards their safety and health, but at the same time contributes to the equal position of economic operators on the market, as it identifies those entities which gain a competitive advantage over responsible entities by failing to comply with legal requirements. This also involves an effort to reduce the number of dangerous or non-conformity products that are placed on the market not only in the Czech Republic but also throughout the European Union. At national level, there is no legal regulation governing market surveillance in products falling within the harmonised sphere. Supervision is regulated separately in a number of legislation and its unification has not been made even by the adoption of a control order. The rules of procedure shall contain provisions of a general nature applicable to all types of controls, including market surveillance in products in the field of the harmonised sphere and its provisions shall apply to all supervisory authorities, but may not logically contain specific provisions relating to market surveillance in products falling within the harmonised sphere. Moreover, it is a national procedural regulation which does not follow up on the European Union's legislation, even if it is not contrary to the supervisory regulation. The new legislation would implement the supervisory regulation taking into account the national general procedural arrangements already in force and would lay down uniform market surveillance rules for products falling within the harmonised sphere. The new legislation would be clear and uniform for both supervisory authorities and economic operators. Ladies and gentlemen, I believe that this is a very important issue which is directly linked to consumer safety and health and the position of economic operators on the market.
