I in point 4 of Article 4 (1) (a) of Regulation (EU) No Now allow me to justify my two amendments to this bill. The first concerns the so-called sub-cost prices, i.e. the need to sell the supplier at a price below its cost. The second amendment concerns the so-called retail price, which means the sale of retail products at a price below the price at which the products were purchased. So first of all, the sub-cost prices. The consumer, of course, is interested in buying at the best possible price, which also leads the retailer to purchase goods at minimum prices without giving up motivation to sell with the highest possible margin. In view of the fact that retail trade has a decisive position on the market, it is often not motivated to respect the costs incurred for the product and requires supplies of goods from its position at a price which does not correspond to the cost of production. In view of the fact that this is not a comparatively unusual problem, most Member States of the European Union already have adequate regulations aim to protect producers and processors, and in this context they set limit average costs for the production of individual types of food under which retail cannot buy. The price of food in these countries, the legislation of which represents a picture of the proposed amendment, in countries such as France, Italy, Spain and Germany, is set as follows. The professional associations shall, according to the actual production costs of each undertaking, calculate the average costs of each commodity of plant and animal production and the processing associations shall calculate the final costs by type of goods by item. Furthermore, in France, 20% of the margin representing a reasonable profit and the cost of logistics is added to the costs, while retail is obliged to accept the resulting price when purchasing goods from primary production and subsequently from processors. In the case of the Czech Republic, it could be considered that the prices of primary producers and processors would be agreed by the Institute of Agricultural Economy and Information and controlled by the State Agricultural and Intervention Fund. The retail pressure at lower prices than the actual costs gradually destroys domestic agricultural production and the processing industry, which are gradually replaced by highly subsidised food from imports, only if there are enough of them abroad. This amendment aims precisely at this practice so that the supplier is not forced to sell at prices below its cost. As regards the second amendment, which concerns, as I have already outlined, the retail price, it is the sale of retail products at a price lower than that at which they were purchased. A key condition for the existence of balanced relationships is the ban on the application of retail retail retail prices. Currently, retail sales are widely used by events, sometimes even promotional sales, mostly at sub-cost prices as a marketing tool to attract customers, while losses from sales of action products are offset by huge margins on other goods. This enables sellers to make the necessary profit, often subsequently taken to foreign countries, where the company owns sales networks in the Czech Republic is located. Especially in the case of food goods, the state is losing up to tens of billions of crowns on VAT. Financial controls in the Czech Republic on each production and processing company always focus on the price of the purchase of a particular product and the price of its sale. If sales happen for a particular product, i.e. stock items, are lower than purchases, each penny is delivered. However, this procedure is not applied to retail sales, particularly in large retail stores. Instead, all purchases and VAT shall be added together, followed by a similar procedure for sales and the difference in value added tax shall constitute a minimum levy. It can therefore be estimated that if the value added tax on both the purchase and sale of food were to be quantified for each item separately, this would represent an increase in the revenue of the state budget of approximately CZK 20 billion or more. It is not clear why this system applies to the business and processing sector and not to retail sales. It is offered that control of these basic conditions of operation of the law on significant market power could be exercised by the Office for the Protection of Competition or at random by the Ministry of Finance. For these reasons, the proposed adjustments aim at increasing the balance of trade relations, in particular between the primary producer and the processor on the one hand and the retailer on the other. Finally, let me stress that the possible failure to accept these changes can ultimately lead to the devastation of national fertile agricultural land, the rise in food prices and, above all, the lack of food for our entire population, although the conditions in the Czech Republic would allow for full self-sufficiency in all products of our temperate zone. Thank you in advance for understanding and supporting these important changes.
