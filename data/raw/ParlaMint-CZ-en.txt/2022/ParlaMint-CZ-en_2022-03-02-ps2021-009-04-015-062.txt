ParlaMint-CZ_2022-03-02-ps2021-009-04-015-062.u2	Madam President, ladies and gentlemen, I would like to present to you today the proposal for a Council regulation amending Council Regulation (EC, Euratom) No 1605/2002. 168/2007 on the establishment of the European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights. This proposal shall be submitted: The Chamber of Deputies with a request for prior consent in accordance with its Rules of Procedure, as this is a proposal adopted on the basis of the so-called flexibility clause. At the same time, EU law requires unanimity between Member States to accept the proposal. This first caused some interruption of negotiations in the EU Council, which was subsequently renewed in May last year. In June the proposal was unanimously approved by the Member States in the Council and the European Parliament also gave its consent at the beginning of July. So now the government is submitting the proposal to the Chamber of Deputies of the Czech Parliament so that it can comment on its final form and agree with it. The content of the proposal is an amendment to the regulation governing the functioning of the European Union Agency for Fundamental Rights. It is one of the agencies which aims to analyse respect for fundamental rights in the EU and to provide expert advice to EU institutions and Member States on how to best protect and respect these rights. It is therefore primarily a technical and analytical body that issues reports, conducts surveys and provides and publishes information. The Agency does not make any binding decisions, has no enforcement powers, does not deal with individual complaints, nor does it itself determine EU or Member State policies. Its basic function is purely advisory and expert.

ParlaMint-CZ_2022-03-02-ps2021-009-04-015-062.u4	The amendment to the Regulation is based on the evaluation of the Agency's activities and has two main objectives. The first is to respond to the fact that the Agency was established in 2007 before the Treaty of Lisbon was effective, and therefore its functioning and organisation do not reflect the current structure of EU law. This is primarily about the demise of the rights of the European Communities and the associated pillar structures of European law. Therefore, the proposal originally aimed at extending the Agency's scope to the former second and third pillars of EU law, a common foreign and security policy and police and judicial cooperation in criminal matters. However, during the negotiations in the working bodies of the Council, consensus was reached only to extend police and judicial cooperation in criminal matters. On the contrary, the common foreign and security policy was ultimately not included in the Agency's remit. This is also related to replacing references to European Community law, references to EU law on many points of the amendment. I would like to stress that, overall, the proposal is a compromise of the ambitions and wishes of the European Commission, the Council and the European Parliament. Both the European Commission and Parliament were more ambitious, on the contrary, the Council and the Member States had a more reserved approach. Each actor brought his imagination into the process, but he was willing to retreat in order to achieve consensus and a common goal. This is a successful and effective functioning of the Agency and its continued action to protect fundamental rights in the EU in accordance with its principles. I therefore wish to ask the Chamber of Deputies, together with the Senate which has already given its consent, to join this general consensus and to give its preliminary consent. Thank you for your attention.
