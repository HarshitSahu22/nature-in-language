This House press number 156, i.e. the Social Security Treaty between the Czech Republic and Bosnia and Herzegovina, also follows up on the period that has been dealt with here in relation to these matters between the Czech Republic and the former Yugoslavia of 1957, which is logically outdated in many cases and no longer meets current needs. Therefore, this newly negotiated contract presented to the House. Both in terms of content and formality, the contract corresponds to internationally recognised requirements for the coordination of social security applied in bilateral and multilateral treaties as well as EU rules. As regards the substantive content of this Treaty, it leads to the definition of participation in social and health insurance in a particular State, the contract provides for sickness and maternity benefits, cash benefits, health care, pensions, occupational injury benefits, unemployment benefits and child allowance. A fundamental change from previous treatment is the provision and payment of health care. The costs will therefore no longer be borne by the State budget, but by the health insurance undertaking for which the person is insured. Of course, the detailed scope of the contract is then defined in the very content of the document, so I would also like to ask for the support of this House press. Thank you for your attention.

Once again, it is a presidential contract which is submitted before ratification to express its consent to both chambers of Parliament of the Czech Republic pursuant to Art. The amount to be reported in column 060 of this row: Original deduction according to Article 112 point (a) of CRR. (a) and (e) the Constitution of the Czech Republic. And now for the differences. Between the Czech Republic and Bosnia and Herzegovina, the social security area is already regulated by the 1957 Convention. The Treaty needs to be adapted to the current needs of both countries, and a new one has therefore been signed to replace the original one. A major change in the new contract is brought only in the field of health care. Its costs are no longer borne by the State but by the relevant insurance undertaking for its insured persons, but in principle only in the case of necessary and urgent care. The proposed contract does not differ much from the 1957 Convention in terms of content. It is, however, a more precise and formal arrangement of a standard document that respects fundamental internationally recognised principles. Once again, the Treaty will not have a major impact on the state budget. It meets both Czech and European standards in both formal and content terms. Thank you.
