ParlaMint-CZ_2022-09-29-ps2021-040-01-005-005.u2	Thank you, Mr President. Colleagues, colleagues, this amendment to Act 155 on Pension Insurance follows up on a topic that was discussed very intensively here in the Chamber of Deputies a few months ago and weeks ago, and the issue of medical paramedics and early departures really did not allow anyone to sleep in the imaginary House bench for a while. However, we are returning here to the situation at the end of the Chamber of Deputies of the last parliamentary term, when an amendment was introduced at the time, which dealt with the earlier departures of people working in the integrated rescue system and which is due to take effect on 1 January 2023. However, the adjustment, as legally in the text at this moment, is actually impossible in practice. That is why we have come to this proposal with the fact that this proposal actually clearly specifies certain definitions. This means that this early, earlier departure will concern selected professions of health care professionals so that their earlier reduction in retirement is also clearly established. This means that the reduction of the general retirement age of 30 calendar months will be in the work of 4,400 shifts, which means that it is an average of 20 years in the employment of a medical paramedic. For each additional 74 shifts, the pension age is reduced by one calendar month. The pension age may be reduced by 60 calendar months, i.e. on average over 30 years worked. The draft law also includes an increase in pension insurance premiums, which will be paid by employers in that part of the contributions. This is the setting of a certain participation of employers to cover those financial costs and financial impacts that will arise in this way and will actually have consequences logically for the pension system. And we are at least partially balancing this so that we do not create another significant deficit in the pension system. This increase will be gradual in order to increase the employer's premium rates by a total of 5 percentage points. In 2023, the rate will increase by 2 percentage points, from 2024 to 2026 thereafter after one percentage point. The increase will only apply to insurance premiums in relation to those professions affected by the reduction in retirement age. It does not therefore concern all employees. The draft law also includes an adjustment of the administrative obligations of health care paramedics employers, in particular with regard to the register of worked shifts. The effectiveness of the Act is proposed on 1 January 2023 and depends logically on the effectiveness of the existing non-functional legislation, which is also repealed. The general solution to reducing the pension age of so-called demanding professions will be part of the comprehensive pension reform that we, as the Ministry of Labour and Social Affairs, are to submit to the legislative process in the second half of 2023.
