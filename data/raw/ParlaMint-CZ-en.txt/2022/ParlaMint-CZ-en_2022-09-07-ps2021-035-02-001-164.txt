The proposal for a new mandate is based on foreign policy and security priorities of the Czech Republic, which are significantly influenced by Russian aggression in Ukraine. For this reason too, the priority of the new mandate is to strengthen the defence of the eastern border of NATO, for which we propose to allocate up to 1,200 people. Since April this year, the Czech Republic has been the leader of the multinational NATO fighting group in Slovakia, thereby increasing significantly our contribution to similar alliance groups in Lithuania and Latvia. The new item is the protection of our diplomatic interests in Ukraine in the form of a protection team for the representative office of the Czech Republic. As far as other foreign operations are concerned, most of them will continue to the same extent as we have so far. The other foreign operations are KFOR in Kosovo, EU IRINI in the Mediterranean Sea, MFO in Sinai, UN MINUSMA in Mali and Golan Heights. An exception is Bosnia and Herzegovina, where we propose a slight increase in our military presence in the operation of the European Union ALTHEA. The proposed proposal does not include the continuation of our military engagement with Iraq and the EU training mission in Mali. The political situation in these countries is problematic and difficult to predict. In the case of an EU training mission in Mali, the Union shall discuss its future. We are constantly analysing the situation, and we are actively involved in these EU discussions. If the situation becomes clear or if we find other options for our future involvement in the Sahel or the wider region of West Africa and Iraq, I will submit a separate proposal to the Government and Parliament in the autumn of this year. In addition to the proposal for action in specific foreign missions and operations, the material informs on the selected forces of the Ministry of Defence resort for possible deployment outside the territory of the Czech Republic in the framework of the forces of NATO's rapid response, the fighting group of the European Union and UN peacekeeping operations. If these separate forces are activated, we will address their deployment with a separate mandate. In the presented material we propose action in total up to 1 362 people in 2023, as well as in 2024. As I have already said, so far without Iraq and the EU training mission in Mali. The estimated amount of funds needed for the proposed activity abroad is approximately 1.4 billion in 2023. For comparison purposes, for 2022, the plan was 2.64 billion, so the amount for this year and for future years is lower. The proposal also includes the stay of NATO armed forces in the territory of the Czech Republic. As part of strengthening common alliance defence, there will be a need to ensure the short-term presence of units of allied states on the territory of the Czech Republic and beyond normal movements and exercises. This may, for example, be a short stay in connection with flights and transits through our territory or the creation of logistical facilities for the operation of allied forces along the eastern border of the Alliance. We propose this possible short-term allied presence in the Czech Republic in the number of up to 800 people. This stay in the territory of the Czech Republic does not in any way mean the mandate for permanent residence of the armed forces of the Member States of the Alliance on our territory or for the establishment of their military bases. Legally, all proposed foreign operations are in accordance with international law. The proposed scope of activity in foreign operations is an expression of the willingness and readiness of the Czech Republic to continue to actively contribute to international security. This makes the Czech Republic a responsible and respected ally and partner who operates in NATO missions and operations, the European Union, the UN and other groups. Thank you for your attention.
