ParlaMint-CZ_2022-10-14-ps2021-041-04-003-099.u2	Ladies and gentlemen, the Ministry of Justice, which I represent here today, has drawn up a draft law in accordance with the Government's legislative work plan for 2020, which amends certain laws in connection with the use of digital tools and procedures in company law and the functioning of public registers. The aim of this proposal is primarily to implement Directive 2019/1151 of the European Parliament and of the Council as regards the use of digital tools and procedures in company law, whose transposition period expired on 1 August 2022. In particular, the directive requires: (a) In all Member States it must be possible at least to establish and register a limited liability company in a business register using a fully online model, i.e. digitally, as it must be possible to register branches of the company completely online. (b) The company must be able to submit the required documents or data to the competent authorities via the Internet. (c) Member States must establish a register of persons who are excluded from the exercise of their duties in the institutions of the company. (d) The fourth, most important pillar is: the exchange of information between Member States' trade registers is strengthened and the amount of data on European companies available free of charge on the Internet is expanded. The proposed changes regarding the process of online establishment of the company follow up on the amendment of the notary order last year, in which it was possible to write notary entries in electronic form and organise a secure videoconferencing with a notary. The process of setting up a company will be further simplified by creating a model of a social contract available on the Internet and by abolishing the requirement to obtain a trade licence before the company is established. [[10.40 hours]] According to the current regulation, it is necessary for the founders of the capital company to meet first with a notary, then report a trade with a business office if they want to do business in a business manner, and only then finish the notary with the company's registration in the business register again. Newly, the founders with the notary will be able to establish and register the company in the Commercial Register and, independently of this, to handle trade licenses by the Trades Office, which will be significantly easier and cheaper. It is also proposed to further reduce the administrative burden on the founders of the companies, where it will not be necessary to document certain data in duplicate both when the company is entered in the Commercial Register and when the business is announced to the Trade Office. For example, the first reason for using the premises in which the company will have its registered office and so on. The draft law also contains changes following knowledge in practice in the field of the functioning of public registers. This is, for example, a modification of the scope of recorded data, such as the registration of organizational units in foreign foundations and foreign institutions or the prevention of simultaneous registrations by registry courts and notaries. The proposal therefore fulfils the requirement of the Government's programming declaration to propose amendments which will further facilitate the creation and registration of limited liability companies in the Commercial Register using electronic communications and identification resources. The effectiveness is set out in the proposal on 1 July 2022, but it will therefore be necessary to postpone it in the light of the negotiation procedure. One of Mr Benda's applied amendments also refers to the shift of efficiency. Otherwise, this was discussed as it was: the Constitutional and Legal Committee discussed the draft law on 27 June 2022 and issued a resolution recommending the approval of the proposal.
