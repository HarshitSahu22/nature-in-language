ParlaMint-CZ_2016-09-08-ps2013-049-03-004-194.u2	Thank you for the floor, Mr Vice-President. honourable Members, this proposal contains a proposal for ratification of this International Labour Organisation Convention on the promotion of collective bargaining, which was already adopted in 1981. The initiative to ratify this Convention came from the Czech Moravian Confederation of Trade Unions, which, in cooperation with our Ministry, controls all ratified conventions, and we find out where the Czech Republic has not yet joined the ratification. In terms of content, there is a factual definition of what is meant by collective bargaining, what is the subject of it, and what measures should be taken at national level for its support. And it can be said that the convention is in accordance with the constitutional order of the Czech Republic. As far as our national legal system is concerned, it can be noted that the obligations arising from this Convention are already contained in our legislation in force. That means we won't have to modify anything. This material was the subject of meetings of the Committee on Foreign Affairs, Defence and Security and the Committee on Health and Social Policy of the Senate. Both committees voted on the approval of ratification. Thank you for your attention.

ParlaMint-CZ_2016-09-08-ps2013-049-03-004-194.u4	Thank you, Mr President. Have a good morning, everyone. Ladies, as the Minister of Labour and Social Affairs has introduced you here, I, as rapporteur, would like to comment on this document. The Czech Republic is from 1. 1. 1993 has become a member of the International Labour Organisation and is actively involved in the activities of the oldest specialised agency of the United Nations. Convention No 154 on the promotion of collective bargaining in eight substantive articles generally defines what is meant by collective bargaining, what is the subject and what measures are to be taken at national level for its support. As has already been said here, this ratification on the date of 2. 10. 2015 ratified a total of 46 states, of which 12 Member States of the European Union. The present Convention is in accordance with the constitutional order of the Czech Republic. [[11.40 hours]] In matters of national law, it can be noted that the obligations contained in the Convention are already valid and effective, legislatively adapted, through the first of the provisions of Law No. 89/2012 Coll., Civil Code, Second Act No. 262/2006 Coll., Labour Code, as amended, for the third time Act No. 234/2014 Coll., on Civil Service, and Fourth Act No. 2/1991 Coll., on collective bargaining, as amended. And fifth, it's Law No. 16/1991 Coll., on intermediaries, judges and deposit of collective agreements of a higher degree, as amended. The Convention is in line with the commitments of the European Social Charter and the European Social Security Code, as well as the obligations arising from the membership of the Czech Republic in the European Union. The material presented does not affect the state budget and business environment, nor does it affect gender equality. The Czech Republic fulfils all the conditions in the laws that I mentioned to you in the previous section, and I recommend, fellow Members, to refer this House press to the Social Policy Committee where we would discuss this ratification. Thank you for your attention.
