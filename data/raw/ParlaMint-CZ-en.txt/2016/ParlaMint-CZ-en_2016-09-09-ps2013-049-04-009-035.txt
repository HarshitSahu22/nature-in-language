ParlaMint-CZ_2016-09-09-ps2013-049-04-009-035.u2	Thank you for your word. Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, I shall repeat briefly the reasons for this rather very subtle legislation, which aims to eliminate the unsystematicness and illogicality of the current legislation and to exclude the right to compensation for damage caused by agricultural or forest management on land owned by the State. [[13.10 hours]] The State, the Czech Republic, is the owner of the land covered by the proposed legislation. And by excluding from the law the existence of a right to injury arising from or continuing on land owned by the State, the State limits only its right of ownership, not the right of ownership of other entities. So much for the first complaint or objection that appeared during the discussion in the committees. The proposed principle, where ownership is limited with the consent of the owner, is a principle of constitutional conformation. The limitation of ownership is justified by the fact that the beneficiaries of the State-owned land-use restriction are exclusively entities, i.e. state-owned undertakings, which were set up by the State for the purpose of ensuring the public interest and are therefore totally against the interests of the State as the owner, in order to at the same time pay the entities set up by it for the restrictions which it itself has imposed on nature conservation. It creates an illogical situation where, in fact, when I say it layly, the state takes money out of one pocket and puts it in another. We are trying to follow this model in the same way as it is supposed to. Slovakia, which also in fact excluded compensation for injury paid to state organisations. Consequently, the compensation will not be paid for the damage caused or sustained on land owned by the State. As regards the financial contribution, there will be a reduction in the administrative burden on state organisations, which will not be seeking compensation, which is approximately CZK million per year, and subsequently for the authorities responsible for administration of claims for injury, which is almost CZK four million per year. This is all about the introduction and, of course, I am ready to comment on a possible discussion. Thank you.

In principle, the proposed regulation creates an inequality of ownership and creates legal uncertainty between economic operators. If the ownership of all owners has the same legal content and protection, it is contrary to the Charter to deny the right to compensation for harm to one group of owners and to another group of owners to retain that right. The claim to adjust the injury does not automatically arise on the basis of ownership of the land with legal restrictions, but only in the event of proof thereof. The current legislation fully corresponds to the state of affairs and ensures a relatively balanced state between requirements and effective conservation of nature and landscape. In the event of the adoption of an amendment to the law, there are restrictions on the management and the damage resulting from them will not really disappear, but will continue. It will not only be possible to apply them as before, which will result not only in negative misrepresentation of the results of management in particular of forest managers owned by the state, but also in data on the total costs of our entire nature protection society. At the same time, the only effective instrument to assess the necessity of restrictive requirements imposed by nature conservation authorities will be removed. The wording of the law after any amendment would allow nature conservation authorities to require further restrictions on land owned by the State on the management and production of timber without any limit.
