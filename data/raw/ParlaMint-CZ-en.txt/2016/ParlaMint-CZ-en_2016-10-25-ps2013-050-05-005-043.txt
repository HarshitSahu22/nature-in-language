ParlaMint-CZ_2016-10-25-ps2013-050-05-005-043.u2	Thank you, Mr President. Dear colleagues, I submit material, a government bill amending Act No. 289/2005 Coll., on Military Intelligence, as amended, and some other laws that were approved at the Government meeting on 5 October of this year. The reason for submitting this material is to meet one of the main objectives in the Action Plan on the National Cyber Security Strategy of the Czech Republic for the period 2015 to 2020, which was approved by the Government by Resolution 382 of 25 May 2015. Specifically, it is a target for active averting and suppressing cyber attacks through the National Cyber Force Centre established in Military Intelligence. The very subject of this material is therefore draft legislative changes that are necessary to ensure full functioning of this National Cyber Force Centre. The draft amendment to the Military Intelligence Act sets out a new scope for the security of cyber security of the Czech Republic. The proposed amendment also lays down rules for the use of technical means, including their location. Their location, or installation into the desired environment, will always be subject to approval by the government of the Czech Republic. In the draft amendment to the Defence Act, Act No. 222/1999 Coll., defence of cyberspace through a new provision becomes an integral part of the State's defence. Cyber defence is thus included in the State's defence system and its specific nature and role in this system is highlighted. The Act on Electronic Communications, which is Act No.127/2005 Coll., supplemented the provision that a legal or business natural person providing a network or providing an electronic communications service is obliged to establish and secure an interface for the connection of cyber defence technical means. The costs associated with this obligation will be compensated under the new provision to entrepreneurs through Military Intelligence, respectively from the chapter of the Ministry of Defence. The rules for this reimbursement are laid down in the proposal for a government regulation which is part of this material. Let me conclude by saying that the last NATO summit in Warsaw recognised cyberspace as the fourth type of war conflict, and that cyber attack was eligible to activate Article 5 of the North Atlantic Treaty. As part of the security strategy of the Czech Republic updated in February 2015, cyber attacks are also listed among identified threats. Therefore, the material presented can help eliminate cyber threats in the future and ensure the safety of the civilian population and our infrastructure. Thank you for your attention.
