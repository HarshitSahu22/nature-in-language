ParlaMint-CZ_2016-09-15-ps2013-049-07-003-196.u2	Thank you, Mr. Vice-President. A serious colleague, another treaty is the Economic Partnership Agreement with the countries of the South African Development Community, which is thus Botswana, Lesotho, Mozambique, Namibia, Swaziland and South Africa. The agreement has a certain asymmetricality in it because it offers countries some broader access to the European Union market, but at the same time they will be able to protect sensitive sectors, so it is the type of agreement that should enable them to develop. It also contains a chapter on sustainable development. It also links the trade agenda with our European objectives in the field of labour law, the environment and climate protection. There are also certain commitments that arise for these African countries, things that are important to us globally and that Europe participates in. If I were to take the trade impact that specifically concerns the Czech Republic, it would probably be the most important impact on relations between the Czech Republic and South Africa, because it is the largest trading partner of the Czech Republic. When I compare it with other countries like Lesotho, Namibia, Mozambique, South Africa really represents the most important partner here. Other countries are in terms of commercial importance, I must say, rather marginal. However, it is this agreement that could mean some change here, because the scope for the development of trade with other countries could also increase. That's just gonna show up. The key is the space that is building up here in the South African trade. There is a certain commercial significance for the Czech Republic. The agreement is a mixed agreement. Some parts of it fall within the competence of the Member States, some parts fall within the competence of the European Union. From a legal point of view, this is a treaty of the so-called presidential category. I think I can also say here that it will have no direct impact on the state budget of the Czech Republic.
