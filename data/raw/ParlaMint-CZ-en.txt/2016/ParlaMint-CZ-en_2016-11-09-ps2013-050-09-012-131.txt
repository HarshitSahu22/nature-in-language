ParlaMint-CZ_2016-11-09-ps2013-050-09-012-131.u2	Thank you for the floor. Ladies and gentlemen, let me introduce you to Act No. 586/1992 Coll., on Income Tax, as amended. At first glance, it's tax law, that's true. However, with a more detailed view of this amendment, you will find that it is primarily the support of voluntary donors of blood, bone marrow and haematopoietic cells. According to the legislation in force, the value of one blood collection or of its components of a donor who has not received the financial reimbursement of expenditure related to blood collection or its components, under the Act governing specific health services, except for the reimbursement of proven travel costs associated with the collection, is valued at CZK 2,000. This is not sufficient in the current circumstances. The tax advantage of CZK 2,000 was fixed in 1996, i.e. 20 years ago. At that time, the average gross wage was around CZK 10,000. It is clear that, in the current economic situation, the amount of this amount can no longer be motivated, so we propose an increase to CZK 3,000. Please note that voluntary blood donors, men, can give blood five times, women four times a year. In this context, I would like to draw attention to this, and you all know this from television and from the media that the number of voluntary blood donors is not sufficient and is about 270 thousand. The recommended number of voluntary donors ranges between four and five percent. It's not the only change we're proposing. The amendment introduces CZK 20,000 for the collection of haematopoietic cells and for the donor to deduct from the tax base. We also propose that a provision should be inserted into the law which favours persons registered in the Czech National Register of Bone Marrow Donors and in the Czech Register of Hematopian Cell Donors, i.e. potential donors. In this case, we responded to a relatively low number of potential donors, where, for example, in Germany there is one potential donor per approximately 16 inhabitants, in the Czech Republic this ratio is 1:110, which is insufficient. [[16.20 hours]] Although in global comparison, the Czech Republic has a very decent location, in any case, the current 90 000 persons registered in the above registers are not ideal. The number of 200 to 300,000 registered persons is considered ideal. In this context, I would like to point out that in the Czech Republic there are about a quarter of donors, the rest abroad. Collection abroad means higher expenditure - about CZK 500,000 in any case. The text of this proposal was consulted by representatives of the Czech Red Cross, the Czech National Register of Bone Marrow Donors and the Czech Register of Blood-Created Cell Donors, who welcomed this amendment. That's why I'm asking you to release her for second reading. Thank you.
