ParlaMint-CZ_2016-04-13-ps2013-044-02-006-162.u2	Mr President, honourable Members, I have already detailed the draft law on the protection of state borders at first and second reading. The proposal follows a directly applicable EU regulation, so-called "EU'. Schengen Borders Code. The aim of the proposal is to protect state borders from their illegal crossing. The proposal also lays down a procedure for the temporary reintegration of internal border control in the event of a serious threat to public policy or to internal security of the Czech Republic. Protection of internal borders, i.e. airports in connection with internal flights, and land borders of the Czech Republic are not normally implemented. With regard to the opinion of the Ministry of the Interior on the amendments applied, I agree with the opinions adopted by the Security Committee on these amendments. However, I do not agree with Mr Lank's amendments. These amendments are contrary to constitutional law, international law and EU law. Thank you for your attention.

ParlaMint-CZ_2016-04-13-ps2013-044-02-006-162.u4	Beautiful early afternoon, esteemed colleagues. I am not surprised that the Minister does not agree with the proposals, but it kind of falls within the policy advocated by the Czech Government. However, I would like to make a few arguments as to why, on the contrary, I think these amendments are necessary, and I would like to ask you for their support. The law we're discussing right now is a good law. It is, however, a good law for times of peace and quiet, for times when Schengen works without problems, when the migration crisis is not raging, for times when more than a million illegal immigrants do not pass Europe annually. But we don't really have that kind of time right now. It is not a law for the time when all surrounding states are forced to take hard measures at their borders, build roadblocks and deploy an army, as we see in Hungary, Austria or Slovenia. We have already criticized the government's bill at first reading and proposed to return it to rework. We do not naively believe that Schengen or the European solutions promoted by Prime Minister Sobotka will protect us. We must ensure the protection of our borders ourselves and, according to the latest survey, 77 percent of the population of this country, i.e. the people who have elected us in our positions - us and you, colleagues and colleagues. Our amendments are taken from the new Hungarian laws that have been approved in response to the mass immigration crisis. They were approved at a time when tens of thousands of migrants stood at the Hungarian border and were conquered on their territory and using violence. Although such a scenario is not excluded anywhere, and therefore not in our country, the Czech Government's proposal will in no way be able to cope with such a situation. We have therefore prepared a series of measures to expand the possibilities of the state to face the immigration crisis. The complex of amendments concerns a substantial tightening of the penalty for illegal crossing the national border. While the government draft proposes to prosecute this as an offence, our amendment leaves this act as an offence only for EU citizens. For third-country citizens, especially for illegal immigrants from Syria, Afghanistan, Iraq, Iran, Somalia, Eritrea and many other African and Asian states, illegal border crossing will be classified as a criminal offence. This regime will be applied primarily if the government or the Ministry of the Interior announces a temporary re-establishing of internal border control. This situation would therefore occur at the moment when the Czech Republic will be immediately threatened by mass migration and illegals will cross our borders uncontrolledly. We're not saying it's happening. We say that it is good to be prepared for this situation, not quickly looking for a solution, when it is too late, as it has happened to some of the surrounding states. Unlike them, we still have the opportunity to react preventively. We come from the fact that, in a situation of mass illegal migration, it will simply not be possible to carry out all criminal acts, because security forces will simply be overwhelmed. Therefore, we propose that the punishment for illegal crossing the national border for immigrants from third countries should be a expulsion accompanied by an automatic loss of the possibility to obtain asylum and additional protection. The Czech authorities would do so even if their identity could not be identified. As far as the argument is concerned that this is contrary to the European treaties, tell me who else is following these agreements in Europe. This hard procedure would ensure that illegal immigrants would not want to enter Czech territory at all. This would mean for them an automatic rejection of asylum and expulsion, the exact opposite of what they want. Yes, I say and admit that we want to make the Czech Republic a place where illegal immigrants will not want to enter at all. And frankly, I don't see anything wrong with it. Thank you for your attention.
