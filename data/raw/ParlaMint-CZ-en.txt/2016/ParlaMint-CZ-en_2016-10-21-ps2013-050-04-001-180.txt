ParlaMint-CZ_2016-10-21-ps2013-050-04-001-180.u4	The draft law on mining activities, explosives and state mining administration has already been discussed in detail at first and second reading. The amendment shall, in particular, incorporate the requirements of Directive 2014/28/EU relating to explosives for civil use. It harmonises the terms of the Act on Explosives and the Act on Weapons, refines the process of deciding whether or not a matter is an explosive, introduces binding opinions of the District Mining Authorities in the construction proceedings concerning explosives installations and also implements some partial changes to existing legislation on the basis of the requirements of practice. The House Press 653 was discussed by the Economic Committee and three amendments, which are exclusively legislatively technical in nature, were read in the second reading. In view of the already mentioned and the fact that this is a transposition of the EU directive, I propose to the House to approve this bill, as amended by the three legislative technical amendments that I have mentioned to you. Thank you for your attention.

ParlaMint-CZ_2016-10-21-ps2013-050-04-001-180.u6	Ladies and gentlemen, I won't be long. Everything important was said in a short debate at first and second readings. However, I would like to make a few comments on the transport of explosives. For no doubt a positive feature of the amendment, I evaluate the change in the deadline for notification of transport to the Police Presidium from 24 hours to 1 hour in relation to a vehicle equipped with GPS tracking device. In fact, along the planned route of transport it certainly does not stand for police troublemakers to protect the cargo carried, so the notification of the transport serves rather to ensure that the police have an overview of whether the vehicle has not deviated from its route, not to take any security measures. In this situation, when the police have an overview of the current location of each truck, it is absolutely unnecessary to report the shipment 24 hours in advance. After all, explosives traders are often business companies that respond to the needs of their customers. If the customer decides on Tuesday morning that he needs to buy the explosive and deliver it the same day, there is no reason to defend such a procedure if the cargo is transported with the GPS system. In conclusion, even if this is not the subject of this amendment, allow me to consider some of the suggestions that are being made in practice and which I think the police should take care of. In particular, it is an existing electronic system for reporting the route of transport of explosives. Some places, even in the territory of cities, are completely missing, and therefore it is necessary to enter a place close but not identical as the starting or final point of transport in the electronic system. As a result, this only increases the risk of police contacting a driver who will not be aware of any breach of the traffic route. My second complaint also has to do with this, namely that the existing system does not allow the planned map to be printed with an exact route. Drivers get a list of different points instead of precise maps, which, of course, reduces user comfort and once again only increases the risk that the driver will inadvertently deviate from the planned route. Let us please look at these points for the future. Thank you for your attention.
