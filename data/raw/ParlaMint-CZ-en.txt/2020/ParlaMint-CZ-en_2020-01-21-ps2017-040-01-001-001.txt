And we all know that stricter regulation is supposed to be for these equity companies, because stock companies generally protect more owners, often small owners, and that, in some way, the law for those limited liability companies can be more benevolent. And here comes a situation that concerns a fixed share of profit, which is a terminus technicus one that is based on the law and mainly on social contracts and it is strange that it is not subject to approval by a general meeting. That is, if the social contract gives an accurate share of the profit that will always be paid, it is sufficient to pay the share of the situation that the financial statements are approved. So far, we have been specifically specified in the Commercial Corporations Act that this fixed share of the profit I am talking about here is always due within three months of the clearance of the financial statements. It was the amendment of the Trade Corporations Act submitted by the Government and which came out of the Chamber of Deputies that a situation occurred when this provision ceased to be a kind of mandatory requirement of the social contract and could be adapted differently. However, this adjustment was precisely lacking in respect of the social contracts relating to the fixed share of the profits of the limited liability companies. [[15.10 hours]] I thought, and of course, I have consulted the legislative department over the long term, that it would be a good idea for these adjustments to be assimilated so that we would not have any stricter regulation for limited liability companies themselves than for public limited companies.
