Its aim is to tax advantage for private donation, i.e. donation by companies and individual donors, who contribute to a publicly beneficial activity. In particular, we propose to increase the possible tax write-off limit from today's 10 and 15 percent, depending on whether it is a natural or legal person - on the proposed 30% and temporarily equal to two years, that is to say 2020 and 2021. The proposal was made in response to the first wave of the covid pandemic, which significantly affected social services organisations, both the residential and the mobile ones. It also hit hard, for example, organisations employing people with disabilities and who, moreover, lost income from their own activities. However, I would like to say that there have been some moments in the meantime that I would like to draw attention to here, or I would like to mention them. First, a second wave of covid came, and it was already in full force that the field of culture and sport began to be crushed. The law, which initiated mostly the social sphere, has become an amendment that can significantly help small sports clubs in the countryside or small amateur theatres. They're just everywhere where they can't and often don't want to rely on public support, but they're used to getting their own donors, like local firms. Secondly, we no longer need to speculate on the impact of the covid on the financing of a publicly beneficial area. The first figures and analyses are available at the moment, and therefore, according to the newly published donor forum numbers, for example, 52 percent of organizations report reductions in donations from the public, 39 percent report cancellations of the promised donor support and about 50 percent report reductions in revenues from their own activities. After the temporary upheaval of solidarity that we experienced in the spring, and help during the spring months, it is clear that the level of donation is falling significantly and falling.
