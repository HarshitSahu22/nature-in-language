Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, the aim of the draft legislation on packaging is to ensure the transposition of the new directives of the European Union in the field of packaging and packaging waste. The subject matter of the regulation is the setting of the conditions for the fulfilment of the obligation to recover, the establishment of minimum density of the collection network, the setting of the amount of fees for the combined fulfilment of the obligation for the recovery of packaging waste and the use of packaging waste by the authorised packaging company, including the establishment of new obligations for authorised packaging companies, and the pursuit of greater transparency and efficiency of the functioning of the authorised packaging company. Allow me to present here two amendments tabled by me. In the case of an application for authorisation, both the applicant and the authorised packing company providing combined transactions in relation to the packaging covered by the authorisation procedure are parties to the proceedings under the draft law, as well as applicants applying for the granting or modification of the authorization in relation to the packaging covered by the procedure. However, this should not apply in the case of an extension of the authorisation decision pursuant to § 17 para. 8 of which only the authorized company concerned should be involved, on the grounds that applicants could obtain confidential information from the collective performance project of the already authorised company. For the same reason, it is proposed that, in the case of a change of authorisation procedure, the authorized company concerned and other authorised companies providing combined transactions in relation to packaging covered by a change of authorisation procedure, and not applicants for authorisation, should be involved. The second amendment to the legislation on packaging, recorded under file number 5643, is devoted to the issue of microten bags, which I co-submit with my colleague František Elfmark, Jan Chvojka, Jan Čižinský, Markéta Adamová and Jana Krutáková. The proposed amendment aims at a non-restrictive reduction in the consumption of microten bags with a thickness of less than 15 microns. In particular, they are typical clear bags in disposable shops, which are commonly used mainly for the carry-off of weighted foods such as fruit, vegetables and other goods. These bags are available in shops free of charge and their annual consumption in the Czech Republic is more than 4 billion pieces. Very light plastic bags can no longer be recycled effectively and thus end up in incinerators, landfills or completely outside the waste management system. Due to their very low weight, they easily escape into the environment and are among the most common types of plastic packaging that make up waste in nature and in cities. [[15.10 hours]] The aim was to find a solution that would not be restrictive, costly, but only through the stores produced the customer and aimed at a voluntary reduction in consumption. The amendment proposes the introduction of an obligation for persons who engage in retail or wholesale trade to inform consumers of the need to consider overuse of very lightweight plastic carry-on bags, in order to reduce their consumption, and to educate consumers about the need to protect the environment. The obligation shall apply to all persons who are engaged in a retail or wholesale business in whose activities, on a sales area of more than 200 m2 or in its immediate vicinity, very light plastic carrier bags are provided to consumers. The introduction of an information obligation therefore brings results both on the part of reducing consumption and information and also has a positive impact on consumer behaviour. Given that other approaches to restrictions based on EU recommendations to reduce this type of plastics are debatable, such as charging or ban, this approach appears most effective in terms of long-term consumption reduction. Sellers themselves, who now provide very lightweight plastic bags free of charge, can also benefit from the solution from the point of view of savings on the side of purchase and logistics. Establishment of an information obligation in Sweden with effect from 1. 6. 2017 led to a reduction of 11 percent in the consumption of thin plastic bags. If it were possible to achieve the same reduction in the consumption of sachets in the Czech Republic, this would be due to a lower environmental burden accompanied by a reduction in the cost of shops to provide disposable bags free of charge in stores. Finally, I would like to emphasise once again that our aim is not to ban plastic bags, because their replacements in the form of paper bags have an even greater ecological footprint, and the hygienic importance of plastic bags is undoubtedly significant, which we can also see in the current situation in the fight against pandemics. Our aim is only to reduce their enormous consumption in cases where this is not necessary, without a type of prohibition or charging measure. I beg you in this way to support both of my amendments. Thank you for your attention.
