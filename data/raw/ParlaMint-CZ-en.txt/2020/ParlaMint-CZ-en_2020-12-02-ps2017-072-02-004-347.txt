I would like to make a brief statement on them, respectively. summarized the position of the Ministry of Health towards them. Mr Pawlas' first proposal, although the Committee on Health has not delivered an opinion, my position and the position of the Ministry of Health on this proposal, which intends to abolish the proposed changes in the Act on Advertising Regulation as a whole, is disagreeable. The proposals contained by the law are not such as to disturb advertising as such and priori, but to give it rules. It is necessary to regulate advertising for products affecting human health and - and this is important - to protect patients. Like medicinal products, medical devices can have a fundamental impact on our health. Even though their sales are not regulated as strictly as with medicinal products, their impact on human health cannot be underestimated or even downgraded. The current regulation therefore needs to be made more effective, and that is what the government proposal does. Mr Pawlas' second amendment is an option for the above, by limiting advertising regulation only to the strict rules contained in the European regulation on medical devices. Unfortunately, even this proposal will not improve patient safety. A bit of the adjustment recommended by this proposal will bring many uncertainties and regulated entities a considerable uncertainty in the application of regulation into practice. This, of course, carries the risks that I have already mentioned. It would not be clear what kind of advertising regulation applies and to what extent. It is also desirable for the safety and appropriate information of advertisers to distinguish the regulation of advertising intended for the general public and for professionals. There's definitely a difference. As far as in vitro diagnostic devices are concerned, it is also very desirable to regulate advertising, since even in this specific group of medical devices, the provision of misleading information may lead to damage to human health. Examples can be e.g. tests for the diagnosis of occult bleeding or certain gynaecological infections that are intended for self-testing, and unregulated advertising for such diagnostic devices can lead to completely incorrect interpellations of the results of such testing, which may have fatal consequences. I therefore ask that this proposal not be adopted, which is also recommended by the Health Committee, for which I thank him. The third amendment - Mrs Adámková and other colleagues - then regulates the issue of regulation of advertising of products aimed at human health and thus addresses adequately the responsibilities of the dissemination of this advertising in the field of medical devices and medicinal products. This proposal, again in agreement with the Committee on Health, I consider it useful and necessary, as it will, on the contrary, help prevent harmful, factually misleading advertising, which can mislead patients and endanger their health. So let me ask you, ladies and gentlemen, to support my views and the proposal as a whole. Thank you for your attention.
