ParlaMint-CZ_2020-02-13-ps2017-040-11-006-192.u2	Thank you for the floor. Mr Vice-President, ladies and gentlemen, let me briefly inform you of the agreement put forward on the transfer and sharing of a contribution to the single crisis management fund referred to as the IGA. IGA is an intergovernmental agreement which complements the text of the Single Resolution Mechanism Regulation and addresses certain issues relating to the provisions and functioning of the Single Resolution Fund. The IGA will apply to the Czech Republic from the date of its possible entry into the banking union, either through the adoption of the euro or through the closure of the Institute. close cooperation with the European Central Bank and involvement in the Single Supervisory Mechanism. For the Czech Republic as a non-euro area Member State, there are currently no further obligations arising from the signing of this Agreement. However, IGA is also relevant for countries which do not yet participate in the banking union. Through the provisions on so-called non-contractual liability means that IGA ensures that states outside the banking union through the European Union budget do not cover any costs of decisions under a single crisis management mechanism in which they will not participate. The IGA also regulates the procedure for the future accession of a new member to the banking union or the possible withdrawal of a member from the banking union. The IGA therefore has a material impact on the position of non-members of the banking union, which is why the Czech Republic has been actively involved in the negotiation process of the text of the agreement and has also become a signatory to it. The Government agreed to negotiate this agreement in May 2014 and, on that basis, the agreement for the Czech Republic was signed by the Ambassador and Permanent Representative of the Czech Republic to the EU, then Mr Martin Povejšil, subject, of course, to ratification. In the same year, this agreement was submitted to the two chambers of Parliament of the Czech Republic to express their consent to ratification, the Senate gave its consent in December 2014. In the Chamber of Deputies, the IGA has been discussed in committees with the recommendation to grant approval to ratification, but it has not been discussed at second reading before the end of the parliamentary term. The IGA is therefore re-presented to the two chambers of Parliament to agree to ratification, only to update the wording of the presentation report, which is part of the material received by you. At the same time, I would like to recall that the ratification has already been completed by all euro area countries and other signatory states outside the banking union, with the exception of the Czech Republic, Denmark, Poland and Croatia, with Croatia planning to do so in the near future. I therefore consider it appropriate that the Czech Republic does not remain too long in the trio of signatory states that still have not ratified the agreement and therefore to complete its ratification process. Thank you for your attention.
