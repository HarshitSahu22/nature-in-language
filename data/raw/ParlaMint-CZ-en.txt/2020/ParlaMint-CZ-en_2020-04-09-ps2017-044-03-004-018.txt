ParlaMint-CZ_2020-04-09-ps2017-044-03-004-018.u2	Mr President, colleagues, the aim of the proposed regulation is to mitigate the effects of the pandemic on the tourism sector, but at the same time to maintain a balance between the legitimate interests of tourism entrepreneurs and customers. In case of cancellation of tours with the start date from 20 February 2020 to 31 August 2020 due to the pandemic, the organiser has the possibility to postpone the refund of the price of the trip to the customer until 31 August 2021, when the withdrawal period ends. During the duration of the protection period, the customer will receive a ticket for a tour which will be able to draw up a replacement tour. The Agency will be obliged to offer the customer an equivalent to a tour for the duration of the protection period. If this is not possible, the protection period will end and the travel agency will be obliged to pay the customer all payments paid by the customer or in his favour for the original tour. Based on the proposed measure, customers will practically be able to move their tours to a later, more appropriate time. In the event that the customer has not exhausted the replacement tour during the protection period, the travel agency shall refund all payments paid after the end of the protection period. The withdrawal period shall not apply to particularly vulnerable groups of customers who will be able to refuse the voucher. For example, persons over 65 years of age, unemployed, also single parents or schools. The draft law also provides for a range of customers who have purchased a tour with the start date until 31 August 2020 and who do not want or cannot complete a tour for a number of reasons, for example for reasons of loss of employment or other socio-economic reasons or only for reasons of fear, concern for their health. These customers have according to § 2533 para. 1 of the Civil Code can withdraw from the contract, but they are obliged to pay the travel agency severance payments, so-called cancellation fees. In this case, the customer may ask the travel agency to issue a voucher for a tour worth part of the severance package and thus move his tour to a later period. The proposal thus balances the interests of customers and travel agencies in the sense that, during the period when the travel agency will be allowed to use the protection period and provide vouchers for alternative tours, customers will also be able to use increased flexibility. This can in principle be agreed with, the voucher will motivate customers and basically the customer will receive a voucher of at least 10 per cent of the severance payment paid. There are also foreign adjustments similar to what we are proposing today. Belgium, Poland, France, Germany or Malta, for example, have already taken these adjustments. The bill balances between the interests of consumers and travel agencies and service providers who have all been affected by pandemics that are unprecedented in modern history.
