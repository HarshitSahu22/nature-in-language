ParlaMint-CZ_2020-11-10-ps2017-062-07-006-013.u2	Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, let me give you the introductory word on the bill on medical devices, House press number 696, for second reading in the Chamber of Deputies. The main part of this bill is the adaptation of the Czech legal order to the regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council of Europe on medical devices, the amendment of Directives 2001/18/EC, the EC Regulation No 178/2000 and Regulation No 1223/2009 and repealing Council Directives 90/385/EEC and 93/42/EEC. This regulation was adopted at European level, together with its twin, which we are likely to present today and which concerns in vitro diagnostic medical devices, in order to ensure a higher level of safety for medical devices used, and thus patient safety throughout the European Union. The draft law therefore, following this regulation, has the task of adapting the Czech rule of law to EU rules. In particular, duplicate national arrangements should be abolished. Furthermore, in the light of the regulation, the proposal regulates, for example, issues of competence, where it designates individual national authorities, in particular the State Institute for Drug Control, as supervisory authorities or empowers them to exercise the powers conferred on each Member State by the regulation. The proposal then regulates in detail the obligations of individual types of economic operators in the field of medical devices, as well as the issues relating to the labelling and identification of medical devices. Furthermore, a new modification of the parties during clinical trials, market surveillance and the vigilance system is provided, as is the case for pharmaceutical products. Last but not least, in order to meet our country's obligations as a Member State of the European Union, it is necessary to ensure the enforcement of the rules laid down in the regulation, by defining the various factual elements of the offences. The proposal therefore ensures that the European rules laid down in particular in order to ensure patient safety are enforceable at the level of our state. In addition to that Union regulation, the issues of prescription, dispensing, use and servicing of medical devices need to be adapted comprehensively in the framework of national regulation. The proposal was discussed by the Health Committee, which supported it at its meeting on 6 September of this year and recommended it for approval. If I were to summarize the whole proposal in one sentence, then I will say that it is a harmonisation of the legislation of the Czech Republic and the European Union of the parties to medical devices. In conclusion, I would like to ask you to support this proposal, which aims to ensure and increase the safety of medical devices and therefore also the safety of patients in the Czech Republic. Thank you in advance for your support and your attention.
