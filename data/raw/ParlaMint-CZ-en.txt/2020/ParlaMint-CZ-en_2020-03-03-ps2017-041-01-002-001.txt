ParlaMint-CZ_2020-03-03-ps2017-041-01-002-001.u4	Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, allow me to give you brief information on House press number 69, Senate press 176, on the bill amending Act No. 219/2000 Coll., on the property of the Czech Republic and its appearance in legal relations, as amended, and Act No. 82/1998 Coll., on liability for damage caused in the exercise of public authority by decision or by maladministration. The Chamber of Deputies passed the Senate Bill on 6 January 2020. The Senate's Organizing Committee ordered the Senate press on February 7th. The Senate Guarantee Committee was the Committee on Economics, Agriculture and Transport, which adopted Resolution 131 on 22 January 2020, which recommended rejecting the bill. The other committee discussing the press was the Constitutional and Legal Committee, which adopted Resolution No 60 on 15 January 2020, which also recommended rejecting the bill. The Senate plenary included the press at the 15th meeting and discussed it on 29 January 2020 by resolution 300 in the vote of 19. The bill was rejected. Shortly to content and progress. The Ministry of Finance, as a factual guarantor of the State Property Act, has long held the theory of divided state subjectivity in a form which can be described in such a way that, in principle, it denies the State's personality as a unitary unit and sees it only as an personality divided between the various organisational units of the State, always to the extent that the organisational component is responsible for managing the State's property. In other words, although the organisational components of the State are not separate legal entities, they are in relation to a specific set of assets, and thus to a specific heading of legal relations by some kind of emancipation of the State's subjectivity, and the State as such cannot express its personality. In a consistent application of the so-selected theory of divided subjectivity, the organizational component of the state acquires the characteristics of a legal entity to such an extent that they do not become de jure but de facto. Otherwise, you have all the information in writing in the information provided, which I'm sure was given to you in good time. I will therefore merely mention the 300th resolution of the Senate of the 15th meeting held on 29 January on the bill by which the Senate rejects the bill and instructs Senators Větrovský, Červíček and Vilímec to justify the Senate at the meeting of the Chamber of Deputies. Thank you for your attention.
