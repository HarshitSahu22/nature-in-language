Ladies and gentlemen, allow me to make a very brief presentation of the Government's bill amending the Bank Act and the Savings and Loan Cooperative Act. As has already been said in the first and second readings, the draft law makes changes of a technical nature in order to facilitate the interpretation of the law or refinement, respectively. supplementing the implementation of European Union law, but at the same time the bill also brings some substantive changes, the most important being strengthening the protection of bank clients in cases where there would be a transformation or any available to the bank's business establishment or part of it and solving problems related to the withdrawal process and its overall efficiency. As regards the amendments tabled in the second reading, I agree with the amendments contained in the budget committee resolution 368 of the 36th meeting held on 18 March 2020, as presented by Mr Dolejš as the rapporteur of the press during the second reading. In the case of Mr Juránek's amendment to the Act on the Distribution of Insurance and Security, when he proposes the postponement of the deadline for the completion of expert knowledge tests and the acquisition of documents to demonstrate universal knowledge, our position is fundamentally disagreeable. Allow me to state the main reasons for this disagreement. 1. The proposal focused on expertise in the distribution of insurance has nothing to do with the material governed by the Bank Act and the Savings and Credit Cooperatives Act. This would be constitutionally speaking, in my view, an unacceptable legislative sticker. 2. Moreover, the proposal formally fulfils the features of a comprehensive amendment, not an amendment, and threatens to replace the whole amendment tabled by the Act on Banks and the Act on Savings and Credit Cooperatives. 3. In fact, it should be pointed out that this is a permanent, not a temporary change. The proposed extension of the duration of the business authorisations of insurance intermediaries in points 1 to 3 means that each new insurance intermediary will be extended by one year to each new insurance intermediary. Such permanent interference with the conditions laid down for insurance intermediaries cannot be considered as a response to the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. In addition, it is a highly non-systemic element in the setting of the verification of financial market expertise, which is currently set uniformly in all sectors such as consumer credit, insurance, capital market, pension savings. 4. From the point of view of the Ministry of Finance, as a gesturer of the insurance and reinsurance distribution act concerned, the proposed change is not necessary. The proposal in point 4, consisting of extending the period for proving universal knowledge, i.e. graduation, by 13 months, is not justified. The transitional period for proving general knowledge according to the current wording of the Act only ends in 2022, and this year despite the restrictions related to the pandemics of graduation will eventually take place and there is no reason to expect that this will be different in the coming years. 5. The amendment proposed in point 5 concerning the extension of the period for demonstrating competence by 12 months shall be disproportionate in length, even according to the observations of the accredited services themselves. Individual and online preparation could also take place within less than two months when it was not possible to personally participate in the preparation. The possibility to hold the examinations was renewed from 11 May 2020, i.e. at a time when more than six and a half months remained until the deadline given by the transitional period under the current law. Moreover, according to data from the beginning of March of this year, the interest in conducting the tests was very weak at that time. So it is obvious that the system is functioning again and is not overwhelmed. Therefore, the extension of the existing deadline for demonstrating professional competence is unnecessary, let alone to enforce the effects of the COVID-19 pandemic. Thank you for your attention.
