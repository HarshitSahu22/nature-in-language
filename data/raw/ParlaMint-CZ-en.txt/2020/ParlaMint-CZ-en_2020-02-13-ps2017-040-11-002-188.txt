Government proposal by which the Parliament of the Czech Republic is to give its consent to ratify the amendment of the schedule of the International Whaling Regulation Convention adopted at the 66th and 67th meetings of the International Whaling Commission (Portoroz, Slovenia, 20. - 28 October 2016; Florianópolis, Brazil, 4. - 14 September 2018) /house print 416 - first reading The opening words will be given by the Minister of the Environment Richard Brabec. Minister, please, you have the floor.
ParlaMint-CZ_2020-02-13-ps2017-040-11-002-188.u2	Thank you for the floor. Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, as always during the discussion of this point, I have noticed some surprising noise when we are talking about the International Whaling Commission in the Czech Republic. Yes, it is still true that we do not hunt a whale, but our membership of the International Whaling Commission dates back to 2005 and originated for pragmatic reasons, i.e. to a certain global contribution of the Czech Republic to the protection of whales, which were threatened with extinction in certain species, and since the European Union as a whole cannot be a member, it cannot vote on the Whaling Commission, or on the International Convention on Whaling Regulation, so individual Member States have joined it and is one of them since 2005 as well as the Czech Republic. Very briefly on this proposal, to ratify the amendments. The delegation of the Czech Republic appeared at the Commission meeting in 2016 and 2018 in agreement with the coordinated position of the European Union in accordance with the relevant Council Decision. The amendments to the timetable relate in particular to the extension of zero quotas for commercial whaling and the establishment of new quotas for indigenous whale fishing for livelihoods. Here I can say that while the ban on commercial whale hunting continues, in certain types of whales or whale species, the share increases, or the potential catch increases in individual years for these whales, but only for indigenous populations. This is allowed under specified conditions so as not to endanger the whale population. The Czech Republic does not make any reservation to the amendments to the schedule of the Convention, adopted at the 66th and 67th sessions, and can therefore accept them. Thank you.
ParlaMint-CZ_2020-02-13-ps2017-040-11-002-188.u3	Thank you. The rapporteur for the first reading is Mr Michal Ratiborský. Please, Mr. Ambassador.
ParlaMint-CZ_2020-02-13-ps2017-040-11-002-188.u4	Thank you for the floor. Mr President, honourable Members of the Government, honourable colleagues, colleagues. The proposed amendments to the schedule of the International Whaling Regulation Convention of 1946 were agreed at two meetings of the International Whaling Commission in 2016 and 2018. They are presented simultaneously in one material. In substance, only the conclusion of the 2018 whaling commission meeting, where a comprehensive change in the timetable was adopted, in relation to quotas for the indigenous way of hunting whales for livelihoods, is essential. Furthermore, the amendment also concerns the process of automatic renewal of the quotas provided for in the absence of a requirement to increase them. The material was approved by the Government of the Czech Republic on 4 February 2019 and is in line with the coordinated position of the European Union in this area. The changes will have no impact on the Czech Republic because whales really do not occur in the Czech fauna. I recommend we release them for second reading and order them to the Foreign Committee. Thank you for your attention.
