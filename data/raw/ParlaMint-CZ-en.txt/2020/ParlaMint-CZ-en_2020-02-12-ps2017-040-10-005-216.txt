ParlaMint-CZ_2020-02-12-ps2017-040-10-005-216.u2	Thank you, Mr President. Ladies and gentlemen, let me really briefly recall the aims of the bill. The main objective is to respond to the Constitutional Court's finding, which has abolished the current arrangements for the establishment of health insurance bodies, and to establish a system of transparent and fair occupation of the administrative and supervisory boards of occupational health insurance undertakings, which will reflect the status of participants in the public health insurance scheme. In general, I can say for the Ministry of Health that, on the basis of discussions to date, I support such a solution which will maintain the existing tripartite division of the occupation of employees' insurance bodies so as to ensure the continuation of a pluralistic public health insurance system. On the contrary, if decision-making on health care costs is almost absolutely entrusted to insured persons, as is one of the amendments, that is to say, to consumers covered by care, we are concerned that this could significantly jeopardise the sustainability of the public health insurance system. For these reasons, we believe that the regulation, which is intended to ensure, in principle, the continuation of the tripartite model, is the best possible. In a very short time, in cooperation with trade unions as representatives of employees and employers as the largest payers of insurance premiums for public health insurance, an amendment has been prepared, which I have read. This amendment preserves the tripartite distribution of forces, where a third of the members of the occupational health insurance bodies will continue to be appointed by the government, a third would be elected from all insured persons by the relevant health insurance company by a group of employers and self-employed persons and a third would be elected again from all insured workers and persons without taxable income. Therefore, on this side of passive electoral law, that is, the possibility of running and being elected to the bodies of occupational health insurance companies, we fully satisfy the Constitutional Court's finding that there are no barriers to who can run, it can really be all the insured persons of the occupational health insurance undertaking concerned, and on the part of the active electoral law, we give the right to those who are taxpayers of insurance against public health insurance, both employees and employers, self-employed persons without taxable income. This proposal, as well as from discussions with all Stakeholders, including occupational health insurance companies, will not be problematic in terms of practical feasibility. We really believe that it is feasible already at that time relatively short, which remains until the first elections to two occupational health insurance companies, which should take place at the end of this year. That means, really, from our point of view, it's the best possible, maybe even a compromise option. The amendment I am referring to is referred to under letter C in the summary of the amendments and is also a response to the Government's supplementary resolution when the government, when it adopted this government bill, has ordered me, as the Minister of Health, to prepare further legislation which will take into account the status of employees as insurance payers for public health insurance in the field of electoral law in elections to health insurance institutions. That is what this amendment contains. This means, both within the coalition board and within the government, there is agreement and I believe that this amendment is a good compromise solution, so as to maintain the functionality of occupational health insurance companies and, at the same time, sufficient representativeness of those involved in the public health insurance system. Thank you for your attention.
