The recent development of market prices for certain minerals has shown that due to technological developments and related changes in the structure of demand for minerals, there may be such unpredictable and rapid developments in market prices that the five-year fixation of remuneration rates makes it impossible for the State to respond sufficiently quickly and effectively to market developments. The removal of the above limit cannot jeopardise the payer's remuneration in the sense of arbitrary and higher remuneration rates by the State, as the limit of 10 per cent of the market price per unit of quantity for each type of minerals obtained referred to in § 33k para. 1 of the Mining Act. [[Nesroz.]] This threshold is unstoppable and gives taxpayers the opportunity to predict the maximum level of rates. As part of the discussion of the amendment to the Mining Act, the Ministry of Industry and Trade was informed by the Government's Legislative Council that the removal of a five-year moratorium could lead to conflict with the principle of legal certainty, so that, in theory, arbitration could threaten and the principle of legitimate expectations of taxpayers in terms of not increasing rates over a period of five years from the entry into force of Government Decree No. 98/2016 Coll., on remuneration rates. The Government's Legislative Council therefore recommended that the relevant transitional provision be supplemented to ensure that the taxpayer will apply the remuneration for the payment periods until 31 December 2021, the rates of the payments of the minerals received provided for by the Government Decree issued pursuant to Section 33k of the Mining Act, as effective before the date on which the amendment of the Mining Act is now in force, if the rates of the payments of the minerals received are more favourable to him. The completion of the transitional provision cannot be considered as non-compliance with the Government's programming declaration, since the government is actively taking steps to change the remuneration rates, but it is not possible to violate the principle of predictability of the law as an important attribute of the rule of law. Of course, it will be possible to increase the remuneration rates of the minerals obtained by means of the relevant government regulation before 1 January 2022. However, the effects would remain unchanged for existing payers until the end of 2021 and would in fact involve a change in the government's regulation which would increase rates, up to the new payers' remuneration rate. However, we are talking here on the theoretical level, because the amendment to the Mining Act has not yet been discussed by the Government or by the Chambers of Parliament of the Czech Republic, and it is not possible to take steps leading to the amendment to the relevant Government Regulation. That will be possible when the amendment to the Upper Law takes effect.
