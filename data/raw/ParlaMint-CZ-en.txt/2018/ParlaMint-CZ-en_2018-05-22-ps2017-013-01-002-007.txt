Mr President, honourable Members, the proposal for an amendment to the Income Tax Act is to remove the exception in Section 17b. a). According to this letter, funds that register their shares on the stock exchange may enjoy a tax advantage of 5%. This possibility has been abused by some funds. In fact, classical stock companies had the opportunity, in quotation marks, to change to the so-called investment fund and merely to register these shares allowed them tax advantages. As a result, in 2016, income tax evasion was estimated at around 600 million. Crowns. This proposal has already been discussed repeatedly in the Committee on Budgets. There has been a reading of the amendment proposing the maintenance of this letter, however, on the condition that the owners of the investment fund have, say, a diversified ownership structure. This means that no legal person has a share of more than 10 per cent. This condition is aimed at the misuse by one legal person or a group of persons who could have placed real estate or other property in those funds and benefited from this tax advantage. [[16.20 hours]] On the Committee on Budgets on this condition, both the Ministry of Finance and, in fact, I, as the plaintiff for the Senate, have taken a neutral position. The reason was that such a condition does not preclude the abuse of this institute by natural persons. That is why it would help this matter if the words "legal persons" were deleted in the amendment in point 1, but it is, of course, up to your decision how to definitely construct those conditions. So, in simple terms, the aim of this proposal is not to limit collective investment in any way. The law in the following points makes it possible for classical public funds to have this tax advantage, as is the case abroad. It is simply that this institute should not be abused by entities which would in fact only be needed by public limited companies of one investor and would benefit from this tax advantage. Thank you for your attention.
