ParlaMint-CZ_2018-12-21-ps2017-024-08-001-129.u6	A1 contains the obligation of distributors to withdraw from the pharmacy the packaging of medicinal products which the pharmacy will not be able to issue for their inability to verify the authenticity of the medicinal products. The proposal further regulates the length of the reduction period, namely 7 days, and addresses as the sole reimbursement of the costs incurred by the pharmacy in vain for all activities with unverifiable medicinal products, by granting pharmacies the right to reimbursement of the costs to the marketing authorisation holder of the product returned. The obligation of marketing authorisation holders to pay the costs to pharmacies will act as an incentive element which will contribute to ensuring that the marketing authorisation holders try to ensure as far as possible that the returned products and, therefore, the defects in the verification are as low as possible. Amendment A2, which in the voting procedure is referred to as A2.1 and A2.2-8, that the postponement of effectiveness must always be adjusted for the whole amendment point, and does not delay the effectiveness of new offences, only corresponds to sanctions of up to 300,000 and more serious offences of up to 2 million. Furthermore, the amendment provides for the postponement of the effectiveness of only certain offences that may be committed in the verification of pharmacists in pharmacies, as opposed to other amendments tabled, which delay the effectiveness of infringements of distributors and marketing authorisation holders. However, it is undesirable to postpone the impunity of marketing authorisation holders as they are those who ensure the verification system. The findings so far confirm that the shortcomings of the system will be caused either by the system itself or by the laxity with which many marketing authorisation holders approach to verify the authenticity of the medicinal products. The advantage of deferred sanctions cannot therefore be granted to those who are responsible for the functioning of the system, otherwise the full functionality of the system cannot be expected even from 1. 1. 2020. The third amendment, which is in the voting procedure as A3, responds to the current long-standing paradox, where the effect of homeopathic medicines is publicly questioned by scientific authorities and compared as similar placebo, but at the same time, homeopathic medicines are marketed as medicines for medical prescription, unlike other countries in the European Union. The main reason for the need to correct the current state of affairs is based on the wording of the Act on Medicinal Products, which defines that medicinal products subject to medical prescription only have to meet any of the following characteristics: high hazards, even in the correct way of use, health hazards in improper use, the content of a new substance with the need for further monitoring, penetrational use, the content of a narcotic or psychotropic substance. Homeopathics do not show these characteristics, and therefore Amendment A3 in the voting procedure excludes homeopathic products from medicinal products issued under medical prescription. Thank you.
