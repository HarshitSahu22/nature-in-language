The Government presents a draft law based on the need to address acute problems in the medical assessment service, which arise as a result of the lack of the capacity of assessors in the labour and social affairs department. This unfavourable personnel situation is based on the long-term considerable vacancy rate of the systemized sites, which is also linked to the high workload of assessors, which also has an impact on compliance with legal deadlines for the development of opinions and their quality. These personnel problems are currently highlighted by the establishment of a 70-year age limit in the Civil Service Act. In addition, on 31 December of this year, medical assessment services will end with the exception of this age limit laid down in the Civil Service Act, which covers a total of 100 assessors. There is therefore a real risk that the necessary capacity of assessors will be lacking from 2019 and therefore the claims of insured persons and persons with disabilities will be threatened, and it is therefore necessary to respond to this situation. This will also contribute to greater interest in activities in the medical assessment service. The conclusion of employment relations with assessors is allowed on the basis of a transitional provision from 1 January next year, so it will be possible to conclude these relations with new assessors from this date. The second part of the proposed amendment proposes the transfer of certain competences from 1 January 2019, i.e. assessment of health status for the purposes of administrative appeal in the field of non-insurable social benefits, from the assessment committees of MPSV to the Czech Social Security Administration. This means that instead of a commissional assessment, today the review committee is three-member, it would be a monocratic assessment by the doctor of the CSSZ, which will enable the use of synergy effects and the associated more flexible response to current problems in the personnel situation in the medical assessment service.
