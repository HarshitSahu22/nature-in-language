ParlaMint-CZ_2018-06-26-ps2017-016-01-009-006.u2	Thank you for the floor, Mr President. Ladies and gentlemen, I submit a proposal for an amendment to Act No. 178/2005 Coll. on the abolition of the National Property Fund of the Czech Republic, and I would like to mention this proposal in a broader context. The National Property Fund has been abolished since 1 January 2006 and its activities have been taken over by the Ministry of Finance. Here, a special account and a special record was set up for the management of the assets and the financing of the liabilities, which are kept separate from the State budget, so that the proceeds of the privatised assets would be used to cover precisely the obligations of the State which were linked to the privatisation. Even then, however, these funds were allowed to be used for other purposes. Over time, however, these other non-privatisation purposes were the main expenditure titles, and they were added to them by partial amendments to the law. Thus, numerous government projects were covered from privatization sources. For example, as early as 2007, investments unrelated to privatisation typically reached the State Fund for Transport Infrastructure, 21 billion. A year later, it was even nearly 34 billion. Since 2012, the possibility of transferring funds from the proceeds of privatisation to the State budget has been enacted. It is often stated in this context that the funds were intended to cover the pension account deficit. However, it should be borne in mind that once they were transferred to the budget, they were typically taxes to be excluded from other revenue of the budget. This issue is indirectly linked to the pension, so that no more could be transferred than the difference between budgeted revenue and budgeted expenditure on pension insurance. Since 2012, an average of 10 billion per year has been transferred to the State budget, while maintaining a safe balance to cover privatisation costs, including the most important ecology. From this general introduction, it is clear that a number of arguments that sound against the proposed amendment are purposeful and misleading, and I would like to deal with the most absurd ones immediately. One. It was never the purpose of special privatisation accounts to make a reserve for worse times. Whoever thinks so should have called back in 2002 when income was almost 130 billion, or 2005 when it was 110 billion. Not even 40 billion from 2008 were enough. Today, when income is basically limited to one year from the year of the declining dividend of ČEZ, Čepra and České Aeroholding, and in total it accounts for about one tenth of the income from 2002 or 2005 and not even half of 2008, will the state's reserves suddenly be discussed? That doesn't seem like a sustainable argument to me. Two. The financing of environmental commitments towards the acquirers of privatised enterprises is not at risk. In the current wording of the Act, there is no insurance policy to prevent any available funds in special accounts from being transferred to the preparation of industrial zones. There wouldn't be a crown left in ecology. However, the proposed amendment cuts industrial zones or transport infrastructure as expenditure titles and leaves basically only environmental commitments, reclamation and what is inherently linked to privatisation. In addition, last year's resolution obliges the government to provide the Minister of Finance with at least CZK 4.5 billion per year in ecology. How will the Minister of Finance ensure that? Well, the easiest way to do that is to just leave them in those accounts. And if something unpredictable happens, such as losing in a privatization dispute, we inherited such things, there is a new introduction of the possibility of adding missing funds from the state budget. I therefore argue that the implementation of environmental commitments is dealt with much better by the proposed amendment than before. Three. It's not an accounting trick to improve the balance. The main objective of the proposed amendment is to ensure that funds that are not used to cover privatisation expenses do not remain inactive in the account, but can be used meaningfully for investment or social needs according to the current state of the economy. Therefore, the proposed amendment allows the transfer of these funds to the State budget without determining a specific purpose. This process will be transparent and controllable as it will be subject to standard processes applied in the establishment of the state budget. In a nutshell, the proposed amendment can therefore be summarised by eliminating all special-purpose government expenditure and leaving only expenditure titles related to privatisation commitments, which will thus regain the priority of funding. I stress that it is currently not possible to cover privatisation costs from any other sources. At the same time, the amendment will enable efficient and transparent use of free funds. Thank you for your attention.
