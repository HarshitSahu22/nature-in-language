I think all the things that have been opened here have been discussed. I will just briefly remind you that after the first reading and, above all, after the debate within the Committee on the Environment, a number of issues have been raised concerning the registration of genetic resources collections, where certain doubts have been expressed as to why, whether and what collections are subject to this registration and what consequences arise from it. I would therefore like to stress that the collection register in the European Union is a voluntary tool that introduces the implemented regulation and that the draft law only specifies how domestic collection holders can apply for registration if - I stress again - they are interested. It is therefore not mandatory. The regulation introduces this instrument because it is from these collections that users get material for their research most often. Therefore, only the collection introduced by the system of control over the origin of the genetic resources it provides to users may be included in the register and can thus guarantee that the genetic resources provided have been obtained in accordance with the legislation of the country of origin. The debate was also conducted on the alleged retroactivity of the present proposal. In this respect, I would like to inform you that the implementing regulation and therefore the present draft law will apply only in relation to those genetic resources acquired only after the entry into force of the Nagoya Protocol, i.e. after October 2014. The other reservations raised concerned the formal aspects of the draft law under consideration, such as the alleged incomprehensibleness of certain terms or terminology. I would just like to point out that the bill itself is merely an adaptation law, and the formal form of the bill depends on it. As I have already said, a round table was arranged on the basis of the first reading of the law, to which representatives of the scientific community were invited, and discussions with scientists continued on the Ministry of Environment. I dare say that all the comments of the representatives of the scientific community have been resolved and the main uncertainties explained. An amendment was also prepared in cooperation with them, which reduces the administrative burden of users as far as possible. The amendment was tabled through Mr Zahradník. It was adopted by the Committee on the Environment and, of course, the Ministry supports this amendment. Thank you for your attention.
