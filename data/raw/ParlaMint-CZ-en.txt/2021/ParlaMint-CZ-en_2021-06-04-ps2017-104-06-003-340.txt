Motivation was then based on the promise and subsequent provision of a financial or factual social benefit for the sake of the quotes of a healthy population, which was regulated by internal and later even legislation. The aim of this state policy was probably to improve the situation of socially weaker families by limiting the number of children they would have. Another objective, however, was to ensure a better population in quotation marks, as is apparent from the reports of the district national committees that provided the levy. From this fact it is undoubtedly possible to import the inadmissible eugenic nature of this practice, which aimed to limit the number of children born in certain groups of the population, which were perceived by the regime at the time as problematic, inadaptable or undesirable, whether it was the Romani population, as indicated by the findings of the protector, "or persons with mental or other disabilities or persons with a larger number of children. Social work managed by the state could thus become an instrument not to help, but to reduce the birth rate of selected population groups. Finally, the protector recommended several legislative and methodological measures for the future, as well as certain reparation measures. The main legislative recommendation in this area was the adoption of a new regulation which would clearly define the elements of informed consent. The Ombudsman's methodological recommendations aimed at raising the awareness of the lay public on the issue of sterilization and deepening the lifelong education of doctors on the fundamental principles of patients' rights. The last recommendation was the adoption of an adjustment to compensate those who were victims of unlawful sterilization. In doing so, the protector focused mainly on those victims whose decision-making was played by the state social sector for whose activity the State was responsible, as it was the exercise of public power, thus, according to the general legal principles of the State, responsibility for interference with the rights of persons who, although in formal compliance with the law at the time, were also in deep contradiction with the fundamental principles of the then state. According to the Ombudsman's view, the time period applicable to compensation should be limited by the social sector, including the existence of legal titles for the payment of eugenically motivated doses for sterilization - the period 1973 to 1991. After this period, it was the personal responsibility of doctors who did not comply with the requirements of the legislation, and the victims should, according to the protector, possibly refer to the courts for personal protection actions. Several cases of unlawful sterilization have been examined before Czech courts, including the Constitutional Court. Therefore, the courts qualified illegal sterilization as an intervention in patients' personal rights and gave them adequate satisfaction, but mostly in the form of an apology of the hospital. As they believe this intervention has significantly reduced the dignity of the patients and this satisfaction has therefore not proved sufficient, it would also be appropriate to grant the right to compensation for non-property damage to money.
