ParlaMint-CZ_2021-07-07-ps2017-111-01-002-331.u2	Thank you, Mr Vice-President. Ladies and gentlemen, once again, let me make a very brief comment on the Government's bill which amends certain laws in connection with the adoption of the Act on the Electronisation of Health, which we voted on a moment ago. This means it is the amendment law of several related legislations. Since the Government's bill has already been presented in the Chamber of Deputies during its discussions, let me just remind you that this Government's bill therefore contains several amendments to the laws in which the institutions and procedures provided for by the Act on the Electronisation of Health are based. These are the following laws: Act on the establishment of ministries and other central bodies of the state administration of the Czech Republic, Act on archivism and written services, Act on sickness insurance, Act on medicines and Act on health services. I would now like to comment on the amendments that have been applied. As regards Mr Vyzula's amendment, which is marked by letter A, I would like to say to him that I fully support this amendment. With regard to Mr Petrtýl's amendment referred to in point B, this amendment complements Section 45 of the Health Services Act on a new obligation for health service providers to inform contract health insurance companies within 24 hours of the results of the required laboratory expert haematological, professional biochemical and microbiological examinations carried out by the patient in order to fulfil the tasks of health insurance companies under other legislation. I am of the opinion that optimization of the scope, effectiveness and availability of laboratory examinations to health insurance companies can be achieved by means other than the establishment of a new obligation in the Health Services Act. The establishment of this obligation would entail an administrative burden for health service providers, and I consider that sending such data on the patient's health is not sufficiently justified by the design of the objectives pursued. It is not for health insurance companies to assess the patient's condition and the need for his examination. They can, of course, do this as part of a review activity. In this context, I also consider it necessary to draw attention to the fact that this obligation is actually introduced within the National Health Services Register, which is part of the NHIS, the National Health Information System, when the results of these laboratory examinations are, of course, included in this register, and the content of this register will be accessible to health insurance companies within the scope of the data on health services that they have paid for in order to carry out public health insurance. Therefore, if this amendment had been adopted, providers would in principle have supplied the data in duplicate, and I cannot support the proposal from this point of view. Thank you for your attention.
