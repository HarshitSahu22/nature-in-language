In a very brief manner, I would mention the Senate bill amending the Charter of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms by inserting it in Article 6 (1). 4 a single sentence: "The right to defend the life of another man or the life of another man with a weapon is guaranteed under the conditions laid down by law." It is therefore the anchoring of the right to defend life with weapons as fundamental human rights, namely to strengthen this right to a constitutional level, i.e. to the level of constitutional order. As in the past, some rights have been governed by a simple law, no one has questioned this right, just as no one has questioned - so far - the right to defend life with a weapon, yet the legislator, aware of the importance of such a right, has allowed such a right to be promoted to constitutional law. At random, it is the right to collect or not to be subjected to forced labour. The purpose of this amendment, the Senate bill, is therefore headed there to preserve this right for futuro in the future, in order to be able to defend itself with a weapon in hand. Of course, this weapon doesn't mean a firearm or just a firearm, it means anything that a person could defend himself without being forced to use his bare hands to defend himself. The Senate proposal responds to trends that occur in the European Union states, where the right to defend itself with an object that can be used as a weapon, or it must be shown that the object of the weapon is not, typically, a walking stick, a ban on cold weapons in Belgium, such as knives. A common argument against this amendment is that it is a strengthening of the right to possess a firearm, or perhaps an introduction, and thus an extension of the ownership of firearms. Here I would like to say that this proposal certainly does not mean this, and it cannot be interpreted in any way, in any way, by any interpretation. Thus, as the definition of the necessary defence changes, or this proposal does not aim to change the definition of the necessary defence, however it does not extend it, it merely imposes the right of a person to defend himself with something, and this right cannot be eliminated, cannot be completely emptied. That is therefore the fundamental objective of this Senate proposal. I do not want to bother you with reading the data as discussed in this Senate proposal, however, it came to the Senate and was presented on 24 September 2019 with the fact that the last vote in the Senate took place on 16 June 2020, in the meantime the proposal was supported twice in plenary as well as by the Committee constitutionally legal as well as by the Security Committee.
