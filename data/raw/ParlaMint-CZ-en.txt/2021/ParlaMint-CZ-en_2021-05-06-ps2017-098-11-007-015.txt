ParlaMint-CZ_2021-05-06-ps2017-098-11-007-015.u2	Hello, Mr President, Madame Members, allow me to mention the Government's bill amending Act No. 156/1998 Coll., on fertilizers. The draft law implements a partial adaptation of the Czech rule of law to Regulation 2019/1009 of the European Parliament and of the Council laying down rules for the supply of European Union fertilizer products to the market. This Regulation shall enter into force on the twentieth day following that of its publication in the Official Journal of the European Union. The concept of auxiliary plant preparation is therefore replaced by this new term in the Fertilizers Act. The draft law also contains an accompanying amendment to the Act on Conformity Assessment of specified products when making them available on the market, which is directly related to the issue. The full adaptation of the Regulation on fertilizer products will be, in view of the effectiveness of other provisions of the adapted regulation, that is to say 1. 1. 2022, by another amendment to the Fertilizers Act, planned in two years. According to the Act on Fertilizers in force, records shall be carried out on quantities, species, periods of use of fertilisers, soil aids, plant biostimulants, substrates, treated sludges and sediments, by land, crops and years. [[12.40 hours]] The obligation to keep records and the quantities of the main and by-product harvested, with the exception of permanent grassland, is now proposed. This provision has been put into effect since 1 January 2022 and is intended to introduce an instrument for sustainable farm management in the field of nutrients. Agricultural entrepreneurs operating on an area of more than 200 hectares, according to the register of use of agricultural land according to user relations, will be newly obliged to keep records according to the law in electronic form. This provision shall also have suspensive effect from 1 January 2022. On the basis of the knowledge of application practice, the new mandatory annex to the application for the registration of fertilisers is the operating order of the waste recovery facility under Act No. 185/2001 Coll., on Waste. The Government's bill was discussed by the Agricultural Committee of the Chamber of Deputies of the Parliament of the Czech Republic at its 35th meeting and recommended its approval by its resolution to the Chamber of Deputies of the Parliament. Thank you.

ParlaMint-CZ_2021-05-06-ps2017-098-11-007-015.u10	Thank you for the floor. Ladies and gentlemen, the amendment to the Fertilizers Act may not seem interesting at first sight, but the opposite is true. The directive implemented has great objectives. It wants to achieve a reduction in pollution caused by nitrates from agricultural sources and to prevent further pollution like this. It would be good, however, if we did not make economic conditions worse in the Czech countryside in the fulfilment of these objectives through unsavory interventions by the Ministry of Agriculture. That is why I am proposing two amendments which address these shortcomings. The first proposal concerns the storage of fertilisers on the same surface. Storage should be allowed after three years, not after four, as is the current proposed version. We must bear in mind that there are overlays in the season, which are weighed in spring, spread in autumn, and therefore this time limit is longer in practice. The second proposal concerns the approval of the embossment into the emergency plan according to the water law. Apparently, it's just another bureaucratic duty that won't help, and that's why we're cutting it off. The concentration of more feed and excrement on bare soil does not arise in any larger clustering of animals, but only in the long-term recurrent state. The risks of pollution are therefore significantly lower than, for example, in the storage of fertilisers with which it is compared in this law. At the same time, the current definition prevents this, as it reads: "Fertilizers must be stored and the food must be operated in such a way that water pollution cannot occur." We can't get to a healthy landscape by restricting and tightening. It is necessary to positively motivate people who manage the landscape and, on the contrary, to remove barriers that prevent it from being restored. Thank you for your attention.
