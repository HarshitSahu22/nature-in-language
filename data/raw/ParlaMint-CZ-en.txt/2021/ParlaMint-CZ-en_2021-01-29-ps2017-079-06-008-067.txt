ParlaMint-CZ_2021-01-29-ps2017-079-06-008-067.u2	Thank you for the floor. In the present case, the Ministry of Justice prepared a draft law amending the law on judicial fees. I would remind you that this proposal was made before the coronation, so it passed successfully through the government. For nine years, court fees have not been increased and are no longer in line with the reality of the time. Court fees are basically three jobs for us. For one thing, it is regulatory to avoid abuse of actions, then incentive to promote alternative dispute resolution, and ultimately fiscal. At present, we have huge expenditures with, for example, IT systems. You want to publish judgments, I'm trying to comply, but all these systems that we're implementing are worth something, and justice has to live off something. In order for these appointments to be fulfilled, the court fees must correspond to the fixed amount, but they no longer correspond. [[11.40 hours]] The last comprehensive amendment was from 2011, but it became effective nine years ago, and the cost of running the judiciary in the Czech Republic has increased many times. The economic situation has also changed substantially - so if the crisis had not come into this now, you have to assess this here - and there has been a significant increase in the costs of the operation of justice by one third. The Act constitutes a comprehensive proposal to increase judicial fees, the first of this kind since 2011. Not all court fees are proposed to increase, only those collected. Fees for which regulatory functions are lacking shall be retained unchanged. These include, for example, registration in a public register. The proposal aims in particular to reduce the number of bullying and special-purpose actions and to promote alternative dispute resolution, such as mediation. The draft law also responds to selected decisions of the Constitutional Court. However, together with the increase in judicial fees, there is also a simplification of the exemption from judicial charges in proceedings before municipal and administrative courts so that even the poorer groups continue to have access to the court. The bill deals with and essentially responds to errors in practice. As far as fees are concerned, I would also like to point out that the increase in the revenue of the state budget will be used primarily to increase the financing of areas that have been underfunded in the long term in the judiciary. I will mention, for example, the equipment of the courts that we urgently need. The rewards of experts and interpreters that we have recently discussed are significantly underfunded. And like I said, IT systems that cost us huge amounts of money. I beg that we are currently having a crisis to support this law, and I ask that it be released for second reading. Thank you.
