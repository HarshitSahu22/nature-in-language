ParlaMint-CZ_2014-05-13-ps2013-008-05-001-032.u2	Mr President, ladies and gentlemen. I'm gonna save your time. I'm just gonna sum up what's really important. As you know, this government proposal has already been discussed. He was also directed to the Foreign Committee. By his resolution of March 2014, he recommended that the Chamber of Deputies agree to withdraw the reservations. In essence, this proposal is triggered by the need to align the new legislation which took effect on 1 January 2014 by adopting a law on international judicial cooperation in criminal matters, in so far as certain reservations need to be withdrawn, so that this regulation can be applied in full. In particular, it is proposed to withdraw the reservation to Article 21 of the European Convention on Extradition, to Parts III and IV of the European Convention on the Supervision of Condemned and Suspended Perpetrators and to the International Convention on the Enforcement of Criminal Judgments abroad. The reservations to Article 21 of the European Convention on Extradition show that, in the context of transit authorisation, it is necessary to assess all conditions for the admissibility of the issue as provided for in Title 25 of the Criminal Code. On the other hand, the law on international judicial cooperation now in force against previous legislation simplifies the conditions for transit clearance, therefore there is no need for this reservation at all. Reservations to Parts III and IV of the European Convention on the supervision of conditionally sentenced and conditionally released offenders and reservation to the International Convention on the Enforcement of Criminal Judgments abroad limit the transmission and acceptance of enforcement of decisions on conditional convictions or conditional release. The withdrawal of these reservations again allows for the fact that we already have new legislation in the law on international judicial cooperation. So I also refer to what has been said here, what the Foreign Committee has recommended. The proposed appeals are fully in line with the foreign policy interests of the Czech Republic, of course with the constitutional order and other legal order of the Czech Republic and with the obligations arising for the Czech Republic from international law and its membership of the European Union. I therefore ask you to make a positive assessment of this government proposal. Thank you.
