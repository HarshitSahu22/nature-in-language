ParlaMint-CZ_2014-09-19-ps2013-017-04-008-161.u2	Thank you, Mr President. Dear colleagues, in connection with the Russian annexation of Crimea and the development of events in Ukraine, NATO has taken some immediate measures to reassure geographically exposed allies about the reliability of alliance collective defense. As part of its contribution to these immediate measures, the Czech Republic proposed the North Atlantic Alliance to send four Gripen aircraft with ground and flying personnel to the Baltic countries, or to extend its planned involvement in the protection of Iceland's airspace. The Alliance has chosen a second offer, which in practice means extending our presence in Iceland from 5 to 9 weeks and increasing the number of flying and ground personnel from the original 50 to 75 people. Four Jas-39 Gripen aircraft will be involved, which is the same number that was planned in the case of the original mission. This will take place during the October-December period of this year. Although Iceland is not directly on the border with the Russian Federation, there was a significant increase in Russian air force activities near its airspace. Therefore, our mission over Iceland will not be any simpler or less significant than the protection of the airspace of the Baltic countries. Our activity in protecting Iceland's airspace will cost us approximately 33 miles overall. CZK and these funds will be drawn from the Ministry of Defence's budget. In addition to immediate measures, the Alliance approved a number of longer-term measures at the recent Wales Summit to strengthen the security of the Member States at its eastern border. Most of these measures, which will also involve the armed forces of the Czech Republic, will require additional military forces and capabilities as well as financial resources. The scope, depth and duration of these measures will, of course, depend on the further behaviour and procedure of the Russian Federation. The Czech Republic is interested in Russia being a readable and responsible actor respecting international legal standards. We do not want confrontation in any way, we do not want conflict, we do not consider Russia a priori enemy, but due to his behaviour we cannot even consider him a trusted partner. Thank you for your attention.
