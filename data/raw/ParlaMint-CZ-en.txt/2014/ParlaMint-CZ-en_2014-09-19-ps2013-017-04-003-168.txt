ParlaMint-CZ_2014-09-19-ps2013-017-04-003-168.u4	So again... I'm just sorry, I've been wondering what to poke out of this so I don't waste your time. First of all, it is the 10th Financial Stability Report. For the first time, it also reflects the new responsibility for macro-prudential policy. In terms of the time report, it summarises the functioning of the Czech financial system in 2013 and in the first four months of 2014. But it focuses mainly on the risks that can come. I will then allow myself to add a few more details of the developments in the month after its closure. First of all, the introduction of new macro-prudential instruments is part of the new mandate that I have already mentioned. This means that we can increase to the banks what they must have on capital. I would therefore like to sum up what we have taken advantage of. We took advantage of the right in that immediate security buffer to demand 2.5 percent of the additional share capital. In addition, from the spectrum of capital reserves that we can introduce today under the new CRM 4 directive in Europe, we have used the capital buffer in the four most important banks to cover systemic risk, in other words, the four most important banks in our country must form higher proportional capital than the rest. On the contrary, we did not use the countercyclical capital buffer simply because the cycle is not overheating in our country at the moment. Rather, we are reflected from the bottom, so there is no need to worry about the excessive optimism of banks in lending. At the same time, we told the banks that we would probably not use it in the next two years. If we look at the risks to future financial stability, we see them as largely low. On the other hand, we assess credit risk in bank balance sheets despite partial improvements as continuing to increase and do not expect any significant decline in the near future. This is due again to the state of the economy and by the fact that there is still no significant improvement in the ability of residents and businesses to reduce problem loans. On the contrary, the overall picture of loans granted to non-residents, which have experienced a sharp increase in the share of loans in default, is a little worse for us. Fortunately, it must be said that these are primarily loans to non-residents from Russia and this is a limited problem of dimension. The increase in this is specific. As a satisfactory assessment of developments in credit supply and credit dynamics. It's developing quite favorably. If I look at other sectors, I must say again, of course, that we have found a unsatisfactory situation in the cooperative base segment, where we will also identify in some cases a high concentration of loans granted to a small group of debtors. However, I must say again that the whole segment represents about 1% of the Czech financial sector, and therefore its importance for the system as such is not great. We have had stress tests, we have done an extremely unlikely and extremely cruel scenario, which was based on a long-term and significant decline in economic activity in our country, which, of course, is not what is happening in the real world, which would be due to adverse developments in the EU countries and low external demand. It would show up at the exhaustion of the financial reserves of both households and normal businesses, causing a significant deterioration in their ability to pay off their liabilities. Moreover, we assumed an increase in long-term interest rates, i.e. losses in banks for this reason. Nevertheless, the stress tests carried out show that the banking sector remains in a strong recession that would be accompanied by deflation, highly resistant to adverse development scenarios. The banking sector is able to pass such a test thanks to its high capital cushion. The insurance sector also showed a sufficient degree of resistance to adverse developments due to its high capital cushion. The sector of pension companies, which is the third sector we work with there, remains sensitive to the growth in the yields of held securities. In the event of a leap in income growth, it would probably have to increase capital. However, I can say that this case was already in some form in 2008 and 2009, and we have an agreement between the Czech National Bank and pension companies set up a mechanism to ensure this. I dare to quit with this. Thank you for your attention.
