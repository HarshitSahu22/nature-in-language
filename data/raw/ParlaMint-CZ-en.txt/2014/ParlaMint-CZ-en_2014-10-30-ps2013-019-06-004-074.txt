Government proposal by which the Parliament of the Czech Republic is to give its consent to ratification of the Protocol on the Prevention, Repression and Punishment of Human trafficking, in particular with women and children, supplementing the United Nations Convention against Transnational Organised Crime of 15 November 2000, signed for the Czech Republic on 10 December 2002 /German press 139/ - Second reading Once again, I will ask the Interior Minister Milan Chovanec to take the floor.
ParlaMint-CZ_2014-10-30-ps2013-019-06-004-074.u2	Thank you for the floor. Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, the aim of the present protocol is to combat trafficking in human beings as a serious form of crime. It is, of course, also a fight against human rights violations, which is generally implemented at international level, in the form of a criminal organisation. The Protocol is the first binding international document which contains both a clear definition of trafficking in human beings and a list of the specific measures to be taken by the Parties to combat this crime. However, the protocol aims not only to criminalise human trafficking and exploitation, but also to consider international cooperation in preventing these harmful phenomena as important. At the same time, the Protocol contains measures to ensure the assistance and protection of victims of trade, including provisions relating to their status in the beneficiary States and their return to their country of origin. Particular attention shall also be paid to the security of travel documents issued by the Contracting Parties to this Protocol. The Protocol is an additional document to the UN Convention against National Organised Crime. All provisions should therefore be interpreted in accordance with this Convention. For example, as regards international police cooperation, legal aid, extradition, liability of legal persons and the like, the arrangements contained in the Convention shall apply. To be completed, I state that the Convention entered into force in the Czech Republic on 24 October last year. On August 1st of this year, an amendment to the Criminal Code came into force, which eliminated the last deficiency in the possibility of full implementation of the Protocol in our national law. In our legal order, there has been no clear criminalisation of the conduct of accepting victims of trafficking in human beings for the purpose of exploitation. This removes the last obstacle to the ratification of this Convention. Thank you for your attention.
