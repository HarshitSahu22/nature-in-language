Proposal to extend the operation of the forces and resources of the Ministry of Defence in the European Union operation in the Republic of Mali in 2014 /German press 193/ The submitted material under the Government's authority is justified by Minister of Defence Martin Stropnický. Please, Minister, take the floor.
ParlaMint-CZ_2014-06-18-ps2013-010-06-012-095.u2	Thank you, Madam President. Ladies and gentlemen, the current mandate for the activities of the Czech Republic in the European Union Training Mission in the Republic of Mali expires on 30 June this year. For this reason, I submitted a proposal with the Minister of Foreign Affairs to the Government to extend the mandate for our troops in Mali by the end of this year, and the Government approved this proposal on 28 April. In order to continue our military activity in Mali, the following facts speak: we are actively contributing to the stabilisation of the security situation in the Sahel, thereby contributing to maintaining the security of Europe, the Czech Republic as well. As a result of the first mandate of the EU Training Mission, there are four trained Malian battalions, each of 700 people, 120 qualified Malian military instructors and around 50 company commanders. Real assumptions are thus created for the Malian party to gradually take over the training of its own units. Of course, Czech soldiers are also involved in these results. Our work in Mali brings results and is very positively evaluated not only in the European Union but also by the Malian Minister of Defence and the Malian Government. At the same time, it is an impulse for strengthening cooperation not only with France but also with other EU Member States operating in this mission. The Czech Republic demonstrates its military engagement in Mali, the first, by the way, in such a visible form on the African continent after 1989, that it is able to contribute in its work to strengthening the role of the European Union as a global security player. The proposal to extend our military presence in Mali in 2014 is submitted separately for time reasons. The issue of military activity in Mali after 2014 will be addressed within the framework of the draft overall mandate for the operation of the Czech Republic in foreign operations in 2015 and 2016, which will be submitted to the government during this year. We want to keep the presence on this mission in numbers of up to 50 people. Currently 38 Czech soldiers are active in the mission, providing protection of forces and participating in training of the Malian army. The amount of funds related to our activities in Mali will be around CZK 72 million 183 thousand by the end of 2014. These costs will be covered by the Ministry of Defence's budget. Our military activity in Mali makes the Czech Republic a fully-fledged and respected partner in ensuring the security of the European space. Although this is a mission that is one of the more demanding, without military involvement outside our borders, it is not possible to ensure the appropriate security of our country. Finally, I would like to state that the proposal to extend the operation of the Czech Army in the EU Mali Training Mission in 2014 was discussed on 21 May 2014 in the Defence Committee of the Chamber of Deputies of the Parliament of the Czech Republic, which recommended that the Chamber of Deputies approve it. The proposal has already been discussed and approved by the Senate of the Parliament of the Czech Republic on 29 May 2014. Madam President, ladies and gentlemen, I ask you to give your consent to the material presented. Thank you for your attention.
