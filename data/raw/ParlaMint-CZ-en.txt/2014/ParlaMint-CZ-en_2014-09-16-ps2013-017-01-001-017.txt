This is an extensive amendment with major implications for the conduct of the service of professional soldiers and the future performance of the tasks of the armed forces. The content of the bill, or the most important moment of the amendment, is the introduction of new rules of the career management system, thereby increasing the attractiveness of the military profession. A new career and pay schedule for soldiers is also intended to contribute to this. Furthermore, it is also about the implementation of European Union law on the allocation of military service times. I think I have presented the amendment in detail at first reading, but I would nevertheless like to recall some of the key principles of the proposed adjustment. First of all, it is the binding of a suspended post with a military rank and a corresponding service salary. This is a system that works successfully in a number of NATO countries, such as the United Kingdom, Belgium, the Federal Republic, France and so on. A paycheck corresponding to a soldier's service, not a contract. Furthermore, it is about eliminating the effects of wage restrictions from past years, including the reduction in the tariff salaries of soldiers in 2010. Most soldiers, especially in executive positions, will thus increase the salary slightly, a fall can only be expected in soldiers with a large proportion of extras, for example for emergency. The introduction of a tool to maintain highly specialised experts is one of the fundamental moments of the amendment. It's about airmen, doctors and IT specialists. This will be achieved by multiplying the so-called stabilization contribution. It is also about suppressing subjective influences in determining the level of soldier's salary and simplifying the structure of soldier's salary, which will contribute to increasing transparency and efficiency of career management systems. Furthermore, it is the creation of a condition for the anticipated increase in recruitment of soldiers for the necessary addition of today's unfulfilled states and the tightening of certain procedures, such as the time of decisiveness, the assumption of performance of the service beyond normal working hours or the inclusion in the standby system and so on. It is also the current preservation or admission of other means of protection or compensation that compensate the difficulties of military service. Overall, the amendment aims to make the use of mandatorial expenditure more effective. I would like to stress that the implementation will only involve the current budgetary resources intended for the Ministry of Defence's mandatorial expenditure. I would like to thank this conclusion for the manner in which the committee responsible for the Chamber of Deputies is being held.
