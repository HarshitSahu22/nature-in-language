ParlaMint-CZ_2014-09-23-ps2013-017-05-005-035.u2	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. Ladies and gentlemen, let me briefly state the so-called. a proper amendment to the Consumer Tax Act. The proposed proper amendment to the Consumer Tax Act relates to one of the priorities of the government, which is to take measures necessary to improve the effectiveness of tax collection and to avoid abuse and circumvention. Two main areas of change are proposed in the Consumer Tax Act. The first is a comprehensive change in the concept of authorisation procedures. This is a tightening-up of the authorisation process so as to limit the maximum number of risky entities that are able to dispose of selected products subject to excise duty and which do not provide sufficient guarantees that they will carry out the activity in accordance with the applicable legislation. The second main change is the introduction of a special register of persons loading special mineral oils. The purpose of this measure is to prevent tax fraud related to the illegal incorporation into fuel, in particular diesel, of non-taxed special mineral oils. A proper amendment to the Consumer Tax Act also includes an amendment to the Act on mandatory marking of alcohol. A partial adjustment of the bail settings of persons obliged to label alcohol is proposed. In order not to discriminate against entities whose activity is directed rather at alcohol in smaller consumer packages, for whose designation requires more control tapes, an adjustment is proposed to establish the limit of the bail zones not to the absolute number of control tapes, but to the number of control tapes which may indicate a certain amount of alcohol. Another bail zone for very small entities is also introduced. The effectiveness of the proper amendment to the Act on excise duties is proposed from 1 January 2015, except for part six, for which it is proposed for legislative technical reasons to take effect on 31 December 2014.

The first and main heading of this adjustment is the reduction of risk subjects who have been able to dispose of selected products subject to excise duty and which do not provide sufficient guarantees that they will carry out the activity in accordance with the applicable legislation. [[unintelligible]] The second area of this legislation is the issue of tax fraud, which is related to the illegal incorporation of untaxed special mineral oils into traditional fuels, especially diesel. The proposed modification introduces a special register of persons loading special mineral oils. It is therefore proposed to introduce a register at national level, which should limit to a certain level the penetration of untaxed special mineral oils into the Czech market for diesel engines. The third circle and subject of the amendment is the law on mandatory marking of alcohol. It is, as the petitioner said, about establishing the limit of the bail zones not to the absolute number of check belts, but to the number of check belts that mark a certain amount of alcohol. Furthermore, against the current regulation, the introduction of a new bail zone is proposed by a person obliged to mark alcohol of CZK 100,000 with the permission to remove control tapes, which can be marked with a maximum of 2,000 litres of alcohol. The introduction of this zone is mainly aimed at very small producers, importers of exclusive spirits, producers of regional specialities à la such, which take very limited numbers of control tapes and for which the efficiency is generally less risky and easier to control during the year. The proposed legislation is fully in line with the constitutional order of the Czech Republic and is also fully compatible with the legal acts of the European Union and the case law of the judicial authorities of the European Union and general legal principles. As rapporteur, therefore, I fully support this proposal and recommend that it be forwarded to the House for second reading and I also recommend that this amendment be discussed in the Committee on Budgets.
