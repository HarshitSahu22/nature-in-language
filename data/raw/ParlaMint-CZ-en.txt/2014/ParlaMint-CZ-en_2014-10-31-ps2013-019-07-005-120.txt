ParlaMint-CZ_2014-10-31-ps2013-019-07-005-120.u2	Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, the material presented informs on the political, military and financial aspects of the activities of Czech troops in Afghanistan, Kosovo, Bosnia and Herzegovina, the Sinai Peninsula, the United Kingdom and the Republic of Mali over the period 2013. Last year, the Czech Republic was particularly involved in Afghanistan, namely in the ISAF operation. Our activity was focused on three areas. The reconstruction and reconstruction were carried out mainly during the five-year operation of the Provincial Reconstruction Team, which ceased its activities in January 2013. Total invested in Logar province about 300 million. CZK 141 reconstruction and development projects and 113 rapid impact projects. The construction of Afghan security forces consisted of training Afghan helicopter pilots, increasing the operational capabilities of the Afghan combat battalion in the province of Vardak and training Afghan police officers. The Czech Republic contributed to the support of deployed international forces by the chemical and biological protection unit, members of the field surgical team and through air traffic controllers at the International Airport in Kabul. As in previous years, the protection of persons and property of the representative office of the Czech Republic in Kabul continued. Now briefly on the next operation. In the Balkans, the Czech Republic worked as part of the KFOR Alliance Operation Headquarters in Kosovo and the European Union Mission ALTHEA in Bosnia and Herzegovina in the deployment of 19 soldiers, of which 4 in ALTHEA and 15 in KFOR. We continued to participate in the MFO mission on the Sinai Peninsula by one CSA aircraft with flying and ground personnel in the number of 13 people. The activities of our soldiers in Sinai have earned considerable recognition from both the leadership of the mission and the partners. The operation in this mission was financially favourable for the Czech Republic because we received 88.6 percent of the financial costs from the MFO. We have engaged in anti-piracy efforts by sending individuals to the headquarters of the Union operation ATALANTA in Great Britain. In addition, since April 2013, the new Czech military unit has been active in the European Union training mission in Mali, where it performs tasks related to the protection of the operation headquarters and partly participates in the training of the Malian Armed Forces. The total number is 38 soldiers. Even there, we have earned the recognition of allies, especially France, which is the leading country of this operation. A total of 15 Czech soldiers were sent to NATO's foreign operations within the alliance command structures in Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany. The Czech Republic has prepared forces and capabilities in its territory for the possible activation of NATO standby forces in the number of 766 people and the UN in the number of 50 people. Six military observers of the Army of the Czech Republic worked in UN missions in Afghanistan, Kosovo and Congo. In terms of financial intensity, less than 1.25 billion were exhausted in individual foreign operations. Kč. The operation was fully financed from the budget of the Ministry of Defence chapter. Finally, I would like to say that working in foreign operations has a significant contribution to maintaining and restoring international peace and security and is an expression of the responsibility of the Czech Republic as a member of the international community. Czech soldiers are traditionally very positively rated by our allies and local population. Experience gained in operations is then used to further improve the quality of training forces and significantly translate into the preparation of new units broadcast into foreign operations. I would therefore like to say that their operation in missions and geographically farther away has a direct positive impact on our safety. Let me conclude by thanking all our soldiers for their missionary engagement in 2013. Thank you.
