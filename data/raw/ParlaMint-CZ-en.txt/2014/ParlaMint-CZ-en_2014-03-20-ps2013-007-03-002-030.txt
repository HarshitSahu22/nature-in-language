Let me therefore present the Government's proposal to agree to ratify the amendments to Annex III to the Rotterdam Convention on the Prior Consent Procedure for Certain Dangerous Chemicals and Pesticides in International Trade. The Czech Republic has been a Contracting Party to the Convention since 24 February 2004. The sixth meeting of the Conference of the Parties to the Rotterdam Convention, held on 28 April to 10 May last year, adopted a decision to extend Annex III to this Convention by four dangerous chemicals, namely pesticide azinphos-methyl and three industrial chemicals: commercial pentabromodiphenyl ether, commercial octabromodiphenyl ether and perfluorooctane sulphonic acid and its derivatives. [[Let's hear it.]] The inclusion of this substance in Annex III will be subject to the prior consent procedure, which in practice means that the delegated authority of the importing State gives its consent to import of the dangerous substance before it is implemented. The treatment of substances newly included in Annex III is already addressed in the Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on the export and import of dangerous chemicals and the Commission Regulation, so that the amendment of Annex III will have no impact on the law of the Czech Republic or on the state budget, state administration and business sector. The inclusion of new substances in Annex III will contribute to greater protection of the environment and human health. The Rotterdam Convention is a Treaty of the presidential category. In order for the amendment of its Annex III to be valid also for the Czech Republic, this requires the approval of the Parliament of the Czech Republic and subsequent ratification by the President of the Republic. Thank you for your attention.
