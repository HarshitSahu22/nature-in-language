Allow me to read to you my amendment, which I have put into the system and to which I will then apply in a detailed debate. Electronic communications services are an essential element for the functioning of the critical information infrastructure system and important information systems. However, these services may also be a source of numerous security risks. Technical outages of electronic communications services may also cause system malfunctions. Insufficient security of electronic communications networks and also the transfer of information through them can be the cause of later successful cyber attacks. In view of the significant security risks posed by electronic communications services, it is necessary to lay down specific requirements for suppliers of electronic communications services for critical information infrastructure systems, critical information infrastructure communication systems and significant information systems. Institutions and persons referred to in Section 3 (a). (c) to (e) should primarily require services from suppliers providing electronic communications services through publicly available electronic communications networks which are simultaneously operated, established and supervised. At the same time, the provider, which is the set-up and operator of the network, has a greater control over the operation and is therefore more efficient in ensuring that the requirements of this Act are met than the provider that only sells the services. Direct demand for services from suppliers who provide services on their own networks will minimise the risks that would otherwise result from complicated contractual relationships between the electronic communications service provider and the network provider. Where the service provider is a person different from the network provider, the service user has a contractual relationship only with the service provider and has a limited number of tools to directly affect the entity providing a publicly available electronic communications network, although the security of the network is crucial to its services. The service provider thus does not have real control over whether the service provider has transferred all the requirements for the availability, security and functionality of the network to an entity that provides a publicly available electronic communications network and thus can raise reasonable doubts as to whether these requirements have actually been met. In practice, it may not only be an exception to the fact that the service provider undertakes to carry out contractual risks to the extent that it is unable to meet the contractual risks, it is further transferred to the entity that provides the publicly available electronic communications network. Therefore, the legitimate interests of the customer of the service need not be sufficiently guaranteed in this case. The chaining of contractual relationships, which occurs in situations where network provision and service provision are broken down, also creates other costs that are reflected in the services provided. In view of the above arguments, I propose that the authorities and persons referred to in Section 3 (a). (c) to (e) have had an obligation to choose as suppliers of electronic communications services for relevant information systems only an entity which also provides a public communications network which has a major impact on the security of the relevant system. As regards the scope of the requirements for the provision of a public communications network, it is clear that the supplier cannot always be the operator of all publicly available electronic communications networks through which the services are provided. In this case, for example, the Internet, which is a global phenomenon, can be mentioned. However, where a part of the publicly available electronic communications network is essential to ensure the security of the system, such as direct connection of critical infrastructure, the authorities and persons referred to in point 3 of Section 3. (c) to (e) must apply such a requirement. In the event that the authorities and persons referred to in Section 3 (a). (c) to (e) violate the obligation laid down in the selection of the supplier, it will be an administrative offence which will be punishable by penalties imposed by the Office. Thank you for your attention.
