ParlaMint-CZ_2014-09-19-ps2013-017-04-005-020.u2	Thank you, Mr. Chairman. Ladies and gentlemen, may I present to you here the Government's bill amending certain laws in the field of the financial market. Certain technical changes to certain laws governing the financial market are made by the draft law. In particular, it is a bill on payment, a law on limitation of payments in cash and an act on insurance intermediaries. The amendment to the law on payment services is implemented into Czech law by a European regulation that provides that payments in euro will have to be made in the future in uniform pan-European standards. This regulation will not become binding on the Czech Republic as a Member State until 2016, but we already need to make some related changes in our legal order. The amendment to the Cash Reduction Act responds to one of the 2011 Supreme Administrative Court judgments. At the time, the Supreme Administrative Court decided somewhat surprisingly that the law on limitation of cash payments only applies to cases where payment is covered by an existing commitment. For example, loans or donations fell out of the scope of this law. The Ministry of Finance considers that there is no factual reason for this and therefore proposes to amend the law on limiting cash payments so as to cover all payments regardless of their legal title. The extension of the scope of this Act is also due to a reduction in the limit for mandatory cash-free payment, i.e. in accordance with the Government's programming declaration, reducing 350 thousand. Kroner to 270 thousand. Crowns. The amendment to the Insurance Intermediaries Act responds to the fact that the new Civil Code has repealed the former Insurance Contract Act without taking over the provisions on information obligations with respect to insurance clients. These provisions are therefore now added to the Insurance Intermediaries Act, which is the most appropriate legal provision for this purpose, since it already regulates, in the current version, the rules governing negotiations with insurance candidates and policyholders, contrary to the Insurance Act. The Committee on Budgets discussed the draft law and recommended its approval without any comments. Thank you for your attention.
