ParlaMint-FI_2018-10-11-ps-99.u207	Mr. Speaker! We have a citizens' initiative on the abolition of inheritance taxes, including gift taxes, signed by more than 57,000 people this evening. President! I support the basic idea of this initiative. In my opinion, we should at least give up inheritance tax in Finland with a reasonable time span, and then, if that gift tax goes along with it, perhaps for that too, but at least for inheritance tax. It is certainly clear that this citizens' initiative is certainly not the way it is, when the budget is being discussed and the budget autumn is being implemented, now go through, but, for example, in the next parliamentary term, this tax should be abolished on a long-term, rational, deliberate basis on some sort of stairway. Inheritance tax must be paid on property obtained by way of inheritance or by way of a will. The greatness is determined by the property of the inheritance and the relative relationship. However, it has been mentioned here in the debate that only large inheritances would be taxed, and I believe that this claim is incorrect because only a inheritance of less than EUR 20000 is exempt. If this inheritance includes immovable property, such as a detached house, a summer cottage, perhaps a few hectares of forested land or even that forest, then very suddenly these inheritances are over — 20000 euros is almost nothing. President! An inheritance tax is often justified in principle, as we have heard today. It is claimed to increase social justice. It is believed that Finland’s economy and employment will decline if heirs continue family businesses, and that young people will become lazy to lie on these inheritances if this tax is abolished. There is also a lot of left-wing romance and a lot of envy in supporting inheritance tax. It is thought that it is appropriate to take from the children of millionaires and to bridge the wealth gap mentioned in this debate. Yet the deep ranks of the people have been against inheritance tax for a long time. In a study a few years ago, 75 percent of respondents were in favour of abolishing inheritance tax. Inheritance tax has often been called even the most hated tax. Inheritance tax is also becoming a rarity on our continent in Europe. If Nordic examples are followed, Sweden and Norway have renounced inheritance tax, and Austria, for example, is one of those who gave up the tax. I also believe that Finland should follow the general Nordic line on this issue and that measures could indeed be taken during the next parliamentary term to abolish the tax. Those who justify the fairness of inheritance tax forget the reality of practical life. In particular, the current system bites into deathbeds with housing or immovable property. This is particularly true in rural areas, where land is owned, i.e. immovable property. Almost always the heirs are unable to pay tax on the estate’s assets, but the tax is paid out of their own funds or taken as a loan. In modern times it is not always easy to convert real estate into money for payment of the tax. Especially if the death has occurred unexpectedly and it is a house or a building in rural areas, in a sparsely populated area. Inheritance taxes are surprisingly hard when a middle-class person dies, for example, if the deceased owned a detached house, a summer cottage and a small forest plot. Nor is it meaningless to pay tax on assets that have been heavily and frequently taxed over the decades. President! For the national economy, the problem with inheritance tax is that it is a counter-attack on saving people and on self-sustaining long-term prosperity. Rather, in Finland, small-scale investment, or so-called national capitalism, should be supported, not punished for it. Finns should be taught financial skills and encouraged to transfer funds from zero-interest bank accounts, for example, to domestic stock exchanges. Small-scale ownership contributes to economic stability and facilitates various debt problems. President! The inheritance tax collects quite a lot of money into the state treasury, and this gap must be fixed in some way. Personally, I would be prepared to bring examples of Sweden, such as, for example, to impose a tax on the proceeds of the special collected property or to provide that inheritance tax should be paid if the property is abandoned within a certain period of time, for example, within five years. Mr President! From the point of view of the red - green parties, it has been pointed out that there are already various benefits in inheritance tax, and that is true. Of course, management tests and various property arrangements can reduce the amount of inheritance tax to be paid. It has also been wrongly stated here that tax planning is somehow illegal. It's not like that. Tax planning is all right, tax evasion is illegal and criminal activity. Unfortunately, it is precisely the low incomes and the middle incomes that the Green Left claims to defend here that do not usually plan it for their own heritage and its own future taxation, and the disproportionate consequences will hit ordinary people and people with lower incomes. A little comes to mind from this excuse for inheritance tax and the fine economic theoretical comments that apparently we still have such political movements in Finland that think that Finland is a poor country and that it remains that way. Mr President! At the end of the day, I would like to raise one last point when we are once again debating the citizens' initiative. Of course, it's wonderful that we have activity. This is from where I came from. I admit to my own ignorance that I did not know before this debate that this initiative was initiated by Vaasa, but the deputies have always started, sometimes with a little overzealous. Of course, it is a valuable thing to pick up names and be on the side of things, but sometimes it has come to mind a little during this parliamentary term, when these citizens' initiatives here almost every month we are dealing with the fact that people have thought that the way things work is to put bank names on the Internet or sign an address.
