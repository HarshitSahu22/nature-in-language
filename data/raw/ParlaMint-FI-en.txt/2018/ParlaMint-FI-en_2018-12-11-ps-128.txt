ParlaMint-FI_2018-12-11-ps-128.u2	Madam President! There is a government proposal that proposes the transfer from the State to the provinces of transport tasks and personnel and property referred to in Article 6 of the Regional Law. The transfer of certain tasks and staff from ely centres to the Väylä Agency and the Transport Management Company is also proposed. The proposal includes the legislative proposals for these amendments and the draft law on implementation. As a result of the regional reform, the majority of the transport responsibilities of the Ely Centres will be transferred to the provinces. In the future, the tasks of road maintenance would be divided between the state and the provinces. The regions would carry out road maintenance tasks in up to nine road maintenance areas in accordance with agreements with the State. Road maintenance would remain under the responsibility of the State, roads owned by the State, and the Väylä Agency would act as road keeper and assume the functions of the owner of the road network. The role of the State as the owner is important in order to ensure the uniform development of the transport network throughout the country and to implement the objectives of debt relief. The financing of road maintenance tasks in the regions would be organised in the form of separate funding. The regions would also be responsible for drawing up the regional transport system plan. The regions could draw up their transport system plans either alone or together with one or more regions. The transport system plan would cover all modes of transport. As already stated in the Regional Law, regions could also, if they so wished, take over the planning and organisation of public passenger transport. The proposal contains provisions on the transfer of these tasks from the State to the provinces. In addition, provisions on the possibility for regions to take on island transport tasks are included in a proposal already submitted to Parliament on the implementation of the regional reform. In addition, the proposal includes changes in the terms and powers of the Ministry of Transport and Communications regarding the reform of the Office, as well as legislative proposals on the amendment of the Road Traffic Act and the Private Road Act. The amendments to the Private Road Act relate to the implementation of state aids and the exercise of official functions. These tasks should be transferred from the Ely Centres to the State Licensing and Control Agency. Mr President! In the regional reform, the funding of transport tasks is divided into general and separate funding. First of all, separate State funding is provided in situations where the task remains the responsibility of the State and where the province acts in accordance with the contract in relation to the mandate. The most significant such task is regional road management, with a contribution of around EUR 410 million. Other tasks to be carried out with separate funding would include state aid for private roads and ferry vessels, island transport and the purchase of air transport. General funding would be provided for the development and organisation of regional transport services, the purchase and development of public passenger transport services, mobility management grants, the construction and maintenance of certain aerodromes and state aids for private roads. Mr President! I hope that the proposal will receive a positive response so that we can implement these reforms. - Thank you.

ParlaMint-FI_2018-12-11-ps-128.u155	Mr President! The proposal proposes to amend, inter alia, the Maritime Labour Contracts Act. It is proposed that the Maritime Labour Contracts Act be amended to require, in addition to the age of 16 years, that the person to be hired has fulfilled his/her compulsory education obligations in respect of work on board a fishing vessel. The motion sounds justified, but it has its own problems. It is clear that each of us should have compulsory education completed. That's what the law says. However, I do know people who have completed it before the age of 16. The presentation states that a 15-year-old can work on a fishing vessel during the summer — so why not on a permanent job if compulsory schooling has been completed? In the end, I do not believe that this will have much significance for the employer or the employee. The publication of Pro Kala ry entitled ‘The Finnish fisheries industry’ describes the state of the fishing industry in Finland. According to it, the number of professional fishermen is falling steadily and in the 21st century it has halved. In Finland, there were only about 700 full-time professional fishermen in early 2018, of which 400 fished in sea areas and 300 in inland waters. There were 3400 by-catches, of which 1500 were in sea areas and 1900 in inland waters. According to the publication of Pro Kala ry, the number of professional fishermen is expected to continue to decline in the coming years, as the majority of current fishermen are approaching retirement age, and it is also expected that small businesses will decrease and large companies will increasingly rely on external labour. This is also why I do not want any unnecessary obstacles for anyone who wants to work on a fishing vessel and work for our table. In fishing, the ship's captain's responsibility is emphasised. He shall be responsible for the organisation of controls on board to ensure that the working conditions of fishermen are as secure as possible. Safety must continue to be the starting point, even though business often misses security. The Safety of Work Act regulates the safety of work. Its second chapter lays down general obligations for the employer, such as Article 8 a general employer’s duty to provide care,10§ Work risk clearance and evaluation laid down in ( c )the provision of education and guidance When a young person has received sufficient training and guidance in his or her work, I think he or she can also work on a fishing vessel at the age of 15. In any case, the captain of the ship will decide what he can do on the ship.
ParlaMint-FI_2018-12-11-ps-128.u156	Mr President! Mr Kulmala gave an excellent speech here on the bureaucracy involved in the work on fishing vessels. I share the same view, given that at present about 20 per cent of fish eaten in Finland is only domestic. Although these directives apply to the whole of the EU, it is the captain's responsibility that employees, regardless of age, can do their job. The damage could happen to anyone. In other words, this dismantling of red tape standards should also be extended to this. But, Mr President, in the context of this Fisheries Directive, I would like to point out that there can be a situation in a country of thousands of lakes and a country on a long sea coast, where only 20% of the fish eaten are domestic. Norwegian salmon are being imported and fish are being caught in overfished sea areas from elsewhere. The main reason for this is the tightening of the environmental authorisation procedure, although this sector has been able to reduce the load on water by 70% in three decades. Nevertheless, environmental standards are constantly tightening up environmental permits. For example, feed in use is 100% recycled goods and technology has improved, which means that the industry has learned and improved its own methods, and here, like a zero-sum game, is simply shifting environmental responsibility elsewhere. Let us imagine that the fish imported from elsewhere is somehow more environmentally friendly. No, it's not. I believe that the next government, whatever its basis, should pay strong attention to the fact that fish farming levels in Finland are rising and targets are set, as in the climate, that by 2030, domestic fish must be 50% of what is eaten. Then we will be on the right track — domestic food production, including the safeguarding of agriculture and forestry and an absolute increase in fish farming. In other words, more farming and more professional fishing in our inner lakes must be one of the key cornerstones of the next government programme when we talk about Finnish food and local food and organic food.
