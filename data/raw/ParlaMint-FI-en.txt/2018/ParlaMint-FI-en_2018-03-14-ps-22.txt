In previous opinions, the Committee on Constitutional Affairs has stressed that social and health services should be organised and produced by a stronger actor than the individual municipality. Another important message was that the reform should build on democracy. In practice, this means that the decision-makers will be elected. It should be recalled that the basis of public administration lies in democracy and the Constitution. The main objectives of the regional and social reform can be summarised as follows: 1) Disabled services. This is particularly the case with social welfare. We must ensure equal social services for all citizens. 2) The smooth functioning of the administration. Public administration is always a service to people, communities, organisations and business. Good and smooth management is based on transparency, customer orientation and interaction. 3) Change in the way things work. Digitalisation and other technological developments offer opportunities to reform the way in which services are produced and organised. 4) More democracy. Public authority must have a face. The elected decision-makers give face to the decisions that have been made. This is at the heart of Nordic and European regional democracy. There will also be more user democracy. Senior and disability councils, youth councils, the right of initiative and customer boards are regional democracy tools. 5) Cost-effectiveness. It is essential for both taxpayers and public finances that we implement services and administration in a high-quality but cost-effective way. 6) Promoting growth. The public administration itself cannot generate economic growth. However, a smooth and efficient administration can contribute to economic activity throughout the country. Minimisation of red tape, smooth service and licensing processes and interactive behaviour are matters that companies rightly expect from the administration. Mr President! The reform is a wide-ranging package. A year ago, a legislative package on the establishment of regions and the organisation of social and health care was submitted to Parliament at the beginning of March. At that time, the committees were consulted on proposals such as the Regional Law, the Law on Social Welfare, the Regional Finance Act, the Law on the Financing of Municipalities and the Act on the Implementation of the Reform. The reform will significantly reduce the number of administrative dims as more than 400 tasks under the responsibility of the current organisation are transferred to 18 provinces. In addition to the Sote services, the provinces will be responsible for, for example, rescue services, environmental health care, regional development tasks, the promotion of economic activities, the use of regions and regional roads. General government funding in the regions amounts to approximately EUR 18.7 billion. Of this, approximately EUR 17.2 billion is funding based on social work. More than 80% of regional funding is determined by the need for services for residents. Factors determining the need include age structure, morbidity and socio-economic factors. The need factors are based on research by the National Institute for Health and Welfare, THL. The financing for 2020 is based on the average of the social spending expenditure of the municipalities of this year and the following year, i.e. 2019. After a transitional period of four years, a gradual shift from expenditure-based funding to needs-based imputed funding will take place. Mr President! I share the idea that the regional financial framework is tight. The Finance Act has been prepared in such a way that the EUR 3 billion cost reduction set for the reform will be achieved by 2030. It is clear that in the future 18 organisers will be able to make better use of information, technology and good practices among 190 municipal organisers. There is research evidence that the savings in social security are higher than the target of EUR 3 billion if the country as a whole moves towards the level of social welfare productivity at which the best municipalities are already today.

This leads to longer problems, to the wrong location for customers and to an increase in the cost of special care. Freedom of choice is therefore an instrument that seeks, in particular, to strengthen basic services. This will be done by improving access to and quality of services by increasing producers and competition. According to the proposed Act on Freedom of Choice, the customer could choose public, private or third sector service providers from the entire mainland of Finland in the future in different services. In addition, the customer could choose a social worker or a multiprofessional team as far as possible. The social welfare and health centre and the oral care unit could therefore be selected from the whole country and, if they so wish, the producer can be changed every six months. The Sote Centre, the heart of the services, would be like the current health centre, supplemented and reinforced with the expertise of social and specialist professionals, and would provide social care guidance and advice, basic health care services and medical specialist consultation and reception services. In addition, the regional business office would bring social welfare consultations to the social welfare centres and other social services that the province may have decided on. Based on an assessment of the service needs of regional professionals, the customer could be granted a customer voucher or a personal budget for certain services on the basis of which a private service provider could be selected if he so wished. However, there is no obligation to accept a customer voucher or a personal budget, and in that case the province must be responsible for providing the service to the customer in its own or other way. Mr President! Freedom of choice brings a completely new instrument to the organisation of services, the personal budget I have just mentioned. This should be introduced by the province for people with disabilities and older people and, when the province so decides, for other population groups as well. In particular, the personal budget increases the opportunities for users to influence the content of services and choose the provider of services. Today's genuine problems and talks about auction practices related to service providers' competitive bidding will hopefully be history in the future, when the customer will be the one who chooses.
