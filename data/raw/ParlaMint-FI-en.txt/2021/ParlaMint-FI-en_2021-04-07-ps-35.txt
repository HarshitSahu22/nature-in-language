ParlaMint-FI_2021-04-07-ps-35.u2	Mr President! There is therefore a government proposal to amend the Waste Act and certain related laws. The main thrust of the proposal is the recycling targets set by the EU and the desire to move towards a circular economy in which an increasing number of products and materials are reused or redirected to recycling. The recycling targets for municipal waste and packaging waste are rising and Finland will face a major challenge in achieving them. In practice, this means that in Finland the recycling rate of municipal waste must be raised from the current 40% to 65 % by weight by 2035. In order to achieve this objective, new means are proposed, the most important of which are the addition of separate collection of waste and the prohibition of incineration or landfilling of waste collected separately for reuse or recycling. It is estimated that the proposed measures would increase the recycling rate of municipal waste by 5-10 percentage points. The development of the recycling rate will be monitored and, where appropriate, additional measures will be proposed, such as extension of the separate collection and guidance to promote the recycling of separately collected waste. The new obligation limits for separate collection of waste are expected to enter into force gradually from July 2022. According to the proposal, separate collection of biowaste will begin in all properties of at least five apartments in agglomerations by July 22nd. Separate collection of small metal waste and packaging waste would start with the same properties in July 23. Then, in July 24 C.E., the separate collection of biowaste in urban areas with more than 10,000 inhabitants would expand to all properties, including detached houses. Of course, instead of separate collections, biowaste can also be composted. Separate collection of textile waste would start at regional collection points no later than the beginning of 23. The above national limits of obligations are to be adopted by Government decree. The municipality could be extended by means of waste management regulations or, under the conditions laid down by law, temporarily reduce separate collection under the Regulation. This would make the obligations more flexible according to local circumstances. The regulation of producer liability schemes will be increased and further refined. The aim is to reduce free travel and guide the production of products in a more ecological direction. The scope of producer responsibility is also extended to international distance trade. Producers established in non-Finnish countries can discharge their producer liability obligations in Finland through an authorised representative or by joining the producer community. In future, the producer responsibility system will support sustainable product design more strongly. Producer liability payments shall favour the recyclability, repairability, updating and reusability of products. Producers should join the producer community in order to fulfil their producer responsibility obligations. In future, the packaging producer community should cover all packaging materials in its activities. According to the proposal, packaging producers and municipalities should organise separate collection of packaging waste from housing in cooperation. This means that the municipality collects packaging waste from residential properties and disposes of it for waste management organised by producers. Producers compensate municipalities for the costs of collection in order to ensure that producers’ cost liability under the Waste Directive is met. In turn, the municipality reimburses the compensation received from the producers to the residents in respect of the waste levy. Producers continue to organise regional collection points for packaging waste for households and businesses, as well as reception terminals for packaging waste collected by municipalities and other operators. In the case of waste collected separately by the municipality, the dual transport system will be abolished and the transport will be centrally competitive by the municipality. In this way, real estate can be more widely covered by separate collections, transport can be carried out logistically efficiently and the municipality can make full use of collection systems that are best suited to each region, such as block collection or multi-box collection. The current dual system will remain in the transport of mixed waste and precipitate and sealed sludges. This means that the municipality could, under the conditions laid down by law, continue to transfer the transport to the holder of the property. The transitional periods for the transport system in the waste fractions where transport is concentrated to the municipality shall be linked to the entry into force of the separate collection obligations. In the case of competitive transport, the potential of companies of all sizes will be better ensured. The Waste Act requires the distribution of municipal waste transport contracts on a regional and time-scale basis so that small companies can also take part in competitions and receive works. In addition, an implementation project will be launched to guide and advise municipalities in organising competitions for waste shipments and transport entrepreneurs in participating in competitions. The provisions on end-of-waste classification are also being reformed. The aim is to streamline and harmonise a case-by-case decision by the authorities, which would contribute to the development of new products from waste materials. According to the proposal, the end of the classification as waste would be decided in a streamlined environmental permit procedure. The Ministry of the Environment will set up a working group to monitor the implementation of the regulations and assess the need for legislative changes. The bill also promotes the digitisation of the waste sector. For example, monitoring the flow of hazardous waste and some other waste will shift to electronic transfer documents, which will reduce the administrative burden of time. The availability of reporting data, the quality of waste statistics and the waste control and tracing system need to be significantly improved in order to ensure adequate waste management and the progress of recycling. The Ministry of the Environment has started extensive research and development work on the waste information system. Mr President! The Government's proposal changes the daily lives of households and companies in many ways. Waste management services are improving and business opportunities are increasing. More recycling opportunities will be improved by finding the right container for recycled waste in the waste ceiling of your own housing company. More than half of the inhabitants of the country come under separate collections of packaging waste and small metal waste and about 65 per cent of the residents are covered by separate collections of biowaste. Regional collection services are also improving. Among other things, the collection of textile waste will begin no later than 23 C.E. and the number of regional reception points for plastic packaging waste will double. The biggest improvements are seen in smaller properties, less than 10–20 apartments. For larger properties, several municipalities have already organised separate collections of biowaste and packaging waste. The cost of separate collection will increase at national level by around EUR 50 million. In some properties, the expansion of separate collection requires investment in new waste containers, for example, but on the other hand waste costs are reduced by reduced mixed waste. The increase in costs mainly affects businesses in the commercial sector and in the food industry, which, as producers, pay a large part of the waste management of packaging waste, and thus encourage the reduction of packaging waste. In particular, waste companies are the winners of the reform, as the business of waste management is increasing: the increase in separate collection of waste will increase the turnover of the waste sector by between EUR 50 and EUR 80 million. The labour demand of enterprises will increase by around 500 person-years. Mr President! The Government's proposal guides Finland towards a sustainable circular economy and the achievement of the objectives set by the EU. However, it's just a starting shot. The effectiveness and impact of the proposed changes, together with other policy actions, must be monitored systematically.
