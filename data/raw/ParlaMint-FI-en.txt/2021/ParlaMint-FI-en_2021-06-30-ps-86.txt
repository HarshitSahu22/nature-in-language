ParlaMint-FI_2021-06-30-ps-86.u3	Mr President! The Audit Committee began to examine the financial management of the State Audit Office and the activities of the Director General in February. The committee consulted experts and made requests for information from different parties. All in all, the committee has had 64 written expert opinions at its disposal. The report is unanimous and contains two statements. Since the end of last year, the Audit Office has been subject to negative publicity. There have mainly been concerns about the use of the State resources of the Executive Director of the Agency. In the view of the committee, what has happened has undermined confidence in the Audit Office as the supreme external auditor of the state administration. We believe it is important that the activities of the State Audit Office assure citizens that state funds are also used by the Audit Office in accordance with the rules and guidelines, that the operating processes are transparent and that the Agency itself operates, as it requires its inspectors to operate. Our report focused on internal control and risk management and compliance with principles of good governance and relevant provisions. In more detail, we have examined the operation of internal audit and compliance with the travel rules, in particular regarding trips by the Director General of VTV, the use of Finnair customer loyalty points, and travel and expense bills. Mr President! The Director-General of the State Audit Office is responsible for conducting the external audit of the State Administration. In addition, the Director General directs the operating culture of the State Audit Office through his or her own activities. The Audit Office has no authority over the Director-General. In conclusion, the committee notes that the activities of the Director-General have significantly undermined confidence in the proper management of the Treasury's own finances and in the functioning of internal control and have damaged the Agency's image. The committee therefore considers that the Parliamentary Committee should examine whether the Director-General is in a position to continue his duties in accordance with the Parliamentary Civil Service Act, and, if the conditions for dismissal are met, refer the matter to Parliament's plenary session for a decision. Our committee considers it important to take corrective action with regard to the structural shortcomings raised in the report. According to the Constitution, the Audit Office operates within the framework of Parliament and is functionally independent, and the audit committee is responsible for overseeing its financial management as far as the Agency is concerned. The Audit Office itself shall decide on its work plan, on the allocation of its audit activities and on the timing and method of carrying out the audits. The activities shall be managed by the Executive Director, who shall, as a general rule, be responsible for all matters of the Audit Office. The Rules of Procedure, for their part, contain more specific provisions. The supervisory function of the Audit Committee shall not include the operational management of the Audit Office or of any other State agency, institution, ministry or state company. The committee's task is to highlight the shortcomings it has identified in the management of central government finances and to propose to Parliament that the government be required to take the necessary measures to resolve and correct these shortcomings. It is up to the administration itself to take decisions and to make decisions in order to correct the shortcomings, and the committee will follow and assess whether the measures taken are sufficient. I shall now go over the key findings made by the committee, which we wanted to bring to Parliament's attention. First of all, on internal control and risk management: The internal audit of the Audit Office shall be carried out under the authority of the Executive Director. This is in line with the general principles of internal audit in the State administration. The internal auditor shall be an official of the Audit Office who shall carry out the duties as part of other official activities. This means that the Audit Office is not separate from the Directorate-General. The internal audit of the Agency’s travel administration for 2019 focused on the general procedures of the Agency’s travel administration, guidance and the procedures for the approval of travel invoices within the Agency. According to the audit, there were no particular comments in the travel administration procedures of the Agency. In the Committee's view, it should be noted that the committee's own report produced a different result than the internal audit of the Agency's travel administration. The Audit Committee pays serious attention in particular to the lack of information on the Director-General's travel invoices and the lack of receipts. The Central Government Financial and Human Resources Service Centre (Palkeet) provides basic financial and human resources management services for government agencies and institutions as a statutory function. The documentation submitted to the committee by the remuneration shows that several additional requests for clarification have been made to the Office on the invoices of the Director-General of the Audit Office and that the Agency has been repeatedly asked to supplement the information and to provide the missing receipts and explanations. According to the remuneration, the shortcomings identified in VTV’s operations are very exceptional and no similar findings have been observed in the rest of the state administration. The committee draws attention to the fact that, during 2019, the cost invoices made by the Director-General's payment card have been approved by the Audit Office in breach of the recommendations of the Service Centre and the Ministry of Finance. The remuneration is justified by the fact that the expenditure in question does not constitute expenditure justified by the Agency’s operational expenditure, since it cannot be considered as expenditure necessary for the performance of its tasks. The Audit Committee invites the Audit Office to review its guidance and policies in line with the principles of the Budget Act. In practice, the approval of the Executive Director's travel plans, travel and expense invoices is always subject to the Executive Director. This reduces the effectiveness of the Director-General’s control over travel and expense bills, but the situation is similar in other management agencies in the state administration. However, the Audit Office does not have a steering ministry, as is the case with other management agencies. Compliance with the so-called one-over-one principle could ensure good governance. A one-over-one principle refers to the adoption of a decision by the decision-making person's superior. However, there is no one above the top management of the state agencies and institutions to whom this task could be assigned. The committee therefore proposes that Parliament adopt a statement on this issue. In its report, the Audit Committee also highlights the organisational changes carried out by the Audit Office between 2016 and 20. The rules of procedure and the financial rules of the Audit Office have often been amended in these years, which has been likely to obscure responsibilities and undermine the functioning of controls. In 2019, the Agency’s organisational and management model reform fully redefined tasks and responsibilities, and there was uncertainty about the allocation of responsibilities for different tasks during the transition period. The committee considers that the control arrangements and the organisation of tasks from the point of view of internal control and risk management and good governance are very important. The committee stresses that the Audit Office should in future pay particular attention to the fact that the processes and controls of the financial management of the Agency have been properly organised and documented. In its report, the Audit Committee has also dealt with the functioning and development needs of the state's travel administration and official travel. The Government’s travel administration and its guidance are dispersed among several actors and, as a result, guidance is given from several sources. The level of knowledge varies widely between agencies and the Agency needs to be well aware of the decentralised management of travel management in order to find the information it needs on the right hand. The expert consultation showed development needs in the coordination of guidance provided by various stakeholders, the availability of advice and support to the Agency and the promotion of common practices and approaches. The committee calls for the administration of the State to harmonise and clarify guidance on travel, to ensure adequate guidance and advice and to monitor compliance with the guidelines. Mr President! The Director-General of the VTV is responsible for the management of the Audit Office, the Supreme External Audit of the State Administration, and the Director-General is a figurehead of the Agency, giving the Agency a face and directing the Agency's culture through his own activities. In its report, the committee examines the activities of the Director-General in the field of travel, the adequacy of decision-making on recruitment and leave of posts, and also highlights the individual costs which are unclear as regards the payment of the Agency's appropriations, as well as the management of the Agency and the well-being of its staff. Finnair Plus points accumulated from the VTV Director General's missions have not been used in connection with the Director General's missions. The Executive Director has replied that the Finnair Plus account information is personal, is not public information and is not obliged to answer any questions about it. The committee deplores and undermines the transparency and credibility of the Agency's own activities that the Director-General has made his choice based on the fact that he is not obliged to provide information on the use of frequent-customer points earned on the basis of missions.
