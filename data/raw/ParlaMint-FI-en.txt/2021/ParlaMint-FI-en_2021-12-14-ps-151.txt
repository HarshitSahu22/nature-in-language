The confidence of households and businesses in the future has been strengthened and vaccination has progressed rapidly. The introduction of a corona pass has also contributed to increasing trust and opportunities for keeping society open. The draft budget was based on the forecast by the Ministry of Finance that economic growth is expected to remain strong at the end of the year 21 and will continue in 22. GDP growth estimate: 3.3% for this year and 2.9% for next year. The experts consulted by the committee considered the forecasts of the Ministry of Finance to be realistic. Economic growth also boosts employment. The draft budget estimates the employment rate at 73.6% and the unemployment rate at 6.8% in 22. According to Statistics Finland's latest Labour Force Survey, the trend figure for the employment rate was already 72.7 per cent in October this year, i.e. just under one per cent from that target for next year, and the trend figure for the unemployment rate 7.1 per cent. Finland’s public finances have been better able to cope with corona pandemic than the average in other EU countries. However, according to the draft budget, the government debt-to-GDP ratio rose to a level of 10 percentage points higher than before the interest rate crisis, when it was just under 60%. The central government has borne most of the costs of the support measures decided on due to the corona virus situation and is in deficit in the subsectors of the public sector. The deficit of central government is expected to decrease in 22 years with strong economic growth and closing support measures. Local government's financial position turned into surplus in 20 as a result of major support measures, but it is projected to return to deficit this year and next year. In 23 C.E., the structure of general government changes when the responsibility for organising social welfare and rescue services is transferred from municipalities to welfare areas, which eases the spending pressure on municipalities. Overall, the general government deficit will fall sharply this year and next year, as economic recovery and employment growth increase tax revenues and reduce unemployment expenditure. Public finances are also reinforced by a reduction in the spending and need for support related to the corona pandemic. The Committee on Finance welcomes the fact that the government's fiscal policy in the recession caused by the corona crisis was rapid, forward-looking and strongly stimulating. The Government took extensive measures to safeguard jobs and livelihoods and to facilitate the financial situation of companies. The temporary measures taken prevented the long-term decline in the growth potential of the economy. Measures affecting the financial position of national accounts have been taken at the level of total public finances by around EUR 9 billion for the year 20 and around EUR 7 billion for the year 21. For next year, measures will be significantly lower, around EUR 3 billion. The committee notes that the corona crisis is not over yet. It is therefore appropriate not to put an end to a stimulating economic policy too soon, but to speed up economic activity in 22 years, in line with government policies. In addition, provision needs to be made for increased support measures in the event of longer and more difficult interest rate conditions. The Committee on Finance considers the 21 decisions of the spring on the need to replace the frameworks and return to them to be justified, as the economy is still below potential production and therefore has unused capacity. According to the National Audit Office, the fiscal impulse will also be cyclically balanced in 22. At the same time, however, the committee stresses that the spending limits system should continue to be a key rule for national fiscal policy to safeguard a responsible spending policy for central government finances. The committee supports the Government's fiscal policy line, in which, as society opens up, central government expenditure decreases towards the end of the parliamentary term and continues to decline after the end of the parliamentary term. The aim is to break the government debt ratio in the middle of the decade. The Committee on Budgets stresses that the consolidation of public finances and structural reforms are even more important as a result of the pressure on expenditure caused by the ageing population and increased debt. The committee welcomes the fact that the European Commission has launched a debate on the review of the EU's fiscal rules. The aim is to reach an agreement on the way forward well before 23 C.E. The European Fiscal Committee has even considered it harmful to return to the rules without reform at the time of the abolition of the exemption clause. The Government is committed to implementing a road map of economic sustainability, in which key measures to strengthen the sustainability of public finances are defined as increasing employment and reducing unemployment and strengthening the conditions for economic growth. The employment rate is to be raised to 75% in the middle of the decade and the number of people employed will continue to increase by at least 80,000 by government measures by the end of the decade. Economic growth has increased employment and the Committee considers it realistic to achieve the employment rate target set by the government. However, decisive work is needed. It is particularly important that active measures are taken to curb long-term unemployment and to help reduce structural unemployment. Measures to reduce disability and improve well-being at work also need to be stepped up in order to improve the availability of labour. In particular, mental disorders have become more common and are already the main cause of disability pensions. This will reduce employment and productivity and also generate considerable costs for society. The Committee on Finance considers it important that these spendings under the Finnish recovery and recovery plan are implemented in a determined and effective manner. The Committee also considers it important that the emerging markets are actively monitored by the recovery plans of other EU countries and that, with the support of the export promotion network, Finnish companies are able to fully exploit the opening export opportunities. In particular, public funds should support the internationalisation of small and medium-sized enterprises and, for example, new business related to the green transition. There is also a need to further promote joint export projects for companies to respond to the large projects on offer. The Committee also stresses the importance of investment in green transition in order to enable sustainable development. The transition must be implemented in a controlled and fair manner and appropriate compensation must be introduced. The committee also stresses the importance of a strong and broad tax base in the tax field. This can combat aggressive tax planning and tax evasion by international companies as well as harmful tax competition between states. The section presidents of the Committee on Finance will give a more detailed description of the Ministry-specific guidelines for the sections. The Committee on Finance has increased expenditure on the budget by some EUR 40 million, which is aimed, among other things, at reducing the debt to repair transport routes, knowledge, promoting outdoor activities and mobility in nature and at the activities of non-governmental organisations. The Government's draft budget for next year is approximately EUR 65 billion, which is approximately EUR 3.3 billion lower than the budget for 21, including amending budgets. The level of appropriations will be reduced, inter alia, by the fall in expenditure on health security and financial investments in the corona virus situation.
