The key objective of the current reform of social and health care and rescue services is to reduce differences in people's well-being and health, to ensure equal and high-quality social, health and civil protection for all Finns — people living in Finland — and to improve the availability and accessibility of these services. The aim of the reform is also to ensure the availability of skilled labour, to meet the challenges of social change, such as the ageing of the population and the deterioration of the dependency ratio, and to contribute to curb the increase in the cost of public finances. The Committee on Constitutional Affairs has repeatedly stated in its discussion of the social welfare proposals of the previous parliamentary term that there are strong arguments for reforming social and health services based on the fundamental rights system, which advocate the organisation of social and health services and the transfer of responsibility for production to larger actors than individual municipalities. The Committee on Social Affairs and Health notes that the proposed reform can undeniably be regarded as a strong social and fundamental need. The aim of the reform is to promote equality both at regional and individual level, so that everyone can have adequate social and health care services that meet their individual needs on a low threshold. The proposal rightly emphasises equality between different population groups and the objective of promoting genuine equality between people. The objective of strengthening basic services is also central to the narrowing of welfare and health inequalities among the population. In the future, 21 welfare areas, the City of Helsinki and the HUS Group will be responsible for social and health care services and rescue services. Well-being areas are formed mainly on the basis of the current regional division, and their administration is directly based on representative democracy, i.e. they have an elected regional council. Well-being areas form an integrated service structure for more portable actors, bringing together all social and health care and rescue services under the management and budgeting of a single decision-making body, i.e. integrating them with the responsibility of the same body. Thus, the service structure creates incentives for the organiser to provide customer services in a timely and cost-effective manner when the organiser cannot transfer responsibility to another. In the Committee's view, integration will also significantly improve the operational reform of services and the strengthening of basic services, with responsibility for the whole being shared by one operator. Although Uusimaa does not form an integrated service structure in the same way, it also creates more bearable organisers. The committee considers the separate solution of Uusimaa to be justified, taking into account the significantly larger size of the other regions of Uusimaa and the different circumstances from the other regions. In order to ensure regional cooperation and an appropriate service structure in social and health care, five areas of cooperation will be established. Well-being areas have the opportunity to obtain a wide range of services from a private service provider. The committee is in favour of a solution which does not impose certain quantitative or percentage shares of production on its own service production.
