ParlaMint-FI_2021-03-23-ps-27.u2	Mr President! The first draft amending budget for 21 will increase the central government budget by around EUR 394 million and increase the actual revenue by EUR 415 million, thereby temporarily reducing the need for net borrowing by EUR 21 million. In 21 C.E., the government has returned to the spending limits, from which the covid-19 pandemic resulted last year. However, the spending limits level was rightly increased by setting aside EUR 500 million for one-off and fiscally mandatory expenditure on corona virus. The amending budget includes EUR 139 million of these expenditure covered by the provision in question. There has been a positive glimpse of the tax revenue estimates. Increases in earnings tax, inheritance tax, gift tax and revenue tax revenue on the basis of more favourable developments than anticipated. Taking into account the amending budget, the net borrowing is estimated at around EUR 11.7 billion this year and the amount of central government debt at the end of 21 is estimated at around EUR 137 billion, which is 56% of GDP. There is an increase in expenditure on art and culture in the administrative sector of the Ministry of Education and Culture. The committee notes that last year's amending budget allocated EUR 110 million to the arts and culture sectors to reduce the difficulties caused by the interest rate, of which EUR 17.5 million was allocated to freelance artists and other art and cultural professionals, and now proposes an increase of EUR 15 plus EUR 5 million. It is important that additional support is now directed specifically at freelance operators and self-employed people, whose social security is often very poor. The Committee notes that, in addition to the aid already in place and now being proposed, further efforts are needed to ensure that the sector operates and is viable beyond the corona pandemic. The Committee therefore considers it important to develop the support system for the cultural and creative sectors and to provide a comprehensive assessment of the different needs of operators and organisers and to identify the diverse ecosystem of the sector and its typical forms of work. It is also necessary to ensure that aid granted by different administrative sectors constitutes a seamless and comprehensive package, avoiding not only the duplication of aid but also the emergence of different categories of interlocutors. In addition to financial support, there is also a need for a so-called exit strategy on existing restrictions for the safe launch of events. The committee also urges the preparation of a so-called event guarantee for the event and cultural sectors. In order to ensure that the sector is able to operate, it is also necessary to explore the possibilities of improving the social and pension security of artists. In particular, the committee points out the correction of the shortcomings in the settlement of intellectual property rights and unemployment benefit, to which the Committee on Finance and the Committee on Education have already drawn attention in the past. With regard to these two issues, the committee has wanted to emphasise the follow-up, both in terms of the development of this agreed daily allowance and in terms of the transaction guarantee, and this report contains two proposals for statements. First of all, ‘the Commission requires the Government to issue a proposal for an event guarantee In addition,‘the Commission requires the government to become involved in measures to There is a significant increase in the administrative sector of the Ministry of Employment and the Economy, EUR 129.9 million in the allocation and EUR 295 million in the allocation mandate for EU regional and structural policy and other cohesion policy programmes. This relates to REACT-EU funding linked to the full recovery package. The committee supports the rapid mobilisation of funding for REACT-EU, as funding supports the recovery of regions. According to the survey, the REACT-EU Regulation has entered into force in December 20, so funding can be allocated to the regions with a very rapid timetable. Arrangements have been made for specific financing of companies in relation to cost support. The cost subsidy is intended for companies whose turnover has fallen significantly due to the covid-19 pandemic and who have difficult to adapt to pay and other costs. The committee welcomes the improvements proposed by the government in terms of cost support. In the past, the committee has expressed its concern that not all companies have been subject to cost support. It is therefore important that the changes allow cost support to better meet the needs of new companies, small enterprises and sole entrepreneurs and that companies outside the sectors mentioned in the Regulation can apply for discretionary support. The committee draws attention to the fact that the cost support granted to companies in Finland is lower than in other Nordic countries, even if the EU's temporary state aid rules allow them to be supported with more significant amounts. The Committee considers it important for the government to monitor the competitiveness of Finnish companies in relation to the control countries, such as Sweden, in this respect too. The committee points out that exceptional circumstances and closures will make the situation of companies even more difficult in spring 21. The committee supports the government's efforts to pay compensation to companies subject to exclusion measures. An important increase of EUR 17.5 million is the development of a service structure in the social welfare and health care services organised by municipalities, and is particularly targeted at young people. The committee considers this increase to be particularly necessary, as the availability of young people's basic mental health services already preceded the corona epidemic, which has continued to worsen. Epidemiology and distance school have been seen as an increase in polarisation between children and young people who are doing well and less well. Well-being families have been able to support children's and young people's schooling and hobbies better, on average, but families with financial difficulties or problems with coping, mental health or substance abuse, children and young people have become increasingly dependent on social services. The Committee considers it important that, in order to ensure that the appropriations are effective, additional resources are allocated to the very group of young people whose needs appear to be the greatest in the region. In order to ensure timely assistance, the Committee stresses the multi-professional support of the low threshold and close cooperation between schools, child protection, youth services, social and health services, the police and organisations.
