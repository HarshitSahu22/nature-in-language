This also brings interest to the normal retail investor. The share savings account is national capitalism at its best, and it enables more and more ordinary Finns to prosper. Madam Speaker! The share savings account has been in use in Finland since 2020. The account has gained great popularity among Finns. Over the past 18 months, nearly 200,000 accounts have been opened. The value of the investments made has risen to around EUR 1 billion. The possibility of a stock savings account has strengthened domestic ownership. With the account, the number of Finnish shareholders has increased by over 100 thousand persons, so that the number of shareholders in Finland is approaching the threshold of one million. The account has proved particularly popular among young people. Nearly 40 per cent of the openings are under the age of 30. The account has become an instrument of long-term, even decades-long saving, by which younger generations want to save their retirements or other dreams of the future. Madam Speaker! However, the right of Finns to use a share savings account is unnecessarily restricted. A maximum limit has been set by law for the account and only one account per investor has been allowed to be opened. Violation of restrictions also leads to sanctions. A maximum deposit limit of EUR 50000 has been set for the account. A penalty shall be imposed for crossing the border. Moreover, this ceiling is limited to only one investor. Opening more than one account leads to violent verosances. For example, if the investor has two equity savings accounts opened for a year, he will be sanctioned by a tax increase of EUR 7300. The restrictions on the equity savings account are not only absurd but also place the equity savings account at a disadvantage vis-à-vis other investment products without similar restrictions. Let me take an example: A wealthy investor can invest unlimited funds through capitalisation agreements, for example, and open as many contracts as it may seem necessary, while an artificial ceiling of this kind has been set on the equity savings account and only one per investor can be opened. In addition, a breach of the restrictions results in severe veracities which are not directed at other investment products. The ceiling is problematic, as the equity savings account is a tool for many, especially young people, investors for long-term savings and investments. Why in the world should people's opportunities for saving and wealth be limited? Mr President! The legislative initiative I have tabled proposes the lifting of these restrictions. The ceiling on the equity savings account of EUR 50000 will be removed, while the limit on the number of accounts will also remain in history. The penalty payments for oversaved shares are also eliminated. The Coalition Party believes that the wealth of Finns should be made possible. The share savings account enables small investments for all Finns who are able to put even a small amount of their income into saving. The interest-to-interest phenomenon creates wealth and saving opportunities for ordinary investors. Saving shares also strengthens domestic ownership. Over EUR 100 billion lie in Finnish bank accounts with almost zero interest rates. Why not use those deposits to boost domestic capital markets? Promoting investment is not only about increasing people's wealth but also about a big boost to the Helsinki Stock Exchange and Finnish, domestic ownership. 40 MPs have signed this bill to dismantle the restrictions on the equity savings account. I would like to thank my fellow Members for their firm support for the bill and I hope that the government will take up these proposals and bring a truly ordinary small investor on the same line as the wealthier investors already have.
