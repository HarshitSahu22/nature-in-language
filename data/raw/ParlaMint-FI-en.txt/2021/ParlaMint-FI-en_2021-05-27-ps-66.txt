The snow goes into the sea with poisons, garbage, plastic — plastic that breaks down into microplastics and never leaves the sea. Various measures have been taken to stop this snowfall. Parliament's Water Protection Group has appealed to the Ministry of the Environment and asked that this be brought down by law, but the Ministry of the Environment concluded that, according to their knowledge, this is only a problem in Helsinki, and decided to follow the matter up, waiting for Helsinki to do something about it. Well, in Helsinki the council has decided to find ways to stop this snowfall, but the means have not yet been found. I myself think that these are the means to be found — if there is a will, then there is a way — and that is why I, for my part, have taken this initiative. In fact, we cannot be sure whether snow will fall somewhere else in Finland and what will then be done in the future, and I think it is absolutely essential that this bill be taken forward and that these decisions be made. And just in the background, I would like to say that in all other Nordic countries, the corresponding legislative changes have been made in one way or another. This has been done in Copenhagen and Oslo. Sweden has also banned the pouring of snow into the sea and lakes, which is also intended to prohibit the pouring of snow into other waters. And I say that this excludes the fact that the free-time residence is being hit with pure snow, which means that this presentation is sensibly formulated. Mr President! I think it is important that Parliament should make every effort to ensure that we keep our seas and the Baltic Sea clean. This is one step.
