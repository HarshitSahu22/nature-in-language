ParlaMint-FI_2021-09-29-ps-106.u5	Mr President! The value base of Finland's foreign and security policy is the promotion of human rights, the rule of law, democracy, peace, freedom, tolerance and equality in all our international activities. In order to promote these values, Finland aims to become a member of the UN Human Rights Council for the period 22-24. The aim of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs' activities is to strengthen Finland's international position and the safety and well-being of Finland and Finns. The Paris Climate Agreement and Agenda 2030, which defines the Sustainable Development Goals, provide the basis for international cooperation in the coming years and for Finland's action. Global challenges, such as climate change, pandemics and technological change, affect our operating environment as independent phenomena. The relationship between the major powers has changed rapidly, significantly hampering cooperation in the rule-based international system and also contributing to the security of Finland and Europe's neighbouring regions. International cooperation structures, including the UN system, are suffering from different conflicts. It is increasingly difficult to defend and develop the principles of a rule-based international system and international law in line with new needs. As a result of the pandemic, inequalities have increased and fragile countries are becoming more fragile. The European Union is the main reference framework and channel for Finland's external relations and the security community. The rise of China as a global player has changed the dynamics of power. It is centred on the competition between the United States and China for global leadership. The situation in Afghanistan and its repercussions will remain on the agenda of the international community for a long time. Africa's importance as a neighbour and strategic partner of the EU is increasing. Developing countries are an important part of the international solution to achieve sustainable development. Russia is seeking a more important role, which is also reflected in the security situation in the Baltic Sea. The geopolitical, environmental and economic importance of the Arctic region is increasing. Finland will chair the Barents Euro-Arctic Council for the period 21-23. The Nordic countries will remain the closest partners in Finland. Comprehensive Nordic cooperation is therefore increasingly important in an unstable international environment. Finland will chair the Nordic Council next year. Mr President! EUR 263 million is proposed for the actual operating expenditure of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The importance of the network for the safety and well-being of Finland and Finns is beyond doubt. The operation of the representative network will continue to be strengthened next year. The aim is to open two new delegations. This year, representations will be opened to Senegal's Dakar and Qatar's Doha. The locations of delegations to be opened in 22 years will be decided at a later stage. As part of the Government's efforts to ensure the availability of skilled labour, the number of staff dealing with residence permits will be increased to speed up the permit processes. In addition, the introduction of a national long-term visa, the so-called D visa, will be promoted and a digital Virtual Finland service platform will be created to enable integrated service paths for companies, employees and students coming to Finland. The introduction of the D visa would speed up and streamline the entry process for specialist experts and start-ups, thus enhancing work-related migration. The proposal is important because it makes it easier for work-related immigration to take place. At the same time, there will also be a basis for extending the national D visa to researchers and students after the necessary studies. Mr President! Conflict prevention, mediation and building are Finland’s foreign policy’s stronger priorities. Finland supports the peace mediation dialogue processes, promotes the resolution of ongoing conflicts and strengthens mediation activities and mediation capacities both nationally and internationally. Finland will also continue to actively participate in international crisis management. Finland’s priorities for international crisis management missions are in Lebanon and Iraq, but Finland also participates in operations in Kosovo, Africa and the Mediterranean region. The increase in the involvement of civilian personnel in crisis management will continue and EUR 18.5 million will be proposed for civilian crisis management in this budget. It is estimated that personnel employed in military crisis management will be up to 400 person-years in 22 years and that approximately 135 experts will be employed in civilian crisis management in 22 years. - Thank you.
