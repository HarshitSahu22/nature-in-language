ParlaMint-FI_2021-11-09-ps-130.u2	Madam President! Ensuring security of electricity supply at all times is an important part of energy policy. Since 2007, Finland has had a power reserve to ensure a balance between electricity generation and consumption. The purpose of the power reserve is to ensure the availability of electricity, especially during the winter season, in situations where electricity consumption is high and market-based supply of electricity is insufficient to meet demand. Generally speaking, we have reliable and stable access to electricity. However, we must also be prepared for electricity shortages and it should be possible to acquire power reserves in the future as necessary. The 2016 energy and climate strategy outlined that the power reserve system will be maintained and developed more flexibly. This is also the starting point for the preparation of a new climate and energy strategy. Changes to the Power Reserve Act are now being proposed as a result of EU regulation. The Regulation of the European Parliament and of the Council on the internal electricity market entered into force in summer 2019. The new regulation contains provisions on capacity mechanisms such as the ‘strategic reserve’. The proposed amendments would bring national legislation into line with the EU Regulation. In accordance with the Regulation, the power reserve system shall be notified to the European Commission as State aid. Current power reserve agreements will remain in force until the end of June 22, and the contracts for the next power reserve period should be concluded under the amended law. Mr President! The proposal proposes to amend the Power Reserve Act, inter alia, to allow for the approval of power reserves not only for electricity generation and energy flexibility but also for energy reserves. Emission limits under the EU Electricity Market Regulation would also apply to power reserve units. The Energy Agency would continue to assess the amount of power reserves required, but the evaluation would be based on the European method. The Government’s decision would set the target level of security of electricity supply, the so-called ‘reliability standard’. The Agency would continue to be responsible for tendering the power reserve. The Act is intended to enter into force on 1 January 2022 so that the power reserve can be acquired during the winter period 22-23. The acquisition of the power reserve may take place once the European Commission has given its approval to the power reserve system as State aid. The implementation of the law will have an impact on the Commission’s assessment of State aid and it is therefore necessary to put the law into force even before the Commission’s decision on the admissibility of State aid. By doing so, we want to secure the power reserve without interruption.

The promotion of more effective advice and guidance is to be welcomed, but it is questionable whether this would be the way it is planned. An operator who has not been subject to an inspection would normally not benefit from guidance and advice related to the inspection. Furthermore, there are no changes in the proposal to implement the preventive advice and guidance proposed as the basis for the introduction of the fee. Although the operator’s own investment and self-control may reduce the fees generated by the controls, it would not have a negative impact on the basic fee for the controls. A particularly questionable proposal is to increase the cost burden on the tourism sector at the moment when the corona virus crisis has brought down the viability of the sector. In 2020 the gross value added in the accommodation and nutrition sectors fell by over one third and many accommodation companies lost more than half of their turnover. If, at the time of the introduction of the basic levy, companies had been given the possibility to reduce the burden of payment for accrual-based payments through their own activities, the basic charge would at least have been justified in some way, but it would have been reasonable to postpone the introduction of the charge for example by one year at the beginning of 2023 due to losses incurred by companies in the form of interest rate restrictions. In other words, on the basis of this, we are going to propose that the bill be rejected, and that is what we did in the Committee on Social Affairs and Health. — — — — Thank you.

ParlaMint-FI_2021-11-09-ps-130.u146	Thank you, Mr President. This proposal proposes to amend the Working Time Act. A national contract of employment and contract of employment would provide for regular working time and daily rest in accordance with the general rule of the Act, which is only carried out from time to time during the daily working time during which the worker is required to be ready for work. The purpose of the proposal is to restore the legal status of the earlier Working Time Act by allowing for a derogation from the restrictions on daily and weekly working hours provided for in Article 5 of the Act and thus to bring the law into line with the content of the earlier Working Time Act. In addition, it is proposed that the employer or his representative, who deliberately or negligently violates the limits laid down for the commission of emergency work, should be punished for the infringement of working time. A technical correction would be made to the exemption section. On the basis of the report it has received, the Committee on Labour and Equal Opportunities considers the proposal necessary and appropriate and advocates the adoption of a legislative proposal without amendment, with a few comments. The Committee on Employment and Equal Opportunities welcomes the extension of the scope for agreement between the parties. According to a report received by the committee, the amendment was launched on a proposal from the employee side. When only part of the working time is worked, it may be appropriate to arrange shifts so that the weekly working time can be over 40 hours for persons to return from work and work. The committee notes that it is appropriate to promote the arrangements which have been found to be good in this area, while pointing out that the amendment of the law does not automatically change anything between the parties, but merely allows the parties to agree. The Committee on Employment and Equal Opportunities notes that the proposed legislative amendment does not conflict with the Working Time Directive. Furthermore, the proposal does not alter Article 34(2)(6) of the Working Time Act. Furthermore, the proposal adds to the penalty provision the violation of the emergency labour provision. The Committee on Employment and Equal Opportunities considers this increase to be justified and notes that the increase will safeguard health and safety at work and prevent the abuse of emergency work. The Committee on Employment and Equal Opportunities also notes the importance of providing sufficient information and guidance on legislative changes in order to avoid any misconceptions about its application.
