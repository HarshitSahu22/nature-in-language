According to the spring forecast by the Ministry of Finance, GDP will increase by 2.6 per cent this year, by two and a half per cent next year and by one and a half per cent in 23. Private consumption is expected to grow rapidly as savings erupt into consumption, and exports and investments will also recover with the growth prospects of the global economy. This has become very positive examples in recent weeks. According to the forecast published by the Bank of Finland on 15 June, growth would be substantially faster than the above-mentioned estimate. The Bank of Finland estimates that the economy will grow by almost 3 per cent this year and 3 per cent next year, but would slow down to 1.3 per cent in 23 years due to an ageing population and low productivity growth. In the latest forecast by the Ministry of Finance, growth is also estimated to be faster than previously estimated. International European examples show that we would still have opportunities for this kind of three-four-percent increase for several years in a row, provided that we take care of the conditions for growth, and that would be a way to solve the problem of the government deficit. The Committee considers it justified that the State has borne most of the costs of the support measures decided on by virtue of the corona virus situation and has supported municipalities and social security funds to alleviate the damage caused by the epidemic. In 20 years, central government was the most in deficit of the subsectors of public finances. The financial position of local government, on the other hand, was largely in surplus as a result of support measures. For the first time, the budgetary plan also includes the areas of well-being in preparation, which will start operating in 23 years due to large investments with a slight deficit. On the basis of the latest statistics and forecasts, the impact of the corona pandemic on public finances is positive. Finland’s public finances have also survived the crisis better than other EU countries’ economies on average. The Committee on Finance welcomes the government's active and responsible fiscal policy during the Corona pandemic, which is geared to the cyclical situation. Fiscal policy has been a strong stimulus in the recession when the government has decided on extensive measures to safeguard jobs and livelihoods and to facilitate the financial situation of companies in difficulties arising from the corona virus situation. A total of EUR 900 million will be allocated to expenditure increases made for the spending limits period, with a focus on next year and next year. The committee considers it justified that the recovery should not be stopped immediately after economic growth has started, as according to the Ministry of Finance's forecast, production will remain well below potential output over the next few years. This means that there is no immediate prospect of overheating of the economy and that there is still unused capacity. In addition, investments in health, well-being and knowledge are generally both well-being investments that increase overall well-being and financial investments that also increase the number of productive labour in the long term and reduce the need for corrective services that bind society's resources. At the same time, however, the Committee considers it important that the system of spending limits for central government finances should be maintained as a key national fiscal policy rule ensuring a responsible, long-term and economic stability-friendly spending policy for central government finances. However, in order to ensure its credibility, it is important that the system is reformed and that the mechanisms for derogating from the spending limits procedure are agreed in advance. The Committee also notes that the EU's fiscal regulation will continue to be subject to the special clause of the Stability and Growth Pact, introduced in spring 20, which will allow for a deviation from the budgetary targets at least until next year. The Committee on Finance stresses the need to resolve the reform of the Stability and Growth Pact and other EU fiscal rules and to ensure compliance with the acute interest rate crisis. The Government has updated the Government Programme's economic policy guidelines in the wake of the corona crisis and has set the target of refraction of the government debt ratio in the mid-20s. According to the Ministry of Finance, the debt ratio will decline if and when the general government balance improves net by EUR 2 to EUR 2.5 billion.
