ParlaMint-FI_2019-11-19-ps-66.u283	Mr President! Industriousness must always be rewarded. Whether it is taxation or social security, the system should encourage work and enterprise in every situation. Unfortunately, this is not the case today. Kela will recover the student allowance from 40,000 students again this year because of too low income limits. I mean, because these students have been working too hard. This is depressing. This is insane. A hard-working student must not be discouraged; he must be rewarded. The current limits to the free income of the Finnish student support system, i.e. the limits to how much money you can make while working together with your studies and study grants, make it difficult to obtain additional student income and increase your own skills during your studies. The current income limits create artificial scarcity and difficulties in living. For these reasons, my bill proposes a 50% increase in the income limits for students' free income and in the income overrun for recovery. Increasing the income limits of student support is supported by research evidence. According to a survey by the Finnish Labour Institute, the current income thresholds can be significantly increased. According to the report, current income limits lead to relatively high welfare losses. They reduce the well-being of students relatively much. At the same time, the state loses millions of euros in net income because students restrict the work. When hard work is discouraged, everyone loses. Reasons therefore require a significant increase in the free income limit for student aid. Indeed, the bill has collected extensive support across the parliamentary group boundaries. Thank you for that. The initiative has been signed by 64 MPs from the parliamentary groups of the National Coalition Party, the Basic Finns, Christian Democrats, the Centre Party and Social Democrats. The only representative of the Greens and the Left did not sign a bill, which I am surprised about. My bill's proposal to increase the income limits for student aid by 50% is part of the alternative budget for the Coalition Party, which was published today. The Coalition Party wants to improve the student's well-being. We stand up for a hard-working student. Mr President! After all, the prerequisite for obtaining student support is sufficient progress in the studies. Many students can easily reconcile studies and work. The limits on the free income of student support have been substantially increased more than ten years ago, in 2008. In this case, the increase was 30 %. Today, the amount of unpaid earnings may be EUR 667 per month of study grant and EUR 1990 per month of study grant. If the annual income limit resulting from this is exceeded, the student allowance shall be recovered unless the excess of the free income has been no more than EUR 222. The student must ensure that the annual income does not exceed the amount of free income. For each of the EUR 1323 starting today, a one-month student grant and housing allowance have been recovered. Recovery is therefore carried out even if the student has made sufficient progress in the studies. We who have worked alongside studies know that student work is often seasonal and part-time. It is often difficult to define an income threshold in advance. This will lead to massive recovery, as it did this year. According to Statistics Finland, more than half of students in Finland work alongside studies. In addition to student support, work therefore plays an important role in securing the student’s livelihood. In addition, the accumulated work experience plays a crucial role in employment at the end of studies and in the transition to working life in many areas. The comparison of the income limits of the Nordic study grants contained in the report of the Finnish School of Education and Culture commissioned by the Ministry of Education and Culture during the last parliamentary term shows that, for example, in Norway and Denmark, income limits are significantly higher than in Finland. In addition, Finland is the only Nordic country in which the student allowance must be fully reimbursed when the income limit is exceeded. In other countries, income tax is higher than income below the income tax threshold, i.e. the marginal tax rate. Based on the calculations presented in the report, the current income limits could be significantly increased. For example, raising the current annual income ceiling of around EUR 12,000 for nine months of student aid to EUR 18000 would lead to a sharp reduction in welfare losses, as few would earn more than this income threshold even if there were no income limits. According to the calculations presented, the average income of students would then increase by around EUR 600 per year and the net income received by the state, i.e. tax revenue less current transfers, would increase by around EUR 6 million per year, i.e. everyone would win. The use of mentality requires a significant increase in the free income limit of the student allowance. Therefore, in my bill of law, I propose that the new limits on free income should be EUR 1001 per month for each student receiving study grant or housing allowance and EUR 2985 per month for each month for which he has not received study grant or housing allowance. In the context of raising the limits of free income, it is appropriate to raise the corresponding amounts of recoveries, as was done in the 2008 reform. Consequently, the limits for overshooting of free income would also be increased by 50%. Mr President! My bill on the income limits of student support is also timely because before the elections, government parties except the centre promised substantial increases in student funding of more than EUR 80 to EUR 100. How much more financial room is the student now getting from the government? EUR 2.50. Can it really be that more than EUR 80 increase promises only amounted to EUR 2.50? That money buys you five bags of noodles. What needs to be done for the student's well-being is to increase the income limits of student support and thus enable the student to improve his/her well-being through his/her own work and to increase his/her skills in working life. As I said, work must be worthwhile and hard work must be rewarded. I am very pleased that the matter of a hardworking student has received strong support from representatives of the people. I hope that my legislative initiative will also be supported here in the Chamber. Should not the objective of improving the well-being of students be shared?
