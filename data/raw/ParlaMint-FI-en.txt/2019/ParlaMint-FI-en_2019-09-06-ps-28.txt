The campaign ceiling I propose would be EUR 7500 in municipal elections, EUR 30000 in Parliamentary elections and EUR 50000 in European Parliament elections. In addition, the initiative proposes to make the prior notification of electoral funding mandatory from a voluntary point of view. When I campaigned in parliamentary elections in central Helsinki, a French tourist group stopped at me. They were curious about the election, the campaign, and me. Then they asked: “Are you really a wonderful person? “ I told them about my background, and then they were surprised that in France it could never be possible to do that. The amazement was amused, but something essential about the polarisation of democracy was summed up in it. Today, the world has more of a rule than an exception that those who stand in elections — and especially those elected — are trained by elite schools, well-resourced, and serve the interests of well-off interest groups. So far here in the Finnish Parliament, the people are represented equally by employees, nurses, teachers, students and policemen, as are researchers, lawyers, doctors and business leaders. We have people who are struggling with a great variety of backgrounds who may have been nominated and become MPs. We should be proud of this. Democracy and democratic representativeness must continue to be defended. Parliament should represent the people as a whole. However, studies in Finland also show that the parliamentary system has been unequaled and democratic participation opportunities have been polarised according to socio-economic background. Candidates are in a better position than the people when it comes to working life status and income levels. [Fresh—The President-in-Office]
