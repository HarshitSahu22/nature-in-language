This schedule allows final emissions data to be reported from two years ago and so-called short-term emissions data from the previous year. This annual climate report is therefore based on the emissions data for 2017 and 2018 and on the assessment of the emission reduction targets and means contained in the medium-term climate policy plan, KAISU. The first KAISU was completed last season, in 2017, and was approved in Parliament in March 2018. This Annual Report does not therefore include the new objectives and means of the Government Programme. The purpose of the report is to assess developments in relation to the objectives of the medium-term climate policy plan currently in force, which was adopted in 2018. We also know that the EU's climate objectives need to be tightened so that global warming can be halted to 1.5°C. Finland and the EU's climate policy consists of three different sectors: emissions trading, burden sharing and land use. The emissions trading sector includes industrial and energy production plants and intra-EU air transport. The burden-sharing sector includes other emissions, such as transport and agriculture. The land use sector includes emissions and sinks from forests, agricultural land and wetlands. The scope of the current climate law is limited to the burden-sharing sector. This means that the Climate Year Report also does not contain data on emissions trading or land use sectors. The climate report deals with emissions from the burden-sharing sector, i.e. transport, agriculture, separate heating, machinery, waste treatment and F-gases. The Government has stated that, in connection with the update of the Climate Act, the land use sector will also be included in the Climate Act and reporting. This is important if we are to have as comprehensive an idea as possible of our emissions in the future. Emissions trading emissions are jointly controlled by EU countries through EU decision-making. In addition to the overall emissions performance of the burden-sharing sector, the report also examines the sector-specific emissions development within it. Transport is central to the emissions development of the entire burden-sharing sector. Every two years, the annual climate report reports on the implementation of policy actions. The evaluation will also cover cross-cutting actions in KAISU concerning consumption, climate action in municipalities and regions and public procurement. Furthermore, at least once in the parliamentary term, the adequacy of the adaptation plan's actions will be reported. This first report examines all of these. The climate report currently under discussion estimates that current measures are likely to achieve the emission reduction target for the period 2013-2020, building on the time flexibility contained in the Effort Sharing Regulation, which allows inter-year transfers of units. In principle, the planned actions under KAISU will also be sufficient for the objectives of the next period 2021 to 2030. The current targets for the burden-sharing sector are based on EU legislation that requires Finland to reduce emissions by 16% by 2020 and 39% by 2030 compared to 2005. However, the report states that the implementation of climate policy needs to be closely monitored and an ongoing assessment of the need for further action. For example, the development of emissions in the transport sector in recent years seems to indicate the need for further effective action in the sector to achieve the 2030 target. The annual climate report contains a more detailed analysis of policy actions. Adaptation to climate change and related activities are also presented in the Annual Report this time. This is important because the analysis shows that climate risk management is partly inadequate. Given the high risks of climate change, a coherent climate policy needs to be strengthened in order to curb climate change, i.e. to reduce emissions while strengthening the climate resilience of society. Overall, the first annual climate report shows that we have made progress on the targets set. It's about how you can find ways when you have the will. At the same time, however, it must be borne in mind that the current emission reduction targets are not sufficient to halt global warming to 1.5°C. Therefore, the new government has in its programme stated that the targets will be tightened and Finland must be carbon neutral as early as 2035. In the future, the land use sector must also be more closely involved in monitoring climate action. The new objectives of the Government Programme will be included in the updated KAISU plan in due course and will be evaluated in future annual reports. However, on the basis of the present annual report, it is already easy to say that the current measures will not be sufficient when the objectives are tightened up.
