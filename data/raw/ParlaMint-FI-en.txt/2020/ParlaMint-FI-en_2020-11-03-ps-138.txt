The aim of the proposal is to raise the level of education and competence and to ensure that every young person has access to a secondary degree. The employment rate for those who have completed comprehensive school education alone is about 45%, while for those who have already completed secondary education, the employment rate rises to more than 70%. The completion of secondary education thus contributes significantly to employment and participation in society. The Government's proposal now under discussion extends compulsory education for the first time since comprehensive school reform and extends it to secondary level. The reform is due to enter into force in 2021, i.e. exactly 100 years after compulsory education was created in Finland. Compulsory education is a message. With it, we will tell our society's young people what kind of knowledge today and tomorrow's society is enough. At the same time, we place a strong obligation on society to take care of every young person's educational path even after secondary school. For decades we have known that basic education alone is not enough, but we have tried to tackle the problem with limited means. Despite the efforts made by previous governments, which are worthy in themselves, but point-blank, around 16% of the age group is still without a secondary degree year after year. This is not a sustainable situation for Finland’s competence level or employment development. Point-by-point actions have not been bitten well enough and it is therefore high time that compulsory education was updated to meet the requirements of the 2020s. A system-level reform is needed, with a strong focus on governance and a clear definition of responsibilities. On the extension of compulsory education, we have not only the recommendations of the OECD and the Economic Policy Review Council, but also research evidence at national and international level. Over the decades, dozens of pilot projects have been carried out. The aim is to support each person's transition to a secondary level and the completion of a secondary degree. After decades, we have no evidence of targeted means of replacing the extension of compulsory education. It is clear that action is needed for the quality and equality of, for example, early childhood education and basic education, and that this will also be done: EUR 350 million will be allocated to the development of the quality and equality of early childhood education and basic education; a model of support for early childhood education will be created; group sizes will be reduced; and the right to full-time early childhood education will be restored. But these are not alternatives to extending compulsory education; they are needed alongside its reform. Parliament now has the opportunity to carry out a historic structural reform based on research evidence. As a result of the reform, a compulsory student would be obliged to apply for secondary education after the completion of basic education. New steering and supervision responsibilities are proposed for the organisers of education and training, as well as for the household of the person responsible for education and training, in order to ensure as smooth a transition to secondary education as possible. In the context of the compulsory education reform, secondary education would be made free of charge until the end of the year in which the young person turns 20. In addition, in the context of the extension of compulsory education, joint training would be reformed, which, if necessary, will serve as a bridge between basic and secondary education. We do not say to 16-year-olds that the base is too weak and the motivation is too lost — that nothing can be done anymore.
