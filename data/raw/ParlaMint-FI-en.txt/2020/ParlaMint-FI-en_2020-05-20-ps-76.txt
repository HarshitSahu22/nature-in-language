The corresponding government proposal has now been discussed and the report has been finalised by the Committee on Economic and Monetary Affairs and Industrial Policy. The idea here is to support restaurants on the basis of two principles. On the one hand, there is such a general compensation for the fact that, as a result of government measures, the sale of restaurants has stopped almost entirely — not entirely, but has fallen significantly. And then the second part of this package of aid that this legislation creates is employment aid, which is already looking forward, at a time when these restrictions are being relaxed. Its idea, on the other hand, is to support restaurants in their employment decisions so that it would be more profitable for restaurants to take workers back to work, and thus to ensure the well-being of both industry and workers. This first package has thought to compensate for the fixed costs which it has not been possible for restaurants to get rid of immediately, and then the second is this employment aid. In relation to the government's proposal, the committee has mainly introduced three types of change which have been agreed upon. On the one hand, a more favourable comparison has been established with regard to this compensation for restaurants. Of course, when you compensate for the loss of sales that restaurants have been targeted for, you have to compare to a time of comparison with a normal time, so that you know how much loss is compensated for. In this original proposal, the reference period was in January-February of the current year, but this was largely thought of and confirmed in the consultation that this is a rather limited starting point. Some restaurants may have a rather low sales rate in January-February. The January-February period is also marked by January, which is familiar to many Finns. Well, we tried to solve this problem and ended up with an alternative reference month in the corresponding months of the previous year, April-May, and the authority that pays this compensation subsidy compares the situation of this spring's lost sales to the sales of the year before and then January-February, and chooses the benchmark that will produce a more favourable result for the restaurant. Here we are talking about subsidies that are paid mechanically without even having to be applied for by restaurants. The second change that has been made here is that the aid received from other sources, in particular from Business Finland, has been modified. The original proposal contained a mechanical cutter in that, if the restaurant company has received support from Business Finland, it will be deducted from this compensatory aid as such. We have assessed this issue and concluded that Business Finland's subsidies are mainly intended for purposes other than compensation and profitability, they are innovation support, although they may have been granted on a broader basis in the early stages of this crisis. Therefore, this automatic cutter has now been removed in this presentation, which means that the restaurant can receive this compensation even though Business Finland's support has previously been received. However, it remains for the Authority, Business Finland, to ensure that there is no overlap in its final payments, but both innovation support and compensation support can be obtained. The third issue which was discussed and which was also examined intensively was the fact that the government's original proposal did not include this employment aid at all in the form of restaurant work carried out through personnel service companies, i.e. so-called temporary employment. In some parts of the country, however, this is a rather important form of work for some restaurants. These personnel service companies act as a kind of broker who reconcile the efforts of employees to make the weeks they want and the efforts of employers, i.e. restaurants, to find the right number of workers, especially when the demand for restaurant services is seasonal or otherwise very variable. This is an important phenomenon in the restaurant sector – for example, in Northern Finland among tourism companies in Lapland – that it was very natural to seek to extend access to employment support to such temporary work, also through HR companies. And this has been a worthy piece of work done by members of the government parties with officials in the committee, and it has finally been possible to develop a model for calculating employment subsidies similar to those for those in their own service and, unanimously, it was included in our report. Mr President! Such changes to this government proposal have taken place.
