In accordance with the vision of the Government Programme, Finland must be reformed without prejudice. State ownership must become a tool for active social renewal. Madam President! The draft law proposes to amend the law on state ownership and corporate governance. This will change the ownership steering structure of the state, which will only have indirect effects on the economy. Long-term value increases in Finnish-owned listed companies will continue to take place in Solidium. The Ownership Steering Unit of the Prime Minister's Office is responsible for managing companies with social and business objectives. The task of the state development company Vake is to put the balance sheets to work in order to create new growth. This law would allow the transfer of shares currently directly owned by the State to the State Development Company. This would happen without the Government giving up its decision-making powers significantly. The role of the State Development Company would be to provide a tool for the management of state ownership by which the State could generate and develop new business activities, strengthen the capital structure of the companies it owns and effectively implement different business arrangements. The purpose of the State Development Company would be to provide state-owned companies with a natural development path for the renewal and development of the ownership structure of the companies. The new provisions would give the State more flexibility to carry out different business arrangements and to increase investment. This would allow the development of new business to take place more quickly. It is above all a question of developing companies. I would stress that the transfer to a development company is by no means a sales decision. If a company or part of it is abandoned, it always requires its own careful consideration. For me, putting a balance sheet to work has never meant putting state companies into meat. The development company would be outside the government budget. This would allow decoupled capital to be used to create new business, to strengthen the capital structure of the companies owned by the development company and to implement the business arrangements effectively. The State Development Company could allocate more capital to the companies it owns more effectively than the direct ownership of the State. The Government would have strategic decision-making in the ownership and development of the development company’s corporate assets. Decisions on the ownership of the development company would be taken by the Minister of Ownership and the General Assembly of the Government. Similarly, they would determine the principles and powers for the decisions of the development company to increase, reduce, change or enter into new business activities. New investments by the development company in existing or start-ups will therefore be decided at the Government General Assembly. The development company’s operations would make it easier to form different project-specific organisations and thus create good conditions for success in projects. The development company could also finance its own financing needs and the companies it owns with debt money. The shares of the nine companies planned to be transferred to the State Development Company in the first phase represent different, different sizes and state-owned companies operating in different industries. Ekokem Corporation's shares were transferred to the State Development Company Vake Oy at the end of August this year, after which these shares were sold on previously agreed commercial terms to Fortum Corporation. The transfers of Neste Corporation and Vapo Oy will be made once this Act has been adopted. Transfers of shares to the State Development Company should be carried out as equity, so to speak. Transfers, i.e. changes in the company's share ownership, do not as such have a direct financial impact on the companies that are the object of the transfer. The fair value of the planned shares to be transferred to the development company is estimated at approximately EUR 2400 million. It is therefore a matter of considerable assets. In the future, the government budget would decide on dividends and sales revenue to be included in the central government budget. The proposal also includes a new ownership limit requiring the consent of Parliament. The State has significant corporate assets in companies with a strategic interest defined. Today, this interest is realised either by a full 100% or 50% majority ownership. The new limit is based on the provisions of the law on minority protection of limited liability companies, which would mean a limited minority holding where the State would have at least 33.4% of the voting rights generated by all shares. The limits of ownership and their changes will continue to be decided by Parliament. If necessary, the State could, on the basis of the law on limited liability companies, alone, secure its status and will with respect to all significant decisions relating to limited liability companies, such as amendments to the articles of association, the issue of shares or other similar arrangements for changes in shareholdings, and the merger, division and liquidation of the company. In practice, more than one third of the ownership will be able to secure sufficient control criteria under the Companies Act. Madam President! Ladies and gentlemen! This is how state ownership policy changes. It will help boost the growth and work that has started in Finland.
