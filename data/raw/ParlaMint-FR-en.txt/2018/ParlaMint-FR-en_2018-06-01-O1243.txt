ParlaMint-FR_2018-06-01-O1243.u558	This amendment is quite different from that of Mrs Taurine. Its purpose is to anticipate, as soon as a building is built, the possible subsequent installation of an elevator. Today, the construction and housing code requires a space dedicated to the subsequent installation of an elevator in buildings of less than three floors but with more than fifteen dwellings. The aim of this amendment is to reduce the number of dwellings to eight, i.e. two units per floor. I would point out that the obligation to install an elevator only concerns buildings of more than three floors. The aim of this amendment is therefore not to impose the installation of an elevator, but only to provide for the possibility of installing it from the moment the building is designed, by anticipating the scalability of the common parts and, consequently, the future accessibility of the building.

ParlaMint-FR_2018-06-01-O1243.u563	Mrs Taurine, Mrs Rossi, you are raising a very important debate, not on the accessibility of housing, but on the accessibility of buildings, especially those that are not currently subject to housing regulations. Mrs Taurine, you have defended your amendment without detailing its content, but it is intended to impose the installation of lifts in collective buildings which would be R + 3, and not R + 4, as the legislation currently provides. The question also arises from the height R + 2. In any collective building, should an elevator be imposed? The debate is obviously important, essential even for this inclusion society, which we all wish for. On the one hand, as the rapporteur has rightly said, this is not a matter of law but of the Rules of Procedure. On the other hand, this provision has other consequences. Firstly, imposing lifts in collective buildings de facto entails an additional cost, which breaks down into a positive cost for society, due to the inclusion that this allows, and a negative cost for those living there, since the installation results on average in an increase of 200 euros per year and per dwelling. Secondly, we must take into account the cost of the installation itself, but beyond that, the local elected representatives – many of you have been or are still – know how this affects densification. When the law reduced the height of buildings from which an elevator was needed, there was less density in the tense areas, i.e. the buildings built were of lower height. We need to think about solutions to make buildings more accessible, including elevators, within an inclusive society. We do not currently have a solution; We must work on it, bearing in mind that the subject is of a regulatory nature. The challenge is to find solutions that do not affect the purchasing power of tenants, or rather to ensure that tenants have the means to benefit from this inclusion solution, which we must prepare. We must continue to work there calmly, because we all call for a much more inclusive society with much more accessible buildings.
