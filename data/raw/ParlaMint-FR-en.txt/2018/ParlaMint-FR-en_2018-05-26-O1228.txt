ParlaMint-FR_2018-05-26-O1228.u677	Studies and surveys show without hesitation the desire of French consumers to be informed of the origin of the food products they purchase. This amendment is compatible with Community law and even makes it possible to comply more rigorously. Article 26 of Regulation No 1169/ 2011 of 25 October 2011 on the information of consumers on foodstuffs, known as "INCO", provides in Article 26, entitled "country of origin or place of provenance", that the indication of the country of origin or place of provenance is mandatory in particular "in cases where its omission would be likely to mislead consumers on the country of origin or place of actual provenance of the food, in particular if the information attached to the food or the label as a whole may suggest that the food has a different country of origin or place of provenance". Article 39 of the same Regulation on 'national measures on complementary compulsory information' provides that 'Member States may adopt measures requiring additional compulsory information for specific types or categories of foodstuffs, provided that such measures are justified by at least one of the reasons', which is subsequently listed in the Article. Among these reasons, the Regulation refers to the "repression of deceptions" and states that "Member States may introduce measures concerning the compulsory indication of the country of origin or place of provenance of foodstuffs only if there is a proven link between certain properties of the foodstuff and its origin or provenance". Finally, the Article states that Member States must then prove that "the majority of consumers attach significant importance to this information". Finally, Directive 2005/29/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 11 May 2005 on unfair commercial practices of undertakings vis-à-vis consumers in the internal market prohibits misleading commercial practices. Article 6 provides that "a commercial practice shall be deemed to be misleading if [...] in any way, including by its general presentation, it induces or is likely to mislead the average consumer, even if the information presented is factually correct, in respect of one or more of the following aspects and that, in either case, it leads or is likely to lead him to make a commercial decision which he would not otherwise have taken". The main features of the product, in particular its geographical origin, are mentioned in the directive.
