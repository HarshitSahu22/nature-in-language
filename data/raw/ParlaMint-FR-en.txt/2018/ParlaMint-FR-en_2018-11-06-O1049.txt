As you know, this is a component of the common European asylum system. It is intended to prevent the asylum-seeker from successively applying for several European countries and from being returned from one country to another without his application ever being examined. It lays down the principle that only one European State is responsible for the application for asylum of a person who is a national of a third State, and provides for a procedure for transfer between the different Member States. The number of asylum seekers under the Dublin Regulation has increased dramatically over the last two years, with these applicants now accounting for 40% of the total number of applications registered in France. As asylum applications decline throughout Europe, they continue to increase in our country: migrants who arrived in Europe in 2015 and 2016, after applying for asylum in a first European country and having been rejected, repeat their request to us. This is the case, for example, for many Afghan nationals from Germany or Italy, who file their third asylum application with us on average. There are several factors that can explain this phenomenon. First of all, first-entry Member States would not systematically register applicants, preferring to allow migrants to pass through. Secondly, some applicants circumvent the application of the Regulation by making use of the too short liability clauses – six or 18 months depending on the case: after this period, the procedure goes back to zero, if a State fails to transfer them. Finally, the rates of grant of protection vary from country to country, prompting a new application after a first failure. The problem is that France is struggling to organize itself in the face of this new phenomenon. The transfer rates remain, for example, particularly low: of 41000 requests sent by France in 2017, 29000 received an agreement from the Member States concerned, of which 13000 were from Italy, but only 2600 were actually made. These low rates weaken France compared to the other Member States, where they are higher; Our country thus becomes, in fact, a centre of attraction for all those rejected of the right of asylum present on the European continent. That is why an upgrade of our system is essential.
