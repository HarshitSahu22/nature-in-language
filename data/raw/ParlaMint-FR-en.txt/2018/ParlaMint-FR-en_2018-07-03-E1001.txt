This amendment, which meets a clarification objective, brings together in a single article the obligations applicable to platform operators in accordance with their duty to cooperate in the fight against false information. These obligations, stemming from Article 9 adopted by the Committee on Cultural Affairs and Education, provide first of all for the establishment of an accessible and visible mechanism allowing users to report false information, not in a sense of censorship, but with a view to raising the awareness of Internet users. The amendment also sets out the obligation for platforms to implement other measures, including an indicative list: transparency of algorithms or promotion of content from companies and news agencies, for example. Platforms will be encouraged to go beyond this non-limitative list, in particular by partnering with journalists specialized in fact-checking or by draining advertising resources from sites dedicated to misinformation. There is also an obligation for platforms to make these measures public and to report to the APF on how they are implemented. In substance, the list of measures proposed in this amendment differs from the Commission's text only on one point: the Government proposes to replace the certification of users' accounts, which, having only an indirect bearing on the fight against misinformation, could be seen as a challenge to the right to anonymity on the Internet, by "the fight against accounts spreading massive false information". As for Article 9, it will be refocused on the role of the APF in ensuring compliance with the duty of cooperation. The Government will submit an amendment of consistency to this effect. Finally, with the objective of legibility, these provisions are being brought together under a new Title IIa.
