ParlaMint-FR_2018-02-20-O1146.u339	To answer your first question, Mr. Bilde, let us recall that the Government has made binding agreements on mobile telephony, in the act itself, since the frequencies will now be renewed with regard to the commitment to develop the territory. It is a very good thing that operators invest, but the real debate is who is the originator. Today, a mobile operator cannot intervene as he wishes in the territory. It must comply with the requirements of the givers of orders, in this case the communities, I repeat. If, tomorrow, a community wants an operator to intervene, it can authorize it. If she does not wish to do so, she also has the opportunity to refuse her installation. As a result, many authorizations depend on the communities, in their capacity as principals. The State must then play its part in ensuring the economic balance of the FIRs, either because of a financial contribution under the very high-speed France plan, as I was saying right now to Mr Larive, or by ensuring that lower-ranking FIRs, as they develop, do not counterbalance a higher-ranking FIR, if I may say so. When we took up the case, Mounir Mahjoubi and I, we had a conviction: there was no question of overthrowing the table by putting an end to the RIPs overnight, which some proposed. We have assumed that the FIRs exist and work very well, even if difficulties may arise. We wanted to speed up and consolidate the FIRs, within the framework of the existing balances.
