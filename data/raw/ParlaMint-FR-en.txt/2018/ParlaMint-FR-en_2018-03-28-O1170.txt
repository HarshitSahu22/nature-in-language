ParlaMint-FR_2018-03-28-O1170.u308	Mr President, Mr President of the Committee on Cultural Affairs and Education, Mr rapporteur, ladies and gentlemen, we have come together to discuss the proposal for a law to simplify and better regulate the regime for the opening and monitoring of private non-contracted establishments. It is complex, in particular, because the four authorities responsible for monitoring the opening of schools – the municipal services, the academic services, the prefecture services and those of the public prosecutor – have very different means of action. It is a regime in some respects obsolete. The regime for the opening of private schools in the Education Code is the result of the juxtaposition of provisions of three laws: the Falloux Act of 1850, the Goblet Act of 1886, and the Astier Act of 1919. All of us in this House are very attached to the great laws of the Third Republic on school, born at the same time as the great laws of freedom and which underpin our republican social contract. There is a relationship between them. To be faithful to their minds is also to bring them the necessary evolutions so that they continue to constitute the framework of collective life and that they remain relevant to the new phenomena of society. However, the existing legal framework is difficult to adapt to the exponential increase in the openings of non-contractual establishments. It must also be said that it is no longer sufficient to deal with the new threats that we must collectively face. We therefore have a duty to modernise this framework within which an essential freedom is exercised: freedom of education. The system of opening private institutions out of contract is complex because it coexists three separate procedures with the mayor, the academic authority, the prefect and the public prosecutor. The time limits for opposition are different for different administrations and for different types of institutions. The grounds for opposition also differ according to the type of establishment. The current legal framework therefore juxtaposes three different regimes, for primary, secondary and technical education. Obsolete, the opening regime is also incomplete. The current legal framework does not allow the four competent administrations to oppose the opening of an establishment a priori. This is due to the fact that the current grounds for opposition in the first and second degrees, namely hygiene and morals, are not sufficient. There may be other very valid reasons for opposing an opening. In the Government's view, the proposed law achieves three major objectives: first, to simplify and unify the opening of all non-contractual establishments; Second, unifying the deadlines for the examination of files; Third, modernising the grounds for opposition to make them more effective and to deal with new phenomena. The proposed legislative amendments would make it possible to implement a simpler and more operational regime. Simpler, because a single diet would replace the three diets I mentioned. Indeed, if the bill maintains, in order to deny the declarant of a new school, the fourfold competence of the mayor, the academic authority, the prefect and the prosecutor, it provides – a salutary novelty – for the establishment of a one-stop shop: the declarant would seize a single administration, which would inform the other three. The new regime would also be more operational as the grounds for opposition would be unified and modernised. The general concept of public order, already used today, but only for technical education, would be generalized to all levels of education, the older concepts being updated by the reference to the protection of children and young people. One could therefore object to the establishment of a school on grounds of public order as well as on grounds of child and youth protection. On the other hand, the grounds already allowing for the closure of an institution – the absence of a diploma from the director, the conviction for a crime or offence contrary to morals – could now be opposed to its opening.
