The latter resulted in the conclusion of bilateral agreements with seven of the Mediterranean countries involved. The Agreement lays down a single legal framework for the operation of air services between the 28 Member States of the Union and Israel. In the territories of the Member States in which it is applicable, it replaces de facto existing bilateral agreements. I would like to stress that the main expectations of this agreement are economic. According to the European Commission, the development of air transport services between the Union and Israel has so far been hampered by the restrictive provisions contained in most of the bilateral agreements negotiated by Israel with the Member States. The European Union is Israel's main trading partner, with EUR 31 billion in trade; It has become both the first source of its imports and the first world market for its exports. Air traffic between the European Union and Israel stood at 8.3 million passengers in 2014, and over 1.05 million passengers took off from our land during the same period. In addition, twenty-one states have ratified the 2013 agreement, the Senate having passed the bill that we are debating on November 9. The single legal framework resulting from the Agreement establishes a common airspace for the Member States and Israel, based on two principles: harmonisation, on the basis of the rules laid down by the European Union, of legislation and market opening. Harmonisation of legislation ensures that air transport users have high standards. In the area of aviation safety, there is convergence of standards since Israel adopts those issued by the United States Federal Aviation Administration. Because of the high level of safety of Israeli operators, negotiators also preferred to set themselves the objective of achieving mutual recognition of their standards in this area. The agreement also provides for cooperation in the field of air traffic management in preparation for the enlargement of the Single European Sky to Israel. The opening up of markets will mean the possibility for carriers to serve all routes between European Union and Israeli airports. The agreement provides that they can operate their services in the countries of the Euromed area and those of the common European airspace, in the other countries of the European Economic Area and in Switzerland. These trade rights will be available in a second phase, once regulatory harmonization is completed. The agreement also allows for the opening of capital and the control of air carriers from one party to the other: this development is innovative, while reflections are being carried out on this subject within the framework of the International Civil Aviation Organisation. Above all, specific environmental provisions affirm the importance of reducing the impact of international air transport and guarantee the right of the parties to take measures to this end. This agreement, which I call for ratification of, is therefore fully in line with the President of the Republic's view of trade policy as it seeks to establish a single legal framework for the operation of air services between the European Union and Israeli territory. It provides for the gradual opening up of markets and the harmonisation of legislation, thereby ensuring high standards for air transport users and allowing the harmonisation of conditions of competition between operators.
