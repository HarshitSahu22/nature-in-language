The SAFE are key players in the preservation of natural, agricultural and forest areas. Above all, we hope to create a sufficiently deterrent tool to encourage the professional who would be tempted to sell his property to an individual not to do so. The concern for the rights of people is not the only one to guide the reflection. That of the general interest orders. Let us ask ourselves what is good for all, and the thing becomes clear: in addition to the need for protection, which might suffice, there is a public health imperative. The consequences of violent education on psychological health, particularly in terms of stress and lack of self-confidence, are multiple. Unlike the vulgar cartoons that can be read here or there, it is not a question of giving in to all the whims of children. It is not a question of proclaiming them king-children or of deciding that nothing could ever be said to them and, above all, never to say no to them. It is simply a question of asserting that one can say no to a child anything other than by resorting to acts of violence, because, contrary to what is too often advanced, violence is not educational. If it educates, it is only violence and conflict resolution through violence. It makes suffering synonymous with coercion. But this equivalence is dangerous: violence against children is used to power relations, to fear more than to respect. To educate by violence is to restore within families a law that society has banned everywhere else: the law of the strongest. It is taking the risk of making it prosper and undermining the foundations of true authority. We must not fear saying this: to ban the use of force is to work for the restoration of this true authority. What is authority? It's a power based on reason. Authority is the exact opposite of arbitrariness. Parental authority, like that of teachers and mistresses, comes not from an ability to harm, but from the ability to protect, cherish and raise, in all senses that this word may take on. To consider that the exercise of authority involves the practice of violence is a dangerous countersense: it is the germ of more serious violence and unjust rebellions. To base education on the balance of power, however reduced its expression, is to justify that one day, in the face of an elderly and forceless parent, filial respect has become useless. The authority of parents and teachers would then be like the power of the tyrant described in Plato's dialogues. This tyrant is the most unhappy man in the world: he lives in fear and waiting for his fall. The violence he sows around him will fall on him. To leave a place for violence in educational relationships is to let violence flourish everywhere. On the other hand, to ban it is to protect both the children and the educated as well as the adults who educate. Violence is used by children to the relationships of domination. Unfortunately, some of them observe it between their parents and suffer the damage. When they grow up, the model anchors in their minds. This violence then becomes the root of abuse, but also of domestic violence and violent acts committed at school. How else would it be since the child is used to the idea of good violence? The confusion is long-lasting in the heads: the fact of loving someone and protecting him would not be contradictory with beating him, mocking him or humiliating him? Think of the terrible testimonies of battered women who so often minimize what they have experienced and claim that the one who hurts them does it out of love! Recurrent humiliations, repeated psychological violence and the most insidious inequalities necessarily distort child conceptions. In this way, children learn to be dominated and to want to dominate to get out of this situation where they are subjected to violence. There is an infernal cycle in which future violence responds to current violence, where battered children who become parents are sometimes unable to educate their own children otherwise. This violence then dominates social relations, at school, and later at work, in society and in the couple. All social relations are then rotten, for some, by the compelling desire to impose themselves in order not to be subjected to violence, and for others, by permanent fear and self-efficacy. [[Applause on the benches of the FI, GDR and MODEM groups, as well as on several benches of the LaREM group]] Only the dominant want to maintain the relations of domination. The disappearance of the latter will not cause chaos or the end of authority, but what is paramount in the Republic, namely the freedom for the child to flourish without fear, the equal treatment of all without one ruling and the other obeying, and of course fraternity. There will be no fully emancipated citizens until violence is removed from education. Today, thanks to you, the rapporteur, we can take a step in this direction.
