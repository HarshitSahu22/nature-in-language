ParlaMint-FR_2018-06-01-O1242.u544	The regional development plan – SAR – is a development document specific to certain ultramarine territories – Guadeloupe, Réunion, Guyana, Martinique and Mayotte. It lays down the basic guidelines for spatial planning and environmental protection and includes a particular chapter, the scheme for the development of the sea – SMVM – allowing the application of the coastal law. As provided for in articles L. 4433-7 et seq. of the General Code of Local Authorities, it is necessary for local planning documents, which must be compatible with its provisions. The Council of State, in a recent opinion, noted the "difficult intelligibility of the regime applicable to regional development schemes" in the overseas regions. Indeed, the numerous additions made over the last 30 years and the shortcomings of updating the regulatory part as a result of legislative developments have led to a corpus of disparate but juxtaposed rules, causing confusion. Article 14 proposes that the Government be empowered to take, by order, any measure falling within the scope of the Act aimed at updating, clarifying, simplifying and supplementing the legal regime for regional development schemes in the overseas regions of the General Code of Local and Regional Authorities. A period of 12 months is foreseen to bring this reform to a successful conclusion. These orders will have to remove the difficulties of intelligibility or obsolescence of the current regime and modify the procedure for the development and content of the SAR, in particular by specifying that it serves as a scheme for the development of the sea, a regional scheme for ecological coherence and a regional scheme for climate, air and energy. These amendments will allow for the start-up of the underlying structuring projects, while reducing litigation costs, which will result in a reduction in some of the expenditures of the supporting communities.

They lay down the basic guidelines for spatial planning and environmental protection and include a particular chapter, the scheme for the development of the sea, allowing the application of the coastal law. SARs are urban planning documents of prescriptive and opposable value, they are binding on local urban planning documents – SCOT and PLU – which must be compatible with their provisions. So you understand how important these SARs are to our territories. What's their main flaw? Undoubtedly the complexity of their method of development. In Guadeloupe, for example, we have taken more than ten years to develop and adopt the SAR, even by hiring the services of a State adviser to do so. SARs are drawn up on the initiative and under the authority of the regional or territorial community, and their content is the subject of a wide consultation of all the actors, i.e. the State, the department, the communes, the EPCIs, the joint unions responsible for drawing up and approving SCOTs and the consular chambers. The document is of such importance that it is up to the Council of State, not the president of the community or the prefect, to approve it: the SAR is adopted by the Regional Council and then approved in the Council of Ministers after advice and decree in the Council of State. This method of development is cumbersome, it is true, but you are now asking us to give you carte blanche to make decisions by prescription, which could upset the development of our territories. The present method is complex, but above all it is intended to guarantee a controlled development of the territory and to protect the natural, historical, material, cultural and human heritage of the territories concerned. After years of work, why do you want, under the pretext of efficiency and pragmatism, to circumvent the will of elected officials and actors on the ground? Colleagues, after the debate on the littoral law – and I thank the Minister and the Secretary of State for their wisdom – do not endanger our development and our environment. The impact assessment says that there may be a concertation, but these orders will be made without us being able to participate in their construction.
