This text gives France the means to protect itself against cyber attacks by requiring operators and digital service providers to identify potential digital security risks, to take the necessary decisions to control them at their own expense and to inform the relevant national authority. Secondly, with regard to the directive on the control of the acquisition and possession of weapons, it should be recalled that it was taken at the initiative of France, following the Paris attacks of January 2015. The purpose of this Directive of 17 May 2017, which is to be transposed before 14 September 2018, is to strengthen the control of the circulation and trade of firearms and intra-European cooperation in this field. However, its scope is limited, since it is aimed only at the control of firearms of persons within a legal framework and therefore does not deal with the fight against arms trafficking, to our great regret. The directive removes category D of firearms, which is beyond administrative control: from now on, all firearms will have to fall under category C at least, i.e. be subject to a declaration and not simply a registration. A category D of weapons that can be acquired and held freely may continue to exist in national law, but without any firearms, except for historical weapons – excluded from the scope of the Directive – and some of their reproductions. Finally, the draft law deals with the European project of strategic importance, the Galileo satellite positioning system, which entered into service in December 2016. In addition to open services for the general public, the Galileo system emits a high-precision secure signal, reserved for authorized users by government decision. This encrypted signal, protected against interference, is intended for uses requiring high reliability, controlled by States. The draft law provides France with the legal tools necessary for the implementation of measures to control access to this secure signal and provides for administrative and criminal penalties in case of non-compliance with the law. These measures will enable France to ensure a high level of security for the regulated public service, particularly in the area of security and defence. They are also the way out of dependence on foreign satellite positioning systems, the control of which is beyond the control of European countries.
