It will only be a consequence. Like the rapporteur, I have heard your reluctance to implement a system which, on an experimental basis, entrusts to an organisation such as the CAF, it is true that it is responsible for a public service mission, the possibility of modulating maintenance. There are several factors that lead me to believe that these reluctances are unfounded. On the legal front, first of all, this experiment does not in any way undermine the established principles. It is possible to entrust such a task to a non-judicial authority: the Constitutional Council has recognized it on several occasions, particularly in 1999, when it examined the law establishing the CMU. I would also point out that, since 2018, the Director of the CAF can issue an enforceable title in respect of family allowances, when both parents agree. You also argued that there was a risk of bias on the part of the CAF. This fear is, in my opinion, unfounded. As I have already pointed out at first reading, any legal person entrusted with a public service mission, such as the CAF, is subject to the principle of impartiality, which is necessary. The conditions for the appointment of the directors of the caisses, an appointment which requires ministerial approval, imply this professionalism, competence, responsibility and impartiality, which I consider to constitute serious guarantees for the performance of this mission. The third element is that the new amount of the pension will be fixed by applying a national scale. This one already exists, you know, and it is widely used by the judge. We propose it to the CAF, in support of their decisions, stating that they will have no margin of appreciation. A parliamentary amendment will propose to include in the law that the FCA simply "recognizes" the evolution of resources. On the basis of this finding, it will fix the new amount of maintenance on the basis of the scale. As soon as the situation is considered complex, it will be necessary to go back to the judge: the FCA will therefore intervene only in the simplest situations. I also understand that the FCA will have an interest in overestimating the amount of support. This reasoning does not seem relevant to me, since an increase in maintenance could result in a default by the debtor. However, in the case of a defaulting parent, the CAF must pay the family support allowance. It will therefore have no interest in varying the level of pensions in one direction or another without taking into account the reality. Some have also invoked European law, in order to infer that only the judge should intervene in this matter. The concept of jurisdiction, mentioned in the European Regulation on Maintenance Obligations, allows administrative authorities such as the CIF to be included when they fulfil the conditions, in particular, of impartiality and respect for adversarial rights. The strongest argument I could hear is that we would create a mechanism that would allow us to reverse a judge's decision. I don't have that feeling. If such an assertion seems to me wrong, it is that the FCA will have the task of seeing an evolution, not of deciding a dispute. The CAF will not come back on the judge's decision: they will have the task of adapting it to a new situation, on the basis of a new fact. Their role will not be to return to the authority of res judicata. In conclusion, I would like to stress, as at first reading, that this mechanism is not new in principle. Over-indebtedness commissions, whose status may be considered similar to that of FCAs, may already modify an over-indebtedness plan ordered by the judge, if a significant change in the debtor's situation makes it impossible to implement the plan. There are, therefore, such mechanisms already in place. [[Applause on the benches of the LaREM and MODEM groups]] The construction of this system, its experimental nature and the possibility of an appeal to the judge, the possibility therefore that the decision of the CAF is not enforceable, constitute sufficient guarantees to envisage this experiment with great interest. I therefore express an unfavorable opinion on the deletion amendments.
