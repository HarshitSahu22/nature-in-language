ParlaMint-FR_2019-03-22-O1193.u18	I will respond to the statements made by the speakers on the article and will take advantage of the consideration of these amendments to delete article 10 to clarify once again the Government's ambition on the subject, as it is a key part of our strategy for transforming the health system. The HHT comes from the January 26, 2016 law of modernization of our health system. They aim to promote access to high-quality, secure care through enhanced cooperation between public health institutions in the same territory. This project aims to strengthen the dynamics of cooperation and integration in hospital matters, in order to avoid inter-hospital competitions. The main thrust of the bill's measures is to refocus HHTs on the transformation issues of medical and health care organizations. Significant efforts have been made to pool support functions. This morning, Jean-Carles Grelier referred in particular to the purchasing function, which is actually shared. This may have led – I recognise – to a decentralization of the reform's ambition in favour of mainly administrative integration. However, the ambition of the new stage we want to take at the GHT is to put the shared medical-care project back at the heart of the reform. To this end, two measures are planned. The first is the integration of clinical governance, through the integration of medical grouping commissions. Our ambition is to ensure that certain medical decisions are taken in a more collective way, at the level of the GHT, in order to support the emergence of structured medical channels within it, to strengthen medical governance in its overall governance and to create a position of chairman of the GHT medical commission, which would be the medical homologue of the director of the support institution. I am well aware that care will have to be taken to protect clinical governance, which must be kept close, particularly in the context of the reform of community hospitals. The second measure is the pooling of medical human resources management. We want to ensure that the priorities in this area will be consistent with the GHT's medical strategy and that the GHT will abandon any competitive practices, which I will take as an example the dumping we see in terms of compensation or acting. We also want HHTs to increase the visibility of career development opportunities for practitioners, in order to improve the attractiveness of positions. It is up to us to define the dividing line between health facilities and GHT accordingly. However, in order not to slow down day-to-day management, responsibilities will be duplicated. I can hear the few concerns expressed by physicians, who fear that the decision-making channels will become more complex and doubt that the respective roles of the HHT and health care institutions allow for a combination of fluidity and clustering strategy. We will learn from the experience of sharing procurement functions. The second part of the reform proposed by this bill aims to support the HHT in terms of their ability to deepen their integration, through a right of option, allowing voluntary HHTs to conclude a single multi-year contract of objectives and means – CPOM – with the ARS. This will also promote a unified investment policy, with a comprehensive multi-year financing plan and a single multi-year investment plan. Cash can also be pooled. Finally, this will allow the merging of bodies of the same family, with the exception of user commissions and supervisory boards, namely the Medical Establishment Commission – CME –, the Health, Safety and Working Conditions Committee – CHSCT –, the Executive Board and the Nursing, Rehabilitation and Medical Technical Committee – CIRMT. These are complementary integration steps, which need to simplify the functioning of the HHT and ensure strategic alignment of the parties. As you have understood, this is a right of option, thus opening up a possibility of increased integration, and not an obligation on the GHT. Like any right of option, it will be subject to the agreement of the institutions concerned. The target model, on the various subjects, has not yet been finalized, for several reasons. First of all, it does not depend solely on the measure envisaged: it interacts strongly with other provisions of the bill. This is particularly the case with the pooling of the management of medical resources, which interacts with the unification of the status of contractual practitioner, and with the revision of the competences of the CMEs, which, for its part, depends on the reform of local hospitals. On the other hand, once the objective has been posted, it is necessary to define the operational modalities of the authorization process for the activation of the right of option and the line of sharing of medical human resources between GHT and health facilities. This is why section 10, after laying down fundamental principles, contains measures to enable legislation to be legislated by ordinances. I would like to take the time to define the target model, in consultation with the relevant partners representing hospitals and professionals. This definition work began in early March as part of working groups. About 30 co-construction workshops, led by DGOS, are scheduled by the summer. As far as the place of parliamentarians is concerned, I have said and I repeat that of course I would like to involve you and honourable senators in the proceedings. Today we have a debate on the provisions of the article; We'll have another one when we ratify the ordinances. In addition, I undertook to present these to the Committee on Social Affairs once they were drafted. You will also have an impact assessment, so that no decisions are hidden from you. I think, in conscience, that we can work by using orders if we are exemplary in terms of the logic of working with parliamentarians and the outcome of the consultation processes with professionals and other stakeholders.
