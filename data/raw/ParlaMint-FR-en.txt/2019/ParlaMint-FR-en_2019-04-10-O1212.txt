These various arguments, echoing the existence of increased risks in sectors deemed critical, therefore demonstrate the need for an adaptation of the legal framework to ensure the security and resilience of electronic communications networks. The purpose of the bill that we will be considering in public session is to meet this imperative: it provides for a system of prior authorization, based on defence and national security grounds, for the equipment of mobile electronic communications networks that will be deployed to broadcast 5G. Article 1 of this bill is directly inspired by a provision of the Penal Code, designed to protect the secrecy of private correspondence. However, extending this relatively narrow legal basis in the light of the new security requirements, it submits to the Prime Minister for authorization "the operation in the national territory of devices, i.e. any hardware or software devices, allowing the terminal of end users to be connected to the mobile radio network", i.e. to the network on which electronic communications of mobile phones transit. The operators covered by this authorisation are vital operators, known as "IVOs", in the telecoms sector. Their list is confidential, but it can be advanced without too much risk that it includes the four national operators. This authorization, intended to preserve "the interests of defence and national security", will be granted for one or more models and for one or more versions of the aircraft concerned. This will avoid possible administrative burdens and thus ensure the freedom and speed of deployment of electronic communications networks. The Prime Minister may also refuse authorization if he or she considers that there is a serious risk of harm to "the interests of defence and national security", i.e. that compliance with the rules of confidentiality, integrity, security and continuity of network operation and service delivery is not guaranteed. Finally, it will have the power of injunction in the event of an operation without authorisation of an apparatus which is subject to that prior authorisation regime. Article 2 lays down a system of criminal penalties for infringements of the provisions of the new control system. As such, it creates two offences: the operation, without prior authorisation, of devices allowing the connection to the mobile network; failure to comply, in whole or in part, with the Prime Minister's injunctions. It provides for a penalty of one year's imprisonment and a fine of EUR 150000 for any natural person declared responsible for any of these offences. The judge may also order the confiscation or destruction of the material and a ban on the establishment of electronic networks for up to three years. Legal persons declared criminally liable shall be liable for a fine at a maximum rate equal to five times that laid down for natural persons. As with natural persons, additional penalties will also apply to legal persons. In this way, the judge may impose a definitive or five-year ban on the operation of mobile radio networks, as well as the dissemination of the decision. Finally, Article 3 provides that the prior authorisation regime will apply to the operation of devices installed since 1 February 2019. The retroactive application of this scheme implies that the operators concerned prepare applications for authorisation to be submitted within two months of the entry into force of the law for equipment already in place. It goes without saying that this mechanism has been developed in close consultation with the private actors directly concerned and with the regulatory authorities. It aims to ensure the sustainable and safe development of 5G and its uses in France. Less than a revolution, it is a matter of evolving a doctrine whose actors are already very customary.
