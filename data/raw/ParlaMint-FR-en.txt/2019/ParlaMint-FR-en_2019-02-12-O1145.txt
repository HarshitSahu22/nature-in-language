First of all, I would like to return to what might be termed "inductive history": the idea that knowledge of regional history is useful in understanding national history, which is obviously quite true. Reference has been made to the very positive things that have happened in the overseas regions in recent years: they could inspire the same approach in the Hexagon regions. However, I do not think it necessary to introduce this approach into the education code. We encourage such approaches in the context of programmes. The Rector of Besançon – not to name him – is a specialist on this subject and has written a magnificent thesis on the history of what he calls "small homelands". His work allows us to revisit the history of the school of the Third Republic: unlike the usual clichés during this period, far from a kind of jacobinism crushing local identities, regional stories have often been taken into account. We must stand in this line and never oppose the local to the national, nor the national to the universal. We can do this on the basis of existing texts. There will be more and more initiatives in this direction, not only during school time, but also during extracurricular time. On the other hand, in response to other comments that have been made, I would like to say that there is no point in caricaturing the existing situation in order to put forward its point of view. In this case, our programs follow a chronological approach to the history of France. Great men are there: let's not pretend they weren't there! The annual benchmarks have also made it clear. I invite everyone, in their assessments of these issues, to really read the programmes as they are written and the annual benchmarks. We will therefore be able to discuss this subject calmly, without ever opposing the different dimensions of history. The history of France is absolutely not sacrificed. The Government's opinion was unfavourable.

ParlaMint-FR_2019-02-12-O1145.u454	History does not have to be instrumentalized for state or political reasons: it is simply a reflection from different stories. I note that some are nostalgic of the national novel of the Third Republic, with the idea that France was a construction, with no doubt the will to dominate the world, step by step. But it is the case of the States as well as the rest: they create themselves and then end up disappearing; It will happen to France one day. Of course, we are not in a hurry – I would point out for the minister – but that is what will happen. The memoirs are necessarily different. Thus, Brittany was an independent state until 1532: the Duke of Brittany had his army, his diplomacy, his currency, and he made the law. Similarly, regarding the French Revolution, the vision of caulianry is not the same according to whether one looks at it from the National Assembly in Paris or through the testimonies of peasants, for example in my region of Ploërmel: the latter had understood that they were a little bit the turkeys of the farce! History is therefore necessarily plural; Studying the sources necessarily leads to a different vision. It is good not to have an ideological and political vision.
