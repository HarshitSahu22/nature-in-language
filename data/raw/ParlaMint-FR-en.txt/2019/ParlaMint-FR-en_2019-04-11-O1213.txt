French Polynesia consists of 118 islands of the Pacific Ocean, five archipelagos – Society, Tuamotu, Gambier, Australes, Marquises –, forty-eight communes. These peoples and territories were violently colonized by France in the 1840s, but not without resistance: resistance from 1843 to 1847 in the Marquises Islands, in 1842 in Vaitahu, in 1844 in Haapa, in 1846 behind chief Pakoko. Attached under the Third Republic and renamed "French Polynesia", the whole was submitted until 1946 to the Code of Indigenate. Following Algeria's independence from nuclear testing, the French State relocated its experiments from the Sahara Desert to the Pacific Ocean. For 30 years, between 1960 and 1996, 193 nuclear launches will be carried out, 46 of which will be in the atmosphere. Thirty years that will have been enough to disfigure the landscapes and destroy the ecosystems of the atolls of Mururoa and Fangataufa, to brutally and sustainably destabilize the environment, lifestyles and well-being of the people. The consequences are still being felt today. Since its launch in 1966, trials have affected the health of residents and thousands of migrant workers. The radioactive cloud resulting from the Aldebaran explosion, the first bomb to explode, on 2 July 1966, over the Mururoa atoll, drifted to Mangareva, resulting in the evacuation of the overseas minister, but without the local population being informed. Drinking water was six times more radioactive and soil samples were fifty times more radioactive. Three months later, an investigation indicated that the radioactivity detected in rainwater in Mangareva reached staggering levels: 11 million times higher than normal. The Pacific Experimental Centre, which was already in possession of this information at the time, nevertheless continued the tests until the programme was finally discontinued in February 1996. In January 2018, following the request of the representative of the Union for Democracy, Eliane Tevahitua, the Polynesian Ministry of Health reported the figures of radiation-induced diseases in the population. The number of malformations at birth has increased fivefold in 25 years: 142 in 1992, compared to 685 in 2017.
