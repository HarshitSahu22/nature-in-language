In particular, it provides that women, if they do not use their embryos in the context of in vitro fertilization, are obliged to give them to scientific research, while French law offers them the opportunity to destroy them, to give them to research or to give them to others. For this reason, the ECHR found that Italy did not respect the status that it itself had given the embryo, since it forced women to give unused embryos to science. There is therefore no parallelism of forms with French law. On the one hand, the ECHR would, in my view, respect the status of the embryo established in French law, as indicated in the judgment in question. On the other hand, in France, women have a choice. So you can't use this stop as an argument.
