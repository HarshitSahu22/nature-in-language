Indeed, as the rapporteur rightly notes in his introduction, the purpose of the bill is to draw attention to a subject of public health. Many provisions, however, fall within the regulatory, not the legislative, area. Who wouldn't agree with the goal of fighting cardiac arrest? This is defined by the dictionary of the National Academy of Medicine: "The sudden cessation of heartbeat (asystoliation, ventricular fibrillation, electromechanical dissociation or cardiac collapse) interrupting circulation. The management of cardiac arrest is the subject of international recommendations updated every five years, the latest version of which dates back to 2015. It is based on the concept of "lifeline", adopted since 1991. In the majority of cases, notes the National Academy of Medicine, cardiac arrest results from a pre-existing pathology. However, some cardiac arrest occurs unexpectedly, for no obvious reason. The number of extra-hospital cardiac arrestes occurring each year is estimated at approximately 40,000. The Anglo-Saxon and Scandinavian countries, where population information is better, have a survival rate between 30% and 40%, while in France this rate does not exceed 7% to 8%. Less than a third of French people are trained in first aid actions, and only 55% of college students, while their initiation is mandatory under the Education Code, which provides for awareness of risk prevention and the missions of relief services. The first minutes of support are decisive – it has been said and it is obvious. A witness is present in 70% of cases, as illustrated by the two examples I cited. The first link in the survival chain is therefore to be able to recognize cardiac arrest and to call first aid as soon as possible. The implementation of basic cardiopulmonary resuscitation by a witness is essential. Today, however, this witness starts a heart massage in only 40% of cases. This massage is the second link in the survival chain, and still it must be of quality. However, it is often insufficiently prepared. The third link is defibrillation, provided that an external automated defibrillator is in the vicinity, as it has begun to be the case in recent years. Finally, with the arrival of medical aid, the implementation of specialized techniques and medicalisation is the fourth and final link in the survival chain before admission to hospital. To achieve the highest survival rate, it is necessary to develop a real strategy for better education of the population to the actions that save. This is recommended by the National Academy of Medicine in its report. Article 1 defines a life-saving citizen as "any volunteer or voluntary person who provides assistance to any person who is clearly in a situation of cardio-respiratory distress". Its purpose is to reduce the criminal liability of the rescue citizen during his intervention, by transposing to volunteers a provision already provided for relief professionals. This is a point that needs to be looked at very closely. The granting of a legal status to the saving citizen will strengthen the work of Professor Alexandre Mignon and encourage the use of applications such as Staying alive, protecting the good Samaritans, who have so far lacked a legal basis. [[Applause on the benches of the LaREM group]] We must commit ourselves to carrying out these reforms, which the National Academy of Medicine also requests, the four proposals of which have been recalled. In my view, the most important thing is to provide, through the statute, a legal basis for volunteers who could try to revive the victim and who are not currently legally covered.
