Let me summarize the situation in a few words. By 2010, France had adopted an ambitious plan to cover the entire territory in very high-speed by 2022, with a focus on fibre optics. What was the "Very high-speed" programme became, in 2013, the "France very high-speed" plan, which maintained the goal of very high-speed deployment for all by 2022. For the implementation of this plan, three areas are identified as a whole. The densest areas, the most profitable in the territory, are equipped, on their own funds, by private operators Orange, SFR, Bouygues Telecom and Free. These operators themselves deploy their networks in the largest cities and offer their commercial offers. Apart from the large agglomerations, two operators, Orange and SFR, shared the main urban areas by responding to a call for investment intent – AMII. In areas where operators have not wanted to invest, where the cost per catch is the highest, local and regional authorities have the responsibility to deploy networks. We are talking about RIP – not the referendum of popular initiative, but the public initiative networks. To ensure the deployment of these public initiative networks, the State has promised grants, initially financed by the first investment programme for the future, the IAP 1, supplemented in a second phase by a new programme of the State budget, the programme 343 of the mission "Economy". These credits are paid to the Caisse des dépôts et consignations – CDC – which manages them through an agreement with the State. Whatever the origin, PIA or "Economy" mission, they feed the National Fund for Digital Society – FSN – a fund without legal personality. It is therefore always in a PIA logic that these funds are spent, despite the state's funding channel. While, today, almost €3.3 billion of commitment authorisations have been paid to the fund for the digital company, only €495 million has been disbursed: government subsidies are, in fact, only repayments to the communities once all the invoices have been paid to the operators. The disbursement schedule provided by the Caisse des dépôts – which you can find in my report – is therefore completely uncertain and is likely to run well beyond 2022. In addition, the monitoring of these appropriations is somewhat weak, some of which have been used to finance actions other than grants to public initiative networks. Thus, an amendment to the initial agreement between the State and the Caisse des dépôts was concluded in 2016 in order to finance mobile – not internet – coverage of the territory or the development of digital infrastructure in schools.
