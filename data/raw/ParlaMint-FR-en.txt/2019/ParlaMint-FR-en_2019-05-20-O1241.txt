In fact, today, when a community wishes to use the public service delegation procedure for some of its missions, the public servant who previously participated in the exercise of these public service missions has the choice: he may refuse a secondment to the organization that will manage this public service mission. This obligation is often, today, a hindrance to the direct return of certain missions. A recent law has created the Single Operating Mixed Economy Companies (SEMOP), which are somewhat specific mixed economy companies, to manage industrial and commercial services. They make it possible to find a new balance between the private and the public, between the territorial community, the public authority and the private enterprise, with an almost equal relationship, since the territorial authorities preside over their board of directors. It is sound, in my view, that the officials who previously managed these missions can integrate these structures, in order to ensure the continuity of public action between the time the mission is governed and the time when it is delegated by contract to this type of structure, and even the time when it returns to government – possible, sometimes desirable return. Staff are thus protected. Article 28 therefore seems to me to propose a good legal framework for this, notwithstanding the problems posed by its application to the sports world. The UDI, Agir and Independent Group is therefore generally in favour of this article.
