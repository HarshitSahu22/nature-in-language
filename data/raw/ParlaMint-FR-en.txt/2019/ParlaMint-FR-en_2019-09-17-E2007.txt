The Government could provide us with a report that would examine the impact of such a measure. I have been conducting the reflection for a while, but it has become glaring on the day of the European elections. In fact, as early as eight and a half hours, half an hour after the opening of the polling stations, some candidates already had no ballot papers because they had not been able to print them for financial reasons. At the same time, the bins in the insulators are filled with paper. This is something of a gabegie: financial first, since the state reimburses the printing of the bulletins; Then, because we print far more bulletins than there are people who will use them, and the excess copies end up either in the trash or in compost in the town hall. With a single newsletter, we would make big savings because the state would take charge of printing in the prefectures. We would also solve a problem that sometimes arises in some elections: that of a printer who suddenly tells the candidate that he may not have the paper in time – many of us have experienced this stress. We would like to have a report to assess the impact of the single bulletin on cost, savings and practicality, but also to determine whether citizens would be willing to check a name rather than take a full newsletter. For my part, I believe that this proposal would make it possible to achieve great savings while ensuring full respect for democracy.
