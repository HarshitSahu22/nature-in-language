ParlaMint-FR_2019-03-20-O1188.u8	Before defending this amendment of the La République group on the move, allow me to thank Dr Janowiak, chairman of the Loire department council of the Order of Physicians, who has slipped some advice to us. The writing of a death certificate is an administrative obligation, which must be preceded by an examination of the deceased patient to identify the circumstances of the death. This act involves the responsibility and expertise of the doctor who established it because, according to the law, "the authorization to close the coffin may be issued only on the basis of this certificate issued by a doctor attesting to the death". Beyond the administrative aspect, the medical component of the death certificate specifies the circumstances of the death and makes it possible to contribute to the health alert, to prioritize public health priorities and to highlight factors of national and international variation. It therefore has a major health issue. For several years, the drafting of death certificates has faced an increasing number of technical and administrative difficulties, compounded by the scarcity of medical resources. In the hospital, interns are often asked to see the death, the signature of the act being compulsory by the doctor. Following the ongoing reform of medical studies, the third cycle will be divided into three phases. In the second phase of the cycle, known as the deepening phase, the interns will be competent to certify a death once the trainee master validates this capacity. From the beginning of the third phase, known as the consolidation phase, the intern, called "junior doctor", will be able to certify and sign death certificates. The National Council of the Order of Physicians will be informed of this and will include the internal part of the RPP – the shared directory of health professionals – in a special table, in order to identify it in the CertDc application and to ensure the traceability of its actions. In addition, in a number of underfunded areas, families face a lack of doctors and intolerable waiting times before obtaining a death certificate. Families, mayors and law enforcement personnel then find themselves in unacceptable situations, especially in the human sphere. To address these challenges, it is proposed that retired doctors be used to establish death certificates in areas where the lack of doctors does not allow this formality to be accomplished in a timely manner. These retired doctors, who will have to be registered on the departmental council of the Order of Physicians, will obviously be paid for these acts. They will be voluntary and will be included in a list of volunteers in order to be identified quickly. They will benefit, for the remuneration received as such, from the scheme applicable to the cumulation of employment and retirement of doctors in sub-dense zones, which we are strengthening precisely in the framework of the plan of access to care. This remuneration will therefore not give rise to the payment of social contributions.
