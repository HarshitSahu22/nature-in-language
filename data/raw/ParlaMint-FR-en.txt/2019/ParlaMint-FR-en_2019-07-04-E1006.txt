What we want, through this public policy to combat hate content, is to be very effective and provide us with the means that truly reflect the will of the legislator. We therefore want to devise effective means of investigation and judicial means, with a view to prosecuting and condemning the perpetrators of the offences in question. To this end, I propose – and the rapporteur has given me a great deal of support for this – to create a specialised prosecutor's office that would ensure the best possible repression of these offences. This prosecutor's office, composed of judges trained specifically for this purpose and particularly competent on these sometimes very complex subjects, would have concurrent competence to intervene in these cases. In the rule of law, we can already organize such concurrent jurisdiction for acts of public cyberhaine, including digital raids, as they fall under the Freedom of the Press Act of 1881. This is not the case, on the other hand, of cyberhaine acts which are not public, e.g. those committed through a private courier. That is why this amendment seeks to create a comprehensive and secure framework, organised as follows. For public hate content, the constant jurisprudence of the Court of Cassation already opens up a possibility of national concurrent jurisdiction for press offences provided for by the Act of 1881 when committed on the Internet. The place of commission of the offence which determines the jurisdiction of the court shall be situated at any point in the territory where the publication of the contested text has been carried out. Since anyone in any part of the country can have access to public content broadcast on the Internet, any public prosecutor can logically seize it. In practice, today, a Parisian court – to take this example – may already decide to declare itself competent in matters of hate messages broadcast on the internet, as all messages are accessible from Paris. In order to achieve this concurrent national competence, I will issue a circular reminding all the public prosecutors of its existence, as soon as this bill is passed. I will ensure that the national prosecutor who will be retained to exercise this concurrent competence has sufficient staff of judges and clerks to deal with the proceedings. This circular, Mr Goasguen, will also explain how this specialized prosecutor's office will have to articulate its action with that of local prosecutors – that it is not, in fact, a matter of dispossessing any attribution in this field. The mechanism will be as follows: upon receipt of a complaint of cyberhaine, an investigation will be immediately initiated under the authority of the specialised prosecutor's office, which will send the first requests to the Internet technical operators with the aim of identifying the perpetrators targeted by the complaint. As a result of these investigations, two assumptions will arise. If these are isolated facts, once an author has been identified and located, the criterion of competence attached to his home will be retained, in order to give priority to the pedagogical dimension of the criminal response. This will therefore be a close response. The prosecutor's office at the author's place of residence will thus be able to easily implement either alternative measures to prosecution, for the least serious acts, or the criminal order provided for in the Justice Reform Act that you have just adopted, if the facts appear to be more serious. Divestiture in favour of the prosecutor's office of the author's place of residence will promote the latter's presence in the judgement and the pedagogical pronouncement of the sanction. In the case of acts involving more than one individual, for example in the case of discriminatory digital raids, or if, for the same publication, there are several complaints from victims, the High Court, whose prosecutor's office has been specialized, will retain its jurisdiction until the trial. The designation of this single specialized court to deal with these cases will ensure the consistency of the criminal response and the relevant case law. In other words, the system we have designed will allow us to maintain close proximity to incitement to hatred resulting from an isolated fact, but the specialized prosecutor's office will be able to deal with the case in case of digital raids. A word, to conclude, about non-public hate content, for example in the context of text exchanges or emails. In this case, one does not fall within the scope of the Act of 1881 and the case law of the Court of Cassation which makes it possible to organize the concurrent competence of a specialized prosecutor's office cannot play. We therefore need a specific text. This is the subject of this amendment. The question we have asked ourselves is: how can we organize, rationally, the referral of cases to this specialized prosecutorial office without causing a thrombosis of the system, which would lead to the opposite result of the one sought? We have opted for a simple solution, that of the online complaint, which, introduced by the Justice Reform Law, will come into being in a few months, in the first half of 2020. A person who will file an online complaint because he or she has been the victim of non-public hate messages will receive his or her complaint systematically from the specialized prosecutor's office. The latter will perform a triage, in good intelligence with the local public prosecutor's offices: the files that will deserve to be kept by the specialized public prosecutor's office will be kept and those that require local treatment will be referred to the local public prosecutor's office. Organizing the re-entry of files for any form of complaint could be too cumbersome and paralyze the system. In addition, this type of crime will involve people who are open to new technologies. That is why we have retained the competence of the specialised prosecutor's office on the basis of an online complaint. Whether things are clear: in any case, it will always be possible for a person who cannot file an online complaint to go to a police station or a gendarmerie to do so. Together with the Minister of the Interior, we will raise awareness of this issue among the investigative services so that they can accompany the complainants. We have thus built, I believe, a balanced system, by distinguishing between public and private hate content and, in each case, by giving the specialized prosecutor's office the opportunity to deal with cases that deserve to be dealt with at that level.
