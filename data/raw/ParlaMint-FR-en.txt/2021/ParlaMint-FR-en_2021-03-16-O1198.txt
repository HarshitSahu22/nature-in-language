The text, largely based on a study by the Council of State, which we had before us, is intended to make the conditions for territorial experiments more flexible, make them both easier to access, faster to implement and more attractive to the communities. Communities meeting the conditions laid down in the law providing for experimentation will now be able to decide on their own participation, whereas at present, as you know, they can only make a request, the final decision of the Government, which determines by decree the list of communities admitted to participate. This procedure should reduce the average time to enter the experiment, which is currently one year, to two months. The acts taken as part of the experiments will no longer be published for information purposes only in the Official Journal, although this publication now requires their entry into force. In addition, the review of legality will be reduced. At present, all the acts taken in the context of the experiments are subject to a strengthened and derogatory legality check, with a suspensive referral. The draft organic law restricts this special regime only to the decision to enter into the experiment, in order to prevent communities that do not meet the conditions laid down in the law from carrying out its implementation. Second objective: to ensure a more relevant evaluation of experiments. The final evaluation report of each experiment submitted to Parliament is, of course, maintained. The legislator will now have four options at the end of the experimental period. Another corrupt republican principle is equality. In order to exercise competence, it is still necessary to have the means to do so. Only the richest communities will be able to take the risk of taking on new skills and ultimately take advantage of them to the detriment of the poorest territories. Several studies attest to this: differentiation is an obstacle to the correcting mechanism of inequalities induced by decentralization. In addition, even as we regularly call for better readability of public policies, the adoption of this text, which aims to ensure that the law can vary between communities rather than apply uniformly in France, would undeniably add complexity to an already unintelligible right. It would also create legal uncertainty for citizens and local elected officials. All of this would ultimately contribute to reducing the legibility of the competences of each public authority, blurring the responsibilities of each and every other, and thus undermining the precious link that I referred to between the citizens and the communities on which they depend. Finally, the strengthening of differentiation feeds into antagonisms dangerous to the unity of the Republic. New thinking emerges around the fuzzy concept of "territory", used to oppose not only communities to the state, but also communities to each other. Such a vision can only reinforce local particularisms and regionalist demands. [[Ms Bénedicte Taurine applauds]] Finally, as you will have understood, we believe that this text can only lead to greater territorial divides, even though we need to fill them urgently. We will therefore vote against this reform.
