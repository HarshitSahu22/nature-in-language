ParlaMint-FR_2021-05-25-O1283.u349	In the preamble, I would like to thank the majority who supported this bill by allowing me to be in front of you tonight; the professional agricultural organizations with which, for months, I have been writing this text hand in hand; but also the other minority trade unions, which were able to enrich it again last week as a result of numerous exchanges; the Council of State, for its fine analysis of the text and its proposals to improve its conformity with our constitutional principles and European law; my colleagues and their rich contributions; the Ministry of Agriculture and Food, with which we exchanged. I am proud of this proposal for a common law: it is all this collaborative work that makes it legitimate, adapted, operational to regulate access to agricultural land on the corporate market and promote installation. Although a major land law has not come into being, our bill is evidence of our commitment to addressing the identified emergencies in order to preserve our agricultural land. Finally, it is a question of breaking through the abscess of regulation that benefits companies. Indeed, the situation is serious. Between 1988 and 2013, the average area of a farm doubled in France. Between 1955 and 2013, the number of holdings was divided by five. In ten years' time, half of the farm managers will retire and, with them, the risk of further expansion of farms to the detriment of the establishment of new farmers, as excessive concentrations of land in the hands of an owner remain partly uncontrolled. That is my observation. Our regulatory tools, such as land development and rural settlement companies – SAFER – and structural control cannot act on the corporate market. In short, there is an inequality between natural persons and companies that needs to be remedied. There are double standards: if in 1960, when our current tools were created, the fact of membership was anecdotal, today it is at the centre of land change since 60% of agricultural land is held in companies and the transmissions through transfers of shares continue to grow. The Court of Auditors has revealed that these transactions, which numbered 275 in 2014, represented, in 2018, 8611 operations, leading the financial magistrates to refer to the Prime Minister, in which they alert him on "the importance of the stakes [which] no longer accommodates the status quo". The legislator has tried well in the past to counter these practices, but each time he has faced censorship from the Constitutional Council. Therefore, I would like to stress before we go into detail: in order for a system to control the transfer of shares to operate, it must be proportionate. The one I submit to the debate is proportionate because it targets excess without being ineffective. It has a dual objective: to combat land grabbing and excessive concentration in the corporate market; act to remobilize land in order to support the installation and consolidation and to renew the generations of farmers. Who will be concerned? A natural or legal person taking control of a company that owns or operates agricultural land by purchasing social security securities and that would hold agricultural areas exceeding a locally fixed expansion threshold between one and three times the average regional useful agricultural area – SAURM. How does control work? First, the transfer of shares is declared, a formality that already exists. If the threshold is exceeded, it shall be subject to prior authorisation. Secondly, the files are reviewed by the SAFER Technical Committee, which gives a simple opinion to the Prefect. Thirdly, there are several situations: the prefect authorizes the transfer if the project does not affect the vitality of the territory; the prefect authorizes it if the beneficiary undertakes to release a compensatory area for a farmer who settles or consolidates; The prefect refuses it. Thus, from the trigger threshold to the controlled people, to the place of the SAFER, everything has been thought out so that the mechanism is effective, adapted to the territories and the actors concerned. In addition, allow me to elaborate on three topics that will be particularly commented on during the discussion of this text. No, the superfacial threshold for triggering control is not too high: it will be between one and three times the SAURM. However, the threshold for authorisation to operate is between one third and once the SAURM has been established. Why don't you align them? The system does not control the same thing as the structure: we control the exploitation, but also the holding, that is to say the pure uncultivated property. This range shall be established on the basis of a proposal from the agricultural professional organisations after consultation. This threshold is chosen in order not to undermine the flexibility of the member market, which is useful to farmers. We control excess. It is in line with the constitutional and European principles of the right to own property and the freedom to undertake. The Council of State has given us a detailed opinion on this point. No, SAFER will not be a judge and a party and their technical committees are best able to analyse the files; They are the land parliaments in the territories. They are efficient, operational and capable of investigating highly technical cases, which would not necessarily be the case with the departmental agricultural guidance committees – CDOA – whose operation is very uneven according to the territories. The SAFER never takes the decision to authorize or refuse a transfer of shares: it is the prefect who decides after detailed advice from the SAFER Technical Committee. He may or may not follow him; SAFER is therefore not a judge. Finally, I would like to point out that the SAFEs are responsible for a public service delegation and are under the supervision of the State. Yes, we will control family operations. Today, there is an identified risk of exempting family operations leading to excessive concentration. There are drifts. In some departments, some families share the territory, which affects its vitality, but also the environment. In our system, some expensive family operations will be effectively controlled. But controlling does not mean prohibiting: if they are virtuous, assignments will be allowed. I insist: the text does not oppose family transmission, in particular because free transactions do not fall within its scope, but also because we will introduce a conditional family exemption. It is a balanced measure that gives the possibility to control extreme cases that harm the territory, agriculture and its added value. [[Applause on the benches of the LaREM and Dem groups]] In conclusion, the stakes are immense in order to preserve our traditional farming model, the vitality of our territories, the quality of our soils, biodiversity and our food sovereignty. This involves regulating access to land for all individuals and companies. This third regulatory tool, which would be a first at European level, intends to meet this need for our agricultural land for the general interest. No agriculture or living territories without farmers; no farmers without agricultural land.
