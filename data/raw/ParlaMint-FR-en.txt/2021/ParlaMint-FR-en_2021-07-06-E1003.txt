The second circle of protection for the child is that of those with whom he or she has been able to establish ties of attachment strong enough to be able to supplement, or even replace, temporarily or permanently, those with whom the parents cannot, or do not want to establish. I had the opportunity to stress this during our debates on the law of bioethics, a child, whatever it develops, is built, through multiple ties of attachment that are not limited to his mother and father, but can be extended to a grandfather, aunt, cousin, godfather, neighbour. This second circle of protection is insufficiently mobilized in our country – unlike other countries such as Germany, for example – while it plays a fundamental role even in cases of parental presence. Family life goes far beyond the biological link, and it can make even more sense, if not necessary, for a child who suffers from parental deficiency or absence. There is a space, links, between the protective family unit and institutional protection that can serve as a benchmark, a refuge, for the child suffering or at risk, and provide him with the security and stability necessary for his development and development. This was already the meaning of the judicial declaration of parental neglect procedure introduced by the law of 2016, aimed at promoting simple adoption in order to create new stronger attachment ties, without necessarily permanently breaking the filiation with biological parents. It is with the same objective that MP Monique Limon's bill facilitates adoption by foster families, dispensing them from the traditional procedure when the famous "tata" has become a family in its own right. The Child Prevention and Protection Strategy already provides funding for 10,000 sponsors; A majority member will defend an amendment to include sponsorship in the Code of Social Action and Families, in accordance with the wishes of the associations involved in this field. This measure will contribute to the development of multiple links, guaranteeing benchmarks and stability for the child. This second circle gives all its meaning and power to Article 1. It is a question of making institutionalization less systematic, even when, in other fields of protection, there is a vast movement towards inclusion, return to home, where people, their needs, their desires are more heard. The first two protection circles sometimes do not suffice, or more, to respond to the best interests of the child and to ensure the emotional and material security necessary for the child's development. By administrative or judicial means – the two sometimes complement each other – the system of institutional protection then intervenes: it constitutes the third circle of protection. When the institution covers the children with its protective veil, it can only be be benevolent, secure, reassuring and enveloping.
