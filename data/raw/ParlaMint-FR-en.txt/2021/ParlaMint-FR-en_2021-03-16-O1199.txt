The purpose of this bill is finally to make it possible to make real use of territorial experimentation by opening more broadly the too restrictive framework of the 2003 procedure, which effectively and strongly limited this possibility. There are four major provisions in this bill. The first is to simplify the conditions for the initiation of the experiment, in different ways: by terminating the system of prior authorisation to replace it with a procedure whereby any community may decide to enter the experiment by reasoned deliberation, and not by seeking prior authorization from the Government; providing that the prefect, rather than the Government, shall verify, under the usual review of legality, that the community fulfils the legislative and regulatory conditions for participation; by relaxing the special review of the legality applicable to experiments by strictly restricting the prefect's right to apply for suspension from the deliberations of the communities. The second objective is to organise more in accordance with the common law the legal regime of acts taken in the context of experiments. The publication of the act in the Official Journal will be for information purposes and the acts will be subject to review under ordinary law. The third provision, which represents, it has been recalled, an important turning point, offers other possible outcomes to local experiments by coming out of a binary logic – abandonment or generalization. The experiment can thus lead to the continuation of the experimental measures taken for the communities that have participated in the project and wish to pursue it, and to the extension to other communities on a voluntary basis. The law may also amend the provisions governing the exercise of jurisdiction which has been tested. The fourth and final provision, you said, Minister, states that the Government shall submit to Parliament an annual report to monitor the initiatives taken and the experiments carried out and to evaluate the implementation processes. From these provisions, I draw three conclusions. First of all, contrary to what has been heard, we do not question the principle of equality since any community will be able to take advantage of this willingness to experiment by justifying the difference in treatment by a reason of general interest linked to objective differences in situation found on the ground. Better: This equality of rights seeks to bridge inequalities between territories and promote equity. Those elected who have identified a particular difficulty in a territory can take advantage of the experiment to try to remedy it. As François Mitterrand said in a well-known sentence, "France needed a strong and centralized power to make itself possible, it now needs a decentralized power in order not to undo itself" and, I would add, to respond on the ground to the needs of the territories and their inhabitants, in all their diversity.
