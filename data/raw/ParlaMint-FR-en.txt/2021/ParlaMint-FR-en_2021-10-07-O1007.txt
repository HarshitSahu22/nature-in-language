ParlaMint-FR_2021-10-07-O1007.u217	The bill to allow the transfer of the rights entered in the personal training account between account holders, which I have the honour to defend before you, is the result of a long-standing reflection, shared with our fellow citizens. Since I introduced this bill a year and a half ago, I have been asked every week by our fellow citizens, who are impatient to see this measure become a reality. I note that I am not the only one, and I am pleased that some colleagues in the majority are echoing this concern with the Government through written questions. Deeply renovated by the law for the freedom to choose its professional future of 2018, the personal training account is one of the few rights only by hand of employees. It is almost unconditional, since it is sufficient for the employee to apply for eligible training in order to benefit from its financing. Of course, the shift from an hour count to a euro count is the most visible transformation introduced by the 2018 law, a transformation that is not without repercussions on how employees perceive their right to training. This monetization has undoubtedly created an appetite among employees: 600,000 account holders in November 2019, we are on track to reach 3 million users, by the end of 2021. This enthusiasm is widely understandable. The CPF has become a personal right of employees, who can use it without any intermediary other than the MyCompteTraining mobile app. You will agree with me that since the CPF is a right acquired by employees, thanks to all the efforts made during their professional career, it seems quite natural for them to be able to dispose of it as they see fit, especially at the time of their retirement. While the personal training account must, of course, be mobilised as a matter of priority to secure career paths and finance professional training, the social partners, whom I have heard at length during the preparation of this examination, agreed that the training effort could be further improved, in particular to avoid the effects of windfalls, which lead to the financing of language courses, the scope of which is sometimes more than questionable. Nevertheless, the CPF is a right and must remain a personal right of the employee. This proposal is aimed at all those who – and fortunately still many – do not have a career hit, are not threatened by unemployment and are not obliged to start a process of vocational retraining at the end of their careers, in order to avoid dismissal. These employees have opened up training rights, thanks to their work, to the amount of EUR 500 per year, for an employee who has worked more than or equal to half of the legal duration, up to a loan of a ceiling of EUR 5000. This amount is far from negligible. How can we justify, therefore, that some employees actually benefit from this money, while others take their virtual rights with them? It is this contradiction that our bill intends to respond to. I propose, by means of a single article, a simple device: any employee will be able to transfer all or part of the rights entered in his professional training account to another account holder. This proposal is both a justice measure and an efficiency measure. It is an obvious measure of justice that allows to break the inequality of treatment between, on the one hand, the employees who mobilised their CPF and, on the other hand, those who did not need to resort to it. The latter are no less legitimate than the former to claim their dues. This is an effective measure, since the transfer of rights will enable those who need it most to have the necessary funding for their training. I think, of course, in particular, of young people. By definition, young people in the labour force have fewer rights than their elders, in the early stages of their careers. The Caisse des dépôts et consignations recalled during the hearings that the cost of training was around 2000 euros, which represents four years of cumulation of rights. Many young people cannot afford to wait so long for training, especially when it comes to obtaining a driver's licence, which, in some territories, is an imperative if a job is to be found. I would like to dwell here for a moment on the question of the financing of this measure and of the personal training account, in general. I must admit that the hearings conducted under this bill have left me puzzled: on the one hand, everyone welcomes the indisputable rise in power of the CPF, since 2019, which has been encouraged and valued by the Government, but on the other hand, the success of this mechanism risks putting it at risk. The financing of the personal training account is based on a subtle financial balance. Only the training requested by the beneficiaries of a CPF is financed: in other words, employees do not have a pre-funded and funded pool at the Caisse des dépôts et consignations, an operator in charge of financing the CPF. However, the shift from hourly to euro-counting suggests the opposite to employees. Is that to say that the personal training account would ultimately only be a right in trompe l'oeil? The risk of the scheme being unsustainable is such that some have already begun to consider its regulation in order not to say its limitation: the moderator ticket, a reduction in the amount of credit increased annually on the employees' account, a reduction in the number of eligible training courses: we imagine that there is no shortage of ideas to reduce the scope of this hard-won right acquired by employees. Similarly, let us not forget the place of companies in the financing of this scheme. Although neither the single contribution to vocational training and alternance nor the contribution to apprenticeship is sufficient to cover France's cash-flow needs, which are forced to borrow bank loans, employers pay for the CPF. Employee training cannot be the variable for adjusting government deficits. As such, I regret that the impact study of the law on the freedom to choose its professional future of 2018 has not brought any more substantial elements on the macroeconomic effects of the rise in the CPF. For the time being, the forecast for funding of $2.2 billion in 2021 is consistent with the reality of the facts, but what about tomorrow, when the CPF is more deployed? Are we going to reverse and explain to employees that their right to training must be sacrificed because we have not been able to anticipate its cost? We cannot resolve this. On the contrary, through this bill, we make the personal training account a full right.
