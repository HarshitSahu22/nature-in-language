Outsourcing is often mistakenly identified as a abandonment of the public service and, as such, leads to a reluctance on the part of some users, while there is no difficulty in entrusting specialist companies with the support functions stricto sensu which are not part of the core mission of the public service: for example, activities involving a relationship with users, back office activities or other non-specific ones such as restoration, cleaning or building monitoring. I insist: outsourcing is neither a abandonment nor a privatisation of the public service. Indeed, the public authority can remain responsible for public services and entrust their execution to a more efficient private partner thanks to its specialized skills, economies of scale and because it is stimulated by competition. The IDU and independent group considers that public authorities are not always the best players in carrying out certain tasks in various fields. This is where the private sector can and must intervene. The example of vaccination is striking: the initiative of platforms such as Doctolib or Vite Ma Dose has undoubtedly helped to speed up and simplify the campaign. These tools probably could not have been created by the administration, which questions our bureaucracy. In March 2019, during the review of the Public Service Transformation Bill, a report on outsourcing in public services was submitted to Gérald Darmanin, then Minister of Public Action and Accounts. He indicated that the costs of public services in France amounted to almost 400 billion euros, or 18% of GDP, and outlined the modalities of a New Deal for public services that would represent a potential savings of 25 billion euros, while ensuring a quality of service at least equal. Economic studies even show that budgetary gains of 20 to 40 per cent can be expected. Up to €6 billion in savings could even be achieved within 10 years, by offsetting the non-replacement of one in two retirements through outsourcing. This is an argument to consider as we have just passed through an economic and fiscal vacuum in 2020 and 2021. The question of optimising the functioning of the State is essential. What about the work of the Public Action 2022 committee, says CAP 2022, which was presented at the beginning of the quinquennium and which encountered the difficulty of drawing up a new organisation of public services while reducing expenditure? Of course, it is important not to allow outsourcing to develop drastically in the area of regal functions, because the question of weakening the strategic capacity of our administration and the loss of autonomy of our public power could then arise. His supervision is therefore necessary. Even if we share some of your reservations, the opportunity to assess the economic, social and budgetary cost of outsourcing public action, carried out in the form of public service delegations or services, is certainly interesting, but not necessarily urgent, while a very detailed first report on the subject was given in 2019. We are not against it, but when we read your explanatory statement, we do not share the reasons behind your motion for a resolution. For all these reasons, the IDU-I group will abstain.
