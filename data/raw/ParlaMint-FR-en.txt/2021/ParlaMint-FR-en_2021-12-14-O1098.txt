ParlaMint-FR_2021-12-14-O1098.u596	Article 128 of the ELAN Act extended the possibility of co-location in the social park, allowing any household to apply for a co-location, where this option was previously reserved for certain audiences, particularly young people. The use of co-location can be a solution in some territories, in particular allowing active households, some of which may belong to so-called essential categories of workers, to access housing more quickly. However, the application of this measure imposes the status of roommate on certain categories of households. The status of co-owner in the social park is reserved for certain situations: cohabitants, married, pacsés, siblings, ascendants or descendants... Households not included in this list are forced to co-locate. Persons must therefore sign their own leases and be separated individually as part of a co-location. If one of them leaves the home, the lessor may impose a new occupant. This provision is inappropriate for households that choose solidarity in the context of their housing occupation. The status of roommate must remain a choice, expressed by the household at the time of application, and must not be imposed. Imagine two or three cousins who develop together and find themselves trapped because they do not fall into the category of those who have the right to be co-owners! The purpose of this amendment is therefore to reserve the co-location for applicants who so wish and request, and to leave it to the co-applicants to opt for the status of roommates or co-owners. It will be for example an uncle and his nephew, friends, cousins...
