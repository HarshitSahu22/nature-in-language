ParlaMint-FR_2021-01-19-O1132.u245	Today we are considering an organic bill that touches on the very foundation of our Republic, since it deals with the election that allows the President of the Republic to be elected – an election that, in our institutions, is one of the cornerstones of democracy. Democracy is obviously not just about the presidential election, but, as General de Gaulle said, "the keystone of our regime is the new institution of a President of the Republic appointed by reason and by the sentiment of the French to be the Head of State". If democracy is played not only at the time of the election, but every day of the quinquennium it inaugurates, the presidential election is an important event for the French that occurs once every five years. It is this unique moment when the people make the choice of the person who will hold the presidential office. Our debates on this text will therefore have a singular resonance for the elected representatives you are, for the democrats we are, and I am sure they will have a certain solemnity. As you know, the rules governing the election of the President of the Republic do not appear as such in the electoral code, since they are laid down in the provisions of Act No. 62-1292 of 6 November 1962, which accompanied the speech I have just quoted. That is why, at least one year before the election, an organic law usually updates this framework to make it applicable to legislative developments since its last amendment. The examination of an organic law amending the 1962 law is therefore a recurrent exercise which takes place before each presidential election. This update also takes into account the comments made by all stakeholders in the presidential election, including the Constitutional Council. It is precisely the purpose of the draft organic law that has been examined in the Committee on Laws and which I have the honour to present to you: it is to make the provisions of the ordinary electoral code applicable and to specify some specific rules that apply particularly to this election. As always, this bill updates the references to the articles of the electoral code to make them applicable in their latest version. This update, which is essentially technical in scope, only applies to the presidential elections the rules governing other elections. As an illustration, I would like to draw your attention to the two recent developments in our electoral law that will apply to the presidential election. This is first of all the restoration of the voting rights of adults under guardianship, voted under the law of 23 March 2019 on programming 2018-2022 and reform for justice. This reflects the Government's commitment to ensuring the most active participation of persons with disabilities in democratic and civic life. This is then the application of the emergency procedure, introduced by the Act of 22 December 2018 on the fight against the manipulation of information. This law has created the possibility to refer the case to a judge for interim measures to stop the dissemination of false information during the election campaign. In addition to these amendments, this draft organic law introduces new provisions aimed at securing this election, modernizing the modalities of the financial campaign and promoting the electoral participation of all citizens. Security of the organization first of all, by setting in law a deadline for the publication of the decree for the convocation of voters, which will take place at least ten weeks before the date of the first round of the ballot. In fact, unlike other elections, the date of publication of the decree calling for the presidential election, which triggers the collection period of sponsorships, is not currently regulated by any text. The inclusion of this provision in the law is therefore such as to secure this operation, since potential candidates know in advance, and in a certain way, the time they will have. Security in the organization then, by postponing to a date fixed by decree, by 1 January 2027 at the latest, the entry into force of the possibility of transmitting sponsorships by dematerialized means to the Constitutional Council. This option was introduced into the law in 2016, by way of an amendment, without prior feasibility study.
