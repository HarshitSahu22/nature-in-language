ParlaMint-FR_2021-10-12-O1011.u390	They were tabled by my colleague Dominique Potier; the first is already well known under the name "factor twelve". The aim is to limit wage differentials in companies, from one to twelve for Amendment No 642 and from one to twenty for Amendment No 2051, which is a fallback amendment. The first tends to ensure that no one earns more in a month than another in a year. The earnings ratio in France is 104:1: to earn what managers earn in a month, the least well paid employees have to work 104 months, i.e. eight years and eight months. This is an average; Some executives earn much more. In the current state of law, it is impossible to impose a ratio of one to twelve. The amendment is therefore intended to create a tax incentive by limiting staff costs deductible from taxable profit to salaries not exceeding 12 times the company's lowest wage. Companies will be able to continue paying their employees more, but will no longer be able to deduct the sums concerned from their profits. In order to keep earnings above this ceiling, they will have to raise the lowest wages, otherwise their tax will increase. Adopting one of these amendments would therefore provide the State with additional resources to finance actions aimed at reducing inequalities.
