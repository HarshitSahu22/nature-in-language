I hope Mr. de Courson will forgive me for responding in advance. The Government's amendment, No. 3404, clarifies the categorization of public managers. The agents of the local social security funds, who are under private law, are affected by the reform as they are currently within the scope of the Court of Budgetary and Financial Discipline (CDBF). It also provides that the scope of the reform includes all personal and financial liability regimes, including those not covered by section 60 of the Finance Act for 1963. This concerns the accountants of the social security funds, but also the military treasurers. The aim of this reform is to create a unified accountability regime for public managers. The framework we are proposing to reform today is based on a public accountants' RPP put at stake by the Court of Auditors and the Regional Chambers of Auditors (CRC), and a responsibility of the public managers of the Court of Budgetary and Financial Discipline. We want to change this system. This is the culmination of old and converging reflections. On the accounting side, personal and financial liability is based on a comprehensive approach, at the first euro, which does not allow the most significant transactions to be targeted. For managers, the liability regime remains limited and suffers from lengthy procedural delays. It is these two defects that we want to correct. We therefore propose a reform drawn up in consultation with the financial judge, that is, the Court of Auditors, but also with the Council of State. It is based on a new judicial organisation: a single chamber within the Court of Auditors, comprising judges of the Court and judges of the regional and territorial chambers of accounts, will deal with cases at first instance; a Court of Appeal, chaired by the first President of the Court of Auditors, shall consist of four members of the Council of State, four members of the Court of Auditors and two qualified persons appointed for their experience in the field of public management. The appeal will be suspensive, and the Council of State will remain the judge of cassation. In this new framework, the intervention of the judge will be less frequent: it will focus on errors of a particular gravity with a real financial impact and on those that have caused significant financial harm. We are thinking here of mistakes whose gravity leads to a change in the large balances of an organization's budget or accounts, thereby undermining the sincerity of financial reporting. Such infringements may be punished by fines linked to the level of remuneration, but also by a new penalty prohibiting the exercise of the functions of accountant or authorising officer. This sanction regime will be linked with the deployment of a managerial response, which will punish errors or errors that are not the responsibility of the judge. This is a sign of our confidence in management to sanction unintentional management errors that have not caused significant harm. I stress that the scope of the litigants concerned will be strictly the same as that of the current CBDF: there is no restriction or increase of the perimeter. I say this in particular in response to the debate on the possible extension of this new regime of responsibility to local elected officials. The reform does not go back to the separation of the authorising officer and the accounting officer, a fundamental principle of our system and a guarantee of security for authorising officers, reliability and accounting quality. The controls of accountants will therefore be maintained, but within a new framework, refocused on the most important issues. Last point: the reform retains the de facto management offence, in order to punish people who handle public funds without having the status of public accountant. I hope that these few elements will provide answers to the amendments tabled. As you have understood, on this article, the Government will only support the drafting amendments of the rapporteur-general.
