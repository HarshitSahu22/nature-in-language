ParlaMint-FR_2021-06-30-O1324.u690	Article 18 tends to create a crime. The new offence of endangering the life of others is punishable by three years' imprisonment and a fine of 45,000 euros. The offence is based on the meeting of two elements. The first is material: "to reveal, disseminate or transmit, by any means whatsoever, information relating to the private, family or professional life of a person identifying or locating him or her". The second relates to intent, since the information must have been transmitted for the purpose of exposing the person or members of his family "to a direct risk of harm to the person or property that the author could not ignore". The offence is likely to be characterized, regardless of whether or not the disclosure is effective. Thus, the new offence does not relate to acts committed, but to a given intention; Consequently, the wording creates a risk of interpreting the criminal intent which the author could not ignore. In addition, it should be noted that the offence against the person and the offence against the property are placed on the same plane. As the Syndicat des avocats de France notes, the sanction is disproportionate and inconsistent with comparable offences. The amendments made to the special committee did not address the concerns about the risks posed by this mechanism. The scope of article 18 is both very broad and imprecise. The blurring of the notions of "information" and "direct risk of harm" "that the author could not ignore" can lead to difficulties of interpretation and undermine freedom of expression. In addition, this new offence appears to be unnecessary under existing legislation and is likely to be subject to difficulties of assessment. The current legislative arsenal is sufficiently precise to suppress the conduct targeted. Thus, the law in force penalizes breaches of privacy, cyber-harassment, defamation, disclosure of identity for the purpose of harming, including on social networks. For all these reasons, we propose the deletion of this article.
