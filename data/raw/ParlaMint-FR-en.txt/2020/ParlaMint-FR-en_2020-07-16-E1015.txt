It protects privacy from cybercontrol and supervises parental authority to protect children of a violent parent. It was enriched during the parliamentary debate, in particular by our Socialist colleagues in the Senate, who wanted to allow tenants who were victims of domestic violence to benefit from a one-month notice period to leave their homes, like other people in vulnerable situations. However, because the subject has been declared a "major cause of the quinquennium" and society has mobilized as never before, thanks to the # MeToo movement and the media that have brought the issue to the public arena, we would have liked a great law against violence against women, which would have included, for example, the issue of economic violence or included the topics of anti-sexism, sexual violence and sexual and reproductive rights. We regret to discuss only a text that, despite real progress – which we welcome – continues to segment the different dimensions of women's rights. Furthermore, as you know, we particularly regret that the advances made by our fellow socialist senators and senators have not been retained in the Joint Joint Committee, as they greatly improve the order for the protection of victims and make it impossible to undermine the decree issued by the Ministry of Justice on May 27, 2020, which many of us have denounced. We deplore this step backwards. Above all, there is a problem with one provision: it is the lifting of medical secrecy. Specialists in domestic violence mechanisms are fiercely opposed to it, because if reporting is good, what happens next? What happens if a woman refuses to testify? What if she's threatened? What happens if the complaint is closed? The problem is not so much reporting as what we do, and our ability to ensure, as far as possible, the protection of a woman reporting what she has suffered. As you will understand, we would have wanted to have voted tonight a real law to transform the status of women, which is not only based on society and its mobilization, but also provides the support of politicians and public authorities. However, we will vote in favour of this text, of course, both for us and for you, the cause is a historic and unfortunately never-completed struggle. However, our moral requirement leads us to urge the Government and the new Deputy Minister responsible for this issue not to be content with one more text. We are not done with the collective understanding of the phenomenon and with public action against domestic violence – against violence against women and children. While the President of the Republic referred to a "red thread" in his statement of 14 July, the number of femicides appears above all as an indelible red spot in the balance sheet of our public policies.
