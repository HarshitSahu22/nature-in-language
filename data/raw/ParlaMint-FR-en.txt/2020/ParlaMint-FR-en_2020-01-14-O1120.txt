This text will fill an important legal gap. It is updating our long-awaited and necessary legislation. As you know, the French Republic grants the ultramarine territories more or less autonomy according to the status and wishes expressed by the people. The communities of the Pacific – New Caledonia and French Polynesia – have broad autonomy under articles 74 and 76 of the Constitution. They have the opportunity to create criminal offences and to impose sanctions on them.
ParlaMint-FR_2020-01-14-O1120.u321	We learn it every day!
ParlaMint-FR_2020-01-14-O1120.u322	In New Caledonia, this jurisdiction is regulated by the Organic Statutory Act of 19 March 1999. The Congress of New Caledonia and the three provincial assemblies may create criminal offences in their own areas of competence and impose sanctions on them. However, this option is regulated by a specific procedure: the approval by Parliament of prison sentences. In the absence of approval, only fines and additional penalties provided for by local regulations may be imposed. This intervention by the legislator is due to two main reasons. First of all, it seeks to verify that the conditions laid down by the organic laws – the length of imprisonment incurred must not exceed the maximum period fixed by the positive law for a similar offence – are fulfilled. It then allows the State to exercise its jurisdiction over criminal justice and the protection of individual freedoms. The mechanism was declared in conformity with the Constitution by the Constitutional Council, which, in its decision of 28 July 2011, affirmed that the certification scheme "implements the competence of the State in criminal law matters". In accordance with this procedure, this bill provides for the homologation of various prison sentences created by Caledonian institutions in recent years. It is the result of an important work carried out with the Chancellery – which I thank for its effectiveness – to identify the various sentences awaiting approval and above all to ensure that the penalties provided for comply with the organic and constitutional rules governing their approval at the national level. First, it was necessary to verify that the imposition of penalties could only take place in an area within the jurisdiction of the territory. Secondly, we had to ensure that the quantum of the penalty did not exceed that provided for in the ordinary law for a similar offence. Finally, we have ensured respect for the classification of offences, which prohibits the localization of an offence if it falls within the ordinary scope of the treaty. As for the substance, the issue that you are referring to, which has been raised for many years by the Comptroller General of Places of Deprivation of Liberty and by the authors of the various missions dealing with the subject, requires special attention, which the Government has, in fact, given to him. For example, prison overcrowding has already fallen sharply, from 300% in 2011 to 130% by the end of 2019. The Justice Programming and Reform Act you have adopted also includes a number of measures whose implementation should improve the situation. The opening in Koné, mentioned by the rapporteur, of a new facility that can accommodate 120 prisoners will also reduce the overcrowding of Camp East. The study phase has been completed; the consultation of enterprises has begun; the work is to start in February, for delivery at the end of 2021. Following Mrs Imbert-Quaretta's report, the choice was also made to rehabilitate the buildings at Camp East. It's not always easy because of the climate. We have decided to restructure the areas of the detention centre. Many renovations have been undertaken since November 2017 – I was on the scene at the time with the Chair of the Law Committee and the Rapporteur. I will not list it here, but know that an investment of about EUR 30 million was spent on the establishment, whose operating expenses also increased by 70% between 2011 and 2018. Finally, criminal policy has evolved in recent years. I have always thought, in fact, that we could not stick to the building component – construction and rehabilitation – without also rethinking criminal policy. It has therefore been adapted to take into account, precisely, the overcrowding in prisons. Two territorial criminal policy circulars, in 2013 and 2017, stressed the need to continue efforts to reduce the rate of incarceration. The 2018 Annual Report of the Public Prosecutor's Office of the Nouméa Court of Appeal reflects this development, at three levels. At the stage of criminal prosecution, first of all, magistrates prefer, in particular with regard to road and drug offences, the way to criminal order, and require fines. Alternatives to prosecution are also recommended where the degree of seriousness of the offence permits, i.e. in more than 41% of cases. At the stage of the hearing, the magistrates of the seat were then made aware of the need to impose, where possible, ab initio sentences and alternative sentences to imprisonment. Finally, at the stage of the execution of sentences, the Public Prosecutor's Office has a very proactive policy of adjusting sentences – in 2018 326 such decisions have been issued, which is not bad – and only incarcerates persons sentenced to prison terms of up to one year only when there is no alternative. The 2018 Annual Activity Report of Nouméa's Penal Enforcement Service highlights the efforts made to ensure the processing of sentencing applications within four months, including by increasing the number of hearings. The rapporteur has also rightly pointed out that offers of work of general interest have been developed and diversified through work with the country so as to link them to custom, which is very interesting. While it remains necessary to continue our action, because all the consequences of these measures are not stabilized at the time we are talking, I repeat: important efforts have been made to improve the conditions of imprisonment in New Caledonia.
