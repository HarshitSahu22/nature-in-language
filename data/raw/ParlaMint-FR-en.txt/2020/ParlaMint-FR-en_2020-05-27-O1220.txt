Better information on agricultural food products has long been requested by French farmers; It could encourage consumers to engage in more virtuous, patriotic, environmentally friendly behaviour. The article on the labelling of beer bottles is a good thing, since it will allow consumers not to be misled when buying. The traceability of all these products must of course be generalised as regards their origin, production conditions, conditions of rearing and slaughter, and the location of production. All this is very important for the preservation of French know-how. The issue of honey is a cruel illustration of China's economic battle. The French consume four times as much honey as the country produces, and among the honeys they consume are products denatured by the addition of sugar. I don't know if people know, only 15% of Chinese honey fits our definition of honey. Yet, it is still present in the feeding rays. In this respect, information will not suffice: Chinese productions should be taxed and producers who add too much sugar to the product punished. However, the ultraliberal globalist ideology prevents us from doing so, by the laws of the World Trade Organization. The covid-19 pandemic acted, as we have seen, as the revealing of national flaws. Our country is no longer sovereign. The protection of our food sovereignty must therefore be a top priority of all political decision-makers for our economy, for our farmers, for our ecology. Indeed, common-sense measures such as those contained in this text will not be enough to stem the spiral that has been set in motion by the lack of public support and, above all, by the free trade treaties, which leave our agriculture defenceless in the face of international competition. Nevertheless, I would like to welcome this progress, which, in my opinion, will mark with a stone the future developments for our agriculture and the defence of local production.
