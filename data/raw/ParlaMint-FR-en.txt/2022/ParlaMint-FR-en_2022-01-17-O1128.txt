ParlaMint-FR_2022-01-17-O1128.u4	The provision in article 9 bis follows the enactment last August of the Bioethics Act. Among other measures, this law allows two women to make use of the LDC – medically assisted reproduction – by ensuring the establishment of a dual bond of filiation for the child. To this end, the mother of intention and the biological mother undertake before notary at the beginning of the reproductive journey. Prior to the enactment of the Act, the mother of intent had no choice but to adopt her wife's child to establish her bond of filiation. However, in a number of cases, the couple of women separated before initiating the adoption procedure, and the biological mother no longer wants to consent to the adoption, nor to enter into a procedure for joint and retroactive recognition of the child before a notary – as permitted by the Bioethics Act within three years of its promulgation. This situation is detrimental to the child, who has established a unique bond of filiation with his biological mother, but who is prevented from benefiting from the same link with the one who is also at the origin of the procreation project and who was involved in its conception at birth and then during its first years of life. For this reason, article 9 bis provides that a judge may order the adoption of the child by the mother of intention, provided that the mother of intention has demonstrated her involvement and presence with the biological mother, and notwithstanding the latter's refusal. Such adoption will not remove any rights from the biological mother, since it will be an adoption between spouses or cohabitants. This will enable the child to be raised by both mothers, both biological and intentional, in accordance with the joint procreation project, and benefit from a dual successional vocation. This provision is derogating from the ordinary law, which provides that adoption may be pronounced only with the consent of the parents, unless the parents have disinterested in the child, which is obviously not the case for the biological mothers referred to in this text. That is why this arrangement will be transitional and provisional: it will be open only within three years of the enactment of this Act. A balance is thus found to allow the child to benefit from a double bond of filiation.
