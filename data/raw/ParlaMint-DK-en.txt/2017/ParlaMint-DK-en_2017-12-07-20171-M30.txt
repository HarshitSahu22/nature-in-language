Therefore, if a base below corporate tax is to effectively prevent tax competition between EU countries, it will involve both a harmonised bottom below the corporate tax rate and common rules for the corporate tax base, thus ensuring that competition does not simply shift to the element that is not bottomed. Such harmonisation poses a number of challenges. Firstly, it will limit Member States' ability to organise the corporate tax system according to national needs and wishes. It is the government's view that direct taxation falls within the competence of the Member States of the European Union and that, at EU level, harmonisation efforts should be extremely reluctant in terms of how EU countries' tax laws are organised. Secondly, a common bottom below corporate tax will not prevent tax competition from countries outside the EU, including European countries such as Switzerland and Norway and perhaps also Britain after Brexit. Thirdly, harmonisation of the corporate tax base may present a number of specific challenges in terms of protecting the Danish corporate tax revenue. We can see this in the negotiations on the Commission's proposal for a common corporate tax base, called the CTB proposal, which aims to establish a common set of EU rules for calculating the taxable income of multinational groups, i.e. companies present in a number of countries. With the proposal, for example, Denmark will: could not keep the current rules against tax avoidance. Furthermore, the proposed common tax base is narrower than the Danish one, and if it is adopted in the form in which it now lies, it will mean that Denmark unintentionally loses a revenue and perhaps also a very substantial revenue. Before working towards such harmonisation of corporate tax, it is therefore important to know what the content of harmonisation can lead to before taking a decision on it. Finally, the government is not convinced that there is a current acute problem in the European Union, which means that we have to work for a common bottom below corporate tax, which involves the risks and challenges mentioned. The authors of the motion for a resolution state that Holland, Sweden and the United Kingdom have announced that they will reduce corporate tax in the coming years, and it can be added that the United States has adopted a tax reform which significantly reduces corporate tax in the United States and is also a country with which Denmark is in competition. There have indeed been statements from some in Britain that company tax should be relaxed when they leave the European Union. However, I am not aware that concrete steps have been taken to implement it at this stage. For both Sweden and the Netherlands, it is also considered that the reduction of the corporate tax rate is combined with an extension of the base, which in practice does not lead to significant changes in relation to the level of corporate tax. This brings Sweden and the Netherlands closer to the Danish corporate tax model, which involves a relatively broad corporate tax base. If we look at the income from corporation tax over the years, we can see that there is no serious concern about the erosion of Danish corporate tax revenue either. Since 1990, this has increased from approx. One-and-a-half percent. of GDP at 21⁄2%. of GDP today, and from approx. 3% of the tax revenue to approx. 5% of the tax revenue. So while I am aware of the concern and welcome the debate, the government cannot support the resolution that we are actively working for a common bottom. Instead, Denmark should focus and use its efforts to influence the EU proposals in the field of taxation that are currently and will be under discussion in the EU in the near future. This applies to proposals in which we have significant interests at stake: proposals for a common corporate tax base, a common consolidated corporate tax base, reporting obligations for tax advisors; the forthcoming proposal for taxation of the digital economy and, last but not least, the proposals for the final VAT system. This assessment by the government will be the most effective way in which we can work on the Danish side in the EU to limit and combat illegal tax evasion and secure Danish tax revenue.
