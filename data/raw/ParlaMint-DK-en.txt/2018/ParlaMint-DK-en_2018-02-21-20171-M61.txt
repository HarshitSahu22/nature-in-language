ParlaMint-DK_20180221154340	Thank you, as well as thank you to the rapporteurs, I do not know whether you can say for the debate, but at least for the receipt of the bill, for which I have noted that there is very broad support. After all, the draft law we are discussing does not involve major reforms, but picks up a number of recent years' actions in the field of asylum, as several rapporteurs have also noted. In other words, we have looked at whether there have been any shortcomings which can then be resolved in a simple way, all in order to ensure the right balance between the rules in the area of accommodation and subsistence. At the same time, the Danish rules on the calculation of the duration of the entry ban are adjusted to comply with EU law. Firstly, the draft law proposes the introduction of an automatic referral of complaints to the Refugee Board on the decisions taken by the National Council on the withdrawal and refusal of the extension of refugee residence permits. This ensures that it is not possible to deliberately extend the stay in Denmark by complaining at a very late time, while at the same time remedying some practicalities in relation to the Foreigners' Board and the municipal commitment. At the same time, it also has the positive effect that, overall, the cases will get through the complaint system more quickly and that the individual foreigner will thus have a definitive clarification of his or her situation more quickly. It is good to bring in democracy with breast milk, if I may say so! In this way, the police will also have better conditions for the work on the expulsion of foreigners whose residence permit is finally withdrawn today. Secondly, it is proposed that asylum seekers should not be able to work and live outside asylum centres if they are sentenced to detention and the like. The proposal therefore means that persons who are convicted of crime but cannot, because of their mental condition, be sentenced to a general prison sentence are treated in the same way as other criminals in this context. Such judgments already have consequences for the person concerned in a number of other areas in the field of foreign law, among others. the possibility of permanent residence permits. In addition, a number of points are also proposed which look at the way in which it is actually administered in practice and which are written into the law. This means that underage asylum seekers can stay with any resident parents soon after arriving in Denmark. This is equivalent to what is legally applicable to spouses. At the same time, in this situation, the National Immigration Agency allows a minor asylum seeker to move out to the parents. If this means that parents have to comply with their obligation to the child under the bill, in these situations the Foreigners' Board will no longer have to bear the cost of the allowance. Finally, as in the past, the National Council for Foreign Affairs must assess, on its own initiative, that an extension can be granted to refugees who have been granted temporary protection status in this country as their residence permits expire. Furthermore, the draft law proposes that the municipalities should be systematically informed of the outcome when the Aliens' Board and the Refugee Board have dealt with complaints about decisions on withdrawal, refusal of extension and withdrawal of residence permits. Last but not least, I should like to mention, as I did a moment ago, an adjustment to how entry bans are permanently calculated. In conclusion, I would like once again to thank the rapporteurs for receiving this bill. I understand that there will be some clarification questions in writing afterwards, and I will of course answer them as soon as possible.
