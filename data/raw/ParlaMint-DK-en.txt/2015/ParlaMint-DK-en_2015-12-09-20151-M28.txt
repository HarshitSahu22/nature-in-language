In 2008, it was agreed in the EU with effect from 7 January 2011 that all passenger and goods vehicles should be equipped with special daytime running lamps. The special daytime running lamps have a lower energy effect than the passing beam and the special daytime running lamps are therefore more energy-friendly than the passing beam headlamps. In fact, however, the requirement for special daytime running lamps was also introduced in order to improve road safety in the EU, for it was not a requirement in many EU countries that the passing beam of cars should be switched on outside the lighting time. However, the special daytime running lamps brought with them another problem, since the International ECE Regulation No. 48-04 was a prohibition that other lights were lit on the vehicle when the special driving light was switched on. This was also true of the rear position lamps, and the reason for this was that the stop lamps of the cars were not clear enough outside the lighting time when the rear position lamps were lit. This has affected and led to new EU type-approved vehicles after 7 February 2011, when the requirement for the special daytime running lamps came into force, not automatically turning on the rear position lamps when the special daytime running lamps are switched on. It is therefore the requirement of the ECE Regulation that some cars run without light in the rear of the lamps outside the lighting time. The Danish drivers have been particularly affected by this, since they have always been used to automatically lighting the rear lights. However, the problem is recognised both internationally and by the EU, which has led to a revision of the said ECE Regulation 48-04.
