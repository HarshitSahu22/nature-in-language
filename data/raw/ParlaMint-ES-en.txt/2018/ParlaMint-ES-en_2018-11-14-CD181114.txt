His idea of public interventionism is as extremist as it is obsolete. So much so that there are only examples of this form of government in countries such as Venezuela. You start from the idea of the exclusive management of the integral water cycle as if each municipality had the resources for capture, transport, potabilization, distribution, sale, purification and pouring. Again, they fall into a remarkable lack of awareness of the reality of our country, in a simplistic reductionism that moves away from the complexity of a serious and safe management framework, as must be the case for water. They also call for priority to be given to uses of human consumption with environmental sustainability criteria. Fortunately, ladies and gentlemen, Royal Legislative Decree 1/2001, approving the consolidated text of the Water Act, already covers these obligations. They also call for recognition of access to basic drinking water and sanitation services as a human right. But, Mrs. Ramos, what country do you think you live in? In the UN, the European Water Charter and the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union take this access as essential. Water supply is compulsory in all Spanish homes, as provided for in the Law on the Basis of the Local Regime, since 1985. You also mix the management of climate change in the third point of your motion, perhaps on the basis of the Minister's suggestion that you call for the Sustainable Development Goals, the UN SDGs. Of course, comprehensive hydrological planning will have to be taken into account to deal with climate change adaptation plans, and we hope that this will be done by the announced climate change law of the Minister of Transition for the Environment, at least as was done by the People's Party bill which you have been responsible for dismissing in this very Chamber. With regard to the abolition of the concessional canon, section 4 of your motion, it seems logical that it is not envisaged in cases of fully municipalized services, although if you look at cases where municipalized management exists, you might find some surprise. Obviously, it must be in accordance with the terms and conditions laid down in each case of the concession, although we must understand that in all cases it must be deducted from the income generated through the fees, not taxes, the costs due to the provision of the service and, in the case of the provision of the service by a company, its right to the corresponding industrial benefit is evident. In contrast, you propose that more expenditure be incorporated into the control and supervision of the concessionaire, but, Mrs Ramos, do you want or do you not want a concession? When you want to control the dealership, as is the case in most cases, that action is incorporated in the package, but you will not pretend that this control is paid for by the dealership for its benefits. It also proposes an amendment to the Public Sector Contracts Act, paragraphs 5 and 6 of its motion, on two issues which again fall within the exclusive scope of the contract specification, Mrs Ramos. The inclusion of social, gender and sustainability criteria is already the subject of public procurement, sustainable and innovative, and is already fully embedded in the new Public Sector Contracts Act. Finally, Mrs Ramos, you propose the definition of a system of common indicators for the monitoring of something that is not homogeneous, and you are again motivated by your ignorance of reality. Nothing has to do with the capture of hydrological resources from the Confederation of the Ebro with that of the Guadalquivir. Orography, the efficiency of the treatments of the processes of potabilization, the hardness of the waters and, finally, the conditions of discharge are susceptible to notable variations according to the receiving medium. To all this you must add the particularities of territorial planning in the autonomous communities, as well as the different figures of protection of the territory, including the Natura 2000 Network. All this significantly complicates the implementation of a unified system of indicators for the provision of this service, although I recognize that this could be a matter of study. For this reason, we do not understand the motion put forward by the Podemos Parliamentary Group in any of its paragraphs as justified. From the People's Party we understand that we must choose at every moment the best option for the supply and purification of the public water service - public, mixed or in full concession - for a diagnosis of the situation shared with the municipalities, cabildos and provinces and, ultimately, in all the policies that we have been developing in our government action, as well as in the actions that we are now developing since the opposition. Thank you very much. [[Applauds]]
