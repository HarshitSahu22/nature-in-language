Ladies and gentlemen, I have the honour to appear in this House to request your support for the validation of the Royal Decree-Law regulating cash withdrawal commissions in ATMs. The purpose of this rule is to ensure the proper functioning of payment services. An essential role for the smooth development of commercial and financial transactions. As you know, in recent months certain changes in the usual system of charging commissions by banks had led to distortions by imposing a double commission on consumers, one by the issuing bank, i.e. the customer's bank, and the other by the bank that owns the cashier. This has also shown that the current regime creates legal uncertainty as to who could and could not, ultimately who was actually providing the service to the cashier's user. The Government has therefore decided to undertake this legal change in order to ensure that the service between payment service providers and users is carried out with competence, transparency and trust between the parties, while preserving the principle of legal certainty at all times. Ladies and gentlemen, before detailing to you the specific content of this royal decree-law, it is important to refer, first of all, to the relevance of this sector in our society. The Spanish banking system is characterized by a great capillaryness of its cashier network. There are more than 50,000, which means one cashier per 1,000 inhabitants, a figure much higher than that of the countries in our environment. In addition, each of them carries out a total of 18,000 annual transactions, giving a total of 900 million annual transactions, and of these more than 20 % correspond to cash withdrawals in cash machines that are not part of the customer's usual bank network. As you see, this is an undisputed amount, which undoubtedly required us to act as a matter of urgency to establish a new system that would restore the principles of security and competition. By means of this royal decree-law we establish a new model through four fundamental premises: first, the cashiers' owners will not be able to charge any commission to customers of other entities when they withdraw cash from their cashiers. Secondly, the cashier's owning entities may require this commission from the card-issuing entity of the cashier's user, i.e. the bank of the cashier's master or lady. Thirdly, the card-issuing entity will have a decision whether or not to pass this commission on to its client, but under no circumstances will it be able to charge above what it has paid. Fourthly, a number of mechanisms are in place to ensure that the model ensures the necessary transparency and preserves the principle of competition. Thus, the cashiers' owners must inform the users of the commission that they are going to charge their bank and this is, as I have explained, the maximum commission that the bank can pass on to them. In addition, commissions agreed between entities are subject to the principle of non-discrimination and to the supervision of the National Commission on Markets and Competition. This implies that where the institutions do not reach an agreement on the fixing of the commission, the commission determining the owner of the cashier, in respect of a particular issuing entity, will have to be the same throughout the national territory, so no discrimination can be made. The National Commission on Markets and Competition will also have to report annually to the Ministry of Economy on these agreements and decisions. The first report has to be ready before the end of the first half of next year and basically communicate whether the agreements reached respect the competition rules or impose some kind of barriers or barriers to entry. As regards the period of adaptation of the institutions, it is stipulated that before 1 January next year this model must be in force, a transitional period for banks, issuer card institutions and cashiers to be able to adapt. However, the Government has wanted to speed up consumer protection to the maximum and has banned the possibility of charging a double commission since the entry into force of the rule on 3 October. Since that day, it has also been prohibited for the issuing entity to pass on to its client more than the cashier's holding entity has charged to it. In conclusion, with this model we have achieved two objectives. Firstly, we have prevented the citizen from being charged a double commission, as has been the case in recent months. Secondly, the cost to the user will be minimised, preserving at all times the principle of legal certainty, free competition, transparency and trust between the parties. Ladies and gentlemen, I would like to point out that this royal decree joins with other measures taken by the Government in the area of commissions. First of all, I would remind you that in July 2014 we limited the fees applicable to card payments, especially of a small amount. Thus, since the entry into force of this measure, the fees charged in card transactions have fallen by about 60 % for credit payments and by almost 85 % in debt cases. In addition, with this royal decree-law we advance European legislation by ensuring the existence of safe, efficient, competitive and innovative payments that drive consumption. Secondly, in August last year we approved the reduction of the maximum statutory fees for the management and deposit of pension schemes. The aim was to adapt them to the reduction, to the low-interest environment in which we live. Thirdly, in the area of transparency, we have taken important steps to strengthen the protection of mortgage debtors, as well as of users of certain financial products. At the same time it respects the investment and maintenance that the owners of the cashiers do, which will also have an impact on a better service to their customers. In short, double commission is prevented and a cheaper service is established with greater control by the supervisory authorities. For this reason, ladies and gentlemen, I trust that I will obtain your support for the validation of this royal decree-law. Thank you. [[Applauds]]
