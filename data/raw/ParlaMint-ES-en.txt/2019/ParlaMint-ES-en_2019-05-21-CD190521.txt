ParlaMint-ES_2019-05-21-CD190521.u40	Ladies and gentlemen, I would like to say a few words to you. Ladies and gentlemen, I begin with the traditional but deeply sincere expression of thanks, both to me and to the entire Bureau, for the confidence they have given us today. It is also a tradition that the President's first intervention highlights the main needs of our society and the fundamental tasks that correspond to this House, to the Congress of Deputies. Finally, I will also try to respect the tradition of speed of this intervention. To be a member of the Bureau of the Congress of Deputies is an honour and a responsibility that we all appreciate. In politics we operate on credit: in each election we receive in advance a deposit of trust, so that the acceptance of the charge entails the recognition of the debt and also the willingness to return it with interest. That return consists in the rigorous and committed fulfilment of our institutional mission, which is the internal government and the external representation of the House as a whole, which obliges us to always be impartial, impartial in protecting the rights of all Members of Parliament equally. However, the House government must not and cannot be neutral in everything that affects the constitutional mission of the Congress of Deputies. The Legislature is one of the most important pieces of our rule of law. Its autonomy from other powers, the sufficiency of the means at its disposal to carry out its work of legislation and control, and the dignity of the institution and its members depend to a large extent on the quality of our democracy. It is we, the Members of Parliament, the most interested and the most obliged to preserve the constitutional role of the House, not to degrade representatives or to trivialise parliamentary activity. Since its creation in Athenian, democracy has always had some spectacle, but that spectacle should not be that of shouting or disrespect, but that of fine intelligence and, where possible, the brilliant oratory and loyal defense of political positions; the other reduces representatives and offends those represented. [[applause]] There are many needs of a society like Spanish, those generated by globalization, climate change and technological change, inequality and poverty of an important part of our citizenship are some of the most evident. There are many goals ahead to make Spain freer, more socially just, more prosperous, more feminist and with more security and confidence in the future. It is not always within the policy's reach to address those needs and achieve those objectives with the desired urgency and effectiveness, but they will not find a solution without the joint and coordinated action of all, and that, ladies and gentlemen, is politics. Because democracy is not only contrast and confrontation, but mainly the building of broad consensus. It is therefore essential that we preserve rational and respectful deliberation as the basis of the agreement, and that is what we are going to do. Parliament is the place of the word and of representation, and we should both agree; we are the plural and diverse expression of a plural and diverse society, in Spain as a whole and in each of its territories. And beyond the symbolic understanding of the mandate of each Member, neither one of us individually nor one of our parties alone represents Spain alone, nor any of its territories, nor the will of all citizens. Each of us is of the people, but none of us is the people; always and everywhere there is another legitimate and different to that which we can only demand respect for the law. Only when Congress speaks with a unanimous voice should we say - and even cautiously - that it expresses the will of the people, of the whole people; only when it speaks from a broad consensus can we affirm that we are approaching that aspiration. This is a first exercise of honesty and transparency that would help us all to carry out our mission effectively. There are no shortcuts to our work as representatives; the way is law, reason and work, lonely and in common. We did not receive Spain in a state of nature but improved by the work of the generations that preceded us. The recent history of Spain and of all Europe is a history of conquests, peace, well-being, freedom, equality and coexistence; of a society that wants to build a shared future. That's the legacy of our elders. It is not exclusively for us, but also for the Spanish who will come after us; that is why we do not have the right to have it capriciously, but we are obliged to preserve it and improve it. A great pact of coexistence, our Constitution, is part of the inheritance received. Strengthening, expanding and making this pact effective is the task of every generation; today of ours. I'm sure we'll know how to leave those who come after an even better Spain than we've received. Many thanks, many graces, eskerrik asko, thank you very much, ladies and gentlemen.
