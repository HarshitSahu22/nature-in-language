i-c0ac993aafa6d90e-0	Ladies and gentlemen! A warm welcome back to the Chamber after a – hopefully – lovely, nice and eventful summer! Political work has already started in various ways, with travel, visits, meetings, political development, exercise writing and much more. I have seen that you have already been active for a number of weeks, and I can assure you that this also applies to the Presidency. Now is the time to start a new parliamentary year with confidence. I hope you all look forward to it as much as I do. The timetable is always pressed on a day like this, but since I have so few opportunities to speak to the whole House, here are a few comments under the heading that the President admonishes and informs. In my summer speech I stuck to horizontal issues and strategies. This is exactly the opposite. I will briefly, most recently in point form and without ranking, raise a few issues that I would like to ask you all to consider during the forthcoming Parliamentary Session. In order for you not to have to sit and take notes, it will also be sent out by e-mail. Here comes index number one: The Parliament should be a living meeting place, and it is good that the members of the Riksdag can take the initiative for different types of meetings. However, for the reasons set out above, I would like to remind everyone that Parliament's premises are to be used for meetings organised and held by Members and parties of the Riksdag. I sent this spring's e-mail to all of you about this, and it will be sent out again. Point number two, which is actually rather a request, is addressed to all of you who write your speeches on a computer in advance. It saves a lot of work for our minutes – those that used to be called parliamentary stenographers – if you take the habit of sending an e-mail with the text to the minutes notaries as soon as it is written, that is, before you give the speech. E-mail even if this will be sent to you. Peestick number three is about the Club Room here in the Parliament House. It is supposed to be a quiet and quiet room where Members should be able to sit quietly or converse in a low tone of conversation. So it is not a place where you can arrange meetings or hold media interviews. A completely different issue, which is not at all a touch stick, concerns the security work in Parliament. I would like to remind you that, as a Member, you can always turn to Parliament's security unit for support and advice in the area of security, for example in terms of threats and hatred, social media, travel security and cyber security. One tip is to contact the security unit for advice even if a police report has also been made of a threat, for example. Outside office hours, as you know, there is a contingency organization. Now some comments on debates and motions. We Presidents are very grateful if the requests in the debates are clear. It is therefore proposed to say that I should like to vote in favour of this reservation. We are therefore grateful, too, if you do not use expressions such as that you are taking a lift, and that you point out a certain reservation in particular, if you really believe that you are in favour of it – for the sake of clarity. One of the favourite expressions in the chamber debates is that of time gain. Linguistically speaking, it's pretty hard to understand-- time should win something, one might wonder. I think the viewers aren't always quite sure what we mean by that. In order to ensure that voting is not so long, we could instead say that only reservation x should be accepted. Then the same purpose has been achieved, but in an understandable way. One suggestion may be that you in the committees make it up to everyone who says first for the benefit of time in a debate to give them a cup of coffee. Incentive structure is called it. We Presidents have tried to protect the traditional way of speaking in this House, that is to say, we are talking to the President and not to each other. That, after all, is why all the speeches begin with the words, Mr President. We will continue to remind ourselves of this with caution. One reason for speaking to the President who has been put to me is that it will then be clearer for those who read the Minutes afterwards to whom the speaker actually refers. Another reason is that it slightly lowers the level of conflict in the parliamentary debates. This leads me to an appeal to everyone to keep a good tone in the debates in this House. We shall have different opinions, but we are all Members and colleagues, and it is important that we treat each other respectfully. Generally speaking, the tone of the House is good, but one time or another it gets rough. Then it is a good starting point to focus on the thing and not on the person. Speaking of chamber debates, it is, of course, important to use social media to tell us what is happening in this House's work, for example, during an ongoing debate, but it is also important to listen to the speaker. I would therefore appeal to all of you to maintain a good balance between attention to the speaker's seat and attention to the telephone. Finally, as you know, in order to counter long votes, the group leaders have agreed that, normally, each party should ask for only one reservation in each report. That agreement is essentially respected, and I would like to thank you all for that. Now we are out of touchsticks, exhortations and wishes! The opening of the Parliamentary Session is marked by celebrations and celebrations. Let us enjoy it and then take on the regular work tomorrow! Let me wish you all a really nice day. Thank you!
