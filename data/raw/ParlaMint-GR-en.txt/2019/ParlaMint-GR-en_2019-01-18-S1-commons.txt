I would like to say, however, that unfortunately - from the outset, of course - the government has repudiated parliamentary control. With few exceptions, which must be stressed, such as the Secretary of State for Labour, not all others come with excuses and not because of a genuine prerogative of mission or anything else. They violate both the Constitution and the Rules of Procedure of the House. On the substance of the matter, however, the problems do not come to the House. And so, Parliament's role cannot help solving the problems. The government, especially lately and after the vote of confidence, considers that it has solved all the problems and does not deal with the daily lives and problems facing every citizen. I've got a thing. As you have said, this issue has been discussed and the Minister has given excellent answers. That's why I'm coming back. There is no provision in the Rules of Procedure which says that, because it has been discussed before, it is not debated. That is not an excuse. The transport equivalent is a measure designed to strengthen the islands and address the permanent handicap to maritime isolation. Article 101 of the Constitution provides that it must apply to all islands. I would ask that we do not exclude Crete, which has already been excluded. And since January 1st, unfortunately, every month we lose 10 to 15 million for the economy of Crete and the Cretans. This cannot be answered by a telegram: 'It has been discussed again'. The Ministry of Shipping must be serious. On the other hand, I asked Mr Kouvelli, the Minister of State, as provided for by the Rules of Procedure, and not the State Secretary. That is how the Rules of Procedure provide. The Minister will tell us why he's not coming. He had to tell us he was in charge of alpha or beta or anything. I would therefore ask you, Mr President, as a member of the Conference of Presidents, to pass on to the President of this House that we cannot continue to deport Parliament from the members of the government with very few exceptions that, as I said, we must highlight them. Because they are the ones who do their duty and others are indifferent and disrespectful in relation to Parliament, in relation to the Constitution, in relation to their obligation and the mandate they have received to discuss and accept proposals to deal with the problems. What else can one say with such conducts: 'Have the matter been discussed before'? Thank you very much.
